# PNLE reasoning archetypes (summary)

Plain-language summary of `archetypes.json` (31 archetypes). All worked examples are original; none are real board items. Created from general nursing-education knowledge; no web sources were consulted (see Limits).

## Reasoning principles

- Maslow order: physiological (oxygen, circulation, fluids, nutrition, elimination, pain, rest) before safety/security, then love/belonging, esteem, self-actualization.
- ABC: airway before breathing before circulation. Airway problems come first even when bleeding is also present.
- Nursing process: assess before intervening in a stable client; evaluate after.
- Stable vs unstable: unstable, changing, new, or unexpected findings outrank stable, chronic, or expected ones.
- Acute before chronic; actual problem before potential problem.
- Least invasive and least restrictive effective option first.
- Safety: protect client and others; verify before acting (identifiers, orders, rights).
- Delegation: stable, routine, predictable tasks only; assessment, teaching, evaluation and unstable clients stay with the licensed nurse.
- Communication: reflect feelings, use open-ended prompts; avoid false reassurance, advice, 'why' questions, and changing the subject.
- Exceptions: complete airway obstruction, cardiac arrest, or other clear life threat overrides assess-first; act immediately.
- Exception: when the option calls the provider, do so only after you have your own assessment data, unless the client is unstable and needs help now.
- Teaching comes after physiological and safety needs are met, unless the teaching itself is the safety measure asked about.

## What each one tests

- **Priority need by Maslow (physiological first)**: Ranking client needs so physiological survival needs outrank safety, love/belonging and esteem needs.
- **ABC ordering (airway, breathing, circulation)**: Applying airway before breathing before circulation when more than one life threat is described.
- **First/initial action: assess before intervening**: Knowing that in a stable client the nursing process requires assessing first, and recognizing the rare exception when a clear emergency demands action.
- **Most appropriate response (therapeutic communication)**: Choosing the reply that is open-ended, reflects feelings, and keeps the client talking.
- **Which client needs immediate attention / should be seen first**: Triaging a set of clients by acuity, using stability and ABC rather than room order.
- **Delegation and assignment**: Matching tasks to the right worker based on stability, predictability, and the nursing-judgment skills required.
- **Evaluation of teaching: statement showing understanding**: Judging whether the client's own words or return demonstration show accurate, safe understanding.
- **Medication safety (rights of administration)**: Applying the rights of medication administration and the safe sequence of checks.
- **High-alert medication reasoning (no doses)**: Reasoning about safe handling of medications with narrow safety margins using principles, not numbers.
- **Identifying the abnormal / reportable finding**: Telling expected findings from those signalling a problem for the given condition or age.
- **Expected vs adverse effect of treatment**: Differentiating a therapeutic or common effect from a toxic or dangerous one.
- **Infection control and isolation precautions**: Matching transmission route to the precaution, and applying standard precautions to everyone.
- **Safety and fall prevention**: Choosing the least restrictive effective safety intervention.
- **Ethics, client rights and informed consent**: Respecting autonomy, confidentiality, and the nurse's role as advocate.
- **Community health prioritization**: Choosing community actions by level of prevention and greatest population benefit.
- **Maternal-newborn reasoning**: Applying ABC and risk reasoning to mother and baby, separating normal changes from danger signs.
- **Pediatric growth, development and safety**: Matching nursing actions and safety teaching to the child's developmental stage.
- **Mental health crisis and safety**: Keeping the client and others safe first, then applying therapeutic communication.
- **Documentation and reporting**: Accurate, timely, objective documentation and effective reporting.
- **Discharge teaching and home-care readiness**: Selecting the safety-critical teaching and confirming readiness for home.
- **Selecting the best response to a distressed client**: Responding to anger, fear, denial, or grief with presence and acknowledgement rather than argument.
- **'Except' / negative stem questions**: Finding the one false, unsafe, or inappropriate option when three are correct.
- **Acute before chronic**: Prioritizing new, sudden problems over long-standing or stable ones.
- **Stable vs unstable**: Separating clients whose condition is predictable from those who are changing.
- **Least invasive intervention first**: Choosing simple, safe, independent nursing actions before invasive or high-risk ones.
- **Scope of practice and legal boundaries**: Recognizing what a nurse can do independently, what requires an order, and what must be refused or escalated.
- **Emergency exception: act before assessing**: Knowing that a clearly stated life threat overrides 'assess first'.
- **Recognizing a postoperative complication**: Spotting early signs of hemorrhage, infection, respiratory or circulatory compromise.
- **Qualitative laboratory-value interpretation**: Interpreting results as normal, concerning, or critical and linking them to client risk, without memorizing exact numbers.
- **Health promotion and prevention levels**: Classifying activities as primary, secondary or tertiary prevention.
- **Nursing process step identification**: Identifying which phase of the nursing process an action belongs to.

## Limits

- PNLE area mapping (NP1-NP5) follows the owner's note in CLAUDE.md and is unconfirmed; use `any` where unsure.
- Difficulty is a 1-5 scale chosen by the author, not measured.
- Worked examples are short and simple by design; a generator should expand them with more data and stronger distractors.
- No drug doses or guideline numbers are included. Facts are widely standard; flag anything AI-added for textbook check.
- Not clinically reviewed by a nurse educator.
