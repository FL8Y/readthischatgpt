# Anxiety — PNLE-Focused Research

## Core concepts
Anxiety is apprehension associated with perceived threat; assess severity, triggers, duration, physical symptoms, avoidance, impairment, and medical/substance contributors.

## Assessment and clinical reasoning
Mild anxiety may improve learning; moderate anxiety narrows attention; severe anxiety markedly limits problem-solving; panic-level distress may disrupt organized behavior. These are teaching descriptors, not diagnostic tests. During acute distress, stay with the patient as appropriate, use short statements, reduce stimulation, and coach slow comfortable breathing if tolerated.

## Nursing priorities, safety, and PNLE distinctions
Rule out medical emergencies such as cardiopulmonary problems, hypoglycemia, medication effects, or intoxication when symptoms warrant. For persistent generalized anxiety or panic disorder, WHO mhGAP 2023 supports CBT-based psychological interventions and consideration of SSRIs for adults after clinical assessment.

Apply the nursing process and prioritize immediate threats to life and safety over routine teaching. Use ABCs when physiologic instability is present and Maslow as a guide rather than a rigid rule. Individualize care, preserve dignity and rights, document observable findings, and escalate according to local clinical protocols.

## Philippine context
Republic Act No. 11036 (Philippine Mental Health Act, 2018) establishes a rights-based national mental health framework. Apply the law and its implementing rules to rights, informed consent, confidentiality, access, and least-restrictive care; consult the actual statutory text and facility policy for case-specific requirements. International guidance does not automatically override Philippine law or local scope-of-practice rules.
