# Anxiety-Related Disorders — PNLE Research File

## 1. Scope and diagnostic boundaries
Anxiety disorders involve excessive fear/anxiety and related avoidance or behavioral changes that create clinically meaningful distress or impairment. Anxiety also occurs in OCD, trauma-related disorders, somatic symptom disorder, depression, substance use and medical conditions; these are related but not interchangeable. Diagnosis requires the full symptom pattern, duration, context, impairment and exclusion of relevant alternatives; one symptom or screening score is not enough.

## 2. Clinical comparison
| Presentation | Core pattern | Assessment focus |
|---|---|---|
| Generalized anxiety disorder (GAD) | Excessive, difficult-to-control worry across multiple areas over an extended period, with possible tension, restlessness, fatigue, sleep and concentration symptoms | Duration, themes, controllability, function, coping, medical/substance contributors |
| Panic attack/panic disorder | Panic attack: sudden surge of intense fear/discomfort with physical/cognitive symptoms; panic disorder: recurrent unexpected attacks plus persistent concern or maladaptive behavior change | Chest pain/dyspnea/syncope red flags, fear of recurrence, avoidance, substances and medical mimics |
| Social anxiety disorder | Fear of scrutiny or negative evaluation and avoidance/distress in social/performance situations | Context, impairment, avoidance, comorbid depression/substance use |
| Specific phobia | Marked fear related to particular object/situation with avoidance/intense distress | Trigger, context, functional effect, readiness for planned exposure-based care |
| OCD | Intrusive unwanted obsessions and/or repetitive compulsions intended to reduce distress/prevent a feared event | Time consumed, ritual cycle, insight, impairment, distress and suicide risk |
| Trauma-related disorder | Symptoms linked to traumatic exposure, possibly intrusion, avoidance, negative cognition/mood and arousal | Trauma connection, symptom duration, dissociation, ongoing danger/safety |
| Somatic symptom disorder | Distressing bodily symptoms plus excessive related thoughts/feelings/behaviors | Appropriate medical assessment, distress/function, health anxiety, non-dismissive approach |
| Substance/medication-induced anxiety | Anxiety temporally associated with intoxication, withdrawal or medication | Exact agent, last use/dose change, autonomic signs, medical urgency |

## 3. Assessment and differential safety
Begin with medical stability when symptoms warrant. New severe chest pain, dyspnea, hypoxia, syncope, focal neurologic deficit, seizure, confusion, fever/hyperthermia or suspected poisoning/withdrawal requires urgent medical evaluation. Depending on symptoms/history, assess possible arrhythmia, asthma, thyroid dysfunction, hypoglycemia, medication reactions and substance effects.

Ask onset/course, triggers, avoidance, sleep, function, trauma, language/cultural context, prescribed/OTC/herbal products, alcohol/caffeine/stimulants and other substances. Assess depression, suicide/self-harm risk, psychosis, comorbidity and safeguarding concerns. Use an interpreter or accessible communication if needed.

## 4. Treatment principles
- **Psychological care:** CBT and disorder-specific interventions are useful across several conditions. Exposure-based treatment is used for phobias; ERP is a specialized CBT component for OCD; trauma-focused CBT and EMDR are trauma-related treatments delivered by trained providers. Do not treat all “anxiety” as one diagnosis.
- **Medication:** SSRIs/SNRIs may be options for selected disorders, depending on prior response, risk, patient preference, interactions and local guidance. Discuss gradual benefit and monitor early activation, agitation, sleep change, adverse effects, discontinuation symptoms and suicidality where indicated.
- **Avoid routine inappropriate prescribing:** NICE CG113 advises against benzodiazepines for GAD except short-term crisis use and against benzodiazepines as treatment for panic disorder. NICE does not recommend antipsychotics routinely for GAD in primary care. These are UK recommendations; contextualize locally.
- **Function and support:** sleep/routine, gradual return to valued activities, coping skills, family/support involvement with consent and treatment for comorbid conditions are important. Exposure is planned collaboratively, never imposed by nursing staff as a test of courage.

## 5. Nursing care and rationale
1. **Match communication to severity.** During severe anxiety, use brief concrete statements; later, when the person can concentrate, explain treatment and choices.
2. **Validate distress without confirming catastrophic conclusions.** Ask what the person fears, what evidence they notice and what would help them feel safe.
3. **Assess physical contributors.** Anxiety-like presentations may be due to hypoxia, pain, medication effects, intoxication/withdrawal or acute disease.
4. **Support collaborative care.** Explore adherence barriers, costs/access, beliefs and preferences; do not shame avoidance or symptoms.
5. **Monitor safety.** Assess self-harm risk when depression, trauma, severe agitation or hopelessness is present; escalate imminent risk and medical red flags.
6. **Provide education within scope.** Teach medication use and adverse-effect reporting from the specific label/care plan, gradual coping practice and follow-up.
7. **Document objective findings.** Include symptoms, triggers, function, observed physical status, risks, interventions and response.

## 6. Medication safety
Benzodiazepines can cause sedation, impaired coordination, falls, tolerance/dependence and dangerous CNS/respiratory depression with alcohol, opioids or other sedatives. Avoid self-medication or unsupervised combinations. SSRIs/SNRIs may cause initial activation, interactions and discontinuation symptoms; screen for bipolar history. Suspected serotonin syndrome, severe allergic reaction, severe activation/mania, overdose or imminent suicidal intent requires urgent escalation. Verify each drug's contraindications, interactions, pregnancy/lactation, age and organ-function cautions against current product labeling and local formulary. Do not supply universal dosing.

## 7. PNLE priorities
- Physiological red flags take precedence over labeling symptoms as panic/anxiety.
- Differentiate GAD, panic, phobia, OCD, trauma-related, somatic-symptom and substance-induced presentations.
- ERP is not the same as trauma-focused CBT; neither is improvised coercive exposure.
- During severe distress, immediate safety and communication precede lengthy teaching.
- Medication class summaries do not override drug-specific contraindications.

## 8. Philippine context
RA 11036 establishes a rights-based framework for mental-health care. Respect dignity, autonomy, informed consent, confidentiality within legal/safety limits and nursing scope. NICE is UK guidance; medication authorization/availability and referral pathways require confirmation against current Philippine DOH/local practice and facility protocol.
