# Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD) — PNLE Research File

## 1. Definition and symptom pattern
OCD involves obsessions, compulsions or both. **Obsessions** are recurrent intrusive thoughts, images or urges that are unwanted and usually distressing. **Compulsions** are repetitive behaviors or mental acts the person feels driven to perform to reduce distress or prevent a feared outcome; they are excessive or not realistically connected to the feared event. The symptoms are time-consuming or cause significant distress/impairment and are not better explained by a substance, medical cause or another disorder.

Common patterns include contamination/washing, doubt/checking, ordering/symmetry, taboo intrusive thoughts and mental rituals. Content varies. Unwanted aggressive or sexual thoughts do not automatically indicate intent; assess the person's intent and safety separately rather than making assumptions from thought content alone.

## 2. Assessment
Ask sensitively about thoughts/images/urges, rituals, avoidance, time spent, distress, insight, impact on work/school/relationships, family accommodation, onset/course, prior treatment, mood, suicidality, tic symptoms, medication/substance use and function. A tool such as the Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS) may structure symptom assessment and follow-up where staff are trained; it does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

Distinguish:
- **OCD vs psychosis:** insight in OCD varies; assess whether thoughts are intrusive/unwanted, degree of conviction, associated rituals, other psychotic signs, context and function.
- **OCD vs generalized worry:** worry often concerns everyday problems; OCD may involve intrusive themes and compulsive rituals, though overlap is possible.
- **OCD vs obsessive-compulsive personality traits:** a pervasive personality pattern is not synonymous with intrusive obsessions plus compulsions.
- **OCD vs autism-related repetitive behavior:** consider developmental history, sensory regulation, function, distress and changes from baseline.
- **OCD vs induced symptoms:** assess medication/stimulant/substance exposure and medical/neurologic contributors, especially with abrupt onset.

Assess self-harm/suicide risk particularly with severe impairment, comorbid depression, hopelessness or early antidepressant activation/dose changes. Ask directly; do not infer risk from OCD diagnosis alone.

## 3. Evidence-based treatment
Cognitive behavioral therapy with **exposure and response prevention (ERP)** is a first-line psychological treatment. ERP is collaboratively planned exposure to triggers while the person practices refraining from compulsions, under the guidance of a trained therapist. It is not forced exposure by nursing staff. SSRIs may be used depending on impairment, prior response, preference and clinical plan. NICE CG31 distinguishes mild, moderate and severe functional impairment and includes CBT/ERP, SSRI or combined treatment pathways. Children and young people need developmentally adapted treatment with appropriate family/carer involvement.

## 4. Nursing interventions and rationale
1. **Validate distress and maintain dignity.** Acknowledge how exhausting symptoms can be without confirming the feared catastrophe as fact.
2. **Do not shame, ridicule, or suddenly block compulsions without a coordinated plan.** Unplanned ritual prevention can sharply increase distress; the nurse should support the collaboratively planned treatment approach.
3. **Avoid enabling accommodation.** Repeated reassurance or joining rituals may maintain the cycle. Use agreed consistent, compassionate responses.
4. **Assess practical effects.** Determine time spent, sleep, nutrition, skin damage from washing, daily functioning, relationships and ability to perform self-care.
5. **Support achievable goals.** Reinforce treatment engagement and effort rather than judging symptom frequency or blaming the person.
6. **Coordinate family support.** With consent and within law, explain the condition, treatment plan and how to respond without arguing, shaming or reinforcing rituals.
7. **Monitor medication safety.** Report new/worsening agitation, akathisia/restlessness, suicidal thinking, severe adverse effects or manic change, especially after starting/dose changes.

## 5. Medication safety
SSRIs can produce gastrointestinal symptoms, sexual dysfunction, sleep changes, early activation, discontinuation symptoms and agent-specific risks such as hyponatremia, bleeding or interactions. Do not discontinue abruptly without a prescribed plan. Screen for bipolar history. Clomipramine may be considered in selected cases after an adequate SSRI trial but has anticholinergic effects, orthostasis, conduction toxicity and serious risk in overdose; it requires clinician-led prescribing and monitoring.

Serious agitation/akathisia, new suicidal intent, overdose, severe allergic reaction or suspected serotonin syndrome requires urgent escalation. Drug choice, washout, interactions and contraindications are agent-specific; verify current label and local formulary.

## 6. PNLE high-yield distinctions
- Obsessions = intrusive thoughts/images/urges; compulsions = repetitive behaviors/mental acts.
- A distressing unwanted thought does not by itself show intent to act.
- ERP is planned treatment, not a coercive nursing technique.
- Severe functional impairment, self-injury from rituals, malnutrition/dehydration or suicidal intent may require urgent assessment.
- Monitor early SSRI activation and suicidal thinking; do not assume increasing agitation is harmless.
- Avoid reassurance cycles and shame; work consistently with the agreed care plan.

## 7. Philippine context
Apply RA 11036 rights-based care, informed consent, confidentiality and non-discrimination, plus current professional scope and local referral pathways. NICE CG31 is UK guidance; prescribing, product labeling and service availability should be checked in the Philippines.
