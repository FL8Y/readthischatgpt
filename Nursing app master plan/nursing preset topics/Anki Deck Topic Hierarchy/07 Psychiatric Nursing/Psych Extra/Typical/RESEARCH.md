# Typical Antipsychotics — Category Research

## Core concepts
Typical (first-generation) antipsychotics primarily antagonize dopamine D2 receptors and can reduce psychotic symptoms. Extrapyramidal symptom risk and prolactin effects vary by agent, dose, and patient factors; no class-level generalization replaces the specific drug label.

## Assessment and monitoring
Monitor therapeutic response, adherence barriers, orthostatic symptoms, sedation, anticholinergic effects, and movement symptoms. Distinguish acute dystonia, akathisia, drug-induced parkinsonism, and tardive dyskinesia. Fever, severe rigidity, altered mental status, and autonomic instability may indicate neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS).

## Nursing priorities
Airway/swallowing involvement with acute dystonia and suspected NMS require emergency escalation. Screen and document new involuntary movements; follow the medication-specific monitoring plan. Do not stop or change medication without prescriber guidance.

## Philippine context
Follow RA 11036 rights-based care, local formulary, current product labeling, and facility protocols.
