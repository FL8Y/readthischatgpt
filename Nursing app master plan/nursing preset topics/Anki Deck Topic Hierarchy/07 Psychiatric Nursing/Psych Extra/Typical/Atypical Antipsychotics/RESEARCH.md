# Typical vs Atypical Antipsychotics — PNLE Comparison

## Core comparison
First-generation (typical) antipsychotics often have greater extrapyramidal symptom liability, while many second-generation (atypical) agents have greater weight/metabolic risk. These are trends, not universal rules; individual medications differ in EPS, prolactin, QT risk, sedation, orthostasis, anticholinergic effects, and metabolic impact.

## Assessment and monitoring
Assess symptom response, movement symptoms (dystonia, akathisia, parkinsonism, tardive dyskinesia), orthostatic symptoms, weight, glucose/lipids, sedation, and medication-specific risks. Clozapine requires specialized blood monitoring for severe neutropenia and prompt assessment for fever/infection, myocarditis symptoms, severe constipation/ileus, and seizures.

## Safety priorities
Fever, severe rigidity, altered mental status, and autonomic instability may indicate neuroleptic malignant syndrome, a medical emergency. Verify current product labeling, monitoring rules, and local formulary; do not treat all antipsychotics as interchangeable or change medication without prescriber direction.

## Philippine context
Apply RA 11036 rights-based care and local policy, including informed consent, confidentiality, and least-restrictive safe treatment.
