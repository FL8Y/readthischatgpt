# Medications for Depression — PNLE Medication Safety File

> Educational reference only. Do not use this class overview to select, start, stop, or change an individual patient's medication. Confirm the exact product label, local formulary, prescriber plan, and current interactions.

## 1. Treatment principles
Antidepressants may be used for major depressive disorder and selected other conditions; psychotherapy and combined approaches may also be indicated. Selection depends on symptoms, prior response, comorbidities, self-harm/overdose risk, adverse-effect profile, interactions, pregnancy/lactation, age, renal/hepatic function, availability, and patient preference. Follow-up is essential, especially after initiation or dose changes.

Before assuming unipolar depression, assess for past mania/hypomania, mixed features, substance use, medication effects, and medical contributors. Antidepressant-induced activation or mania requires prompt reassessment and prescriber review.

## 2. Major classes compared
| Class | Examples | Mechanism (simplified) | High-yield safety focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| SSRIs | Sertraline, fluoxetine, escitalopram, citalopram | Reduce serotonin reuptake | GI effects, sexual dysfunction, hyponatremia risk in susceptible patients, bleeding risk with NSAIDs/anticoagulants, serotonin syndrome; QT risk varies by agent |
| SNRIs | Venlafaxine, duloxetine | Reduce serotonin/norepinephrine reuptake | SSRI-like risks plus blood-pressure effects, discontinuation symptoms; renal/hepatic issues are agent-specific |
| TCAs | Amitriptyline, nortriptyline, imipramine | Monoamine reuptake inhibition and other receptor effects | Anticholinergic effects, orthostasis, conduction/arrhythmia risk, dangerous overdose toxicity |
| MAOIs | Phenelzine, tranylcypromine; other agents vary | Inhibit monoamine oxidase | Serious drug/food interactions; serotonin toxicity or hypertensive reactions; washout periods are agent-specific |
| Other agents | Bupropion, mirtazapine, trazodone | Mechanisms vary | Bupropion: seizure risk; mirtazapine: sedation/weight gain; trazodone: sedation/orthostasis among other risks |

## 3. Contraindications, interactions and serious adverse effects

### SSRIs/SNRIs and serotonin toxicity
- **Avoid contraindicated combinations.** The accessed DailyMed sertraline label contraindicates concomitant MAOI use, use within 14 days of stopping an MAOI, pimozide co-administration, and known hypersensitivity. Washout intervals for other switches are medication-specific; never assume one interval fits every antidepressant.
- **Serotonin syndrome** is potentially life-threatening. Warning findings may include agitation/confusion, diaphoresis/hyperthermia, tachycardia or labile blood pressure, tremor, clonus, hyperreflexia, rigidity, seizures, or GI symptoms. New symptoms after serotonergic combinations or dose changes require urgent assessment.
- Review serotonergic co-medications, including certain opioids (for example tramadol/fentanyl), other antidepressants, lithium, triptans, buspirone and St John's wort. The exact contraindication or warning depends on the medication and label.
- Bleeding risk can increase with aspirin, NSAIDs, antiplatelets, warfarin or other anticoagulants. Review the complete medication list; do not independently stop prescribed medicines.
- Screen for bipolar disorder. Monitor for activation/mania and worsening mood/suicidal thinking after initiation or dosage changes.

### Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs)
Overdose can be fatal because of cardiotoxicity, seizures and hypotension. If overdose risk is high, raise safe supply/storage and monitoring arrangements with the treating team. Monitor orthostasis/falls, urinary retention, constipation, blurred vision and confusion, especially in older adults.

### Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs)
Avoid contraindicated drug combinations and observe the exact product's washout rules. Tyramine-related hypertensive reactions and interactions with serotonergic/sympathomimetic drugs are major risks. Food and medication advice must be agent-specific; do not provide a generic complete “safe-food list” without checking the current label.

### Bupropion — explicit contraindications in the accessed U.S. label
DailyMed labeling for bupropion tablets lists contraindications including seizure disorder; current or prior anorexia nervosa or bulimia nervosa; abrupt discontinuation of alcohol, benzodiazepines, barbiturates or antiepileptic drugs; and prohibited MAOI combinations/intervals. Seizure risk is also affected by head injury, metabolic disorders, hypoxia and concomitant drugs that lower seizure threshold. Formulations and national labeling can differ; confirm the exact product locally.

## 4. Nursing checks and monitoring
Before administration, verify order/product/formulation, current medicines (including OTC/herbal agents), allergies, history of mania, seizure/eating disorders where relevant, substance withdrawal, pregnancy/lactation, age, renal/hepatic function, and interactions. Document baseline symptoms, safety concerns and the agreed monitoring plan.

After initiation or a change, assess response, adherence barriers, sleep, agitation/activation, suicide risk, adverse effects and discontinuation symptoms if doses are missed or stopped. Escalate suspected severe reactions; serotonin syndrome, serious overdose, seizure, severe allergic reaction or imminent suicidal intent is not a routine teaching issue.

Patient education: do not stop suddenly without a plan; do not double a missed dose unless directed; disclose OTC/herbal medicines; and seek urgent care for severe agitation/confusion with fever/clonus, seizure, collapse, severe allergic symptoms or new suicidal intent. Do not give a universal dose or promise a fixed onset; instructions are agent- and patient-specific.

## 5. Special populations
- **Children/adolescents:** follow current product label and local guidance; monitor for clinical worsening or new suicidal thoughts/behavior.
- **Older adults:** watch for falls, hyponatremia, bleeding, interactions, anticholinergic burden and confusion.
- **Pregnancy/lactation:** weigh individual risks of untreated illness and medication exposure with the treating clinician; do not abruptly discontinue solely because pregnancy is discovered.
- **Renal/hepatic impairment:** adjustment may be required depending on agent; check the product label.
- **Substance withdrawal/eating disorders/seizures:** bupropion is contraindicated in several such situations under the accessed U.S. label.

## 6. PNLE priorities
- Serotonergic medicine + agitation/confusion + hyperthermia/clonus/hyperreflexia = urgent assessment for serotonin syndrome.
- Past mania/hypomania should prompt reassessment before labeling symptoms uncomplicated MDD.
- Bupropion with seizure disorder, current/prior anorexia or bulimia, or abrupt alcohol/sedative/barbiturate withdrawal is a high-risk contraindication pattern in the accessed product label.
- TCAs can be dangerous in overdose.
- Distinguish common adverse effects from severe toxicity; escalate red flags.

## 7. Philippine context
Respect medication consent, confidentiality, documentation, patient rights under RA 11036, and nursing scope. Medication withholding/administration must follow authorized orders and local policy. Escalate a clinically relevant contraindication, severe reaction or unsafe order according to protocol; do not make independent prescribing decisions.
