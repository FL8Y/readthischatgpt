# Schizophrenia — PNLE Research Notes

## Core concept and manifestations
Schizophrenia is a psychotic disorder involving a combination of symptoms such as delusions, hallucinations, disorganized thinking/speech, disorganized or catatonic behavior, and negative symptoms (e.g., diminished emotional expression, avolition, alogia, anhedonia, asociality). Diagnosis requires duration, functional impact, and exclusion of mood, substance-induced, medical, and other causes; a single psychotic symptom is not sufficient.

## Assessment and nursing care
Assess mental status, baseline function, sleep, nutrition/hydration, self-care, substance use, medication history, side effects, suicide risk, command hallucinations, and risk of harm to others when indicated. Use a calm, non-confrontational approach. Do not validate delusions as factual, but do not ridicule or argue; focus on feelings, safety, and observable reality. For example: “I understand this feels real and frightening to you. I don't see anyone here threatening you. What would help you feel safer right now?”

## Medication classes and safety
Antipsychotics reduce psychotic symptoms but differ in adverse-effect profiles. Monitor response, adherence barriers, orthostatic symptoms, sedation, weight/metabolic parameters, and movement symptoms according to the medication and local protocol.
- **Extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS):** acute dystonia, akathisia, and drug-induced parkinsonism; new severe dystonia involving airway/swallowing requires emergency response.
- **Tardive dyskinesia:** potentially persistent involuntary movements; screen using an appropriate structured tool and report new movements.
- **Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS):** rare emergency associated with antipsychotics; fever, rigidity, altered mental status, and autonomic instability require immediate medical evaluation and escalation.
- **Metabolic effects:** some second-generation agents increase weight, glucose, and lipid risk; monitor as recommended.
- **Clozapine:** requires strict blood monitoring for severe neutropenia risk and monitoring for myocarditis, seizures, constipation/ileus, and metabolic effects per current label/local protocol. Fever or infection symptoms warrant prompt evaluation.
Do not stop, start, or alter medication without prescriber direction. Confirm specific contraindications and monitoring intervals against current product labeling.

## Safety and PNLE priorities
Command hallucinations require assessment of content, perceived authority, intent to obey, access to means, and immediate safety—not automatic assumptions of violence. Sudden onset of psychosis, fluctuating attention, fever, neurologic signs, or intoxication/withdrawal should trigger assessment for medical/substance causes. Use the least restrictive safe response; de-escalation and respect for rights remain essential.
