# Schizophrenia — PNLE Research File

## 1. Definition and clinical concept
Schizophrenia is a psychotic disorder characterized by a combination of changes in perception, thought, behavior, affect, cognition, and functioning. Diagnosis requires a structured clinical assessment of symptom pattern, duration, functional impact, and alternative explanations. A hallucination or delusion alone is not sufficient to establish schizophrenia.

## 2. Symptom groups
| Group | Findings | Nursing significance |
|---|---|---|
| Positive symptoms | Delusions, hallucinations, disorganized speech/thought, markedly disorganized or catatonic behavior | Assess distress, reality testing, command content, behavior, medical/substance causes, and safety |
| Negative symptoms | Reduced emotional expression, avolition, alogia, anhedonia, asociality | May impair self-care and functioning; avoid mislabeling symptoms as laziness or refusal |
| Cognitive symptoms | Problems with attention, working memory, processing speed, executive function | Use short clear instructions, repetition, concrete goals, and functional supports |
| Mood/sleep symptoms | Depression, anxiety, insomnia, irritability | Assess comorbidity and suicide risk; these symptoms can affect recovery and medication adherence |

## 3. Etiology, risk, and differential assessment
Schizophrenia reflects complex interactions of genetic vulnerability, neurodevelopmental factors, and environmental exposures; there is no single cause and no nursing assessment can establish the full etiology. Consider mood disorders with psychotic features, substance/medication-induced psychosis, delirium, neurologic illness, trauma-related symptoms, sleep deprivation, and culturally contextual beliefs.

Obtain onset and course, baseline function, sleep, nutrition, substance use, medication history, family/collateral history where authorized, trauma/context, self-care, ability to meet basic needs, and risk to self/others. New onset with fluctuating attention, fever, neurologic changes, altered consciousness, or intoxication/withdrawal warrants medical assessment rather than assuming primary psychosis.

## 4. Mental status and safety assessment
Assess:
- Appearance, behavior, psychomotor activity, speech, mood/affect, thought process/content, perception, cognition, insight and judgment.
- Suicidal thoughts, intent, plan, access to means, prior behavior, hopelessness, depression, command hallucinations, and ability to maintain safety.
- For command hallucinations, ask what the voice says, whether it instructs harm, how powerful/authoritative it feels, whether the person expects to comply, and what might help keep everyone safe. Do not infer violence solely from diagnosis or hallucinations.
- Hydration, nutrition, sleep, hygiene, function, supports, housing, substance use, and medication adverse effects.

## 5. Nursing interventions and rationale
1. **Build a consistent therapeutic relationship.** Use calm, respectful, simple language and predictable follow-through; consistency lowers confusion and supports trust.
2. **Respond to hallucinations without endorsing them as objective fact.** Example: “I don't hear the voice, but I can see this is frightening. Is it telling you to do anything unsafe?” This validates distress and assesses risk without reinforcing the belief.
3. **Do not argue aggressively with delusions.** Acknowledge emotion, state your perception calmly, and redirect to shared reality and immediate needs. Confrontation can increase mistrust and agitation.
4. **Support function, not only symptom reduction.** Break tasks into steps; encourage self-care, sleep, meals, hydration, social connection and rehabilitation according to ability.
5. **Support shared decisions and adherence.** Ask about adverse effects, beliefs, cost/access, forgetfulness, and preferences; communicate concerns to the prescriber instead of assuming “noncompliance.”
6. **Plan discharge and relapse prevention.** Identify early warning signs, follow-up, crisis plan, available community resources, family education with consent, and how to access help promptly.

## 6. Antipsychotic therapy and high-risk adverse effects
Antipsychotics are prescribed to reduce psychotic symptoms; choice depends on response, adverse-effect profile, comorbidity, prior treatment, access, and patient preference. Nursing checks include current medicines, allergies, orthostatic symptoms/falls, sedation, movement symptoms, weight/metabolic parameters where indicated, and relevant ECG/laboratory monitoring from the specific label and local protocol. Do not prescribe, discontinue, or adjust doses independently.

| Problem | Recognition | Nursing response |
|---|---|---|
| Acute dystonia | Sustained painful muscle contraction; neck/jaw/tongue/eye deviation; airway/swallowing involvement can occur | Airway or swallowing involvement is an emergency; alert clinician and follow emergency orders/protocol |
| Akathisia | Inner restlessness, pacing, inability to sit still | Assess rather than assuming anxiety/aggression; notify prescriber because it can be intensely distressing |
| Drug-induced parkinsonism | Tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, shuffling gait | Fall precautions, assess onset and severity, report for treatment review |
| Tardive dyskinesia | Repetitive involuntary movements, often mouth/tongue/face but may affect limbs/trunk | Document and report; use structured monitoring per protocol; do not dismiss as a habit |
| Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS) | Fever, severe rigidity, altered mental status, autonomic instability; may include elevated CK | Medical emergency: stop further exposure only per emergency protocol/clinician direction and escalate immediately; supportive medical treatment is required |
| Metabolic effects | Weight gain, dyslipidemia, hyperglycemia | Baseline/periodic metabolic monitoring and education matched to drug, risk and local protocol |
| Orthostatic hypotension/sedation | Dizziness, falls, reduced alertness | Check orthostatic symptoms, fall risk, and level of sedation; advise slow position changes where appropriate |

### Clozapine — special safety profile
Clozapine is used for selected patients, including treatment-resistant schizophrenia and in some settings to reduce recurrent suicidal behavior in schizophrenia/schizoaffective disorder. It can cause severe neutropenia, myocarditis/pericarditis/cardiomyopathy, seizures, orthostatic hypotension/syncope, metabolic effects and severe gastrointestinal hypomotility/ileus. Follow current local product labeling and mandated monitoring requirements; do not assume U.S. REMS logistics are identical to Philippine requirements. Fever/infection symptoms, chest pain/dyspnea/palpitations, seizure, or severe constipation/abdominal distension require prompt clinical evaluation. Severe constipation is not a trivial side effect.

## 7. Patient/family education and recovery
Explain that symptom control may take time and medication is only one part of care. Encourage regular review, discussion of adverse effects, sleep routines, avoidance of intoxicants that worsen symptoms, and early help-seeking for relapse signs. Include family/support persons only with the person's consent or as permitted by law and safety needs. Use person-first language and ask the individual what recovery means to them.

## 8. Philippine context and ethical safeguards
RA 11036 (Philippine Mental Health Act, 2018) establishes a rights-based framework for mental-health care. Respect dignity, autonomy, informed consent, confidentiality and freedom from discrimination/coercion; capacity and emergency decisions must be assessed under applicable law and policy rather than presumed from diagnosis. Follow current Philippine regulations, professional scope, facility policy and local formulary. This file is educational, not legal advice.

## 9. PNLE high-yield distinctions
- A new mental-status change with inattention/fever/altered consciousness requires medical differential assessment.
- Command hallucinations require content/intent/safety assessment; diagnosis alone does not predict violence.
- Negative symptoms are not synonymous with unwillingness.
- Fever + rigidity + altered mental status on antipsychotic therapy is an emergency red flag for NMS.
- Clozapine requires serious attention to blood monitoring, myocarditis symptoms, and bowel function.
- Use Maslow and ABCs appropriately: immediate airway/medical danger or imminent harm outranks routine psychoeducation.
