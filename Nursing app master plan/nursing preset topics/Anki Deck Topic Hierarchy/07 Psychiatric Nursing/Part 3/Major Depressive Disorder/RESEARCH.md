# Major Depressive Disorder — PNLE Research Notes

## Core concept
Major depressive disorder (MDD) involves a persistent depressive episode with clinically significant distress or functional impairment. Diagnosis requires a full clinical assessment, including duration, symptom pattern, impairment, differential diagnoses, substance/medication effects, and medical causes; a symptom checklist alone is not sufficient.

## Common manifestations
Depressed or irritable mood, diminished interest/pleasure, changes in sleep/appetite/weight, psychomotor change, fatigue, impaired concentration/decision-making, guilt/worthlessness, and thoughts of death or suicide. Presentation varies by age, culture, and individual; somatic complaints may be prominent.

## Assessment and immediate priorities
- Assess suicide/self-harm thoughts directly, including intent, plan, access to means, recent behavior, and ability to stay safe.
- Evaluate severity, functioning, supports, substance use, psychotic symptoms, agitation, and prior episodes/treatment.
- Screen for past mania/hypomania before assuming unipolar depression or initiating an antidepressant plan; bipolar disorder changes treatment decisions.
- Investigate possible medical or medication-related contributors as indicated.
- Urgently escalate imminent risk, psychotic depression with severe impairment, inability to maintain hydration/nutrition, catatonia, or other medical instability.

## Treatment overview
Evidence-based care can include psychotherapy, antidepressant medication, combined treatment, and selected specialist interventions depending on severity, history, preference, and availability. Nursing care includes therapeutic engagement, monitoring sleep/nutrition/hydration/function, supporting adherence and follow-up, and teaching that many antidepressants take several weeks for full benefit. Do not advise abrupt discontinuation.

## Medication safety — general principles
- SSRIs/SNRIs: monitor for worsening agitation or suicidal thinking, especially early in treatment or after dose changes; assess bleeding risk and interactions as appropriate. Serotonin syndrome is an emergency concern with serotonergic combinations and may include mental-status change, autonomic instability, hyperthermia, tremor, clonus, or hyperreflexia.
- MAOIs: serious interactions with serotonergic drugs and certain sympathomimetics/tyramine-containing foods can cause dangerous reactions; require prescriber/pharmacist review and washout rules.
- Tricyclic antidepressants: anticholinergic effects, orthostatic hypotension, conduction abnormalities, and toxicity in overdose make overdose risk assessment important.
- Bupropion: seizure risk; generally contraindicated in seizure disorders and current/prior anorexia nervosa or bulimia nervosa under product labeling.
- All classes require individual review of contraindications, interactions, pregnancy/lactation, age, renal/hepatic function, and current product labeling. Do not provide patient-specific medication changes.

## PNLE clinical reasoning
Safety and physiological stability come before routine psychoeducation. A patient who appears calmer after severe depression still needs assessment; behavior alone cannot establish low suicide risk. Ask about mania/hypomania history before treating as MDD. Avoid stigmatizing labels and validate distress without reinforcing hopelessness.
