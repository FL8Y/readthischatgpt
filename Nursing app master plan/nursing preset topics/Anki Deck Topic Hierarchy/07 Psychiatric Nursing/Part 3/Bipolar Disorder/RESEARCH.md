# Bipolar Disorder — PNLE Research File

## 1. Definition and episode concepts
Bipolar disorders involve episodes of mania/hypomania and often depression, with meaningful changes in mood, energy/activity, sleep, cognition and functioning. Bipolar I includes at least one manic episode; a major depressive episode may occur but is not required for Bipolar I. Bipolar II involves hypomanic and major depressive episodes and no history of mania. Diagnosis requires longitudinal assessment and consideration of medical or substance/medication causes.

**Mania vs hypomania:** mania causes marked change and substantial impairment, may require hospitalization, and can include psychosis. Hypomania is a sustained observable change but lacks the severe impairment, hospitalization or psychosis that defines mania. An elevated episode with psychosis is mania, not hypomania.

## 2. Manifestations and differential
Mania may include decreased need for sleep (not simply insomnia), pressured speech, grandiosity, racing thoughts, distractibility, increased goal-directed activity, impulsive spending/sexual or other risky behavior, irritability, and sometimes psychosis. Depression may involve low mood/anhedonia, fatigue, sleep/appetite changes, psychomotor changes, guilt, impaired concentration and suicidality.

Differential assessment includes stimulant/substance effects, medication-induced mood changes, thyroid or other medical disorders, sleep deprivation, trauma, ADHD and other psychiatric conditions. Obtain onset/course and collateral history when authorized; a patient may not recognize elevated mood or impairment as a problem.

## 3. Initial assessment and priorities
Assess:
- Current mood/energy, sleep need, speech, attention, insight/judgment, psychosis, impulsivity and functional change.
- Suicide/self-harm risk during depressive, mixed or agitated states; risk to others when indicated.
- Nutrition, hydration, exhaustion, exposure to danger, financial/sexual risk, driving, self-neglect and safeguarding needs.
- Current/past medications, adherence, adverse effects, substance use, pregnancy possibility when relevant, renal/thyroid/liver health, and prior episode pattern.

**Acute mania:** reduce stimulation, use clear short statements and consistent boundaries, offer simple choices, support fluids/nutrition/rest, protect from risky behavior, and escalate severe agitation, psychosis, inability to meet basic needs or imminent risk. Do not debate grandiosity or shame impulsive behavior.

## 4. Treatment overview
Care can include mood stabilizers, selected antipsychotics, psychotherapy, psychoeducation, sleep/routine stabilization, and relapse-prevention planning. Selection depends on episode phase, previous response, severity, comorbidity, pregnancy/lactation, risks, local formulary and specialist judgment. Antidepressant monotherapy can be inappropriate in bipolar disorder and may contribute to mood switching in some patients; assess prior mania/hypomania before assuming unipolar depression.

## 5. Medication safety: lithium
Lithium has a narrow therapeutic range; toxicity can occur close to concentrations ordinarily considered therapeutic.
- **Baseline and monitoring:** follow local protocol for renal function/electrolytes, thyroid function, calcium, weight and ECG where indicated. Draw serum levels at the time specified by the product/protocol; many protocols use a trough about 12 hours after the prior dose. Target levels and monitoring intervals vary by indication, phase, age, product and jurisdiction.
- **Interactions:** NSAIDs, thiazide/other diuretics, ACE inhibitors/ARBs and certain medicines can raise lithium concentrations. Illness, fever, vomiting/diarrhea, dehydration, sodium depletion and renal impairment increase toxicity risk. Review new medication and illness changes promptly.
- **Possible toxicity:** worsening/coarse tremor, ataxia, unsteady gait, slurred speech, confusion, marked drowsiness, hyperreflexia, vomiting/diarrhea, seizures or reduced consciousness. Do not rely on a serum result alone if clinical toxicity is suspected.
- **Nursing action:** promptly escalate suspected toxicity, follow authorized instructions/protocol about holding further doses and obtaining urgent laboratory/medical evaluation. Do not restart independently. There is no specific antidote; severe poisoning may require specialist treatment, including dialysis.
- **Education:** maintain usual consistent hydration and salt intake; seek clinical advice during vomiting/diarrhea/fever or significant fluid loss; do not add OTC NSAIDs or change interacting medicines without clinician/pharmacist review. Do not stop abruptly without a medical plan.

## 6. Medication safety: valproate and antipsychotics
Valproate can cause liver injury, pancreatitis, thrombocytopenia and fetal harm, including congenital malformations and neurodevelopmental effects. Pregnancy potential and current local restrictions require explicit prescriber risk assessment. Follow the current product label for monitoring such as liver function and blood counts/platelets. Severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, jaundice, unusual bruising/bleeding or marked lethargy needs prompt assessment.

Antipsychotics may be used during acute mania or maintenance, depending on agent and plan. Monitor agent-specific movement, metabolic, orthostatic, sedation, QT and other risks. Fever, rigidity, altered mental status and autonomic instability can indicate neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS), an emergency.

## 7. Nursing process and relapse prevention
- **Assessment:** episodes and triggers, sleep/routine, function, risk, comorbidity, medication issues and supports.
- **Planning:** safety goals, daily routines, monitoring, treatment engagement, and early warning signs.
- **Implementation:** predictable communication; protect sleep, nutrition/hydration and safety during mania; provide nonjudgmental support during depression; coordinate monitoring.
- **Evaluation:** mood/sleep, function, adverse effects, laboratory monitoring where applicable, risk, follow-up access and personal recovery goals.

Teach early warning signs (reduced sleep need, rising energy/irritability, escalating risky behavior, worsening depression), the crisis plan, continuity of care, and the potential destabilizing effects of recreational drugs/stimulants.

## 8. PNLE high-yield distinctions
- Decreased need for sleep plus increasing energy is more suggestive of mania than insomnia alone.
- Psychosis or severe functional impairment during an elevated episode indicates mania rather than hypomania.
- During acute mania, prioritize safety, hydration/nutrition, sleep/rest and reduced stimulation before lengthy teaching.
- Lithium toxicity risk increases with dehydration, acute illness, renal impairment or interacting drugs; assess clinically, not by laboratory value alone.
- Valproate requires particular attention to fetal risk and current local product/regulatory guidance.

## 9. Philippine context
RA 11036 establishes a rights-based mental-health framework. Respect autonomy, informed consent, confidentiality and dignity; do not presume incapacity from diagnosis alone. Follow Philippine professional scope, local formulary, prescriber orders and emergency policy. UK/US recommendations are jurisdiction-specific and not automatically Philippine law or formulary requirements.
