# Behavioral Management — PNLE-Focused Research

## Core concepts
Behavioral management in psychiatric nursing focuses on preventing harm, understanding the function and triggers of behavior, and supporting recovery with the least restrictive effective response.

## Assessment and clinical reasoning
Assess antecedents, behavior, and consequences; check pain, delirium, intoxication/withdrawal, unmet needs, sensory overload, and communication barriers. Set clear respectful limits, offer choices, reduce stimulation, maintain personal space, and use one calm spokesperson. Reinforce safe coping and document objective observations and response.

## Nursing priorities, safety, and PNLE distinctions
Seclusion/restraint are last-resort measures only under applicable law, policy, trained staffing, time limits, monitoring, and documentation. Never use punishment, humiliation, or convenience as justification. Escalate imminent danger or medical instability.

Apply the nursing process and prioritize immediate threats to life and safety over routine teaching. Use ABCs when physiologic instability is present and Maslow as a guide rather than a rigid rule. Individualize care, preserve dignity and rights, document observable findings, and escalate according to local clinical protocols.

## Philippine context
Republic Act No. 11036 (Philippine Mental Health Act, 2018) establishes a rights-based national mental health framework. Apply the law and its implementing rules to rights, informed consent, confidentiality, access, and least-restrictive care; consult the actual statutory text and facility policy for case-specific requirements. International guidance does not automatically override Philippine law or local scope-of-practice rules.
