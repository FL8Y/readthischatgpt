# Crisis and Crisis Intervention — PNLE Research Notes

## Scope and safety
A crisis is a time-limited state in which a stressor overwhelms a person's usual coping resources and functioning. A crisis is not itself a psychiatric diagnosis. Assessment must consider the person's context, supports, culture, developmental stage, medical status, and immediate danger.

## Initial nursing priorities
1. **Immediate safety and medical stability:** assess airway/breathing/circulation when indicated, injuries, intoxication/withdrawal, delirium, overdose, and other urgent medical causes. Do not assume new confusion or agitation is purely psychiatric.
2. **Direct risk assessment:** ask clearly and nonjudgmentally about suicidal thoughts, intent, plan, access to means, recent attempts, self-harm, and ability to remain safe. Ask about thoughts of harming others when indicated. Do not rely on a denial alone if behavior or collateral information raises concern.
3. **Protective supports and context:** identify trusted supports, reasons for living, prior attempts, recent losses, substance use, psychosis, severe insomnia, impulsivity, and access to lethal means.
4. **Escalate and document:** follow facility protocols, promptly involve the responsible clinician/mental-health team, and arrange emergency evaluation for imminent danger, serious injury/overdose, inability to maintain safety, or severe medical instability. Maintain appropriate observation and handover.
5. **Stabilize:** use calm, brief communication; listen, validate distress without endorsing harmful beliefs, offer simple choices, reduce stimulation, and collaboratively identify the next safe step.

## Suicide and self-harm
Warning signs can include talking about wanting to die, hopelessness or feeling trapped, feeling like a burden, withdrawal, giving away possessions, marked mood changes, escalating substance use, and making a plan. New or intensifying warning signs warrant prompt assessment. NIMH's US crisis number is not a Philippine service; use local emergency pathways and facility protocols in Philippine practice.

**Do not:** promise secrecy, shame or challenge the person, use threats, leave a person at imminent risk alone when policy requires continuous observation, or treat a screening score as a substitute for clinical assessment. Use the least restrictive safe observation and environmental precautions under local policy. Ask about and collaboratively reduce access to lethal means when feasible.

## Crisis intervention framework
- Establish rapport and a safe environment.
- Identify the precipitating problem and the person's perception of it.
- Assess immediate risks, coping strategies, supports, and functional disruption.
- Set a short, concrete, achievable goal focused first on safety and stabilization.
- Help the person use existing strengths and supports; problem-solve one step at a time.
- Arrange follow-up, referral, and a clear safety plan; reassess response and document.

## PNLE priority reasoning
- A person with an active plan, intent, access to means, recent attempt, or inability to stay safe requires immediate safety action and urgent escalation—not routine teaching first.
- ABCs and medical causes take precedence over a purely psychosocial formulation when the patient is unstable.
- Maslow is a guide, not a rigid algorithm: immediate threats to life and safety outrank lower-acuity therapeutic goals.
- A calm presentation does not by itself establish low risk. Assess change from baseline, context, intent, access, and available supports.

## Philippine legal/ethical context
Republic Act No. 11036, the Philippine Mental Health Act, establishes a rights-based framework for mental health services. For individual cases, apply the current statutory text, implementing rules, professional standards, privacy requirements, emergency protocols, and facility policy. This educational note is not a legal opinion.
