# Hormones of the Menstrual Cycle: PNLE Nursing Review

## Overview
The menstrual cycle is coordinated by the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis. Cycle length varies; a 28-day cycle is a teaching model, not a universal normal. Day 1 is the first day of menstrual bleeding. Ovulation usually occurs about 14 days before the next period, not necessarily on day 14 for every cycle.

## Hormonal sequence
| Hormone | Source | Main role |
|---|---|---|
| GnRH | Hypothalamus | Pulsatile release stimulates pituitary FSH and LH; pulse pattern affects secretion. |
| FSH | Anterior pituitary | Stimulates ovarian follicle development and granulosa-cell estrogen production. |
| LH | Anterior pituitary | LH surge triggers ovulation and supports corpus luteum formation. |
| Estrogen (especially estradiol) | Developing follicles; corpus luteum | Builds proliferative endometrium; contributes to feedback on FSH/LH and cervical mucus changes. |
| Progesterone | Corpus luteum after ovulation | Converts endometrium to secretory phase, supports implantation conditions and raises basal body temperature slightly. |
| Inhibin | Ovary | Suppresses FSH secretion. |

## Phases
1. **Menstrual phase:** corpus luteum regresses if pregnancy does not occur; estrogen/progesterone fall and endometrium sheds.
2. **Follicular/proliferative phase:** FSH supports follicular growth; estradiol rises and endometrium proliferates. The follicular phase varies most in length.
3. **Ovulation:** sustained high estradiol triggers positive feedback and LH surge, leading to release of the oocyte.
4. **Luteal/secretory phase:** corpus luteum secretes progesterone and estrogen; if no pregnancy occurs, it regresses and hormones fall, initiating menstruation. If pregnancy occurs, hCG supports the corpus luteum early until placental hormone production is sufficient.

## Feedback concepts
Most of the cycle, estrogen exerts negative feedback on FSH/LH. Sustained high estradiol before ovulation switches to positive feedback, producing the LH surge. Progesterone predominates in the luteal phase and suppresses GnRH/LH/FSH through negative feedback.

## Clinical and nursing relevance
Cycle history helps assess menstrual disorders, fertility timing and gestational dating. Ask about cycle regularity, last menstrual period, bleeding amount/duration, pain, contraception, medications and pregnancy possibility. Abnormal heavy bleeding, severe pelvic pain, syncope or possible pregnancy with pain/bleeding needs assessment. Irregular cycles make calendar-based ovulation estimates unreliable.

## PNLE high-yield points
- FSH promotes follicular growth; LH surge triggers ovulation.
- Estrogen proliferates endometrium; progesterone supports the secretory endometrium after ovulation.
- Ovulation timing is usually about 14 days before the next period, not always cycle day 14.
- Corpus luteum regression lowers estrogen/progesterone and leads to menstruation.
- hCG maintains the corpus luteum early in pregnancy.

## Verification limitations
Cycle timing varies between individuals and between cycles. Calendar estimates cannot confirm ovulation or exclude pregnancy.
