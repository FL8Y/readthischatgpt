# Proper Cord Clamping and Cutting: PNLE Nursing Review

## Definition and rationale
Delayed umbilical cord clamping allows placental transfusion of blood to the newborn, supporting blood volume and iron stores. WHO recommends delaying clamping for at least one minute for most births while essential newborn care proceeds; many EINC protocols use approximately 1–3 minutes or until pulsations cease, depending on the applicable protocol and clinical context.

## Nursing responsibilities
- Prepare sterile cord clamp/tie and cutting instrument before birth.
- After birth, assess newborn breathing/tone and maintain warmth through drying and skin-to-skin when clinically appropriate.
- Follow the current protocol for timing; keep the newborn near the mother while awaiting clamping when feasible.
- Clamp and cut using aseptic technique and appropriate equipment.
- Inspect the stump for bleeding after clamping and during newborn checks; teach clean, dry cord care according to local guidance.
- Document timing, newborn condition, any deviation and rationale.

## Safety exceptions
Do not delay urgent neonatal resuscitation or maternal emergency treatment. If the newborn requires immediate resuscitation and cannot be stabilized at the bedside, follow neonatal resuscitation protocol and local cord-management policy. Maternal hemorrhage or other urgent conditions may also require individualized management. Do not apply a single rule without assessing the clinical situation and current protocol.

## Delayed clamping versus early clamping
| Issue | Delayed clamping | Early clamping |
|---|---|---|
| Usual rationale | Allows placental transfusion; supports neonatal blood volume/iron stores | May be necessary for specific urgent clinical circumstances |
| Routine stable birth | Generally recommended by WHO/ACOG guidance | Not routine for most stable newborns |
| Safety focus | Continue drying, warmth and newborn assessment while cord remains intact | If needed, ensure prompt resuscitation/urgent care and document indication |

## Nursing priorities and rationale
- **Prevent hemorrhage/cord bleeding:** ensure secure clamp and inspect stump.
- **Maintain asepsis:** clean equipment and technique reduce infection risk.
- **Maintain thermal care:** do not let the infant become wet/cold while waiting for clamping.
- **Do not delay resuscitation:** newborn ventilation is time-critical when indicated.
- **Avoid unnecessary separation:** routine clamping can often occur while the infant is on the mother's chest if stable and feasible.

## PNLE high-yield points
- Delayed clamping is generally recommended for stable newborns; exact timing must follow current local guidance.
- Drying, skin-to-skin and observation can continue while clamping is delayed.
- Early clamping is not routine for all births; emergency circumstances may alter timing.
- Always inspect for cord stump bleeding after clamping.

## Philippine practice context
The Philippine EINC/Unang Yakap package includes properly timed cord clamping. AO 2009-0025 is an important historical national policy source, but current DOH and facility protocols should be checked for current operational details.

## Current guidance and interpretation

WHO recommends that cord clamping not occur earlier than 1 minute after birth for newborns who do not require positive-pressure ventilation, with early clamping considered when urgent resuscitation requires moving the newborn. ACOG's 2025 Clinical Practice Update specifically revises prior guidance for **preterm** neonates based on newer evidence about deferred clamping, cord milking and immediate clamping. Therefore, do not apply one timing rule mechanically to every gestational age or clinical state; use the current preterm-specific guidance and the local neonatal resuscitation/EINC protocol. If effective ventilation can be provided with the cord intact by a trained team, local protocol may permit that approach; do not delay effective resuscitation.
