# Immediate Drying and Skin-to-Skin Contact: PNLE Nursing Review

## Immediate thorough drying
Immediately after birth, dry the newborn thoroughly with a warm, clean, dry towel, including the head, while assessing breathing and tone. Remove wet linen promptly. Drying reduces evaporative heat loss; newborns have a large surface-area-to-mass ratio and limited thermoregulation, so hypothermia can develop quickly. Do not delay assessment of breathing or resuscitation when indicated.

## Immediate skin-to-skin contact
For a stable newborn and stable mother, place the dried newborn prone on the mother's bare chest, with the head turned to the side, neck neutral/slightly extended, face visible and airway unobstructed. Cover the infant and mother with dry warm linen and maintain an appropriate environment. Continue uninterrupted skin-to-skin for at least the first hour or until after the first feed according to protocol.

Benefits include thermal stability, improved early breastfeeding, bonding and reduced unnecessary separation. Skin-to-skin is appropriate after cesarean birth when mother and newborn are stable and the operating team can safely support it.

## Safety monitoring
Observe breathing, color, tone, temperature and feeding cues. Ensure the infant's nose and mouth remain visible and unobstructed; avoid flexed neck or face pressed into the breast. Do not leave an infant unattended on a sedated, unconscious or unstable mother. If the mother cannot safely hold the infant, provide an appropriate alternative warm-care plan and reunite as soon as safe.

## Exceptions
If the newborn is apneic/gasping or requires resuscitation, follow neonatal resuscitation guidance immediately; lifesaving ventilation must not be delayed for routine skin-to-skin. If the mother has hemorrhage, altered consciousness or another emergency, stabilize her and ensure the newborn is supervised by trained staff.

## Nursing priorities and rationale
| Action | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Dry promptly and remove wet towels | Prevents evaporative heat loss. |
| Place stable newborn skin-to-skin and cover both | Supports thermoregulation, bonding and breastfeeding. |
| Maintain visible face/clear airway and neutral neck | Reduces airway obstruction risk. |
| Observe breathing, color, tone and temperature | Identifies transition problems early. |
| Avoid unnecessary separation for routine weighing/bathing | Protects uninterrupted contact and early feeding. |
| Escalate abnormal breathing or maternal instability | Resuscitation and stabilization override routine EINC sequence. |

## PNLE high-yield points
- Drying comes immediately after birth and occurs while assessing breathing.
- Wet linen must be removed to prevent heat loss.
- Skin-to-skin requires airway-safe positioning and observation; it is not “place and leave.”
- Resuscitation must not be delayed to preserve routine EINC sequence.
- Routine bathing should be delayed according to current newborn-care guidance.
