# Essential Intrapartum and Newborn Care (EINC): PNLE Nursing Review

## Definition
Essential Intrapartum and Newborn Care (EINC), promoted in the Philippines as “Unang Yakap”/First Embrace, is a time-bound, evidence-based package of practices for the mother and newborn around birth. It aims to prevent hypothermia and infection, support early breastfeeding, improve transition and avoid unnecessary separation or harmful routine procedures.

## Core sequence for a stable newborn
1. Immediate and thorough drying.
2. Immediate skin-to-skin contact and thermal protection.
3. Properly timed delayed cord clamping, while observing the newborn and maintaining warmth.
4. Non-separation and support for breastfeeding initiation within the first hour when mother and newborn are stable and ready.
Other essential care includes assessment of breathing, resuscitation when indicated, infection prevention, vitamin K, eye prophylaxis and immunization according to current national policy. These interventions should be organized so that non-urgent procedures do not interrupt early contact and breastfeeding.

## Assessment and exceptions
Immediately assess breathing, tone, heart rate and overall transition. A newborn who is not breathing or is gasping requires prompt resuscitation according to neonatal resuscitation protocol; do not delay ventilation for routine skin-to-skin or delayed clamping if immediate resuscitation needs require moving the newborn. Maternal instability, major hemorrhage or other emergency can also change the sequence. Follow the current facility protocol for intact-cord resuscitation when equipment and trained staff permit.

## Nursing responsibilities
- Prepare warm environment, dry towels, sterile cord clamp/cutting equipment and resuscitation equipment before birth.
- Dry thoroughly, remove wet linen and maintain skin-to-skin with a dry covering and head protection as appropriate.
- Monitor breathing, color, tone and temperature while supporting contact.
- Facilitate delayed clamping according to current protocol and clinical circumstances.
- Support early feeding cues and latch; avoid forced feeding or separation for non-urgent routine tasks.
- Document timing and completion of interventions, maternal/newborn status, deviations and rationale.

## Safety and contraindications
EINC is a package for routine care but must not delay lifesaving interventions. If newborn resuscitation or maternal stabilization is needed, activate the appropriate emergency response and follow neonatal/maternal protocols. Maintain safe positioning during skin-to-skin: ensure the newborn's face is visible, airway unobstructed, head turned with neck neutral/slightly extended, and mother is alert and able to hold the infant safely. Continuous observation is important, especially if the mother is sedated, exhausted or medically unstable.

## PNLE high-yield points
- EINC sequence: dry → skin-to-skin/thermal care → delayed cord clamping → non-separation/early breastfeeding, with ongoing assessment.
- Immediate drying prevents evaporative heat loss.
- Routine suction is not indicated for a vigorous newborn born through clear fluid; use current resuscitation guidance.
- A newborn needing resuscitation requires prompt action; EINC does not override lifesaving stabilization.
- Vitamin K and other routine newborn interventions remain necessary but should be timed to avoid unnecessary disruption when safe.

## Philippine practice context
The Philippine DOH adopted Essential Newborn Care policies in Administrative Order No. 2009-0025. WHO Philippines describes EINC/Unang Yakap as a time-bound package. Facilities should follow the latest DOH circulars and current institutional protocol if they update older procedures.

## Current neonatal resuscitation guidance

The 2025 American Heart Association/American Academy of Pediatrics neonatal resuscitation guideline states that routine oral, nasal, oropharyngeal, or endotracheal suctioning is not recommended for newborns, whether amniotic fluid is clear or meconium-stained. Suction may be considered when airway obstruction is suspected, particularly when ventilation is ineffective and obstruction is suspected. Ventilation is the primary intervention for apnea, bradycardia or inadequate respiratory effort. Do not delay effective ventilation to perform routine suction or to complete non-urgent EINC steps. Follow the current neonatal resuscitation algorithm and facility capability.

## Verification notes and limitations
The 2025 AHA/AAP neonatal resuscitation guideline was opened and checked for the recommendations against routine suctioning and for the role of ventilation in newborns with inadequate respiratory effort. These recommendations support selective suction only for suspected obstruction and avoiding delay of effective ventilation.

## Verification limitations
AO 2009-0025 is an older policy document. Verify current DOH updates and facility-specific protocols before using any exact timing or procedural detail as a current national requirement.
