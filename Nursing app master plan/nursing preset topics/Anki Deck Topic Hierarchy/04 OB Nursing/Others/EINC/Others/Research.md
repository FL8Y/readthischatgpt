# Additional Essential Newborn Care: PNLE Nursing Review

## Scope
This folder covers EINC elements not represented by the dedicated drying/skin-to-skin, cord-clamping and breastfeeding folders: immediate assessment, thermal care, resuscitation, infection prevention, vitamin K, eye prophylaxis, immunization, cord care and recognition of newborn danger signs. Follow current Philippine newborn-care protocols for exact timing and products.

## Immediate assessment and transition
Assess breathing/cry, tone, color and heart rate promptly. Dry and warm a stable newborn while observing transition. A newborn who is apneic/gasping or has poor tone requires immediate neonatal resuscitation according to current protocol; routine procedures must not delay effective ventilation. Routine suctioning is not recommended for a vigorous newborn born through clear fluid; suction is used only when indicated by current resuscitation guidance.

## Thermal protection
Prevent hypothermia through immediate drying, removal of wet linen, skin-to-skin or an appropriate warmer, a warm environment and temperature monitoring. Avoid unnecessary bathing and separation. Monitor temperature according to facility protocol; temperature outside the normal neonatal range requires assessment and action.

## Vitamin K, eye prophylaxis and immunization
- **Vitamin K:** administer according to national neonatal policy and prescribed route/dose to prevent vitamin K deficiency bleeding. Verify infant identity, product, dose, route, timing and documentation.
- **Eye prophylaxis:** use the product and timing required by current national policy to prevent ophthalmia neonatorum; check allergies/product contraindications and avoid contaminating the applicator.
- **Immunization:** administer birth-dose vaccines according to the current Philippine immunization schedule, eligibility, consent and contraindications. Do not substitute outdated schedules for current DOH policy.
These interventions are essential, but should be organized so they do not unnecessarily interrupt early skin-to-skin and breastfeeding when safe.

## Cord care and infection prevention
Use clean/aseptic technique for cord clamping and cutting. Follow current guidance for clean, dry cord care or any indicated antiseptic regimen. Perform hand hygiene before and after care; teach caregivers to keep the stump clean and dry and seek care for spreading redness, pus, foul odor, fever or poor feeding.

## Newborn danger signs
Urgent assessment is needed for apnea/gasping, respiratory distress, central cyanosis, inability to feed, convulsions, lethargy/unresponsiveness, temperature instability, severe jaundice (especially within first 24 hours), persistent vomiting, signs of infection or cord bleeding. Follow local neonatal escalation and referral pathways.

## Routine newborn preventive care: distinct responsibilities

### Vitamin K prophylaxis
Newborns have low vitamin K stores and are at risk of vitamin K deficiency bleeding. Administer the prescribed vitamin K preparation by the nationally/facility-approved route and dose after verifying infant identity, weight/gestational context if relevant to the protocol, correct product, dose, route, timing, allergies/contraindications and documentation. The routine prophylactic dose is a neonatal medication order; do not calculate or substitute a dose from an adult formulation or from a memory aid. Oral regimens, where used in a jurisdiction, require a specific repeated-dose protocol and may be less reliable than IM prophylaxis. Explain the purpose to parents and record refusal or delay per policy.

### Eye prophylaxis
Use only the eye prophylaxis product and timing approved in the current Philippine/facility protocol. Check the product's indication, allergy/contraindication, expiry, storage, aseptic administration and documentation. Apply to the correct eye(s) without contaminating the tube/tip or injuring the cornea. Explain that prophylaxis is intended to prevent serious neonatal eye infection; it does not replace maternal infection screening, intrapartum management or assessment of eye redness/discharge.

### Birth-dose immunization
Check the current DOH Expanded Program on Immunization/Philippine Immunization Schedule for hepatitis B birth dose and BCG eligibility, contraindications, product and timing. These schedules and products can be updated; the 2009 EINC administrative order should not be treated as a complete current vaccine schedule. Verify maternal/newborn history and any special clinical circumstance (including very low birth weight, medical instability or suspected immunodeficiency where relevant to the vaccine) against current vaccine-specific instructions. Use the correct vaccine, lot/expiry, dose, route, site, cold-chain requirements and documentation; counsel caregivers and arrange catch-up/referral if a dose is deferred. Do not invent doses or injection sites in a generic topic file when the current national schedule/product insert has not been checked.

### Newborn screening and post-discharge continuity
Explain the Philippine Newborn Screening Program and ensure specimen collection, consent/counseling as required, transport and follow-up comply with current DOH/NSRC instructions. Timing can be affected by early collection, transfusion, prematurity, or NICU course; these situations require the screening program's instructions rather than an improvised repeat schedule. Record completion/status and make the follow-up plan visible at discharge. Newborn screening is not a substitute for assessment of a symptomatic baby.

## Resuscitation and immediate escalation
For newborns who are apneic/gasping or have inadequate breathing, activate neonatal resuscitation and support effective ventilation per the current algorithm. Routine suction is not recommended for a vigorous newborn; suction is reserved for suspected airway obstruction or other algorithm-based indication. Do not delay effective ventilation for suction, weighing, vitamin K, eye prophylaxis, a vaccine or completion of EINC documentation.

Urgent assessment is required for apnea/gasping, central cyanosis, persistent respiratory distress, poor tone/response, inability to feed, convulsions, temperature instability, jaundice in the first 24 hours, signs of sepsis, ongoing bleeding, or rapidly worsening condition. Use the newborn emergency/referral pathway and communicate the time course, maternal/perinatal risk factors, interventions and response.

## Parent teaching and documentation
Before discharge, verify caregiver understanding of feeding, warmth, cord care, jaundice and infection warning signs, follow-up appointments, screening/vaccination status, and where to return urgently. Document all prophylaxis and immunizations (product, dose/route/time, batch/lot as required), screening collection/referral, consent/refusal, any deferral and the reason, newborn assessment, teaching and follow-up plan.


## Nursing priorities
| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Breathing | Assess promptly and begin/activate resuscitation if indicated. | Ventilation is time-critical in newborn compromise. |
| Temperature | Dry, remove wet linen, maintain skin-to-skin/warmth and monitor. | Hypothermia increases morbidity. |
| Medication safety | Verify vitamin K/eye prophylaxis/vaccine product, dose, route, timing and eligibility. | Prevents medication errors and ensures effective prophylaxis. |
| Infection prevention | Hand hygiene, clean equipment and cord-care teaching. | Reduces infection risk. |
| Early detection | Teach danger signs and where to seek care. | Newborn deterioration can be rapid. |

## PNLE high-yield points
- A vigorous newborn does not need routine suctioning simply because birth occurred.
- Effective ventilation takes priority when resuscitation is indicated.
- Vitamin K prevents vitamin K deficiency bleeding; it is not a vaccine.
- Jaundice in the first 24 hours is a red flag.
- Keep newborn interventions aligned with current DOH schedules and facility policy.

## Philippine practice context
The Philippine DOH adopted essential newborn care policies in AO 2009-0025. Current immunization schedules and neonatal protocols may be updated after that policy; verify current DOH issuances and facility policy rather than treating the 2009 order as the only source.
