# Common Discomforts and Risk Factors of Pregnancy: PNLE Nursing Review

## Common discomforts and safe teaching
| Symptom | Common mechanism | Nursing teaching / when to escalate |
|---|---|---|
| Nausea | Pregnancy-related hormonal effects | Small frequent meals and fluids as tolerated; persistent vomiting/dehydration needs assessment. |
| Heartburn | Reduced lower esophageal sphincter tone and uterine pressure | Smaller meals, avoid lying flat after meals; medication only after pregnancy-safety review. |
| Constipation | Slower GI motility, iron, reduced activity | Fluids, fiber and safe movement; severe pain, vomiting or blood requires evaluation. |
| Urinary frequency | Increased renal filtration and uterine pressure | Often common, but dysuria, fever or flank pain suggests infection. |
| Back/pelvic discomfort | Postural change and ligament laxity | Safe posture/activity, supportive measures; severe pain, fever, weakness or contractions require assessment. |
| Leg cramps/varicosities | Circulatory and musculoskeletal changes | Safe movement and comfort measures; unilateral swelling/pain is a VTE red flag. |
| Mild dependent edema | Venous pressure and fluid changes | Assess BP and associated symptoms; sudden face/hand swelling with headache/visual changes is concerning. |

## Risk assessment
Review prior obstetric complications, hypertension, diabetes, renal/cardiac disease, autoimmune conditions, thrombosis history, multiple gestation, age-related risk, BMI/nutrition, medication/substance exposure, infection risks, mental health, social determinants and access to care. Risk factors guide surveillance and referral but do not determine outcomes by themselves.

## Red flags
Prompt/urgent assessment is needed for:
- Vaginal bleeding or fluid leakage.
- Severe/persistent abdominal pain or regular contractions before 37 weeks.
- Reduced fetal movement after a usual pattern is established.
- Severe headache, visual changes, epigastric/RUQ pain, seizure or severe-range BP.
- Fever, foul discharge, painful urination with fever.
- Chest pain, severe dyspnea, syncope, hemoptysis or unilateral leg swelling.
- Persistent vomiting with dehydration, hematemesis or altered mental status.

## Nursing approach
1. Differentiate expected discomfort from new, severe, persistent or associated red-flag symptoms.
2. Assess vital signs, gestational age, symptom onset, fetal movement and relevant history.
3. Provide symptom-specific education only after screening for danger signs.
4. Review all prescription, OTC, herbal and supplement use; confirm pregnancy-specific safety before recommending medication.
5. Use teach-back and provide clear instructions for urgent contact/referral.

## Medication safety
Do not assume OTC products or herbals are safe in pregnancy. Avoid ACE inhibitors/ARBs in pregnancy unless specialist guidance specifically addresses an unusual circumstance; NSAID use is gestational-age dependent and requires professional advice. Do not stop essential prescribed treatment abruptly. Antiemetics, antacids, laxatives and analgesics require agent-specific safety checks.

## PNLE high-yield points
- Common discomforts are diagnoses of exclusion when symptoms are severe or atypical.
- Unilateral leg swelling/pain or sudden dyspnea/chest pain suggests possible VTE/PE and requires urgent assessment.
- Severe headache, visual symptoms or epigastric/RUQ pain may signal preeclampsia.
- Preterm contractions, bleeding, fluid leakage and reduced fetal movement are not routine discomforts.
- Prioritize ABCs and immediate danger before routine teaching; Maslow is a supportive framework, not a substitute for clinical urgency.
