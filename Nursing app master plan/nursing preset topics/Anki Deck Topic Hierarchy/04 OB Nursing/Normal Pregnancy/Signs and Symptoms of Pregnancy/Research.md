# Signs and Symptoms of Pregnancy: PNLE Nursing Review

## Classification
Traditional nursing texts group pregnancy findings as presumptive, probable and positive signs. These categories help learning, but no symptom should substitute for appropriate testing and assessment.

| Category | Meaning | Examples | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Presumptive | Subjective symptoms reported by the patient | Amenorrhea, nausea, fatigue, breast tenderness, urinary frequency, perceived fetal movement | Can occur with conditions other than pregnancy. |
| Probable | Objective findings that suggest pregnancy | Positive urine/serum hCG, uterine enlargement, cervical/uterine changes | hCG may be positive in some nonpregnancy conditions; ultrasound/clinical context may be needed. |
| Positive/diagnostic | Findings that directly demonstrate embryo/fetus | Fetal cardiac activity visualized by ultrasound, embryo/fetus visualized, fetal movement palpated by trained examiner (traditional textbook classification) | Timing and method matter; handheld Doppler detection alone is not equivalent to all diagnostic criteria. |

## Pregnancy testing
Urine and serum tests detect hCG. Testing too early may produce a false negative; repeat testing or serum testing may be needed when suspicion remains. hCG values vary widely, and a single value cannot reliably establish gestational age, viability or pregnancy location.

## Dating and confirmation
Confirm last menstrual period, cycle regularity, contraception, pregnancy history and symptoms. Ultrasound is used when indicated to confirm location, dating, viability and number of fetuses. In early pregnancy with pain or bleeding, consider ectopic pregnancy until location is established.

## Differential diagnosis and red flags
Amenorrhea may result from stress, endocrine disorders, weight change, lactation or other causes. Nausea, breast tenderness and fatigue are nonspecific. Urgent evaluation is needed for severe unilateral pain, shoulder-tip pain, syncope, heavy bleeding, fever or hemodynamic instability.

## Nursing responsibilities
- Use a respectful, confidential history and clarify pregnancy intention where relevant.
- Explain test timing and the possibility of early false-negative results.
- Refer for prenatal care after confirmation and review medications/exposures.
- Do not assume pregnancy is intrauterine solely from a positive test.
- For pain/bleeding, assess stability and escalate possible ectopic pregnancy or hemorrhage.

## PNLE high-yield points
- Presumptive = subjective; probable = objective but not definitive; positive = direct evidence of fetus.
- Amenorrhea and nausea do not confirm pregnancy.
- A positive hCG test confirms hCG detection, not pregnancy location or viability.
- Early pregnancy pain/bleeding plus syncope or shoulder pain is an emergency red flag.
