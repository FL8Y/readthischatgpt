# Pregnancy Trimesters: PNLE Nursing Review

## Definitions
Pregnancy is conventionally dated from the first day of the last menstrual period and lasts about 40 weeks. Trimesters are commonly grouped as:
- **First trimester:** 0 weeks through 13 weeks and 6 days.
- **Second trimester:** 14 weeks through 27 weeks and 6 days.
- **Third trimester:** 28 weeks until birth.
Some resources use slightly different boundary wording; state gestational age in weeks and days when clinical precision matters.

## Dating and trimester boundaries versus clinical milestones
Use gestational age in weeks and days for decisions. The trimester cutoffs above are a conventional nursing framework; screening windows are tied to the specific test and guideline, not to the trimester label alone.

| Approximate timing | Common focus (subject to local protocol) | Safety note |
|---|---|---|
| First trimester | Establish dating and pregnancy location when indicated; baseline risk/history and screening; review medicines, folate, exposures and symptoms. | Pain/bleeding with a positive test requires consideration of ectopic pregnancy until location is established. |
| Around 11–14 weeks | Some fetal screening/dating options occur in this window, depending on national program and test availability. | Screening options and eligibility differ; explain screening vs diagnostic testing and obtain appropriate consent. |
| Around 18–22 weeks | Detailed fetal anatomy ultrasound is commonly scheduled during this interval in many systems. | A normal anatomy scan does not exclude every structural/genetic condition or later complication. |
| Around 24–28 weeks | Gestational-diabetes screening commonly occurs if not diagnosed earlier, with timing/strategy depending on local guideline. | Do not combine thresholds from one-step 75-g OGTT and two-step screening systems. |
| Third trimester | Fetal growth/movement, maternal BP and preeclampsia symptoms, fetal presentation and birth planning are reviewed according to risk. | Surveillance frequency and delivery timing are individualized, not determined by trimester alone. |

These windows are general clinical examples, not a claim of a single Philippine DOH timetable. Confirm the current local antenatal testing calendar and individual indications.


## First trimester: priorities
- Confirm pregnancy, establish gestational age and assess pregnancy location/viability when indicated.
- Review medical/obstetric history, medicines, allergies, exposures and prenatal screening.
- Discuss folic acid, nutrition, infection prevention, avoidance of alcohol/tobacco/non-prescribed drugs, and medication review.
- Nausea/fatigue/breast tenderness are common, but severe vomiting, pain, bleeding, syncope or fever needs assessment.
- Organogenesis makes early embryonic development sensitive to some teratogens.

## Second trimester: priorities
- Continue antenatal visits, screening and assessment of maternal wellbeing.
- Assess fetal growth and movement awareness according to gestational age and local guidance.
- Screen for gestational diabetes commonly around 24–28 weeks according to local protocol.
- Assess anemia and other tests as indicated.
- Teach preterm labor, fluid leakage, bleeding and reduced fetal movement warning signs as relevant.

## Third trimester: priorities
- Monitor maternal blood pressure, symptoms of preeclampsia, fetal growth and movement, and other risk-based surveillance.
- Discuss birth preparedness, transport, facility plan, support person, breastfeeding, postpartum recovery and contraception.
- Assess signs of labor, membrane rupture, bleeding and fetal movement changes.
- Review fetal presentation and delivery planning according to local protocol and clinical indications.

## Gestational age and dating
LMP dating assumes reasonably regular cycles and can be inaccurate. First-trimester ultrasound is generally the most accurate ultrasound method for dating. Do not change an established due date without applying the current dating guideline and documented clinical reasoning.

## Red flags at any trimester
Heavy bleeding, severe or persistent abdominal pain, syncope, fever, fluid leakage, severe headache/visual changes, epigastric/RUQ pain, seizure, chest pain, severe dyspnea or maternal instability require urgent assessment. Reduced fetal movement after a usual pattern is established also requires prompt maternity assessment.

## Nursing implications
Tailor teaching to gestational age and individual risk; explain the purpose and timing of screening, medication safety, nutrition, activity and warning signs. Use teach-back and ensure referrals are completed. Immediate maternal threats take priority over trimester-specific routine teaching.

## PNLE high-yield points
- First trimester includes the period of greatest structural organogenesis vulnerability.
- Common GDM screening is often at 24–28 weeks, but use current local protocol.
- Third-trimester care emphasizes preeclampsia warning signs, fetal movement, birth planning and readiness for labor.
- Weeks-plus-days are more precise than trimester alone.
- Bleeding, severe pain, syncope, severe hypertension symptoms or reduced fetal movement should not be dismissed as routine.
