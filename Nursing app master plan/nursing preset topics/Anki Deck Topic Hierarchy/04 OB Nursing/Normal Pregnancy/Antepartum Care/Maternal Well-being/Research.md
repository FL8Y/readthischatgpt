# Maternal Well-Being in Antepartum Care: PNLE Nursing Review

## Goals of antenatal care
Antenatal care promotes maternal health, detects pregnancy complications early, supports fetal growth, provides preventive care and prepares the family for birth and postpartum needs. Care should be respectful, accessible, culturally responsive and individualized to risk, patient preferences and local resources.

## Core assessment
At booking and follow-up, assess:
- Medical, obstetric, surgical, family, medication, allergy and mental-health history.
- Gestational age/dating, blood pressure, weight trend, symptoms and fetal growth/wellbeing as appropriate.
- Screening and labs according to current protocol (e.g., blood group/Rh and antibody screen, anemia, urine testing, infections and diabetes screening).
- Nutrition, food security, activity, sleep, substance exposure, intimate partner violence/safety when appropriate, social support and barriers to care.
- Medication/supplement use, immunization status and occupational/environmental exposures.

## Preventive care and education
- Attend scheduled antenatal visits and complete screening/referrals.
- Use a balanced diet and appropriate pregnancy weight-gain advice; follow current local folic acid/iron and other supplementation recommendations.
- Avoid alcohol, tobacco/nicotine and non-prescribed drugs; review prescribed medicines with a clinician before changing them.
- Encourage safe physical activity when not contraindicated, oral health, hand hygiene, food safety and infection prevention.
- Discuss birth preparedness, transport, emergency contacts, blood-donor arrangements where locally relevant, breastfeeding, postpartum contraception and newborn care.
- Provide mental-health support and screen/refer for depression, anxiety or other concerns according to protocol.

## Warning signs requiring prompt care
Heavy vaginal bleeding, fluid leakage, reduced fetal movement after movement is established, severe or persistent abdominal pain, severe headache or visual changes, epigastric/right upper quadrant pain, seizures, fever, chest pain, severe dyspnea, syncope, persistent vomiting/dehydration, painful urination with fever, or unilateral leg swelling/pain require urgent assessment. Explain how to contact the maternity service and avoid vague advice to “wait and see.”

## Nursing priorities and rationale
| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Risk detection | Review history, BP, symptoms and ordered tests at each appropriate contact. | Complications may arise between scheduled visits. |
| Medication safety | Reconcile all medicines, OTC products and supplements; verify pregnancy safety before changes. | Some agents are teratogenic or require dose/monitoring changes. |
| Health literacy | Use plain language and teach-back; adapt to language, culture and access barriers. | Improves understanding and follow-through. |
| Emergency readiness | Teach red flags and where/how to seek urgent care. | Early escalation can prevent severe maternal/fetal outcomes. |
| Psychosocial health | Assess support, mood and safety respectfully; refer appropriately. | Social and mental-health needs affect maternal outcomes. |

## Medication safety
Do not recommend a medication or supplement dose without checking the current Philippine guideline, gestational age, comorbidities and concurrent medicines. ACE inhibitors/ARBs and some other medicines are generally avoided in pregnancy; NSAID use is gestational-age dependent and requires professional guidance. Do not abruptly stop essential prescribed treatment—arrange clinician review.

## PNLE high-yield points
- Antenatal care is preventive and risk-based, not merely a series of routine visits.
- Severe headache/visual changes, epigastric pain or seizure may signal preeclampsia/eclampsia.
- Bleeding, fluid leakage and reduced fetal movement require assessment.
- Medication reconciliation includes prescriptions, OTC drugs, herbals and supplements.
- ABCs and immediate threats take priority over routine teaching.

## Philippine practice considerations
Use current DOH antenatal-care and maternal-health policies, local referral pathways, and PRC/BON scope-of-practice standards. International recommendations inform care but should be distinguished from official Philippine requirements and facility protocols.
