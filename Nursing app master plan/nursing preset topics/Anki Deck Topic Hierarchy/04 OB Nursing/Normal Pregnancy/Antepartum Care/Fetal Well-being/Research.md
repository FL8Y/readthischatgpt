# Antepartum Fetal Well-Being Assessment: PNLE Nursing Review

## Purpose
Antepartum fetal surveillance evaluates risk of fetal compromise before labor. The choice and frequency of testing depend on maternal conditions, gestational age, fetal growth, prior history and whether results would change management. No test guarantees that stillbirth or fetal compromise cannot occur.

## Maternal assessment and fetal movement
Ask about usual fetal movement pattern and any reduction/change. A pregnant patient reporting reduced fetal movement should contact the maternity service promptly for assessment; do not delay care to try repeated home remedies or wait until the next routine visit. Movement awareness and formal counting instructions vary by guideline and local protocol.

## Common surveillance methods
- **Ultrasound:** assesses fetal anatomy, growth, amniotic fluid and placental location as indicated.
- **Nonstress test (NST):** external fetal heart-rate monitoring to assess accelerations associated with fetal movement. Interpretation depends on gestational age, medications, fetal sleep and local criteria.
- **Biophysical profile (BPP):** combines ultrasound observations (such as breathing movements, body movements, tone and fluid) with NST in some protocols.
- **Modified BPP:** commonly combines NST and amniotic fluid assessment.
- **Doppler velocimetry:** evaluates blood-flow patterns, especially in suspected fetal growth restriction or placental insufficiency.
- **Maternal kick/movement awareness:** can identify a change from an individual fetus's usual pattern, but a normal count does not override other symptoms.

## Nursing responsibilities
1. Verify indication, gestational age, test type, preparation and relevant medicines/conditions.
2. Explain the procedure and position the patient safely; avoid prolonged flat supine positioning if symptomatic.
3. Ensure tracing quality and document test duration, maternal report of movement, maternal medications and relevant events.
4. Recognize that a nonreactive NST is not by itself a definitive diagnosis of fetal compromise; further assessment (e.g., prolonged monitoring, stimulation, BPP or clinician review) may be required.
5. Escalate abnormal testing, reduced fetal movement, bleeding, fluid leakage, severe pain or maternal deterioration promptly.

## Antepartum test interpretation: high-yield criteria and limitations

### Nonstress test (NST)
For a fetus at **32 completed weeks or later**, a commonly used reactive NST definition is at least **two accelerations** of fetal heart rate peaking at least **15 beats/min above baseline and lasting at least 15 seconds**, within a 20-minute period. Before 32 weeks, criteria are commonly modified to **10 beats/min for 10 seconds** because of gestational-age differences in autonomic maturation. If the tracing is not reactive after 20 minutes, the observation period may be extended (often to 40 minutes) to allow for a fetal sleep cycle, following the local protocol.

A **nonreactive NST is not a diagnosis of fetal hypoxia or fetal death**. It requires assessment of gestational age, fetal sleep/activity, medication effects and tracing quality, and follow-up testing/clinician review according to protocol. A reassuring NST reflects the observed period only; it cannot guarantee future fetal wellbeing.

### Biophysical profile (BPP)
A full BPP generally assesses five components: NST reactivity, fetal breathing movements, gross body movements, fetal tone and amniotic-fluid volume. Many protocols score each component 0 or 2 for a total of 10; a modified BPP commonly combines NST and amniotic-fluid assessment. Interpretation cutoffs and decisions depend on gestational age, fluid findings, maternal conditions and the specific guideline. Do not use a score alone to determine delivery timing without clinician interpretation.

Amniotic-fluid assessment is important because oligohydramnios can be associated with membrane rupture or placental insufficiency, while polyhydramnios can be associated with diabetes or fetal conditions. Neither finding, in isolation, specifies a single cause.

### Doppler velocimetry
Umbilical-artery Doppler is mainly used in pregnancies with suspected fetal growth restriction/placental insufficiency, with surveillance frequency and delivery planning determined by the flow pattern, gestation and the complete clinical picture. A normal Doppler does not mean risk is zero, and Doppler is not a universal routine screen for every uncomplicated pregnancy. Middle cerebral artery Doppler has a different use in selected situations (for example, fetal anemia surveillance with maternal red-cell alloantibodies); do not confuse it with umbilical-artery Doppler.

### Reduced fetal movement
A report of reduced or changed fetal movement relative to that fetus's usual pattern requires prompt contact with the maternity service and appropriate assessment. Do not instruct the patient to wait until the next routine visit, rely on a previously reassuring test, or use a home Doppler to rule out compromise. Movement awareness/counting instructions vary by guideline; local patient instructions should be specific and consistent.

## Nursing response to an abnormal test
1. Confirm maternal identity, gestational age, test indication, technical quality and relevant medication/clinical factors.
2. Reassess maternal status and obtain fetal tracing/findings as appropriate; identify urgent symptoms such as bleeding, rupture of membranes, severe pain, hypertension symptoms or reduced movement.
3. Notify the responsible obstetric clinician promptly for nonreactive NST, abnormal fluid/Doppler results, or any concerning fetal pattern according to the facility's response threshold.
4. Continue monitoring and prepare the follow-up test or escalation ordered; do not independently interpret an abnormal screening result as a definitive diagnosis.
5. Document time, test condition/results, maternal report, whom you notified, instructions, disposition and follow-up completion.

## High-yield test concepts
- A reactive NST indicates reassuring accelerations for the gestational age and test conditions, but does not guarantee future wellbeing.
- A nonreactive NST may occur because of fetal sleep, prematurity or medication effects; follow protocol for further evaluation.
- Oligohydramnios can be associated with membrane rupture or placental insufficiency; polyhydramnios may be associated with diabetes or fetal conditions, but neither is diagnostic of a single cause.
- Doppler studies are particularly useful in selected high-risk pregnancies such as fetal growth restriction; they are not a universal screening test for every uncomplicated pregnancy.

## Nursing priorities and safety
Maternal stabilization comes first in maternal emergencies; fetal assessment follows in parallel when feasible. For reduced fetal movement or abnormal test results, arrange timely clinical review and do not reassure solely because a prior test was normal. Document communication and disposition.

## PNLE high-yield points
- NST evaluates fetal heart-rate reactivity; BPP adds ultrasound observations.
- A nonreactive NST requires protocol-based follow-up, not automatic assumption of fetal death or automatic discharge.
- Reduced fetal movement is a reason for prompt assessment.
- Test selection depends on risk and gestational age; there is no universal surveillance schedule for every pregnancy.
