# Antepartum Fetal Well-Being Assessment: PNLE Nursing Review

## Purpose
Antepartum fetal surveillance evaluates risk of fetal compromise before labor. The choice and frequency of testing depend on maternal conditions, gestational age, fetal growth, prior history and whether results would change management. No test guarantees that stillbirth or fetal compromise cannot occur.

## Maternal assessment and fetal movement
Ask about usual fetal movement pattern and any reduction/change. A pregnant patient reporting reduced fetal movement should contact the maternity service promptly for assessment; do not delay care to try repeated home remedies or wait until the next routine visit. Movement awareness and formal counting instructions vary by guideline and local protocol.

## Common surveillance methods
- **Ultrasound:** assesses fetal anatomy, growth, amniotic fluid and placental location as indicated.
- **Nonstress test (NST):** external fetal heart-rate monitoring to assess accelerations associated with fetal movement. Interpretation depends on gestational age, medications, fetal sleep and local criteria.
- **Biophysical profile (BPP):** combines ultrasound observations (such as breathing movements, body movements, tone and fluid) with NST in some protocols.
- **Modified BPP:** commonly combines NST and amniotic fluid assessment.
- **Doppler velocimetry:** evaluates blood-flow patterns, especially in suspected fetal growth restriction or placental insufficiency.
- **Maternal kick/movement awareness:** can identify a change from an individual fetus's usual pattern, but a normal count does not override other symptoms.

## Nursing responsibilities
1. Verify indication, gestational age, test type, preparation and relevant medicines/conditions.
2. Explain the procedure and position the patient safely; avoid prolonged flat supine positioning if symptomatic.
3. Ensure tracing quality and document test duration, maternal report of movement, maternal medications and relevant events.
4. Recognize that a nonreactive NST is not by itself a definitive diagnosis of fetal compromise; further assessment (e.g., prolonged monitoring, stimulation, BPP or clinician review) may be required.
5. Escalate abnormal testing, reduced fetal movement, bleeding, fluid leakage, severe pain or maternal deterioration promptly.

## High-yield test concepts
- A reactive NST indicates reassuring accelerations for the gestational age and test conditions, but does not guarantee future wellbeing.
- A nonreactive NST may occur because of fetal sleep, prematurity or medication effects; follow protocol for further evaluation.
- Oligohydramnios can be associated with membrane rupture or placental insufficiency; polyhydramnios may be associated with diabetes or fetal conditions, but neither is diagnostic of a single cause.
- Doppler studies are particularly useful in selected high-risk pregnancies such as fetal growth restriction; they are not a universal screening test for every uncomplicated pregnancy.

## Nursing priorities and safety
Maternal stabilization comes first in maternal emergencies; fetal assessment follows in parallel when feasible. For reduced fetal movement or abnormal test results, arrange timely clinical review and do not reassure solely because a prior test was normal. Document communication and disposition.

## PNLE high-yield points
- NST evaluates fetal heart-rate reactivity; BPP adds ultrasound observations.
- A nonreactive NST requires protocol-based follow-up, not automatic assumption of fetal death or automatic discharge.
- Reduced fetal movement is a reason for prompt assessment.
- Test selection depends on risk and gestational age; there is no universal surveillance schedule for every pregnancy.
