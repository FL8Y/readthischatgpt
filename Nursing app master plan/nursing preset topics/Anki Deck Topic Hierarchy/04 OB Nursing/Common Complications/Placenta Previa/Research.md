# Placenta Previa: PNLE Nursing Review

## Definition
Placenta previa is placental tissue covering the internal cervical os; a low-lying placenta is near but does not cover the os. Ultrasound terminology and diagnostic thresholds may vary by guideline. Placental location can change as the lower uterine segment develops, so follow-up imaging may be needed.

## Risk factors
Prior cesarean birth or uterine surgery, previous placenta previa, multiple gestation, multiparity, smoking and advanced maternal age are associated factors. Placenta previa can occur without these risks.

## Clinical manifestations and assessment
The classic presentation is painless, bright-red vaginal bleeding in the second half of pregnancy. Bleeding can recur and may become severe. Some patients have contractions or pain, so pain does not exclude previa. Assess maternal vital signs, perfusion, bleeding amount, gestational age and fetal status. Obtain ordered CBC, blood group/crossmatch and other investigations according to severity.

## Diagnostic safety
Ultrasound, usually transabdominal and/or transvaginal performed by qualified staff, establishes placental location. Transvaginal ultrasound is considered safe when performed appropriately and can be more accurate. **Do not perform a digital vaginal examination in suspected placenta previa until previa has been excluded**, because it can provoke major hemorrhage. If bleeding and instability are present, stabilize and escalate immediately.

## Management principles
Management depends on bleeding, gestational age, placental location, fetal status and local protocol. Stable patients without significant bleeding may have activity/monitoring instructions and follow-up ultrasound. Significant bleeding requires hospital assessment, maternal stabilization, fetal evaluation and preparation for blood products. Persistent placenta previa covering the os generally requires cesarean birth; timing is individualized. Consider placenta accreta spectrum risk, especially with previa and prior cesarean scars.

## Nursing priorities and rationales
| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Hemorrhage | Assess vitals, perfusion and bleeding; establish IV access and prepare ordered labs/crossmatch. | Previa can cause sudden major blood loss. |
| Avoid provoking bleeding | No digital vaginal examination until placental location is known and previa excluded. | Cervical manipulation may disrupt placental tissue over the os. |
| Fetal status | Assess fetal heart rate and gestational age; escalate abnormalities. | Maternal hemorrhage can compromise fetal oxygenation. |
| Delivery readiness | Communicate prior cesarean/uterine surgery and accreta risk; prepare team per plan. | Placenta accreta spectrum increases hemorrhage and surgical complexity. |
| Education | Explain urgent return for bleeding, contractions, pain, dizziness or reduced fetal movement. | Recurrent bleeding can worsen rapidly. |

## Medication and intervention safety
- Blood products and emergency hemorrhage protocols are guided by severity and local policy.
- Corticosteroids may be considered when preterm birth risk is present, based on gestational age and protocol.
- Tocolysis, if considered at all, is patient-specific and inappropriate when urgent delivery is required or maternal/fetal status is compromised.
- Do not use digital cervical examination to assess labor before previa is excluded in a bleeding patient.

## Placenta previa versus abruption
| Feature | Previa | Abruption |
|---|---|---|
| Typical bleeding | Painless, bright red | Often painful, may be concealed |
| Uterus | Often soft/nontender | May be tender, irritable or rigid |
| Ultrasound | Locates placenta | Can miss abruption |
| Digital vaginal exam | Avoid until excluded | Avoid while previa remains possible |
Both can cause major maternal hemorrhage and fetal compromise; classic patterns are not absolute.

## Current guideline update: RCOG Green-top Guideline No. 27a (2026)
RCOG published the fifth edition of Green-top Guideline No. 27a on 30 June 2026; the RCOG page was opened and checked during this research cycle. The update highlights earlier detection of placenta accreta spectrum (PAS), assessment during the first antenatal visit and 18–20-week anatomy scan, transvaginal ultrasound for placental position/cervical length, specialist multidisciplinary planning and delivery at a specialist centre when PAS probability is high, and intraoperative cell salvage as beneficial/safe in placenta previa/PAS. The guideline page says review will commence in 2029 unless otherwise indicated. This new edition supersedes treating the 2018 guideline as the most current version.

**Important correction to risk teaching:** Previous cesarean delivery is a major PAS risk factor when placenta previa coexists, but PAS is not exclusively linked to previa or cesarean scar. Other uterine surgery/trauma (including curettage, hysteroscopic surgery, endometrial ablation, uterine artery embolization and myomectomy) can also be relevant. Ensure the history and referral pathway do not screen only for cesarean delivery.

**PNLE and bedside priorities remain:** any second-half pregnancy bleeding requires assessment and ultrasound-based placental localization; do not perform a digital vaginal examination while previa remains possible. If hemorrhage or instability occurs, activate emergency response and stabilize the mother while assessing fetal status. Delivery timing, inpatient/outpatient planning and steroid use depend on bleeding pattern, gestational age, placental/PAS findings and locally adopted protocol; do not infer a universal schedule from this summary.

## PNLE high-yield points
- Painless late-pregnancy bleeding suggests placenta previa.
- The priority safety rule is **no digital vaginal examination until previa is excluded**.
- Ultrasound determines placental location; do not rely on symptoms alone.
- Previa with prior cesarean delivery raises concern for placenta accreta spectrum.
