# Cervical Insufficiency (Historically “Incompetent Cervix”): PNLE Nursing Review

## Definition
Cervical insufficiency is painless cervical shortening/dilation in the second trimester or early preterm period that can lead to pregnancy loss or very preterm birth, often without regular contractions. “Cervical insufficiency” is preferred terminology because “incompetent” is stigmatizing and medically imprecise.

## Risk factors
History of painless second-trimester pregnancy loss or early preterm birth, prior cervical trauma/procedures (e.g., some excisional cervical procedures), congenital uterine/cervical anomalies and certain connective-tissue disorders may increase risk. Many patients have no known risk factor.

## Clinical manifestations and diagnosis
Possible findings include pelvic pressure, increased discharge, spotting, or painless cervical dilation; symptoms can be absent. Diagnosis combines obstetric history, examination by a qualified clinician and transvaginal ultrasound cervical-length assessment. Rule out labor, membrane rupture, bleeding/abruption and infection. A short cervix is a finding, not automatically equivalent to cervical insufficiency; management depends on history, gestational age and exact findings.

## Management options
- **Vaginal progesterone:** may be recommended for selected patients with a short cervix, according to guideline and history.
- **Cervical cerclage:** a suture placed around the cervix in selected patients, such as some with a classic history of painless mid-trimester loss or history plus ultrasound findings. Timing and eligibility vary.
- **Expectant surveillance:** repeat cervical-length assessment and symptom monitoring for selected patients.
- Activity restriction/bed rest is not routinely recommended as a blanket intervention; evidence of benefit is lacking and immobility can cause harm.
- Cerclage may be inappropriate with active labor, significant bleeding, ruptured membranes, intra-amniotic infection or fetal demise; specialist assessment is required.

## Nursing priorities
| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Early recognition | Ask about prior mid-trimester losses/preterm births and current pressure, bleeding, discharge or fluid leakage. | History and symptoms guide timely assessment. |
| Diagnostic safety | Arrange/confirm clinician review and transvaginal cervical-length assessment as ordered. | Accurate diagnosis prevents inappropriate intervention. |
| Post-cerclage monitoring | Observe for contractions, bleeding, fluid leakage, fever or increasing pain; follow orders. | These may indicate labor, membrane rupture or infection. |
| Medication safety | Verify progesterone indication, formulation, route and prescribed schedule. | Treatment is individualized; formulations are not interchangeable. |
| Education | Give clear return precautions and avoid blame. | Symptoms can be subtle and patients may have prior pregnancy-loss distress. |

## Emergency red flags
Regular contractions, heavy bleeding, gush/leakage of fluid, fever, foul discharge, severe pain or reduced fetal movement at a gestation where movement is established require prompt assessment. Do not advise waiting for the next appointment if symptoms suggest labor or membrane rupture.

## PNLE high-yield points
- Classic cervical insufficiency is painless cervical dilation with pregnancy loss/preterm birth, often without regular contractions.
- Transvaginal cervical length and obstetric history guide management.
- Cerclage is not appropriate for every short cervix and may be contraindicated in labor, infection, significant bleeding or ruptured membranes.
- Routine bed rest is not a universal treatment.
- Maternal-fetal status and urgent complications take priority over routine teaching.
