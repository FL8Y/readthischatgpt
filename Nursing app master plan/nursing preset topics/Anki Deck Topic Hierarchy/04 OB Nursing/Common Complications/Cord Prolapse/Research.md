# Umbilical Cord Prolapse: PNLE Nursing Review

## Definition
Umbilical cord prolapse occurs when the cord descends through the cervix alongside (occult) or past (overt) the presenting part after membrane rupture. Cord presentation means the cord lies between the presenting part and cervix; membrane rupture may or may not have occurred. Cord compression can abruptly reduce fetal oxygen delivery, making this an obstetric emergency.

## Risk factors
- Non-cephalic presentation (breech/transverse), unstable lie.
- Prematurity, multiple gestation, polyhydramnios, small fetus or a high/unengaged presenting part.
- Artificial rupture of membranes when the presenting part is high or not well applied.
Risk factors are not required; prolapse can occur unexpectedly.

## Recognition
- Sudden fetal bradycardia or recurrent severe variable decelerations, particularly after spontaneous or artificial rupture of membranes.
- Cord visible or palpable in the vagina.
- Maternal report of something protruding after waters break.
A normal fetal heart rate does not exclude occult prolapse if the cord is suspected.

## Immediate nursing response
1. **Call for help immediately:** activate obstetric emergency response; notify obstetrician, anesthesia, neonatal and theatre teams.
2. **Relieve cord compression** while preparing urgent birth: use a trained, protocol-approved position such as knee-chest or exaggerated lateral/Trendelenburg positioning; a clinician may manually elevate the presenting part. Maintain elevation until relieved by definitive delivery or directed otherwise.
3. **Do not handle the cord unnecessarily.** If the cord is outside the vagina, do not attempt to push it back in; minimize manipulation and follow local protocol.
4. **Assess fetal heart rate continuously** if possible without delaying decompression or delivery.
5. If confirmed, expedite birth. Emergency cesarean is usual when vaginal birth is not imminent; operative vaginal birth may be considered by the responsible clinician only when fully dilated and birth can be achieved safely and faster.
6. If exposed cord is drying or exposed to air, follow local protocol to keep it warm/moist with sterile saline-soaked gauze; avoid pressure or unnecessary manipulation.

## Rationale and priorities
Cord compression can obstruct umbilical blood flow and cause acute fetal hypoxia. The immediate goal is to reduce compression while definitive delivery is arranged. Do not prioritize lengthy documentation, routine observations or teaching over emergency decompression and delivery. Record timing of membrane rupture, fetal heart-rate changes, recognition, position, calls, interventions and birth.

## Prevention and procedural safety
- Before amniotomy, confirm presentation and assess engagement/station according to scope and protocol; communicate a high or mobile presenting part.
- Use caution with membrane rupture when malpresentation, polyhydramnios or a high presenting part is present.
- Following membrane rupture, promptly assess fetal heart rate and report acute abnormalities.
- Do not perform amniotomy casually or outside authorized scope/protocol.

## Common pitfalls
- Waiting for fetal heart-rate abnormality when cord is visibly/palpably prolapsed.
- Pushing the cord back into the uterus.
- Releasing manual elevation or removing decompression positioning before directed by the team.
- Delaying escalation while attempting to complete a full assessment.
- Assuming cord pulsation is a reliable indicator of fetal wellbeing.

## PNLE high-yield comparison
| Scenario | Priority |
|---|---|
| Sudden bradycardia immediately after rupture of membranes | Suspect prolapse; assess rapidly and summon help. |
| Cord palpable/visible | Emergency response and immediate compression relief. |
| High presenting part before planned amniotomy | Alert clinician; assess whether amniotomy is safe and appropriate. |
| Birth not imminent vaginally | Prepare for urgent cesarean according to obstetric team decision. |

ABCs and fetal oxygenation threats take priority. Maslow's framework is not a reason to delay a time-critical obstetric emergency.
