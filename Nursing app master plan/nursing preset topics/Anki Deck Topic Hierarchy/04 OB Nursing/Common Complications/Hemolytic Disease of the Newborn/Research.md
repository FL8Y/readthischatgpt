# Hemolytic Disease of the Fetus and Newborn (HDFN): PNLE Nursing Review

## Definition and mechanism
HDFN occurs when maternal IgG antibodies cross the placenta and destroy fetal/newborn red cells. Rh(D) alloimmunization is a classic cause; other red-cell antibodies can also cause disease. Sensitization may follow fetomaternal hemorrhage, pregnancy/birth, miscarriage, procedures or transfusion. Consequences include fetal anemia, hydrops, neonatal jaundice and bilirubin-related neurologic injury.

## Assessment and screening
- Review maternal ABO/Rh type and antibody screen early in pregnancy and as directed by antenatal protocol.
- If antibodies are detected, identify the antibody and its clinical significance; specialist surveillance may be needed.
- Consider prior affected pregnancy and transfusion history.
- Fetal surveillance for significant antibodies may include ultrasound and middle cerebral artery peak systolic velocity Doppler.
- Newborn tests may include blood group, direct antiglobulin (direct Coombs) test, hemoglobin/hematocrit, bilirubin and reticulocyte count.

## Manifestations
Fetal disease may cause anemia, tachycardia, hepatosplenomegaly, hydrops, heart failure or fetal demise in severe cases. Newborn disease may cause pallor, anemia, jaundice especially within the first 24 hours, rising bilirubin, hepatosplenomegaly, lethargy or poor feeding. Early jaundice is a red flag and needs prompt evaluation.

## Prevention and management
- **Rh(D) immune globulin (RhIG)** prevents sensitization in eligible unsensitized Rh(D)-negative patients. It does not treat established alloimmunization; a patient already sensitized to D antigen does not benefit from RhIG for that antibody.
- Established clinically significant antibodies require specialist surveillance; severe fetal anemia may require intrauterine transfusion or planned delivery.
- Newborn treatment may include phototherapy, serial bilirubin/hemoglobin monitoring, feeding/hydration support, and exchange transfusion in severe cases according to neonatal protocol.

## Nursing priorities
| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Prevention | Verify blood group, antibody screen and RhIG eligibility/order. | RhIG prevents sensitization only in eligible unsensitized patients. |
| Fetal surveillance | Ensure referral/follow-up for significant antibodies and ordered fetal anemia assessments. | Fetal anemia may progress before maternal symptoms appear. |
| Newborn jaundice | Report jaundice in the first 24 hours and follow bilirubin orders. | Bilirubin can rise rapidly and injure the developing brain. |
| Medication safety | Verify identity, Rh type, sensitization status, product, route, timing and dose. | Incorrect use may be ineffective or inappropriate. |
| Family education | Explain follow-up and future-pregnancy implications compassionately. | Alloimmunization can affect later pregnancies. |

## Medication safety
RhIG is a human plasma-derived immunoglobulin. Check product-specific contraindications, hypersensitivity history, dose, route, timing and local protocol. Follow local guidance for sensitizing events, postpartum administration and fetomaternal hemorrhage testing; eligibility and schedules vary by jurisdiction and gestational age.

## Source-checked prevention and follow-up distinctions

### Screen for sensitization, not just Rh status
Merck Manual Professional Edition's peer-reviewed HDFN review (full review March 2024; last updated April 2024) states that all pregnant patients should be screened at the first prenatal visit for ABO/Rh type and antibodies that can cause HDFN. If a clinically significant maternal antibody is present, specialist follow-up may include serial antibody levels and fetal middle cerebral artery (MCA) Doppler assessment for anemia risk. MCA Doppler is a fetal surveillance tool used in at-risk pregnancies; it is not an ordinary screening test for every pregnancy. The same source discusses intrauterine transfusion for severe fetal anemia and newborn evaluation for possible exchange transfusion.

### Rh(D) immune globulin (RhIG): prevention only
RhIG is for **eligible, unsensitized Rh(D)-negative** patients when the fetus may be Rh(D)-positive; it is not a treatment for established immune anti-D. Confirm the antibody screen and clinical event before administration. The typical US routine schedule often involves an antenatal dose around 28 weeks and a postpartum dose within 72 hours when the newborn is Rh-positive or status meets the protocol criteria, but schedule, product/dose and event-specific eligibility depend on local guidance. Do not copy the Merck page's cross-country dosing discussion as a universal Philippines schedule.

### Early pregnancy loss under 12 weeks: guideline change and genuine variation
Older general RhIG references and some patient FAQs tell patients to seek advice about RhIG after abortion or pregnancy loss under 12 weeks. ACOG's **December 2024 Clinical Practice Update** on Rh D immune globulin after abortion or pregnancy loss at less than 12 weeks suggests forgoing routine Rh testing and RhIG prophylaxis in this setting. Merck's 2024 HDFN review acknowledges that professional recommendations for less-than-12-week loss vary. Therefore, do not present the older ACOG patient FAQ wording or one country's rule as a universal rule. Follow the current locally adopted protocol and consult the responsible clinician; this file does not decide an individual patient's eligibility. ACOG update catalogue: https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/clinical-practice-update ; older general patient FAQ (not a substitute for the 2024 update): https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/the-rh-factor-how-it-can-affect-your-pregnancy .

### Newborn jaundice safety
Merck's HDFN review confirms that maternal antibodies can cause fetal anemia and neonatal indirect hyperbilirubinemia. Jaundice during the first 24 hours, pallor, poor feeding, lethargy, rapid breathing or worsening anemia require prompt neonatal assessment. A positive direct antiglobulin test (DAT/Direct Coombs) supports immune hemolysis in context but must be interpreted with bilirubin trajectory, hemoglobin, reticulocytes, smear and clinical status; neither blood group mismatch nor DAT result alone determines treatment. Follow the current neonatal bilirubin guideline for phototherapy/exchange thresholds; thresholds depend on age in hours, gestational age and neurotoxicity risk factors.

## PNLE high-yield points
- RhIG is prevention, not treatment, for established anti-D alloimmunization.
- Rh-negative status alone does not prove sensitization; antibody status matters.
- Jaundice in the first 24 hours requires prompt evaluation, not reassurance as routine physiologic jaundice.
- Severe fetal anemia can cause hydrops and heart failure.
