# Shoulder Dystocia: PNLE Nursing Review

## Definition
Shoulder dystocia is a birth emergency in which the fetal shoulders fail to deliver after the head has emerged and additional maneuvers are required. It is often recognized when routine gentle axial traction does not deliver the shoulders and the “turtle sign” (head retracting against the perineum) may be seen. It is unpredictable and can occur without known risk factors.

## Risk factors and complications
Risk factors include fetal macrosomia, maternal diabetes, prior shoulder dystocia, obesity and prolonged second stage, but many cases occur without identifiable risks. Maternal complications include postpartum hemorrhage and severe perineal tears. Neonatal complications include brachial plexus injury, clavicle/humerus fracture, hypoxia and rarely death.

## Immediate response
1. Recognize and announce the emergency; call obstetric, neonatal, anesthesia and additional skilled staff.
2. Note the time of head delivery and start an emergency sequence according to local protocol.
3. **McRoberts maneuver** (hyperflex maternal hips) and **suprapubic pressure** are common first-line maneuvers; suprapubic pressure is directed to dislodge the anterior shoulder, not fundal pressure.
4. Additional maneuvers (e.g., internal rotational maneuvers, delivery of posterior arm, all-fours position) are performed by trained clinicians according to the sequence and circumstances.
5. **Never apply fundal pressure**: it can worsen impaction and increase risk of uterine rupture or fetal injury.
6. Avoid excessive downward/lateral traction on the fetal head/neck because of brachial plexus injury risk.
7. Prepare neonatal resuscitation and assess mother/newborn after birth.

## Nursing role and rationale
| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Rapid mobilization | Call for help, announce dystocia and document time. | Coordinated skilled response is time-critical. |
| Positioning | Assist with McRoberts and safe positioning as directed. | Changes pelvic geometry and can help release the shoulder. |
| Safe maneuver support | Assist clinician with suprapubic pressure only as instructed; never fundal pressure. | Fundal pressure worsens impaction; suprapubic pressure targets the shoulder. |
| Neonatal readiness | Ensure neonatal resuscitation equipment/team present. | Hypoxia and birth trauma may occur. |
| Maternal assessment | After birth, assess bleeding, uterine tone and perineal trauma; support ordered neonatal assessment. | Hemorrhage, severe tears and neonatal injury are recognized complications. |
| Documentation | Record timing, maneuvers, staff, traction/maternal positioning and newborn status objectively. | Supports safe handover and accurate clinical record. |

## Medication/intervention safety
There is no medication that replaces the immediate mechanical emergency response. Uterotonics may be used after birth for hemorrhage prevention/treatment according to protocol. Do not delay maneuvers to obtain medications or complete documentation. Never perform unfamiliar internal maneuvers without training/authorization.

## PNLE high-yield points
- Shoulder dystocia is diagnosed after delivery of the head when shoulders do not deliver with routine gentle traction.
- McRoberts and suprapubic pressure are common initial maneuvers.
- **Fundal pressure is contraindicated.**
- Excessive traction on the head/neck risks brachial plexus injury.
- Prepare for postpartum hemorrhage and neonatal resuscitation/assessment.
