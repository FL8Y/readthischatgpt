# Hydatidiform Mole (Molar Pregnancy): PNLE Nursing Review

## Definition and classification
Gestational trophoblastic disease (GTD) is a group of disorders arising from abnormal trophoblastic tissue. A hydatidiform mole is a form of GTD:
- **Complete mole:** typically no embryo/fetus; diffuse abnormal trophoblastic proliferation and hydropic villi; commonly androgenetic diploid genetics.
- **Partial mole:** often abnormal fetal/embryonic tissue may be present; typically triploid; focal trophoblastic changes.
Persistent gestational trophoblastic neoplasia can follow a molar pregnancy, so follow-up is essential.

## Risk factors and manifestations
Risk is higher at extremes of reproductive age and after a prior molar pregnancy. Possible findings include vaginal bleeding in early pregnancy, uterine size greater than expected, severe nausea/vomiting, early-onset hypertension/preeclampsia, hyperthyroid symptoms, or passage of vesicle-like tissue. Some cases are detected on ultrasound before classic symptoms develop. Findings are not diagnostic by themselves.

## Assessment and diagnosis
- Assess hemodynamic stability, bleeding, pain, vital signs, and signs of anemia or thyroid excess.
- Quantitative serum beta-hCG is often markedly elevated but is not diagnostic alone.
- Pelvic ultrasound and histopathology after evacuation support diagnosis/classification.
- CBC, blood group/crossmatch when bleeding, renal/liver/thyroid tests as clinically indicated.
- Evaluate urgently for heavy bleeding, respiratory symptoms, severe vomiting/dehydration, or features of preeclampsia/thyrotoxicosis.

## Management and follow-up
Uterine evacuation is commonly performed by suction evacuation by an experienced clinician, with hemorrhage preparedness and appropriate anesthesia planning. Hysterectomy may be considered in selected circumstances where fertility is not desired, but it does not remove the need for hCG surveillance. Chemotherapy is specialist treatment for persistent GTN, not routine treatment for every mole.

Serial quantitative hCG monitoring is required after evacuation until it becomes undetectable and for the duration specified by the treating GTD service. Use reliable contraception during surveillance so a new pregnancy does not complicate interpretation of hCG. Exact monitoring schedules vary by diagnosis and guideline; follow the specialist's current plan.

## Nursing priorities and rationales
| Priority | Nursing action | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Hemorrhage | Monitor bleeding, vitals, perfusion and ordered CBC/crossmatch; escalate instability. | Trophoblastic tissue can cause significant bleeding. |
| Diagnostic safety | Ensure pathology follow-up and serial hCG appointments are understood and scheduled. | Persistent trophoblastic disease can be detected by hCG trends. |
| Preeclampsia/thyrotoxicosis | Assess severe headache, visual changes, hypertension, palpitations, tremor, fever or altered status; escalate promptly. | These complications can occur unusually early in molar pregnancy. |
| Emotional support | Explain that this is an abnormal pregnancy condition and provide compassionate loss support. | Diagnosis and fertility uncertainty can be distressing. |
| Contraception/follow-up | Reinforce contraception and the importance of completing hCG surveillance. | A new pregnancy makes hCG monitoring difficult and may delay detection of persistent disease. |

## Medication and safety notes
Medication decisions are clinician-led. Uterotonics, anesthesia, antiemetics, thyroid management and chemotherapy are selected according to clinical findings and protocols. Avoid starting treatment based only on hCG level without diagnosis and specialist assessment. If chemotherapy for GTN is required, use specialist protocols and detailed reproductive, hematologic, hepatic and renal safety monitoring.

## PNLE high-yield points
- Molar pregnancy may present with bleeding, very high hCG, hyperemesis, uterine size greater than dates, or early preeclampsia, but not every patient has all findings.
- Complete mole usually lacks fetal tissue; partial mole may have abnormal fetal tissue.
- Evacuation is not the end of care: serial hCG surveillance is critical.
- A rising or plateauing hCG after evacuation needs prompt specialist evaluation for persistent GTN.
