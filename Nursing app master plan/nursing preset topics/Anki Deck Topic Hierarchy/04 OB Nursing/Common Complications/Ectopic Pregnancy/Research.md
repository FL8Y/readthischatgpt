# Ectopic Pregnancy: PNLE Nursing Review

## Definition and sites
An ectopic pregnancy implants outside the endometrial cavity. More than 90% occur in a fallopian tube, but other sites include interstitial/cornual region, cervix, ovary, abdomen and cesarean-scar implantation. A tubal ectopic pregnancy cannot progress to a viable intrauterine birth; rupture can cause life-threatening internal hemorrhage.

## Risk factors
Prior ectopic pregnancy, tubal surgery or damage, pelvic inflammatory disease, infertility treatment/assisted reproduction, smoking, and pregnancy occurring with an intrauterine device in place (the absolute pregnancy risk with an IUD is low, but an established pregnancy requires prompt location assessment). Many patients have no known risk factors. Prior sterilization does not eliminate ectopic pregnancy risk.

## Presentation and red flags
- Early pregnancy with vaginal spotting/bleeding and pelvic or abdominal pain.
- Unilateral pain may occur but is not required.
- Shoulder-tip pain, syncope, dizziness, pallor, tachycardia, hypotension, guarding or collapse may indicate rupture/internal bleeding.
- Some patients are initially asymptomatic.

**Emergency:** suspected rupture or hemodynamic instability requires immediate emergency response and urgent surgical assessment. Do not wait for serial hCG or ultrasound if the patient is unstable.

## Assessment and diagnosis
1. ABCs, vital signs, perfusion, mental status, pain and bleeding.
2. Pregnancy testing and quantitative serum beta-hCG.
3. Transvaginal ultrasound to assess pregnancy location and findings of free fluid/adnexal mass.
4. If stable but pregnancy location is uncertain, serial hCG and repeat ultrasound may be required. A single hCG value or an early nondiagnostic scan may not definitively establish location or nonviability.
5. CBC, blood group/Rh and crossmatch as clinically indicated; prepare for hemorrhage management when concerning findings exist.

The differential includes miscarriage, ovarian pathology, appendicitis and other causes of abdominal pain. A positive pregnancy test plus pain or bleeding must prompt consideration of ectopic pregnancy until location is established.

## Management
- **Surgery:** urgent for rupture, instability, significant internal bleeding, or when medical management is unsuitable. Procedure depends on location, findings and fertility considerations.
- **Methotrexate:** may be considered by a qualified clinician for selected stable, unruptured ectopic pregnancies with reliable follow-up and no contraindications. It is not appropriate for a patient with suspected rupture or hemodynamic instability.
- **Expectant management:** only for carefully selected stable patients under specialist follow-up when clinical and biochemical findings support it.
Follow-up to resolution is essential; symptoms and hCG trends must be reviewed according to protocol.

## Methotrexate safety
Methotrexate is a folate antagonist that impairs rapidly dividing trophoblastic tissue. It is teratogenic and must not be used for a potentially viable intrauterine pregnancy. Important contraindications/unsuitable situations include hemodynamic instability or rupture, inability to follow up, significant blood dyscrasias, immunodeficiency, clinically important hepatic or renal dysfunction, active pulmonary disease or peptic ulcer disease, and breastfeeding according to the applicable protocol/product guidance. Check CBC, renal and liver function and medication interactions as ordered. Avoid folic acid supplements and interacting drugs only as directed by the treating protocol; do not independently stop prescribed medicines without clinician review. Counsel about follow-up hCG testing and urgent return for worsening pain, dizziness, syncope or heavy bleeding. ACOG patient guidance also advises avoiding alcohol, folic-acid supplements, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) such as ibuprofen, heavy exercise, sexual intercourse and prolonged sunlight during methotrexate treatment; verify the treating clinician's individualized instructions and do not independently stop essential prescribed medicines.

## Nursing priorities
| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Possible rupture | Immediate escalation, ABC assessment, IV access and ordered labs/crossmatch; prepare for urgent surgery. | Internal bleeding can progress rapidly to shock. |
| Diagnostic safety | Track pregnancy dating, symptoms, hCG/ultrasound plans and follow-up. | Early findings may be indeterminate; missed follow-up can be dangerous. |
| Medication safety | Verify eligibility, baseline labs, contraindications, interactions and follow-up schedule before methotrexate. | Methotrexate has significant toxicity and is inappropriate for rupture or a viable intrauterine pregnancy. |
| Emotional support | Provide nonjudgmental communication and acknowledge grief/fertility concerns. | Ectopic pregnancy is a pregnancy loss and can be traumatic. |

## PNLE high-yield distinctions
- Shoulder-tip pain or syncope with early pregnancy pain/bleeding is a rupture red flag.
- A stable patient may be evaluated with serial hCG and ultrasound; an unstable patient needs emergency management, not delayed testing.
- Methotrexate is not used for suspected ruptured ectopic pregnancy.
- Do not assume that bleeding means miscarriage; pregnancy location must be assessed.
- Maternal stabilization and urgent treatment take priority over routine teaching.
