# Hyperemesis Gravidarum: PNLE Nursing Review

## Definition
Hyperemesis gravidarum (HG) is severe pregnancy-related nausea/vomiting causing substantial impairment, dehydration, weight loss and/or metabolic disturbance. It is more than routine morning sickness. Ketonuria alone should not be used as a definitive measure of severity.

## Causes and risk factors
Mechanisms are multifactorial. Risk may be higher with prior HG, multiple gestation or molar pregnancy. Consider other diagnoses such as gastroenteritis, urinary infection, thyroid disease, pancreatitis, hepatitis or other abdominal pathology when presentation is atypical.

## Manifestations and red flags
Persistent vomiting, inability to retain fluids/food, weight loss, oliguria, tachycardia, orthostatic symptoms, dehydration and electrolyte disturbance. Confusion, ataxia, abnormal eye movements or altered mental status may suggest thiamine deficiency/Wernicke encephalopathy. Hematemesis, severe abdominal pain, fever, syncope or major metabolic abnormalities require urgent assessment.

## Assessment
Assess symptom duration, oral intake, weight change, urine output, vital signs, hydration, medicines and effect on daily function. Depending on severity, investigations may include electrolytes, renal function, CBC, liver tests, urinalysis and ultrasound to assess gestational age, multiple gestation or possible molar pregnancy. Investigate alternative causes as indicated.

## Management
- Individualized fluids, nutrition and guideline-supported antiemetics for milder symptoms.
- IV fluids, measured electrolyte correction, antiemetics and possible admission for severe symptoms or failed oral intake.
- Give **thiamine before dextrose-containing IV fluids or parenteral nutrition** in prolonged vomiting/malnutrition to reduce risk of Wernicke encephalopathy.
- Consider nutritional support and thromboprophylaxis for hospitalized patients according to risk and protocol.

## Medication safety
- Pyridoxine (vitamin B6), with or without doxylamine, is a first-line option in many guidelines. Doxylamine may cause sedation.
- Other agents (such as antihistamines, phenothiazines, metoclopramide or ondansetron) depend on current local guidance, previous response and patient-specific risk. Consider sedation/hypotension, extrapyramidal effects with metoclopramide, and QT-prolongation risk with susceptible patients, electrolyte abnormalities or interacting medicines.
- Check pregnancy-specific recommendations, allergy, interactions and clinician orders. Do not self-start or combine antiemetics without professional review.
- Correct potassium/magnesium abnormalities and evaluate QT risk when relevant.

## Nursing priorities
| Priority | Intervention | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Hydration | Monitor vital signs, intake/output, weight and labs; administer ordered fluids. | Dehydration and electrolyte loss can impair renal and cardiovascular function. |
| Neurologic safety | Assess mental status, gait and eye movements in prolonged severe vomiting; administer ordered thiamine. | Deficiency can cause Wernicke encephalopathy. |
| Medication safety | Check adverse effects, sedation, interactions and response. | Anti-emetics have distinct risks and are not interchangeable. |
| Nutrition | Offer tolerated small portions/fluids when appropriate; coordinate dietitian support. | Supports intake without worsening symptoms unnecessarily. |
| Escalation | Report syncope, oliguria, confusion, severe pain, hematemesis, fever or abnormal electrolytes. | May indicate severe dehydration or an alternative emergency. |
| Support | Validate symptom burden and assess emotional wellbeing. | HG can significantly impair function and mental health. |

## PNLE high-yield points
- HG involves severe symptoms and possible dehydration/weight loss/metabolic disturbance, not just ordinary nausea.
- Ketonuria alone does not grade severity reliably.
- Give thiamine before dextrose-containing fluids in prolonged vomiting/malnutrition.
- Consider multiple gestation or molar pregnancy where indicated.
- Stabilization and electrolyte correction precede routine teaching.
