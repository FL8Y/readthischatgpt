# Stages of Labor: PNLE Nursing Review

## Core concepts
Labor is the process of regular uterine contractions with cervical change leading to birth and placental delivery. The traditional four-stage framework remains useful for nursing assessment:
1. **First stage:** onset of true labor through complete cervical dilation (10 cm); includes latent and active phases. Phase definitions and active-labor thresholds differ among guidelines.
2. **Second stage:** complete dilation through birth of the infant.
3. **Third stage:** birth of infant through delivery of placenta and membranes.
4. **Fourth stage:** immediate recovery after placental delivery, commonly the first 1–2 hours in nursing teaching frameworks; local protocols define monitoring duration.

## First stage
Assess contraction frequency, duration and intensity, cervical change as assessed by authorized clinicians, membrane status, maternal coping/vitals and fetal heart rate. Latent labor may progress slowly; active labor is associated with more rapid cervical change, but a rigid dilation-rate rule should not be used in isolation to diagnose arrest. Avoid unnecessary vaginal examinations, particularly after membrane rupture, because they can increase infection risk.

## Second stage
Begins at complete dilation. Monitor fetal heart rate, maternal vital signs, urge to push, descent/rotation, bladder status, hydration and fatigue. Support effective pushing and positions consistent with clinical status and patient preference. Escalate prolonged second stage, abnormal fetal heart rate, maternal exhaustion or signs of obstruction. Do not encourage pushing before complete dilation unless directed for a specific clinical reason.

## Third stage
Placental separation and expulsion occur. Assess for signs of separation and monitor bleeding and uterine tone. Active management may include a prophylactic uterotonic, controlled cord traction by a trained clinician and uterine tone assessment according to local protocol. Oxytocin is widely recommended for prevention of postpartum hemorrhage; dose/route and timing follow current institutional guidance. Do not pull forcefully on the cord or apply uncontrolled traction.

## Fourth stage / immediate postpartum recovery
Closely assess uterine tone/fundal position, vaginal bleeding/lochia, vital signs, bladder, perineum, pain, consciousness and bonding/feeding. A boggy uterus with heavy bleeding suggests uterine atony and requires immediate action under the postpartum hemorrhage protocol. Monitor more frequently according to acuity and local policy.

## Nursing priorities and rationale
| Stage | Priority assessments/actions | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| First | Maternal-fetal status, contractions, cervical progress, membranes, pain/coping. | Detects labor progression and complications. |
| Second | Fetal heart rate, descent, maternal effort/fatigue, bladder and hydration. | Identifies fetal compromise or obstructed/prolonged birth. |
| Third | Bleeding, placental delivery, uterine tone; administer ordered prophylaxis. | Postpartum hemorrhage risk is concentrated around placental delivery and immediately after birth. |
| Fourth | Frequent uterine tone, bleeding, vitals, bladder and recovery checks. | Hemorrhage and maternal deterioration may occur soon after birth. |

## Safety and escalation
- Abnormal fetal heart rate, heavy bleeding, maternal collapse, seizure, suspected cord prolapse, shoulder dystocia or suspected uterine rupture are emergencies.
- Follow local protocols for fetal monitoring, escalation, emergency response and documentation.
- Use ABCs and immediate threats first; do not let stage labels delay action.
- Medication administration must verify order, dose, route, indication, allergies and contraindications. Oxytocin requires monitoring for uterine hyperstimulation and fetal effects when used for induction/augmentation; postpartum prophylaxis uses a separate protocol.

## PNLE high-yield distinctions
- Complete dilation marks the transition from first to second stage; birth of the infant marks the transition to third stage.
- Placenta delivery does not end risk: the fourth stage requires close surveillance for hemorrhage.
- Labor progress varies; do not apply one universal cervical dilation rate to every patient.
- In the third stage, controlled cord traction is a trained technique, not forceful pulling by an untrained person.

## Verification limitations
Stage terminology is stable, but active-labor thresholds, monitoring frequency and management of prolonged labor vary by guideline and clinical context. Follow the current Philippine facility protocol.
