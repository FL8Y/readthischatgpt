# True Labor vs False Labor: PNLE Nursing Review

## Definitions
**True labor** involves regular uterine contractions associated with progressive cervical effacement and dilation. **False labor/Braxton Hicks contractions** are uterine tightenings that do not cause progressive cervical change. Symptoms alone may not reliably distinguish them; assessment and observation are often needed.

## Comparison
| Feature | True labor | False labor/Braxton Hicks |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern | Usually becomes regular, closer together and stronger | Often irregular and may not intensify consistently |
| Cervical change | Progressive effacement/dilation over time | No progressive cervical change |
| Effect of activity/rest | Often persists despite rest or position change | May ease with hydration, rest or position change |
| Location | May begin in back and wrap toward abdomen; pattern varies | Often felt mainly in front; pattern varies |
| Bloody show/membrane rupture | May occur, but not required | Not expected as a defining feature |
| Diagnostic basis | Contractions plus progressive cervical change | No progressive cervical change after appropriate assessment |

These are tendencies, not absolute rules. Back pain, contraction intensity, or relief with rest alone cannot confirm the diagnosis.

## Nursing assessment
- Ask gestational age, contraction onset/frequency/duration, pain location, fetal movement, fluid leakage, bleeding, prior preterm birth and relevant obstetric history.
- Assess maternal vital signs and fetal heart rate according to acuity.
- Clarify possible membrane rupture, bleeding, reduced fetal movement, severe constant pain or preterm symptoms.
- Cervical examination should be performed only by an authorized clinician when indicated; avoid unnecessary digital exams after membrane rupture.
- If symptoms are preterm, evaluate promptly for preterm labor rather than reassuring based only on contraction pattern.

## Red flags requiring prompt evaluation
- Any suspected labor before 37 completed weeks.
- Rupture or leakage of fluid.
- Vaginal bleeding beyond minor expected show, heavy bleeding or clots.
- Reduced fetal movement, abnormal fetal heart rate, fever, severe persistent abdominal pain, syncope or maternal instability.
- Prior cesarean with severe persistent pain or other concern for uterine rupture.

## Nursing education
For a stable term patient with no red flags, explain how to time contractions and follow the clinician/facility's instructions for when to present. Teach immediate contact/return for fluid leakage, bleeding, decreased fetal movement, preterm symptoms or severe/constant pain. Do not instruct a patient to remain at home when symptoms suggest an emergency.

## Labor diagnosis vs active-labor threshold
The presence of true labor is established by regular uterine contractions with progressive cervical change, not by a particular single dilation number. The *active/established first-stage* threshold differs by guideline: WHO 2018 uses 5 cm for active labor, NICE NG235 uses 4 cm for established labor, and ACOG's 2024 labor-management guideline uses 6 cm as the start of active phase for active-phase management/arrest decisions. These are not interchangeable rules and should not be used as a shortcut to diagnose false labor. A patient below the active-phase threshold may still be in true/latent labor.

**Triage safety:** cervical assessment and serial change may be needed when indicated, but avoid unnecessary digital exams after suspected membrane rupture. Preterm symptoms, bleeding, fluid leakage, reduced fetal movement, severe/constant pain, fever, maternal instability or a concerning fetal heart rate require prompt assessment even if contraction timing seems irregular.

Sources: ACOG Clinical Practice Guideline No. 8 (January 2024), https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/clinical-practice-guideline/articles/2024/01/first-and-second-stage-labor-management ; NICE NG235, https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations ; WHO intrapartum-care recommendations (2018), https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241550215 .

## PNLE high-yield points
- **Progressive cervical change** is the key discriminator; contraction pattern alone is insufficient.
- False labor does not mean symptoms are imaginary; it means contractions are not producing progressive cervical change.
- Preterm contractions deserve assessment because early symptoms may be nonspecific.
- Membrane rupture, bleeding or reduced fetal movement changes the triage priority.

## Verification limitations
Triage and admission criteria depend on gestational age, parity, risk factors, distance to care and local protocols. Do not rely on a single contraction-frequency rule for every patient.
