# Cardinal Movements of Labor: PNLE Nursing Review

## Overview
The cardinal movements describe how the fetus adjusts to the maternal pelvis during vaginal birth. The classic sequence is **engagement, descent, flexion, internal rotation, extension, restitution/external rotation, and expulsion**. Some texts count or group these movements differently; learn the sequence used by the assigned PNLE reference, but understand the mechanism.

## Movements and significance
1. **Engagement:** the widest transverse diameter of the presenting part passes the pelvic inlet. In a vertex presentation, this is commonly described by the biparietal diameter.
2. **Descent:** downward movement through the pelvis, occurring throughout labor and influenced by contractions, maternal pushing, fetal size and pelvic factors.
3. **Flexion:** fetal head flexes so a smaller diameter presents to the pelvis, facilitating passage.
4. **Internal rotation:** the head rotates to align with the maternal pelvic outlet, commonly bringing the occiput anterior in a typical vertex birth.
5. **Extension:** as the head reaches the pelvic outlet, it extends beneath the pubic arch and the head is born.
6. **Restitution/external rotation:** the head realigns with the shoulders, which rotate to fit the pelvic dimensions.
7. **Expulsion:** anterior shoulder, posterior shoulder and the rest of the body are delivered.

## Assessment
Nurses monitor maternal-fetal wellbeing and labor progress within scope; position, station, presentation and cervical findings are assessed by authorized clinicians. Progress is not identical in every birth, and malposition or disproportion can alter the sequence. Do not infer normal progress from one finding alone.

## Nursing priorities
- Monitor fetal heart rate and maternal status throughout labor according to risk and protocol.
- Support positions and coping measures compatible with clinical status.
- Report prolonged or arrested descent, persistent malposition, abnormal fetal heart rate, severe maternal exhaustion or suspected obstruction.
- After head delivery, observe for shoulder dystocia if the shoulders do not deliver with routine gentle traction; call for help and follow emergency protocol. Never use fundal pressure.
- Explain findings in clear, non-alarming language and document objective changes.

## PNLE high-yield points
- Engagement concerns passage of the presenting part through the pelvic inlet.
- Flexion presents a smaller head diameter.
- Internal rotation aligns the head with the pelvic outlet; extension allows the head to pass under the pubic arch.
- Restitution/external rotation aligns head and shoulders.
- Failure of shoulder delivery after the head emerges raises concern for shoulder dystocia, an emergency.
