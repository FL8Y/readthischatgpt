# Intrapartum Fetal Heart-Rate Monitoring and Interpretation: PNLE Nursing Review

> This topic covers intrapartum fetal heart-rate (FHR) surveillance. It complements, and does not duplicate, the antepartum NST/BPP material in `Normal Pregnancy/Antepartum Care/Fetal Well-being`. Interpret any tracing alongside maternal status, gestational age, labor progress, uterine activity, medications and local protocol.

## 1. Purpose and clinical context
FHR monitoring assesses fetal response to labor and can identify patterns concerning for impaired oxygenation. A tracing is not a standalone diagnosis: variability, accelerations, deceleration patterns, uterine activity, maternal condition and changes over time must be considered together. Monitoring may use intermittent auscultation or continuous electronic fetal monitoring (EFM), according to risk, labor course, patient factors and facility policy. EFM has limitations and false positives; no tracing predicts all outcomes.

## 2. FHR features

### Baseline
The baseline is the average FHR over a 10-minute segment, excluding accelerations, decelerations and marked variability. Common baseline range is **110–160 beats/min**.
- **Tachycardia:** baseline above 160 beats/min for at least 10 minutes.
- **Bradycardia:** baseline below 110 beats/min for at least 10 minutes.
Tachycardia may be associated with maternal fever/infection, dehydration, medications or fetal conditions. Bradycardia may be associated with maternal hypotension, cord compression/prolapse, placental abruption, uterine rupture, medications or another urgent cause. Assess the clinical picture, not the number alone.

### Baseline variability
| Descriptor | Approximate amplitude | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Absent | Undetectable | Concerning with recurrent decelerations or bradycardia; assess urgently. |
| Minimal | Detectable, 5 beats/min or less | May reflect fetal sleep, prematurity, medication or hypoxia; use context and duration. |
| Moderate | 6–25 beats/min | Generally reassuring in the appropriate context. |
| Marked | Greater than 25 beats/min | Interpret with trend and other tracing features. |

Variability is not a perfect measure of fetal acid-base status. Reassuring variability does not neutralize every emergency; decreased variability may have non-hypoxic explanations.

### Accelerations
A commonly used definition at **32 weeks or later** is an increase of at least 15 beats/min lasting at least 15 seconds and less than 2 minutes; before 32 weeks, a 10-beat/min increase for 10 seconds is often used. Accelerations are generally reassuring, but their absence during labor alone does not prove fetal acidemia. Apply gestational-age and protocol-specific criteria.

### Decelerations
| Pattern | Description | Common mechanism / response |
|---|---|---|
| Early | Gradual decline and return broadly mirror the contraction | Often related to fetal head compression; assess the whole pattern. |
| Variable | Abrupt decline; timing/shape vary with contractions | Commonly associated with cord compression; recurrent deep or slow-recovering decelerations need assessment and response. |
| Late | Gradual decline begins after contraction onset; nadir after contraction peak, recovery after contraction ends | May indicate impaired uteroplacental oxygen transfer; recurrent late decelerations need prompt action. |
| Prolonged | Decline lasting at least 2 minutes but less than 10 minutes | Assess immediately for reversible cause and emergency conditions; at 10 minutes, a change is classified as a baseline change. |

Interpret recurrence, depth, duration, recovery, variability, uterine activity and clinical context. Do not make a decision from a single contraction or monitor artifact.

## 3. Three-tier interpretation framework (NICHD terminology)

Jurisdictions may use different systems; follow the system adopted by the current facility and PNLE reference.

### Category I — normal
- Baseline 110–160 beats/min.
- Moderate baseline variability.
- No late or variable decelerations.
- Early decelerations and accelerations may be present or absent.

This is reassuring at the time observed, not a guarantee of future fetal well-being.

### Category II — indeterminate
Patterns that meet neither Category I nor Category III criteria. Examples can include minimal variability without recurrent late/variable decelerations, tachycardia, a resolving prolonged deceleration, or recurrent variable decelerations with preserved variability. This broad category requires clinical context, continued assessment and reassessment; not every Category II tracing has the same risk.

### Category III — abnormal
Either:
- Absent baseline variability with recurrent late decelerations, recurrent variable decelerations or bradycardia; **or**
- A sinusoidal pattern.

Category III requires prompt evaluation and corrective action for reversible causes; expedite birth if the pattern does not resolve according to the situation and protocol. Do not leave the patient unattended or wait for routine rounds.

## 4. Uterine activity and tachysystole
Contractions transiently reduce uteroplacental blood flow; the fetus normally recovers between contractions. Excessively frequent contractions shorten recovery time and may compromise oxygen delivery, particularly with oxytocin.

**Uterine tachysystole** is commonly defined as more than five contractions in 10 minutes, averaged over 30 minutes. It can occur with or without FHR changes. Assess contraction frequency, duration, resting interval, uterine relaxation, FHR response and oxytocin/prostaglandin exposure; apply the facility's definition and treatment algorithm.

## 5. Nursing priorities when FHR becomes abnormal
1. **Assess immediately:** verify fetal rather than maternal pulse; evaluate baseline/trend, variability, decelerations, uterine activity, maternal BP/pulse/oxygenation/temperature, bleeding, pain and recent medicines, epidural or position change.
2. **Call for help early** with sustained bradycardia, Category III pattern, prolonged deceleration, suspected cord prolapse, abruption, uterine rupture or maternal instability.
3. **Correct reversible causes under protocol:** reposition laterally or use another appropriate position, stop/reduce oxytocin when indicated by tachysystole or abnormal FHR, and assess/treat maternal hypotension using the prescribed pathway.
4. **Assess emergencies:** bleeding, sudden persistent pain, loss of fetal station, visible/palpable cord after membrane rupture, collapse or severe hypertensive symptoms may change the response and require urgent delivery planning.
5. **Reassess and document:** time abnormality began, pattern and uterine activity, maternal findings, calls, interventions, response and decision-to-delivery timeline.

### Oxygen is not routine for a normoxic mother
Do not apply oxygen automatically for Category II/III FHR when the mother is not hypoxemic. Oxygen may be indicated for maternal hypoxemia or another maternal condition, using the appropriate target/protocol. Routine oxygen in normoxic patients has not been shown to improve fetal outcomes. Follow current professional guidance and facility policy.

### Avoid
- Do not finish routine charting before responding to sustained prolonged deceleration or Category III tracing.
- Do not continue oxytocin unchanged when unsafe uterine activity and concerning FHR require stopping/reducing it under protocol.
- Do not diagnose fetal acidemia from one feature alone.
- Do not perform a digital vaginal examination during antepartum bleeding until placenta previa has been excluded.
- Do not delay urgent delivery for persistent Category III tracing or a time-critical cause that does not resolve with initial measures.

## 6. Medication and intervention safety
| Intervention | Safety note |
|---|---|
| Oxytocin induction/augmentation | Requires a valid indication, correct pump/rate and protocol-based maternal, fetal and uterine monitoring. Reduce/stop according to the emergency algorithm when indicated. |
| Maternal repositioning | Often a fast, low-risk response to positional compression; choose based on maternal condition and suspected cause. |
| IV fluids | Use when hypotension/dehydration or another indication is present; not every FHR abnormality needs an automatic bolus. |
| Tocolytic | May be ordered for persistent tachysystole in selected cases. Drug, dose and contraindications are protocol-specific; do not improvise. |
| Amnioinfusion | May be considered for selected recurrent variable decelerations after membrane rupture by the responsible clinician. Not a general treatment for every abnormal tracing. |
| Operative vaginal birth/cesarean | Decision depends on dilation, station, presentation, cause and whether birth can be achieved safely and promptly. Prepare team/equipment and continue closed-loop communication. |

## 7. High-yield comparisons

| Pattern | Likely concern | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Early decelerations with moderate variability | Often head compression during descent | Continue assessment if the whole pattern remains reassuring. |
| Recurrent variable decelerations | Commonly cord compression; consider prolapse if sudden after membrane rupture | Reposition, assess and escalate if severe/persistent. |
| Recurrent late decelerations | Possible uteroplacental insufficiency | Correct reversible cause and reassess immediately. |
| Prolonged deceleration/sustained bradycardia | May reflect a time-critical event | Call for help and evaluate cord prolapse, abruption, uterine rupture, hypotension or other causes. |
| Tachysystole with FHR changes | Inadequate recovery between contractions | Stop/reduce oxytocin per protocol and escalate. |
| Absent variability plus recurrent late/variable decelerations or bradycardia | Category III under NICHD system | Immediate response; expedite birth if unresolved. |

## 8. Philippine practice considerations
Work within PRC/BON scope, the facility's monitoring policy and obstetric emergency protocol. Confirm who may classify tracings, alter oxytocin, perform examinations, initiate resuscitative measures and activate escalation. International classification systems are educationally useful, but do not replace the Philippine institution's adopted charting/escalation pathway.

## What to remember for the PNLE
- Category I: baseline 110–160, moderate variability, no late or variable decelerations.
- Category III: absent variability with recurrent late/variable decelerations or bradycardia, or a sinusoidal pattern.
- Late decelerations raise concern for uteroplacental oxygen transfer; variable decelerations commonly reflect cord compression.
- Tachysystole is commonly >5 contractions/10 minutes averaged over 30 minutes.
- Assess maternal status, fetal tracing, uterine activity and reversible causes together.
- Follow protocol for reducing/stopping oxytocin; do not continue an unsafe infusion.
- Do not routinely give oxygen to a normoxic mother just because the tracing is Category II/III.
- Persistent Category III and maternal-fetal emergencies take priority over routine documentation or teaching.
