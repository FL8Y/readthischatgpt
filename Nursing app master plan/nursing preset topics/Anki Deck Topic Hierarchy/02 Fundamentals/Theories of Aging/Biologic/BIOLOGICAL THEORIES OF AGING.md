# Biological Theories of Aging

**Status:** Research draft. Models describe proposed mechanisms; they are not mutually exclusive, and no single theory fully explains human aging.

## Major families
| Theory / mechanism | Core idea | Clinical interpretation and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Evolutionary theories | Aging may reflect trade-offs shaped by natural selection: mutation accumulation, antagonistic pleiotropy, disposable soma/resource allocation | Helps explain why selection may not eliminate late-life deterioration; does not predict an individual prognosis |
| Cellular senescence | Cells may permanently stop dividing after stress/damage and develop altered secretory activity | Senescence has protective roles, but accumulation may contribute to tissue dysfunction; effects vary by tissue |
| Telomere-related hypotheses | Telomeres shorten during many cell divisions and can trigger senescence when critically short | Relevant biology, but telomere length alone is not a simple clock for a person's age or health |
| DNA/protein damage and repair | Accumulated damage or imperfect maintenance/repair may contribute to age-related dysfunction | Damage is one component, not a complete explanation |
| Mitochondrial/oxidative-stress hypotheses | Altered energy metabolism and reactive species may contribute to cell injury and signaling changes | Oxidative processes matter, but classic “free radicals explain all aging” models are too simple |
| Immunologic changes | Immune functions change with age, including altered responses and chronic low-grade inflammation | May influence infection/vaccine response; do not assume every older adult is immunocompromised |
| Neuroendocrine/regulatory hypotheses | Changes in signaling networks and homeostatic regulation may affect multiple systems | A systems lens, not a single proven master cause |
| Epigenetic and gene-regulation changes | DNA methylation, chromatin regulation, and related processes change with age and exposures | Emerging field; biomarkers and causal claims need careful interpretation |

## Why no single theory is enough
Aging reflects interacting genetic, epigenetic, metabolic, immune, cellular, environmental, behavioral, and social factors. Evidence varies by species, tissue, sex, health status, and method. Model-organism findings do not automatically establish a human intervention. Avoid presenting antioxidant supplements, extreme diets, hormone manipulation, or unproven products as established ways to slow aging.

## Nursing relevance
- Distinguish age-associated trends from pathology; do not attribute acute change to age without assessment.
- Review medication burden, nutrition, activity, sleep, chronic disease, sensory function, and environmental exposures individually.
- Promote evidence-based prevention and function: vaccination per current guidance, ability-appropriate activity, balanced nutrition, sleep, social connection, and chronic-disease management.
- Chronological age alone does not define physiologic reserve, goals, or treatment preferences.

## PNLE summary
Programmed/regulatory explanations emphasize regulated biological processes; damage/maintenance explanations emphasize accumulated injury and imperfect repair; evolutionary explanations consider survival/reproduction trade-offs. Categories overlap and are not diagnoses.
