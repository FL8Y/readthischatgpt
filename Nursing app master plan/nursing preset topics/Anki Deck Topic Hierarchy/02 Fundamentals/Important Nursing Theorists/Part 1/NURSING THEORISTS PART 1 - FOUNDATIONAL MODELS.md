# Nursing Theorists — Part 1: Foundational Models

## Florence Nightingale — Environmental Theory
Nightingale emphasized modifying environmental conditions so the body has the best opportunity to recover. Nursing observations include ventilation, cleanliness, light, noise, warmth, nutrition, and drainage. In present practice, this lens supports infection prevention, a clean care environment, rest, environmental safety, hydration/nutrition assessment, and noticing how surroundings affect healing. It does not mean environment alone explains illness or replaces medical treatment.

## Virginia Henderson — Need-Based Nursing
Henderson described nursing as helping a person perform activities contributing to health, recovery, or a peaceful death that the person would perform unaided with sufficient strength, will, or knowledge. Her framework draws attention to fundamental needs such as breathing, nutrition, elimination, movement, rest, clothing, temperature regulation, hygiene, safety, communication, beliefs, work, recreation, and learning. Use it to assess what the patient can do independently and where assistance or teaching is needed. Avoid treating the list as a substitute for individualized priorities.

## Hildegard Peplau — Interpersonal Relations
Peplau placed the therapeutic nurse–patient relationship at the center of nursing. Commonly taught phases are orientation, working (which may be described as identification and exploitation in the original framework), and resolution. The relationship involves communication, agreed goals, role clarity, boundaries, and purposeful use of self. Clinically, introduce your role, establish trust, assess concerns, agree on goals, evaluate progress, and close or transition the relationship appropriately. Professional boundaries, confidentiality, consent, and safety remain essential.

## Dorothea Orem — Self-Care Deficit Nursing Theory
Orem's theory asks whether the person's self-care agency is adequate for the therapeutic self-care demands. Nursing is needed when an actual or anticipated self-care deficit exists. Three familiar nursing systems are:
- **Wholly compensatory:** the nurse provides most or all required care because the person cannot do it.
- **Partly compensatory:** nurse and patient share actions.
- **Supportive–educative:** the person can perform self-care but needs teaching, support, or decision assistance.

Assessment therefore includes functional ability, knowledge, motivation, resources, and barriers. The goal is safe independence where possible, not unnecessary dependence.

## Imogene King — Goal Attainment
King's framework emphasizes interaction, perception, communication, transactions, and mutually established goals between nurse and patient. Ask what the patient understands and values, develop measurable shared goals, agree on actions, and evaluate whether the agreed outcomes were achieved. If goals are not achieved, revisit barriers and understanding rather than assuming noncompliance.

## How to compare these theories
| Clinical question | Useful theoretical lens |
|---|---|
| Can the environment be modified to support recovery? | Nightingale |
| Which basic activities does the person need help with? | Henderson |
| How can communication and the relationship improve care? | Peplau |
| What can the person do independently, and what support is needed? | Orem |
| What goals can the nurse and patient agree on together? | King |

## Shared safety application
A theory never overrides an acute clinical threat. If airway, breathing, circulation, severe bleeding, altered consciousness, or another emergency is present, prioritize immediate assessment and escalation. Incorporate the patient's values and preferences, but clarify risks and obtain appropriate consent for care. Reassess outcomes and document clinically relevant observations and agreed plans according to facility policy.

## PNLE-oriented cautions
- Do not confuse Orem's self-care systems with a list of basic needs.
- Peplau is associated with the therapeutic interpersonal process; professional friendship is not the goal.
- Henderson's assistance is directed toward activities the person would perform independently if able.
- Nightingale's environmental approach includes much more than cleanliness.
- King's goal attainment emphasizes mutually agreed goals, not goals set only by the nurse.
