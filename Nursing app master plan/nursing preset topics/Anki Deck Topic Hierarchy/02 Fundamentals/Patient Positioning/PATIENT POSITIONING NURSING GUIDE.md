# Patient Positioning: Nursing Guide

**Status:** Research draft. Positioning must reflect diagnosis, mobility, airway/aspiration risk, surgery-specific restrictions, circulation, comfort, and the care plan.

## Purpose
Positioning supports ventilation, alignment, comfort, safe procedures, mobility, circulation, and skin protection. Prolonged pressure, shear, friction, poor alignment, or device pressure can contribute to pressure injury, nerve injury, contractures, foot drop, pain, or respiratory compromise.

## Common positions
| Position | Description / typical use | Cautions |
|---|---|---|
| Supine | Lying on the back; assessment and selected procedures | May worsen some breathing problems, reflux/aspiration risk, or pressure at sacrum/heels |
| Fowler's / semi-Fowler's | Head of bed elevated; may support breathing and meals when appropriate | Monitor sliding/shear, hypotension, and prescribed restrictions |
| High Fowler's / orthopneic (tripod) | Upright; tripod leans forward with support | Often helps dyspnea; assess fatigue and hemodynamic tolerance |
| Lateral / side-lying | On the side with support and alignment | Relieve pressure; consider drains, surgery, aspiration and respiratory effects |
| Sims' | Semi-prone/lateral, used for selected procedures | Preserve dignity and protect lines; check restrictions |
| Prone | On abdomen for selected respiratory/surgical indications | Needs careful airway, line, pressure-point, and hemodynamic monitoring |
| Trendelenburg / reverse Trendelenburg | Whole bed tilted head-down/head-up | Use only for a clear indication; head-down may worsen reflux/aspiration or respiratory mechanics |

## Safe method
1. Explain the maneuver; assess pain, strength, cognition, lines/tubes, weight-bearing status, fall risk, and surgery/spinal precautions.
2. Use appropriate staff and transfer aids; apply brakes and adjust bed height.
3. Maintain neutral alignment, support joints, and avoid dragging that increases friction/shear.
4. Ensure devices/tubing are not trapped under the body or pressing on the skin.
5. Reassess breathing, circulation, discomfort, neurovascular status where relevant, alignment, and device security afterward.
6. Document positioning, tolerance, skin findings, and restrictions.

## Pressure injury prevention and special considerations
Use a validated risk tool together with skin/clinical assessment. Individualize repositioning frequency based on tissue tolerance, mobility, support surface, perfusion, condition, and policy rather than relying on one fixed schedule for every patient. Offload heels when appropriate, manage moisture, support nutrition/hydration as indicated, and inspect skin under devices. Ring/donut cushions are generally not recommended because they can concentrate pressure.

Follow procedure-specific restrictions after hip/spinal or other surgery. For dyspnea, choose a position that supports breathing while monitoring for deterioration. During meals or enteral feeding, follow aspiration precautions. For pressure-relief turns, use adequate assistance and protect lines, drains, ostomies, and fragile skin.

**Priority:** Immediate airway/breathing/circulation problems take precedence. If deterioration occurs during a move, stop, make the patient safe, reassess ABCs, and summon assistance.
