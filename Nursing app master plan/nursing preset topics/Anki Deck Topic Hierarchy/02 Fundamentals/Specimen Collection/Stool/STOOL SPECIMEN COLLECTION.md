# Stool Specimen Collection

**Status:** Research draft. Collection, container, preservation, dietary/medication restrictions, and transport vary by test.

## Uses and specimen types
Stool may be tested for occult blood, infectious organisms, parasites, inflammatory markers, fat, or other chemical/microscopic characteristics. A stool culture, ova-and-parasite test, occult blood test, and molecular pathogen test have different requirements. Never assume one container or preparation works for all tests.

## Collection sequence
1. Verify the order and laboratory instructions: container, amount, timing, preservatives, transport, and preparation.
2. Identify the patient using approved identifiers. Explain the procedure and protect privacy, dignity, and infection control.
3. Ask the patient to pass stool into a clean, dry approved receptacle. Avoid mixing it with urine, toilet water, cleaning agents, or tissue unless test instructions specify otherwise.
4. Transfer the required amount using the supplied device, avoiding contact with the outside of the container. For multi-site occult blood sampling, follow kit instructions exactly.
5. Secure the lid, label at collection, document as required, and transport promptly under laboratory instructions.
6. If the patient cannot toilet independently, preserve privacy and safe positioning; do not use a diaper/absorbent material unless the test permits it.

## Assessment and reportable findings
Note clinically relevant characteristics such as frequency, consistency, visible blood/mucus, unusually pale or black/tarry stool, and recent changes. Appearance alone is not diagnostic. Promptly report significant bleeding, black/tarry stool with symptoms of blood loss, severe abdominal pain, dehydration, or rapid deterioration. Diet, medication, disease, timing, and contamination may alter test results.

Use standard and additional precautions as indicated. Remove gloves safely, perform hand hygiene, and disinfect contaminated surfaces. For suspected infectious diarrhea, follow facility/contact precautions and specimen handling instructions.

## Common errors
Wrong container/preservative; contamination with urine or toilet water; failure to sample required locations/sites; delayed transport or incorrect temperature; missing identifiers; or using a sample from an absorbent pad when not accepted by the lab.

## Source-checked handling principles

The U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) DPDx stool-collection instructions specify a dry, clean, leakproof container and emphasize preventing contamination by urine, water, soil, or other material. Fresh stool for some parasitology testing may need immediate processing or preservation; commercial kit instructions and the laboratory's test-specific requirements take precedence. This timing is not universal to every stool test.

## PNLE priorities and safety traps
- Verify the exact ordered test and its kit instructions before advising dietary changes, medication holds, preservatives, refrigeration, or transport; these are **not universal across stool tests**.
- Correct patient identification and contamination prevention come before collection convenience. Do not mix stool with urine or toilet water.
- If the patient has significant GI bleeding, severe abdominal pain, shock signs, or rapid deterioration, prioritize clinical assessment and urgent escalation over a routine specimen.
- A positive/negative result must be interpreted in context; do not diagnose or exclude disease from stool appearance alone.
