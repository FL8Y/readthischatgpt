# Specimen Collection: Core Safety and Quality

**Status:** Research draft. Follow the exact laboratory order, container, timing, transport condition, and local laboratory manual; different tests require different procedures.

## Why technique matters
Contamination, incorrect timing, wrong container, inadequate volume, mislabeling, or delayed transport can produce inaccurate results and unnecessary repeat collection or treatment.

## Universal safety sequence
1. Verify the order and test requirements before collecting: timing, fasting, medication timing, preservatives, temperature, and transport.
2. Use at least two approved patient identifiers per policy; never use room/bed number alone.
3. Explain purpose/procedure, provide privacy, and assess barriers such as pain, mobility, language, cognition, or infection-control needs.
4. Perform hand hygiene and use PPE based on anticipated exposure and transmission precautions.
5. Use the correct container, maintain required clean/aseptic technique, and prevent contact with nonsterile surfaces or other body substances.
6. Collect adequate quantity/quality. Do not pool specimens unless instructions expressly require it.
7. Label at point of collection with identifiers, date/time, specimen type/source, and collector information according to policy. Do not pre-label a batch of empty containers for later use.
8. Put the closed container in required biohazard packaging; complete the requisition and transport promptly under test-specific conditions.
9. Document collection, source/timing, tolerance, deviations, and communication with the laboratory/provider.

## Rejection risks and infection prevention
Common rejection/misinterpretation risks include wrong/missing identifiers, wrong container, leakage, insufficient volume, contamination, expired container, missing requisition, incorrect preservative, and transport delay/temperature error. Do not transfer samples between containers without authorized instructions. Ask the lab rather than guessing.

Treat specimens as potentially infectious. Use sharps-safety practices for invasive collection; do not recap contaminated needles. Place requisition paperwork as instructed, often separate from the specimen container in the transport bag. For suspected airborne infection, use the designated collection location and required respiratory protection/ventilation controls; do not ask a patient to produce an aerosol-generating respiratory specimen in an unsafe open area.

## Nursing reasoning
Accurate collection is part of patient safety, not merely a clerical task. If a result conflicts with the clinical picture, verify identity, specimen quality, timing, and preanalytic factors with the laboratory and clinician before acting. A specimen result does not replace assessment or urgent escalation for a deteriorating patient.
