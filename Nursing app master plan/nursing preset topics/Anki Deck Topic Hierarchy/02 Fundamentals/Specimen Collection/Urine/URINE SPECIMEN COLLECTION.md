# Urine Specimen Collection

**Status:** Research draft. Follow the order and laboratory instructions for method, container, preservatives, timing, and transport.

## Common methods
- **Random urine:** collected at a specified time for tests that accept it.
- **First-morning urine:** collected after waking when a concentrated sample is required.
- **Clean-catch midstream:** cleans the area as instructed, begins voiding, then collects midstream without touching the inside of the container.
- **Timed/24-hour collection:** collects all urine over a prescribed period; missing a void can invalidate the collection.
- **Catheter specimen:** taken from the approved disinfected sampling port of a closed drainage system when ordered and appropriate—not from the drainage bag.
- **Other methods:** pediatric bag collection, catheterization, and suprapubic aspiration have distinct indications, contamination risks, and competency requirements.

## Clean-catch midstream basics
1. Verify the test and exact collection kit/protocol. Explain why cleanliness and midstream collection matter.
2. Perform hand hygiene; use the cleansing wipes and sequence specified by the test/facility.
3. Begin urinating into the toilet; collect the midstream portion in the sterile container, avoiding contact between its interior and hands/skin.
4. Secure the cap, label at collection, and send promptly according to laboratory requirements.

## Timed collection safeguards
Follow the laboratory's exact start/end instructions. A common 24-hour pattern is to discard the first void at the start time, collect every subsequent void, and include a final void at the stated end time—but verify the actual test instructions. Store the container as specified, including refrigeration/preservative when required. If any urine is lost or timing is wrong, report it and ask the lab/provider whether collection must restart; never conceal a missed specimen.

## Catheter specimen safety
Use aseptic technique and the approved sampling port. Disinfect the port for the required contact/drying time and use the approved sterile collection device while maintaining the closed drainage system. Do not obtain a culture from the drainage bag, which can be contaminated and does not represent fresh urine. Do not disconnect the system unnecessarily. Catheter insertion solely to obtain a routine sample has risks and requires valid indication/order and appropriate authority.

## Interpretation and escalation
Color, clarity/turbidity, odor, dipstick, microscopy, and culture must be interpreted with symptoms, collection quality, and test purpose. Cloudy or odorous urine alone does not prove a urinary tract infection. Report gross blood, inability to void with discomfort, concerning systemic symptoms, or clinical deterioration.

## PNLE priorities and safety traps
- A specimen from a catheter drainage bag is not the correct source for a urine culture from an indwelling catheter; use the approved sampling port and aseptic procedure when ordered and within competency.
- A missed void or wrong start/end time can invalidate a timed collection; report the error and ask whether collection must restart.
- Cloudiness or odor alone does not establish urinary tract infection. Correlate symptoms, assessment, urinalysis/culture, and clinician evaluation.
- Acute urinary retention with pain, gross hematuria with instability, fever with systemic deterioration, or other urgent findings require prompt assessment/escalation rather than just routine collection.
