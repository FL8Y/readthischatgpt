# Chest Physiotherapy and Airway Clearance

**Status:** Research draft. Select techniques by indication and current respiratory-therapy/provider plan. Chest physiotherapy (CPT) is not universal therapy for every respiratory diagnosis.

## Purpose
Airway-clearance techniques mobilize retained bronchial secretions so the patient can cough or otherwise clear them. CPT may include postural drainage, percussion, vibration, coached breathing, and directed coughing. Related approaches include active-cycle breathing, positive expiratory pressure devices, and assisted cough. Choice depends on diagnosis, secretion burden, cough effectiveness, oxygenation, tolerance, and local expertise.

## Assessment
Confirm the indication and prescribed technique. Assess respiratory effort, SpO₂ trend, breath sounds, cough, secretion amount/character, pain, fatigue, ability to cooperate, reflux/aspiration risk, bleeding risk, bone fragility, recent surgery, chest trauma, cardiovascular stability, and devices affected by movement. Explain the procedure and how the patient can signal distress.

## Nursing support
1. Position as prescribed and tolerated; do not force a posture that worsens breathing, pain, reflux, or hemodynamics.
2. Protect skin and fragile bones. Percussion is performed with cupped hands or an approved device—not over the spine, sternum, kidneys, wounds, chest tubes, or sites contraindicated by protocol.
3. Coach controlled breathing and effective cough when appropriate; allow rest.
4. Monitor oxygenation, work of breathing, pulse, symptoms, and tolerance.
5. Help the patient expectorate into an appropriate receptacle; assess sputum as clinically relevant.
6. Stop and obtain help for worsening dyspnea, desaturation, chest pain, hemoptysis, syncope, arrhythmia, distress, or sudden deterioration.
7. Reassess secretion clearance, breath sounds, respiratory effort, and comfort. Document technique, response, and complications.

## Contraindications and evidence
Precautions depend on technique and condition. Obtain specialist/provider guidance before percussion or postural drainage with unstable fractures, severe osteoporosis, active hemoptysis, untreated pneumothorax, recent thoracic/abdominal surgery, unstable cardiovascular status, raised intracranial pressure, significant reflux/aspiration risk, or fragile/painful skin. Do not use head-down positioning merely because a traditional chart suggests it; consider reflux, aspiration, intracranial pressure, and cardiopulmonary tolerance.

The AARC guideline reports that routine CPT is not recommended for uncomplicated pneumonia and that routine airway-clearance therapy is not recommended for all patients with COPD. Selected patients with symptomatic secretion retention may benefit based on tolerance and effectiveness. Patients who can mobilize secretions by coughing may benefit from cough instruction rather than unnecessary procedures. Check for newer disease-specific recommendations.

**Priority:** Acute airway obstruction or respiratory deterioration outranks planned physiotherapy. Activate emergency response and manage ABCs according to protocol.
