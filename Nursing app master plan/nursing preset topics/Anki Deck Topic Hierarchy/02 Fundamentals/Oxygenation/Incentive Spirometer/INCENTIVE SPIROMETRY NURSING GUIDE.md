# Incentive Spirometry: Nursing Guide

**Status:** Research draft. Follow the treating team's plan and device instructions; this is not a patient-specific order.

## Purpose and assessment
An incentive spirometer provides visual feedback to encourage a slow, sustained deep inspiration. It may support lung expansion in selected patients at risk of reduced inspiratory effort, including some postoperative patients. It does not directly measure oxygen saturation, replace mobilization, or treat every cause of atelectasis alone.

Assess baseline breathing, pain, alertness, ability to seal lips around the mouthpiece, ability to follow directions, inspiratory effort, and any prescribed target. Treat pain that limits deep breathing according to orders and reassess. Severe dyspnea, unstable status, inability to understand/perform the maneuver, or other relevant contraindications require an individualized plan. Do not substitute the device for escalation in an unwell patient.

## Teaching sequence
1. Explain and demonstrate the device; sit upright or in the best tolerated position.
2. Keep the device upright if instructed by its manufacturer. Exhale normally away from the mouthpiece.
3. Seal lips around the mouthpiece and inhale slowly and deeply; do not blow out into the device.
4. Sustain inspiration and briefly hold the breath if tolerated and directed by the device/clinical plan.
5. Remove the mouthpiece, exhale normally, and rest between attempts to prevent dizziness or fatigue.
6. Encourage a controlled cough afterward when appropriate; splint a surgical incision only if instructed and helpful.
7. Ask for return-demonstration. Confirm understanding of frequency, goals, and when to stop/seek help.

Frequency and repetitions follow the prescribed plan or local pathway; no universal number suits every patient.

## Monitor and evaluate
Monitor pain, dizziness, fatigue, cough, respiratory effort, SpO₂ trend when indicated, and technique. Stop for worsening dyspnea, chest pain, presyncope, significant desaturation, or deterioration, and escalate. Document teaching, return-demonstration, barriers, tolerance, and response.

## Evidence and PNLE distinctions
Do not describe incentive spirometry as a stand-alone solution or mandatory routine for every postoperative patient. AARC guidance concluded that incentive spirometry alone may be inappropriate to prevent or treat postoperative complications and did not recommend routine prophylactic use by itself in postoperative patients. Early mobility and a broader coached respiratory-care plan can be important; follow current local/population-specific guidance.
- The patient **inhales slowly and deeply**; they do not blow into the device.
- Return-demonstration is better than verbal instruction alone.
- It does not replace respiratory assessment, appropriate analgesia, mobilization, cough/deep-breathing teaching, or escalation.
- Low/falling SpO₂ with respiratory distress requires assessment, not simply repeated device use.
