# Tracheostomy Care and Emergency Response

**Status:** Research draft. Care and emergency response require competency-based training and the facility's current airway emergency algorithm.

## Core concepts and routine assessment
A tracheostomy is a surgically created opening into the trachea. A tube may include an inner cannula, cuff, flange, and other design-specific parts; not every tube has every component. A cuff may support a ventilatory seal but does not eliminate aspiration. Speaking-valve use requires confirmation that cuff/airway status and the specialist plan make it safe.

Assess respiratory effort, SpO₂ trend, breath sounds, secretion amount/viscosity, cough, tube position/securement, stoma, surrounding skin, and signs of infection/pressure injury. Verify tube type/size, cuff status/plan, humidification, oxygen/ventilator connections, and prescribed inner-cannula care. Ensure policy-required emergency equipment is present, usually including working suction, oxygen/ventilation equipment, appropriate replacement tube(s), an obturator as applicable, and a way to summon help.

Provide humidification and hydration as appropriate; follow ordered inner-cannula, dressing, tie, and stoma care. Use aseptic technique and secure the tube during care; a second trained clinician may be needed based on patient stability and policy.

## Red flags
Sudden distress, inability to pass a suction catheter when normally possible, absent/reduced airflow, new stridor, rapidly decreasing saturation, cyanosis, bleeding, tube displacement, or expanding neck swelling is an emergency. Call for immediate help, assess breathing, summon the airway/rapid-response team, provide oxygen/ventilation according to the algorithm, and determine whether obstruction/displacement is present. Do not delay escalation by repeatedly attempting routine care.

## Dislodgement and obstruction
A fresh tract can close or a tube can enter a false passage if reinsertion is attempted incorrectly. Do not blindly force a tube into a fresh stoma. Follow the trained emergency algorithm, which differs with tract maturity, upper-airway patency, and tube design. Remove/replace parts only in the sequence taught by the current protocol. If the inner cannula is obstructed, its removal/replacement may be indicated according to the plan; do not remove components without understanding their role.

## Infection prevention, communication, rehabilitation
Use hand hygiene and appropriate PPE; assess skin under ties/flange and avoid pressure/moisture injury. Provide a communication method to patients who cannot speak. Swallowing assessment, cuff management, speaking-valve use, weaning, and decannulation need an interprofessional plan and protocol. AARC guidance supports evaluated care bundles, multidisciplinary teams, and protocols in adult acute-care tracheostomy care.

Document respiratory findings, tube/cuff/inner-cannula status as required, stoma/skin condition, care completed, secretion findings, patient tolerance, teaching, and escalations.
