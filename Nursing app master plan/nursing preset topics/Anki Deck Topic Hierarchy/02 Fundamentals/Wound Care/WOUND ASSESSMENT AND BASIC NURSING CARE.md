# Wound Assessment and Basic Nursing Care

## Wound assessment

Document wound location, likely cause, dimensions using a consistent method, wound-bed tissue, edges, surrounding skin, drainage amount/type/odor, pain, and signs of infection. For pressure injuries, use the recognized staging system and current policy; do not stage a pressure injury based only on redness or use pressure-injury stages for other wound types.

Assess systemic context: perfusion, diabetes, nutrition, hydration, smoking, immunosuppression, medications (including corticosteroids/anticoagulants), mobility, pressure/shear, and ability to perform wound care.

## Healing principles

Wounds heal by primary intention (edges approximated), secondary intention (tissue loss with healing from the base), or tertiary/delayed primary intention (closure delayed until contamination/infection risk is managed). Healing is affected by perfusion, oxygenation, infection, nutrition, glycemic control, pressure, age, and comorbidities.

## Basic nursing care

1. Verify the wound-care order, patient identity, allergies/sensitivities, analgesia needs, and required precautions.
2. Explain the procedure, provide privacy, perform hand hygiene, and prepare clean/sterile supplies according to the wound, procedure, and institutional policy.
3. Remove the old dressing carefully; assess drainage and the wound before cleaning. Use appropriate PPE and avoid contaminating clean supplies.
4. Cleanse using the ordered solution and technique. Avoid unprescribed caustic agents or routine topical antiseptics that may damage tissue.
5. Apply the prescribed dressing based on wound characteristics and goals; do not pack a wound tightly unless specifically indicated.
6. Dispose of waste safely, perform hand hygiene, reassess pain/tolerance, and document findings and response.
7. Reassess at the ordered interval and escalate deterioration.

## Infection and emergency red flags

Urgently report rapidly spreading erythema, increasing warmth/swelling, purulent drainage, worsening pain, wound separation, necrosis, crepitus, fever with systemic illness, hypotension, or acute confusion. Severe bleeding, evisceration, or suspected deep infection requires emergency response. For evisceration, do not push organs back in; cover with sterile saline-moistened dressings, keep the patient supine with knees flexed if tolerated, call for immediate surgical help, and follow emergency protocol.

## Pressure injury prevention

- Assess risk and skin routinely; inspect bony prominences and under devices.
- Reposition based on individualized risk, mobility, skin response, support surface, and care plan rather than relying on a single universal interval.
- Offload heels, manage moisture/incontinence, minimize friction and shear, encourage mobility, and support adequate nutrition/hydration when appropriate.
- Do not massage persistent nonblanchable erythema or bony prominences to “improve circulation.”

## PNLE clinical judgment

If a wound suddenly separates or organs are visible, treat it as an emergency rather than continuing routine dressing care. For possible infection, assess the patient and wound together; systemic deterioration takes priority over routine documentation. A dressing choice should follow the wound's needs and a valid care plan, not a one-dressing-fits-all rule.
