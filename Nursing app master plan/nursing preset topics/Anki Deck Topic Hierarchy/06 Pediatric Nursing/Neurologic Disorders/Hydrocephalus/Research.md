# Hydrocephalus — Pediatric Nursing Research Notes

**PNLE-focused educational summary; not an individualized clinical order.**

## Core concept
Hydrocephalus is abnormal CSF accumulation with ventricular enlargement and potential raised ICP. Infants may have increasing head circumference, bulging fontanelle, vomiting, irritability, or sunset eyes; older children may have headache, vomiting, visual or gait changes.

## Assessment and diagnostics
Assess ABCs, age-appropriate neurologic status, level of consciousness, pupils/eye movements, motor symmetry, seizures, pain, hydration, vital signs, feeding, elimination, and caregiver-reported changes. Use focused examination and indicated imaging/laboratory tests; avoid delaying stabilization for routine diagnostics.

## Nursing priorities and rationale
- Protect airway and detect deterioration; trend neurologic findings and escalate acute change immediately.
- Use seizure, aspiration, fall, pressure-injury, and infection precautions appropriate to the child's condition.
- Maintain safe positioning and follow neurosurgical/medical orders; do not perform contraindicated procedures independently.
- Include caregivers, explain care developmentally, and coordinate rehabilitation and support services.
- Reassess response to interventions and document changes from baseline.

## Medication and procedure safety
Use only current verified orders. Confirm weight in kilograms, dose units, route, concentration, interactions, contraindications, and monitoring. Never invent pediatric doses. Verify seizure rescue plans, antibiotics, steroids, or hyperosmolar therapies against the actual protocol and current authoritative monograph.

## Red flags and PNLE distinctions
Altered consciousness, abnormal breathing, new focal deficit, unequal pupils, prolonged/repeated seizure, bulging fontanelle with deterioration, or signs of infection are urgent. ABCs and neurologic stabilization outrank routine education. Do not restrain a seizing child or put anything in the mouth.
