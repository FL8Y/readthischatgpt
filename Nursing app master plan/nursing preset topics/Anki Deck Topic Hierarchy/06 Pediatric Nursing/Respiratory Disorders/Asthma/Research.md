# Asthma — Pediatric Nursing Research Notes

**PNLE-focused educational summary; not an individualized clinical order.**

## Core concept
Asthma is chronic airway inflammation with variable wheeze, cough, dyspnea, and airflow limitation. Assess work of breathing, speech, oxygenation, air entry, and response to reliever therapy; exhaustion, cyanosis, altered consciousness, or a quiet chest can signal severe obstruction.

## Assessment and diagnostics
Assess age, respiratory/cardiac/renal status relevant to the topic, vital signs, work of breathing, perfusion, hydration, growth, pain, symptom duration, current medicines, allergies, and caregiver observations. Use focused examination and indicated laboratory tests/imaging; interpret pediatric findings against age-specific ranges.

## Nursing priorities and rationale
- Prioritize airway, breathing, circulation, perfusion, and rapid deterioration.
- Trend relevant vital signs, oxygenation, pulses/BP, urine output, growth, and response to therapy.
- Keep the child calm when distress/agitation can worsen symptoms; use age-appropriate positioning and safety measures.
- Administer prescribed therapies safely and reassess response; escalate if symptoms persist or worsen.
- Teach family symptom recognition, correct device/medicine technique, follow-up, and emergency thresholds.

## Medication and procedure safety
Use current verified orders and protocols. Confirm weight in kilograms, dose units, concentration, route, frequency, maximum dose, allergies, interactions, contraindications, and required monitoring. Never invent pediatric doses. Check drug-specific cautions and home action plans against current authoritative guidance.

## Red flags and PNLE distinctions
Escalate respiratory distress, cyanosis, exhaustion, altered consciousness, poor perfusion, severe chest pain/syncope, or rapidly worsening condition. ABC threats outrank routine teaching. A child may deteriorate without dramatic early signs; trend findings and reassess.
