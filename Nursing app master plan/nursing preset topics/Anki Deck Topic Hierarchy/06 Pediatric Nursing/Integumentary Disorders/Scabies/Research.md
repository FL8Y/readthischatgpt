# Scabies — Pediatric Nursing Research Notes

**PNLE-focused educational summary; not an individualized clinical order.**

## Core concept
Scabies causes intense itch, often worse at night, with burrows/papules; infants may have palm/sole or scalp involvement. Use prescribed age-appropriate treatment and manage close contacts simultaneously per guidance. Itch can persist after successful treatment.

## Assessment and diagnostics
Assess age, baseline function, symptom onset, pain/itch/bleeding, skin integrity, circulation, hydration, mobility, allergies, current medicines, and caregiver observations. Use focused examination and indicated testing; refer when severity, recurrence, complications, or diagnostic uncertainty warrants it.

## Nursing priorities and rationale
- Prioritize ABCs and acute deterioration; urgent circulation, infection, bleeding, or neurovascular concerns come first.
- Inspect affected skin/limb and document changes; reassess response to prescribed treatment.
- Prevent spread, injury, pressure damage, and medication error using condition-appropriate precautions.
- Teach caregivers safe treatment technique, adherence, and clear criteria for reassessment.
- Support the child's comfort, participation, and psychosocial needs without blame or stigma.

## Medication and procedure safety
Use only current, age-appropriate orders or label directions. Verify dose, concentration, route, duration, allergies, interactions, contraindications, and monitoring. Never invent a pediatric dose or use household chemicals/animal products as treatment. Check current authoritative monographs and local protocols.

## Red flags and PNLE distinctions
Escalate fever/systemic illness, rapidly spreading infection, severe pain, impaired circulation/sensation/movement, uncontrolled bleeding, or clinical deterioration. Distinguish expected symptoms from complications and avoid unsafe home remedies.
