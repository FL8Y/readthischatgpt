# Intussusception — Pediatric Nursing Research Notes

**PNLE-focused educational summary; not an individualized clinical order.**

## Core concept
Intussusception is telescoping bowel; episodic severe pain, vomiting, lethargy, and bloody mucus stool may occur. The classic triad is not always present. Urgent imaging/reduction may be needed; monitor for shock, perforation, and peritonitis.

## Assessment and diagnostics
Establish age, measured weight in kg, baseline development, allergies, current medicines, symptom onset/progression, vital signs, pain, perfusion, hydration, nutrition/growth, elimination, and caregiver observations. Use appropriate examination, laboratory tests, imaging, and specialist evaluation; interpret results in age- and context-specific ranges.

## Nursing priorities and rationale
- Prioritize airway, breathing, circulation, perfusion, and acute deterioration before routine tasks.
- Trend vital signs and relevant findings; compare with baseline and reassess after every intervention.
- Use age-appropriate precautions and prevent medication errors, aspiration, falls, and infection.
- Include caregivers as partners; explain procedures clearly and confirm understanding with teach-back.
- Escalate worsening symptoms promptly rather than waiting for every test result.

## Medication and procedure safety
Use only current, verified orders. Confirm weight in kilograms, dose units, concentration, route, frequency, maximum dose, allergies, interactions, contraindications, organ-function considerations, and monitoring. Never infer or invent a pediatric dose. Verify drug- and procedure-specific cautions against current authoritative monographs and institutional protocols.

## Complications and red flags
Urgently escalate respiratory distress, poor perfusion/shock, altered consciousness, new neurologic deficit, seizure emergency, severe dehydration, uncontrolled bleeding, or rapidly worsening topic-specific symptoms.

## Family teaching and PNLE distinctions
Teach condition-specific home monitoring, adherence, follow-up, prevention, and urgent warning signs. ABCs and immediate safety threats generally outrank routine comfort/teaching; use Maslow as a supporting framework, not a rigid algorithm. A classic sign may be absent, so do not rely on a single finding.
