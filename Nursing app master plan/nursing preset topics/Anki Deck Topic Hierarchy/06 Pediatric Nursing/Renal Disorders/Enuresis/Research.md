# Enuresis — Pediatric Nursing Research Notes

**PNLE-focused educational summary; not an individualized clinical order.**

## Core concept
Enuresis is repeated involuntary urination during sleep in a child developmentally old enough for continence, after excluding relevant causes. Assess constipation, UTI symptoms, sleep, stress, daytime symptoms, and history; avoid punishment or shame.

## Assessment and diagnostics
Assess age, growth, blood pressure, edema, urine appearance/output, hydration, pain, vital signs, intake/output, medication history, and caregiver observations. Review urinalysis, renal function, CBC, electrolytes, imaging, or other investigations as ordered; interpret pediatric values in context.

## Nursing priorities and rationale
- Prioritize ABCs, perfusion, severe hypertension, oliguria/anuria, and acute deterioration.
- Trend daily weight when ordered, intake/output, BP, edema, urine, labs, and response to treatment.
- Protect skin, support nutrition and fluid plan, and use infection precautions appropriate to treatment.
- Avoid unapproved nephrotoxic medicines and confirm renal dose adjustment with prescriber/pharmacist.
- Include caregivers in hygiene, medication, follow-up, and warning-sign education.

## Medication and procedure safety
Verify each drug's renal considerations, contraindications, interactions, dose units, route, and monitoring. Never invent pediatric doses. Fluid restriction, diuretics, steroids, antibiotics, and prophylaxis depend on diagnosis and current orders; do not independently change them.

## Red flags and PNLE distinctions
Escalate oliguria/anuria, rapidly increasing edema/weight, respiratory distress, severe headache/neurologic change with hypertension, fever in an immunosuppressed child, bloody diarrhea with reduced urine, or clinical deterioration. Distinguish hematuria/proteinuria patterns and monitor for complications rather than relying on one symptom.
