# Osteosarcoma — Pediatric Nursing Research

**Purpose:** PNLE-focused educational notes, not an individualized treatment order. **Evidence review:** 17 October 2026.

## 1. Core concept and risk
Osteosarcoma is a malignant bone-forming tumor in which tumor cells produce osteoid. It most often affects adolescents during periods of rapid growth and commonly arises in the metaphyses of long bones around the knee; other sites are possible. Risk associations include prior radiation/chemotherapy and selected inherited conditions (e.g., hereditary retinoblastoma/RB1-related disease and Li-Fraumeni syndrome), but most cases do not have a single identifiable cause.

## 2. Assessment and manifestations
- Persistent, progressive localized bone pain, often worse with activity or at night; swelling or a palpable mass may develop.
- Reduced range of motion, limp, reduced activity, or refusal to bear weight if a lower limb is affected.
- A pathologic fracture can occur through weakened bone; sudden severe pain, deformity, inability to bear weight, or neurovascular changes require urgent assessment.
- Pulmonary metastases may be asymptomatic or cause cough, dyspnea, or chest symptoms.
- Assess pain characteristics, functional impact, swelling, gait, distal circulation/sensation/movement, skin, vital signs, and psychosocial concerns. Do not dismiss persistent pain as a routine sports injury, especially when progressive or associated with swelling.

## 3. Diagnostics and referral safety
Evaluation may include plain radiographs, MRI of the primary site, chest CT for pulmonary metastases, staging imaging, and biopsy with specialist pathology. Laboratory tests such as alkaline phosphatase and LDH may contribute to assessment but are not diagnostic alone. **Refer to an orthopedic oncologist before biopsy or definitive surgery.** Biopsy incision/track placement must be planned with the team that will perform definitive resection; an improperly placed biopsy can contaminate tissue planes and compromise limb-sparing surgery. Avoid unsupervised biopsy or excision of a suspected bone tumor.

## 4. Treatment overview
Treatment usually combines systemic chemotherapy and complete surgical resection when feasible. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy may be given before surgery, followed by postoperative chemotherapy; exact sequencing and agents are protocol-specific. Limb-sparing surgery is possible for many patients, but amputation may be needed when required for safe complete resection. Radiotherapy has a more limited role than in some other tumors but may be considered in selected circumstances. Treatment planning should include rehabilitation, prosthetic/functional needs, fertility preservation discussions, psychosocial support, and school continuity.

## 5. Nursing interventions and rationales
| Nursing action | Rationale / evaluation |
|---|---|
| Protect the affected limb and follow prescribed weight-bearing/activity restrictions; assist with transfers and mobility. | Weight-bearing bone tumors can fracture pathologically; injury may worsen function and complicate surgery. |
| Assess pain regularly and evaluate response to prescribed analgesia; use positioning and nonpharmacologic support. | Pain control supports rest, function, and coping without masking new deterioration. |
| Check distal neurovascular status after surgery or immobilization and escalate changes promptly. | Detects vascular compromise or compartment-related emergencies. |
| Prepare for imaging/biopsy/surgery with identity, consent/assent, fasting, allergies, and ordered tests verified. | Supports safe procedures; biopsy planning must be coordinated by the tumor team. |
| Monitor CBC, renal/hepatic function, hearing, cardiac status, nausea, mucositis, hydration, and other agent-specific toxicities as ordered. | Chemotherapy can cause marrow suppression and drug-specific renal, auditory, cardiac, and other toxicities. |
| Prevent infection and follow central-line/antineoplastic precautions. | Immunosuppression and treatment access increase infection and medication hazards. |
| Coordinate physical/occupational therapy, prosthetics if needed, school reintegration, body-image support, and family counseling. | Supports recovery, independence, and developmental/psychosocial well-being. |

## 6. Medication and treatment safety
Common multiagent regimens may include high-dose methotrexate, doxorubicin, and cisplatin, with other agents in selected protocols. These drugs require specialist orders and close monitoring. Verify patient identity, protocol/version, cycle/day, dose calculation, units, route, infusion timing, allergies, laboratory and organ-function thresholds, hydration/rescue medicines, and required independent checks. High-dose methotrexate requires protocol-specific hydration, urine/renal monitoring, serum level monitoring, and leucovorin rescue; do not improvise timing or rescue. Cisplatin can cause nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity, and electrolyte disturbances; follow ordered renal, electrolyte, and hearing surveillance. Doxorubicin has cumulative cardiotoxicity; follow baseline and serial cardiac monitoring orders. Do not provide or calculate chemotherapy doses from this summary, and do not independently hold or modify therapy.

## 7. Complications and family education
Potential complications include pathologic fracture, local progression, pulmonary metastasis, postoperative infection, thrombosis, neurovascular compromise, chemotherapy-related cytopenias/infection, renal/hearing/cardiac toxicity, infertility, and late effects. Teach the family to report fever per the oncology center's written threshold, sudden pain/deformity, inability to bear weight, new numbness/coolness/pallor, uncontrolled pain, breathing difficulty, reduced urine, hearing changes, or unusual bleeding. Follow prescribed weight-bearing limits and mobility aids; keep appointments for imaging, chemotherapy, rehabilitation, and survivorship care.

## 8. PNLE high-yield priorities
- Adolescent with progressive bone pain and swelling warrants evaluation for bone tumor rather than repeated treatment as a simple strain.
- Protect a weight-bearing bone from fracture; follow ordered activity/weight-bearing restrictions.
- Biopsy must be planned by an orthopedic oncology team before the procedure.
- Monitor for chemotherapy emergencies and agent-specific renal, auditory, cardiac, and marrow toxicity.
- After surgery, neurovascular changes or sudden severe pain require urgent assessment.

## 9. Philippine context and limitations
This summary reflects international evidence and does not establish Philippine policy. A current Philippine DOH osteosarcoma protocol was not verified for this entry. Follow the treating pediatric oncology/orthopedic oncology center's protocol, high-alert medication policy, and applicable Philippine professional/legal requirements.
