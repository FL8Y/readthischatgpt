# Osteosarcoma — Pediatric Nursing Research Notes

**PNLE-focused educational summary; not an individualized order.**

## Core concept
Osteosarcoma often presents with persistent localized bone pain or swelling, commonly around long bones. Assess function and pain, protect the limb, and report new respiratory symptoms. Treatment is specialist-led and may combine surgery and chemotherapy.

## Assessment and diagnostics
Establish age, measured weight in kg, baseline development, allergies, medicines, symptom onset/progression, vital signs, pain, perfusion, hydration, nutrition/growth, elimination, and caregiver observations. Use condition-appropriate examination, laboratory tests, imaging, or specialist evaluation; interpret results in age- and context-specific ranges.

## Nursing priorities and rationale
- Prioritize airway, breathing, circulation, perfusion, and acute deterioration before routine tasks.
- Trend vital signs and relevant findings; compare with baseline and reassess after every intervention.
- Use age-appropriate safety precautions and prevent medication errors, aspiration, falls, and infection.
- Include caregivers as partners; explain procedures clearly and confirm understanding with teach-back.
- Escalate worsening symptoms promptly rather than waiting for every test result.

## Medication and procedure safety
Use only current, verified orders. Confirm weight in kilograms, dose units, concentration, route, frequency, maximum dose, allergies, interactions, contraindications, organ-function considerations, and monitoring. Never infer or invent a pediatric dose. Topic-specific drug and procedure decisions require current authoritative monographs and institutional protocols.

## Complications / red flags
Urgently escalate respiratory distress, poor perfusion/shock, altered consciousness, new neurologic deficit, seizure emergency, severe dehydration, uncontrolled bleeding, or rapidly worsening topic-specific symptoms.

## Family teaching and PNLE distinctions
Teach condition-specific home monitoring, adherence, follow-up, prevention, and urgent warning signs. ABCs and immediate safety threats generally outrank routine comfort/teaching; use Maslow as a supporting framework, not a rigid algorithm. A classic sign may be absent, so do not rely on a single finding.
