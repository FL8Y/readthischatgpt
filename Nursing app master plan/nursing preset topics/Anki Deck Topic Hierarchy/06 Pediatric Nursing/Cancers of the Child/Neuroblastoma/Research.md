# Neuroblastoma — Pediatric Nursing Research

**Purpose:** PNLE-focused educational notes, not an individualized treatment order. **Evidence review:** 17 October 2026.

## 1. Definition and biology
Neuroblastoma is a tumor of immature sympathetic nervous-system cells, most often arising in the adrenal medulla or paraspinal sympathetic chain. It is the most common extracranial solid tumor of childhood. Presentation and behavior are heterogeneous: some tumors in infants regress or mature, while biologically high-risk disease can progress or metastasize. Age, stage/risk classification, histology, MYCN amplification and other genomic features, and treatment response inform prognosis and treatment.

## 2. Age-specific assessment
- **Abdominal/adrenal primary:** abdominal mass or distention, pain, poor appetite, constipation, or a palpable mass noticed by a caregiver.
- **Thoracic/paraspinal primary:** cough, respiratory symptoms, back pain, weakness, gait change, or signs of spinal-cord compression.
- **Metastatic disease:** bone pain/limp, periorbital bruising/proptosis, skin nodules in infants, hepatomegaly, pallor, fatigue, or irritability.
- **Catecholamine-related findings:** hypertension, sweating, tachycardia, or flushing may occur, but are not present in every child.
- **Opsoclonus-myoclonus syndrome:** chaotic eye movements, myoclonus, ataxia, or behavioral/developmental change can be paraneoplastic.
- **Emergency red flags:** new weakness, paralysis, sensory change, urinary retention/incontinence, severe back pain, respiratory compromise, rapidly enlarging abdomen with respiratory effect, or hemodynamic instability. Suspected spinal-cord compression is time-critical; escalate immediately.

History includes onset, growth, pain, mobility, bowel/bladder function, fever/weight change, feeding, development, and neurologic symptoms. Assess ABCs, age-appropriate vital signs and blood pressure, neurologic function, gait, pain, abdominal appearance, perfusion, intake/output, and developmental baseline.

## 3. Diagnostics and risk classification
Specialist-directed evaluation may include urinary catecholamine metabolites (VMA/HVA), imaging of the primary and metastatic sites, MRI/CT, functional imaging such as MIBG when appropriate, bone marrow evaluation, and tumor biopsy with histology, molecular/genetic testing, and MYCN assessment. Not every test is appropriate for every child; use the protocol and minimize avoidable radiation/sedation risk. Stage and risk classification require the appropriate International Neuroblastoma Risk Group (INRG) or protocol-specific system and biologic data; do not classify risk from age or imaging alone.

## 4. Treatment overview
Management is risk-adapted and may include observation for selected low-risk disease, surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, autologous stem-cell rescue, differentiation therapy (e.g., isotretinoin), and anti-GD2 immunotherapy for selected high-risk settings. Treatment intensity and sequence depend on biology, stage, symptoms, age, response, and current protocol. Some infants with special metastatic patterns may have a distinct course, but clinical stability and organ involvement must be assessed by the oncology team.

## 5. Nursing interventions and rationales
| Nursing action | Rationale / evaluation |
|---|---|
| Perform and trend neurologic assessment, limb strength, gait, pain, and bowel/bladder function when paraspinal disease is possible. | Detects spinal-cord compression early; new deficits require urgent escalation. |
| Monitor respiratory effort, abdominal distention, perfusion, blood pressure, pain, and urine output. | Large tumors or metastatic disease can impair organ function or cause deterioration. |
| Trend CBC, renal/hepatic function, electrolytes, intake/output, weight, nutrition, and treatment toxicities as ordered. | Detects cytopenias, infection, organ toxicity, dehydration, and nutritional decline. |
| Use chemotherapy, central-line, hazardous-drug, and vesicant precautions according to agent-specific policy. | Reduces infection, extravasation, and medication exposure risks. |
| Monitor for infusion reactions and immune-related toxicities during immunotherapy according to protocol; promptly report breathing difficulty, hypotension, severe pain, or neurologic changes. | Immunotherapy can produce serious acute reactions requiring rapid treatment. |
| Provide age-appropriate preparation, pain management, play/distraction, family support, and developmental follow-up. | Supports coping and detects effects on function and development. |

## 6. Medication safety
Neuroblastoma regimens vary widely. Chemotherapy, isotretinoin, radiopharmaceuticals, and anti-GD2 immunotherapy have distinct risks and monitoring needs. Verify exact drug, dose, units, route, timing, cycle, organ-function requirements, and supportive medicines against the current signed pediatric oncology protocol. For high-alert agents, follow independent-check policy. Monitor for marrow suppression, infection, bleeding, renal/hepatic toxicity, neuropathy, mucositis, nausea, and agent-specific adverse effects. Isotretinoin is teratogenic; adolescent counseling, pregnancy-prevention requirements, and monitoring must follow the current prescribing program and local rules. Immunotherapy may cause infusion/hypersensitivity reactions, pain, hypotension, capillary-leak-like or neurologic complications depending on agent; follow protocol-specific premedication and rescue procedures. Do not invent doses or independently change treatment.

## 7. Complications and family teaching
Complications may arise from tumor burden (pain, spinal-cord compression, organ compromise, bone marrow involvement), treatment (cytopenias, infection, organ toxicity, infusion reactions), or late effects. Families should promptly report new weakness, altered walking, back pain, bowel/bladder change, breathing difficulty, persistent fever during immunosuppression, bleeding, reduced urine, uncontrolled pain, or altered consciousness. Reinforce line care, infection prevention, nutrition/hydration plans, medication safety, follow-up, and after-hours contact instructions. Discuss developmental, psychosocial, fertility, and survivorship needs with the multidisciplinary team.

## 8. PNLE high-yield priorities
- Neuroblastoma commonly arises in adrenal/paraspinal sympathetic tissue; it is not the same disease as Wilms tumor, which arises in the kidney.
- New weakness, gait change, or bowel/bladder dysfunction can indicate spinal-cord compression: urgent assessment and escalation take priority.
- Periorbital ecchymosis/proptosis can reflect metastatic disease.
- Treatment is risk-adapted; not all children receive the same multimodal regimen.
- Do not infer diagnosis from catecholamine symptoms alone; use specialist-directed biochemical, imaging, pathology, and biologic assessment.

## 9. Philippine context and limitations
This note summarizes international evidence and does not establish Philippine treatment policy. A current Philippine DOH neuroblastoma protocol was not verified for this entry. Follow the treating center's pediatric oncology protocol, local high-alert medication and immunotherapy procedures, and applicable Philippine professional/legal requirements.
