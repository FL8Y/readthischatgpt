# Stages of Separation — Pediatric Nursing Research Notes

**PNLE-focused educational summary; not an individualized clinical order.**

## Core concept
Hospitalized young children may show protest, despair, or detachment-like behavior; responses vary. Support caregiver presence, consistent routines, transitional objects when safe, and honest preparation. Quietness does not prove the child is coping well.

## Assessment and diagnostics
Establish age, measured weight in kg, baseline development, allergies, current medicines, symptom onset/progression, vital signs, pain, perfusion, hydration, nutrition/growth, elimination, and caregiver observations. Use appropriate screening, examination, laboratory tests, or specialist evaluation; interpret results in age- and context-specific ranges.

## Nursing priorities and rationale
- Prioritize airway, breathing, circulation, perfusion, and acute deterioration before routine tasks.
- Compare findings with baseline and expected developmental stage; document objective observations and reassess after interventions.
- Use age-appropriate precautions and prevent medication errors, falls, aspiration, and infection.
- Involve caregivers and the child at a developmentally appropriate level; use plain language and teach-back.
- Escalate regression, severe symptoms, or worsening function promptly.

## Medication and procedure safety
Use only current verified orders. Confirm weight in kilograms, dose units, concentration, route, frequency, maximum dose, allergies, interactions, contraindications, organ-function considerations, and monitoring. Never infer or invent a pediatric dose. Verify drug-specific cautions against authoritative current monographs and institutional protocols.

## Red flags and PNLE distinctions
Acute ABC threats take priority over routine comfort/teaching. Regression or new functional loss warrants assessment. Use Maslow as a supporting framework, not a rigid algorithm. Family teaching should be practical and nonjudgmental.
