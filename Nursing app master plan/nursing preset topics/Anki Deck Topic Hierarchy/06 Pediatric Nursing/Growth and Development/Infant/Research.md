# Infant — Pediatric Nursing Research Notes

**PNLE-focused educational summary; not an individualized clinical order.**

## Core concept
Infants may not localize symptoms and can deteriorate quickly. Assess feeding, wet diapers, weight/growth, respiratory effort, tone, interaction, fontanelle, and caregiver observations. Safe sleep: supine on a firm, flat surface without soft bedding.

## Assessment and diagnostics
Establish age, measured weight in kg, baseline development, allergies, current medicines, symptom onset/progression, vital signs, pain, perfusion, hydration, nutrition/growth, elimination, and caregiver observations. Use age-appropriate screening or condition-specific examination/tests; interpret results in context and use validated tools where available.

## Nursing priorities and rationale
- Prioritize airway, breathing, circulation, perfusion, and acute deterioration before routine tasks.
- Compare findings with baseline and expected developmental stage; document objective observations and reassess after interventions.
- Use age-appropriate safety precautions and prevent medication errors, aspiration, falls, and infection.
- Involve caregivers and the child at a developmentally appropriate level; explain care clearly and use teach-back.
- Escalate regression, severe symptoms, or worsening function promptly.

## Medication and procedure safety
Use only current, verified orders. Confirm weight in kilograms, dose units, concentration, route, frequency, maximum dose, allergies, interactions, contraindications, organ-function considerations, and monitoring. Never infer or invent a pediatric dose. Verify drug-specific cautions against authoritative current monographs and institutional protocols.

## Red flags and PNLE distinctions
Acute ABC threats take priority over routine comfort/teaching. Regression is more concerning than a single milestone that is somewhat later than average. Use Maslow as a supporting framework, not a rigid algorithm. Family teaching should be practical, nonjudgmental, and adapted to developmental stage.
