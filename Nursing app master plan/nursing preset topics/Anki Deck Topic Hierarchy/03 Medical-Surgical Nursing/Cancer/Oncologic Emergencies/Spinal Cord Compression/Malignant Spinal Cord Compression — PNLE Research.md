# Malignant Spinal Cord Compression

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Recognition
Tumor in vertebrae/epidural space can compress the spinal cord or cauda equina. Warning symptoms include new severe/progressive back pain (often worse at night or with movement), radicular pain, limb weakness, sensory changes, gait difficulty and bladder/bowel dysfunction. It is an oncologic emergency; deficits may become irreversible.
## Immediate nursing priorities
Urgently notify oncology/emergency team, assess motor/sensory function and bladder/bowel symptoms, institute safe movement/spinal precautions as directed and expedite MRI. Do not delay for routine appointments or physiotherapy. Dexamethasone may be ordered promptly; monitor glucose, infection risk and GI effects.
## Treatment
Management may include corticosteroids, urgent radiotherapy or surgical decompression/stabilization depending on tumor, stability, neurologic status and prognosis. Provide pain control and prevent pressure injury/VTE while avoiding movement that risks instability.
## PNLE traps
New back pain in a patient with cancer plus weakness or sphincter change means emergency imaging/referral. Do not wait for paralysis. Steroid orders and definitive treatment are time-sensitive.
