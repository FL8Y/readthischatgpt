# Metastatic Spinal Cord Compression

## Why it matters
Cancer-related spinal cord or cauda equina compression is an oncologic emergency. Vertebral metastases may cause epidural tumor growth or collapse of a vertebral body, damaging spinal cord/nerve roots. Delay can lead to irreversible weakness, paralysis, sensory loss and bladder/bowel dysfunction.

## Recognition
New severe or progressive back pain in a patient with cancer is concerning, especially night/rest pain, pain worsened by movement/cough/strain, band-like radicular pain, new weakness, gait difficulty, sensory level, saddle anesthesia, urinary retention/incontinence or bowel dysfunction. Symptoms may precede major neurologic deficit. Document baseline mobility and neurologic function, onset and progression, pain, sensation, strength and sphincter symptoms.

## Nursing priorities and collaborative care
- Escalate immediately to oncology/emergency team for suspected cord compression; do not wait for complete paralysis.
- Follow spinal precautions/immobilization orders when instability is suspected; avoid unsupported movement that could worsen injury.
- Prepare for urgent MRI/other imaging and specialist assessment.
- Glucocorticoids, radiotherapy, surgery or systemic treatment may be used according to the specific case; these are time-sensitive clinician-directed therapies.
- Monitor motor/sensory function, pain, urine retention, bowel function, skin integrity and mobility; prevent falls and pressure injury.
- If the patient cannot void, assess bladder status and notify the team; bladder management is part of urgent neurologic care.

## Differential and risks
Back pain is common and not always malignant, but a cancer diagnosis plus new progressive pain or neurologic change lowers the threshold for urgent investigation. Do not attribute new weakness to fatigue, opioids or deconditioning without assessment. Spinal instability and cord compression may coexist.

## PNLE distinctions
- New back pain plus weakness or bladder/bowel dysfunction in a cancer patient = emergency evaluation.
- Preserve neurologic function: early escalation before severe deficits is critical.
- A normal limb movement check earlier does not exclude rapidly progressive compression.
