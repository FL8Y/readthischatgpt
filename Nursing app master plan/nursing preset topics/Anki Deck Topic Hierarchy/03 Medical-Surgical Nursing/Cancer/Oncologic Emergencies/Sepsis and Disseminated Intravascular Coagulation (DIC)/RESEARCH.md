# Sepsis and Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)

Sepsis is life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated response to infection. Cancer, neutropenia, invasive devices and treatment-related immunosuppression raise risk, and fever may be absent. Red flags include confusion, tachypnea, hypotension, mottling, oliguria, hypoxemia and rapid decline. Activate the local sepsis pathway promptly. Assess airway, breathing, circulation, mental status, perfusion, urine output and likely source. Obtain cultures/lactate when ordered, but do not delay time-critical antimicrobials. Resuscitation and antimicrobials are selected based on cause, hemodynamics, cardiac/renal reserve and protocol; reassess frequently.

DIC is systemic coagulation activation with widespread clot formation, consumption of platelets/coagulation factors and variable fibrinolysis. It can cause both microvascular thrombosis/organ dysfunction and bleeding. Triggers include severe sepsis, malignancy, major trauma and obstetric emergencies. Findings include line-site oozing, petechiae/ecchymoses, mucosal/GI bleeding, hematuria, thrombosis, ischemia or organ dysfunction. Supporting labs may include falling platelets, prolonged PT/INR, variable aPTT, elevated D-dimer and falling fibrinogen; trends are more useful than one value, and fibrinogen may be normal/high early in inflammation.

## Nursing priorities
- Escalate suspected sepsis or DIC urgently; do not wait for all classic findings.
- Trend hemodynamics, mental status, bleeding, skin, urine output, lactate and coagulation labs as ordered.
- Use bleeding precautions and avoid unnecessary invasive procedures.
- Blood components and anticoagulation are selected by clinicians based on bleeding/thrombosis phenotype and protocol; treatment of the underlying cause is central.
- Document onset, infection source clues, laboratory trends, interventions, response and communication.

## PNLE distinctions
- Sepsis is infection-associated life-threatening organ dysfunction; SIRS signs alone do not establish sepsis.
- DIC can cause simultaneous bleeding and thrombosis.
- Cultures should be obtained when feasible but must not delay time-critical antimicrobials.
- Immunocompromised patients can have severe infection without high fever.
