# Sepsis and Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation in Cancer

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Why it matters
Cancer patients may develop sepsis from neutropenia, mucosal injury, devices or treatment-related immunosuppression. Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) is systemic coagulation activation with microthrombi and consumption of platelets/clotting factors, causing both organ dysfunction and bleeding. Sepsis and DIC can coexist.
## Recognition and assessment
Fever may be absent in severe immunosuppression. Assess new confusion, tachypnea, hypotension, rigors, reduced urine output, skin changes, central-line sites and recent chemotherapy. Obtain CBC with differential/ANC, cultures, lactate, renal/liver tests, coagulation studies, fibrinogen and D-dimer as ordered. Do not delay urgent treatment while awaiting results in suspected neutropenic sepsis.
## Immediate nursing priorities
Activate sepsis/neutropenic fever pathway and promptly notify the oncology/emergency team. Obtain cultures promptly if this does not materially delay empiric antimicrobials; administer prescribed broad-spectrum antibiotics urgently under local protocol. Monitor perfusion, urine output, mental status, lactate and hemodynamics; prepare fluid/vasopressor support as indicated and reassess response.
## DIC pattern and management
Possible findings include thrombocytopenia, prolonged PT/INR, elevated D-dimer, falling fibrinogen, bleeding from puncture sites, petechiae, hematuria or organ ischemia. Trends and clinical context matter; no single test proves DIC. Treat the trigger. Blood components are guided by active bleeding, procedures and lab findings under specialist orders; avoid treating laboratory abnormalities in isolation.
## Medication and safety considerations
Review chemotherapy timing, anticoagulants, antiplatelets, allergies, renal function and prior resistant organisms. Neutropenic precautions follow institutional policy; avoid rectal temperatures/procedures in severe neutropenia or thrombocytopenia unless specifically directed.
## PNLE traps
In suspected neutropenic sepsis, fever may be the only early clue and antibiotics are time-critical. DIC can cause simultaneous thrombosis and hemorrhage. Do not wait for full DIC labs before escalating a deteriorating patient.
