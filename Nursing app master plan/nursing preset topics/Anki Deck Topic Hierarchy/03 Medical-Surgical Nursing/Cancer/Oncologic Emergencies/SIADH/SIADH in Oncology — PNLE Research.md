# SIADH in Oncology

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Mechanism and cancer associations
Some tumors, especially small-cell lung cancer, can produce ectopic ADH. Other contributors include CNS disease, pulmonary illness, postoperative stress and medicines. Excess water retention causes hypotonic hyponatremia with inappropriately concentrated urine.
## Findings and diagnosis
Nausea, headache, confusion, gait disturbance, seizures and coma depend on severity and speed of sodium fall. Check serum sodium/osmolality, urine osmolality/sodium, volume status, medication list, renal/adrenal/thyroid function and cancer context.
## Management and nursing
Treat underlying cause and review causative medicines. Fluid restriction may be used in stable cases; severe neurologic symptoms require emergency protocol, possibly carefully monitored hypertonic saline. Check sodium frequently and document neurologic status, I&O and fluid adherence.
## Safety
Overly rapid sodium correction can cause osmotic demyelination. Do not administer desmopressin or encourage free water without a clear indication; both may worsen water retention in SIADH.
## PNLE traps
Low serum osmolality with inappropriately concentrated urine suggests SIADH after exclusions. Seizure/declining consciousness is emergency. Correction must be monitored.
