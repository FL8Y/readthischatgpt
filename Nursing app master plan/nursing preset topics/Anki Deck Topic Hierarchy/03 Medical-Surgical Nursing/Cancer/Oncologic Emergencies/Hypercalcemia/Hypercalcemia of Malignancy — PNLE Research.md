# Hypercalcemia of Malignancy

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Mechanism and findings
Cancer-related hypercalcemia may arise from PTH-related peptide, bone metastases or excess calcitriol. Findings include thirst/polyuria, dehydration, constipation, nausea, weakness, confusion, shortened QT and renal dysfunction; severe cases can cause arrhythmia/coma.
## Assessment and treatment
Check corrected/ionized calcium, renal function, electrolytes, ECG, hydration and mental status. Treat underlying cancer. IV isotonic fluids are often initial therapy if not contraindicated; antiresorptive therapy (e.g., IV bisphosphonate or denosumab) and calcitonin may be used according to severity and renal function. Monitor for fluid overload and subsequent hypocalcemia.
## Nursing priorities
Altered mental status, severe dehydration, dysrhythmia or marked calcium elevation needs urgent escalation. Strict I&O, daily weight and repeat calcium/renal labs. Encourage mobility and hydration when safe and ordered.
## Medication cautions
Bisphosphonates can affect renal function; check renal parameters and infusion instructions. Denosumab may cause hypocalcemia, especially with renal impairment; monitor calcium/vitamin D per oncology plan. Avoid unreviewed calcium/vitamin D supplements in active hypercalcemia.
## PNLE traps
Malignancy hypercalcemia often causes dehydration, constipation and confusion. Assess ECG/renal function and avoid indiscriminate fluids in heart/renal failure.
