# Hypercalcemia in Cancer and Medical-Surgical Nursing

## Definition and mechanism
Hypercalcemia is elevated serum calcium. In oncology, causes include tumor secretion of parathyroid hormone–related peptide, osteolytic bone metastases and selected hematologic malignancies. Dehydration may worsen calcium elevation by reducing renal clearance. Interpret total calcium with albumin or use ionized calcium when indicated; laboratories and correction equations vary.

## Clinical findings and diagnostics
Symptoms can include thirst, polyuria, dehydration, constipation, nausea, abdominal discomfort, weakness, lethargy, confusion and shortened QT interval; severe cases can cause arrhythmia, stupor or coma. Assess mental status, hydration, cardiac symptoms, renal function, medication/supplement use and cancer history. Tests may include repeat calcium, albumin/ionized calcium, creatinine/eGFR, phosphate, magnesium and cause-directed tests such as PTH/PTHrP or vitamin D metabolites.

## Management
Urgent evaluation is required for severe elevation, neurologic change, arrhythmia or kidney injury. Treatment depends on severity and cause and may include IV isotonic fluid when appropriate, calcitonin for a rapid temporary effect, bisphosphonate or denosumab for selected malignancy-associated cases, and treatment of the underlying cancer. Fluid therapy must be individualized in heart/kidney failure. Avoid routine calcium or vitamin-D supplements until the treating team reviews the cause and plan; hold medications only under clinician direction. Loop diuretics are not routine before rehydration and are used only for selected volume-management indications.

## Nursing care
Trend neurologic status, cardiac rhythm when indicated, intake/output, urine output, daily weight, hydration, calcium and renal function. Encourage mobility and oral fluids only when safe and not contraindicated. Monitor for treatment complications including fluid overload, hypocalcemia after antiresorptive therapy and renal injury. Provide constipation prevention and fall precautions when appropriate; confusion and weakness increase fall risk.

## PNLE priorities and distinctions
The classic description “stones, bones, abdominal groans and psychiatric overtones” summarizes kidney stones/polyuria, bone pain, GI symptoms and neurocognitive changes but is not a diagnostic rule. New confusion, severe dehydration, arrhythmia or falling renal function takes priority. In malignancy-associated hypercalcemia, treat the emergency and underlying malignancy while monitoring renal status.
