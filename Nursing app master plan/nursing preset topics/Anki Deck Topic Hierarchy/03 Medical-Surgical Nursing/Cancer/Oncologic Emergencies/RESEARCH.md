# Oncologic Emergencies: Recognition and Nursing Priorities

Cancer and its treatment can produce emergencies through tumor effects, metabolic changes, infection, treatment toxicity, bleeding, thrombosis or organ compromise. Severity and speed of deterioration matter more than the underlying cancer diagnosis. Use local oncology/emergency pathways and escalate early.

## High-risk syndromes
- **Febrile neutropenia/sepsis:** infection can progress rapidly and fever may be the only early sign or may be absent in immunosuppressed patients. Follow the local febrile-neutropenia/sepsis protocol and do not delay indicated antimicrobials.
- **Tumor lysis syndrome (TLS):** cell breakdown causes hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hyperuricemia, secondary hypocalcemia and kidney injury; monitor ECG, renal function, urine output and serial labs.
- **Spinal cord compression:** new severe back pain, radicular pain, weakness, gait change, saddle sensory change or bowel/bladder dysfunction requires urgent evaluation and imaging.
- **Superior vena cava syndrome:** facial/neck/upper-extremity swelling, venous distention, dyspnea, cough, headache or altered mentation can indicate impaired venous return; airway/cerebral symptoms raise urgency.
- **Hypercalcemia of malignancy:** dehydration, polyuria, constipation, weakness and confusion; severe neurologic/cardiac/renal manifestations require urgent treatment.
- **DIC/bleeding or thrombosis:** oozing, petechiae, hematuria, ischemia or acute organ dysfunction require assessment.
- **Tumor-related airway obstruction, hemorrhage or raised intracranial pressure:** manage immediate ABC/neurologic threats first.

## Nursing response
Rapidly assess ABCs, vital signs, mental status, pain, neurologic function, bleeding, infection exposure, intake/output and recent cancer therapies. Identify current chemotherapy/immunotherapy, time of last treatment, vascular devices, allergies and latest labs. Call the relevant emergency/oncology team early; obtain cultures/labs/imaging according to orders and protocol without delaying life-saving care. Monitor treatment effects closely and communicate trends.

## Patient/family education
Teach specific symptoms that require immediate contact or ED evaluation. Patients receiving systemic anticancer therapy need regimen-specific instructions for fever, diarrhea, dehydration, bleeding, dyspnea and neurologic changes. Do not supply generic threshold instructions when the patient's oncology plan specifies a different emergency trigger.

## PNLE traps
- New neurologic deficit/back pain in a cancer patient may represent cord compression, not just musculoskeletal discomfort.
- TLS can occur after therapy but sometimes spontaneously.
- Fever during neutropenia is time-critical; do not wait for a high fever or full culture results.
- Treat the emergency syndrome while identifying/treating the underlying cause.
