# Tumor Lysis Syndrome (TLS)

## Definition and pathophysiology
Tumor lysis syndrome is a potentially fatal oncologic emergency caused by rapid tumor-cell breakdown, spontaneously or after anticancer treatment. Intracellular potassium, phosphate and nucleic acids enter circulation; uric acid and calcium-phosphate precipitation can contribute to acute kidney injury. Electrolyte disturbances may cause dysrhythmias, seizures and sudden deterioration.

## Risk and recognition
Risk is greatest with highly proliferative, treatment-sensitive or bulky malignancies and high baseline tumor burden, but depends on disease biology, renal function, hydration, baseline labs and regimen. Assess before and after therapy for weakness, nausea/vomiting, muscle cramps, paresthesia, tetany, oliguria, flank discomfort, confusion, seizures or palpitations. Laboratory pattern can include hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hyperuricemia, secondary hypocalcemia and rising creatinine. Trends and validated diagnostic criteria should be interpreted with oncology team; thresholds vary by criteria and timing.

## Prevention and collaborative management
Risk-stratified prevention may include hydration, close laboratory monitoring, uric-acid-lowering treatment (allopurinol or rasburicase in selected risk groups), and specialist-directed oncology protocols. The choice depends on baseline uric acid, risk, kidney status, contraindications and local protocol. Rasburicase is contraindicated in G6PD deficiency because of risk of severe hemolysis/methemoglobinemia; screening and emergency precautions are important when indicated. Do not assume all patients need the same preventive drug or fluid volume. High-risk patients may need inpatient monitoring.

Established TLS requires urgent oncology/critical-care involvement. Treat dangerous hyperkalemia and other electrolyte abnormalities, protect renal function, measure intake/output and consider renal replacement therapy for severe/refractory complications. Calcium is not routinely replaced for asymptomatic hypocalcemia associated with TLS because calcium-phosphate precipitation may worsen; symptomatic hypocalcemia needs urgent specialist-directed management.

## Nursing responsibilities
- Confirm baseline risk assessment, orders and laboratory schedule before therapy.
- Trend potassium, phosphate, calcium, uric acid, creatinine, ECG and urine output closely.
- Escalate dysrhythmia, seizure, symptomatic hypocalcemia, oliguria/anuria or rapidly changing labs immediately.
- Ensure hydration plan is appropriate; monitor for overload in cardiac/renal impairment.
- Verify rasburicase contraindications and specimen-handling instructions because sample uric acid can be affected if not processed as directed.

## PNLE distinctions
TLS involves **high K, high phosphate, high uric acid, low calcium** as a classic pattern. Hyperkalemia can cause lethal dysrhythmias; neurologic changes/seizures are emergencies. Do not treat isolated lab numbers without clinical context, but do not delay escalation for critical values.
