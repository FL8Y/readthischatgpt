# Superior Vena Cava (SVC) Syndrome

## Pathophysiology
SVC syndrome results from obstruction or compression of the superior vena cava, impairing venous return from the head, neck and upper extremities. Malignancy (including lung cancer and lymphoma) is a major cause; thrombosis related to central venous devices can also contribute.

## Findings and severity
Symptoms may include facial/neck/arm swelling, prominent chest/neck veins, dyspnea, cough, hoarseness, headache, dizziness and upper-body fullness. Severity ranges from gradual swelling to life-threatening laryngeal edema, cerebral edema or severe respiratory compromise. Stridor, increasing work of breathing, confusion, syncope, reduced consciousness or rapidly progressing symptoms are emergency signs.

## Nursing priorities
Assess airway, breathing, circulation, neurologic status, oxygenation, edema and symptom progression. Escalate urgently if airway or cerebral compromise is suspected. Position to improve breathing as tolerated; avoid positions that worsen symptoms and follow clinician instructions. Protect swollen skin, monitor IV/device sites and avoid unnecessary upper-extremity procedures when venous access is compromised. Do not assume all SVC syndrome is cancer-related; the cause guides therapy.

## Diagnostics and treatment
Contrast-enhanced imaging is commonly used to define obstruction and cause. Management may involve endovascular stenting, anticoagulation for appropriate thrombotic causes, radiotherapy, chemotherapy or other cancer-directed treatment; urgent treatment is driven by severity and tumor biology. Tissue diagnosis is important when feasible and safe, but life-threatening airway/cerebral compromise takes priority. Medication and procedural choices are specialist-led.

## PNLE distinctions
- Facial/neck swelling with distended upper-body veins and dyspnea suggests impaired SVC drainage.
- Stridor or altered mental status signals potential airway/cerebral emergency.
- Central venous catheter thrombosis is a possible cause; not every case is due to new tumor growth.
