# Superior Vena Cava (SVC) Syndrome

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Mechanism and findings
SVC obstruction, often from thoracic malignancy or thrombosis, impairs venous return from head, neck and upper extremities. Findings include facial/neck/arm swelling, distended neck/chest veins, dyspnea, cough, hoarseness, headache and worse symptoms when bending/lying flat. Stridor, confusion, syncope or severe respiratory distress suggests critical compromise.
## Assessment and priorities
Assess airway, breathing, oxygenation, mental status, facial/arm edema and positional symptoms. Keep head elevated if tolerated, avoid unnecessary upper-extremity venous access/pressure when feasible, and urgently notify oncology/emergency team. Severe airway or cerebral symptoms require immediate escalation.
## Diagnosis and treatment
Contrast-enhanced CT often helps define obstruction and cause. Treatment depends on cause and severity: endovascular stent, radiotherapy/chemotherapy for sensitive tumors, or anticoagulation if thrombosis is present. Do not assume every case should receive empiric anticoagulation or radiation without diagnosis.
## PNLE traps
Facial swelling plus dyspnea/venous distention in cancer may signal SVC syndrome. Stridor or altered consciousness is an airway/neurologic emergency. Positioning can relieve symptoms but is not definitive treatment.
