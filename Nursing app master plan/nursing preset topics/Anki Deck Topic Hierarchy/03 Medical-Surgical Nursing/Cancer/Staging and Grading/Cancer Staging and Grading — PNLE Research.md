# Cancer Staging and Grading

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Difference
**Grade** describes how abnormal tumor cells appear and may reflect aggressiveness; grading systems differ by tumor type. **Stage** describes extent of disease, commonly using TNM: primary tumor (T), regional nodes (N), distant metastasis (M). Stage group and prognostic systems vary by cancer.
## Clinical use
Stage/grade help guide prognosis, treatment selection, clinical-trial eligibility and communication. Staging may combine physical exam, imaging, pathology, surgery and molecular markers. Clinical stage before definitive treatment can differ from pathologic stage after surgery.
## Nursing responsibilities
Explain terms clearly, verify which cancer-specific system applies and refer prognostic questions to oncology team. Support informed consent, treatment decisions, psychosocial needs and documentation. Avoid interpreting stage as a certainty about an individual's outcome.
## PNLE traps
Stage and grade are not interchangeable. TNM is common but not universal; hematologic cancers and some tumors use distinct systems. Metastatic disease is not defined by grade.
