# Basic Concepts in Cancer

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Biology
Cancer is uncontrolled clonal cell growth with capacity for invasion and, in malignant disease, potential metastasis. Tumors may be benign or malignant; histology and molecular features affect behavior and treatment. Carcinogenesis involves accumulated genetic/epigenetic changes, host factors and exposures.
## Clinical assessment
Assess new/persistent masses, unexplained weight loss, bleeding, nonhealing lesions, persistent cough/hoarseness, bowel/bladder changes, dysphagia and fatigue. Symptoms are not diagnostic; age-appropriate screening and targeted evaluation matter. Biopsy/pathology usually establishes diagnosis; imaging and biomarkers support staging/monitoring in selected cancers.
## Treatment modalities and nursing
Treatment may include surgery, radiation, cytotoxic chemotherapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy, hormone therapy and cellular therapies. Nursing care includes infection/bleeding assessment, symptom control, nutrition, psychosocial support, fertility/sexual health discussion, medication reconciliation and safe handling of hazardous drugs according to policy.
## Safety
Fever during chemotherapy can be neutropenic fever and requires immediate assessment under local oncology protocol. New neurologic deficit, spinal pain with weakness, dyspnea/facial swelling, confusion or reduced urine output may signal oncologic emergency.
## PNLE traps
Cancer is not one disease; treatment depends on histology, stage, biomarkers and patient factors. Never dismiss fever in a potentially neutropenic patient.
