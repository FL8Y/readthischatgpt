# Basic Concepts in Cancer

## Definition and biology
Cancer comprises diseases in which abnormal cells grow uncontrollably, invade nearby tissue and may spread to distant sites (metastasis). Genetic changes can activate proto-oncogenes, disable tumor-suppressor genes or impair DNA repair. Changes arise from inherited variants, replication errors, aging and environmental exposures. Benign tumors do not invade/metastasize, though location can still make a benign growth dangerous. Leukemias generally involve blood-forming tissue rather than forming a typical solid tumor.

## Terminology
- **Primary tumor:** original site of malignant growth.
- **Metastasis:** spread to distant tissue; a metastatic tumor retains the identity of the original cancer type (e.g., breast cancer spread to lung remains metastatic breast cancer).
- **Differentiation/grade:** how abnormal tumor cells look and sometimes how aggressively they behave.
- **Stage:** anatomic extent/spread; staging system differs by cancer.
- **Biomarker/molecular testing:** may identify tumor features that affect prognosis and targeted treatment.
- **Remission/response:** reduction or disappearance of measurable disease; the meaning depends on cancer and criteria.
- **Palliative care:** symptom relief, quality of life and support; it can be delivered alongside cancer-directed treatment.

## Clinical manifestations and assessment
Findings depend on primary site and spread and may include a mass, unexplained weight loss, persistent fatigue, bleeding, pain, altered bowel/bladder function, persistent cough/hoarseness, dysphagia or changing skin lesion. Symptoms alone do not diagnose cancer. Assessment includes history, examination, risk/exposure history, screening history, family history and diagnostic referral as indicated. Tissue diagnosis by biopsy/pathology is often essential; imaging and labs characterize extent and guide treatment.

## Treatment modalities and nursing role
Treatment may include surgery, radiation, cytotoxic chemotherapy, endocrine therapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy, stem-cell transplant or combinations. Selection depends on histology, stage, biomarkers, patient goals, performance status and risks. Nursing responsibilities include verification of regimen/patient/labs, adverse-effect assessment, infection/bleeding precautions, symptom control, safe handling of hazardous drugs and treatment-specific education. Avoid assuming all cancers follow one sequence or are curable.

## Psychosocial care and PNLE priorities
Use clear, nonfatalistic communication; assess distress, cultural preferences, finances, transportation, caregiver support and health literacy. Urgent red flags include febrile neutropenia, tumor lysis syndrome, spinal cord compression, superior vena cava syndrome, severe bleeding, airway compromise and acute neurologic change.
