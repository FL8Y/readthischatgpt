# Cancer Prevention: Evidence-Informed Nursing Education

## Primary, secondary and tertiary prevention
**Primary prevention** reduces exposures or risk before cancer develops; **secondary prevention** seeks early detection through evidence-based screening; **tertiary prevention** limits recurrence, complications and functional loss after diagnosis. Not every cancer has a screening test, and screening recommendations vary by organ, age, risk group and jurisdiction.

## Modifiable risk and protection
Tobacco exposure is a major preventable cancer risk. Support tobacco cessation and smoke-free environments. Other preventive measures may include UV protection, limiting alcohol, maintaining healthy weight/physical activity, healthy diet, reducing occupational/environmental carcinogen exposure, vaccination against oncogenic infections (such as HPV and hepatitis B according to national program eligibility), and safer sexual/injection practices where applicable. Inherited risk and unavoidable age-related mutations mean prevention cannot reduce risk to zero.

## Screening and early detection
Screening is for people without symptoms, while diagnostic evaluation investigates symptoms. Evidence-based screening may include selected breast, cervical, colorectal and lung programs for eligible groups; exact starting ages, intervals, test options and risk criteria vary by guideline and country. A symptom must not be dismissed because a screening test was recently normal. In the Philippines, use current DOH/local program recommendations and referral resources rather than importing another country's age thresholds unchanged.

## Nursing responsibilities
- Assess smoking, alcohol, UV/occupational exposure, family/personal history and vaccination/screening history.
- Use shared decision-making and explain benefits, harms, false positives, false negatives and follow-up.
- Avoid overpromising that screening prevents cancer; many tests aim to find disease earlier, while some screening/prevention interventions reduce cancer incidence.
- Address access barriers: cost, distance, privacy, stigma, language and caregiver/time constraints.
- Encourage prompt assessment of persistent or concerning symptoms.

## PNLE traps
- Prevention and screening are not synonymous.
- Screening eligibility depends on age, history, risk and jurisdiction.
- A negative screening result does not rule out all cancer or replace diagnostic evaluation of symptoms.
- Vaccination can prevent some infection-related cancers but not every cancer.
