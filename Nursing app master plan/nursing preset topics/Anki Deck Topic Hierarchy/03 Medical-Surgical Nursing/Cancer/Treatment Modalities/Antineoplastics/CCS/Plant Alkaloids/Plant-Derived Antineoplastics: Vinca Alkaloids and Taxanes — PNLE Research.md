# Plant-Derived Antineoplastics: Vinca Alkaloids and Taxanes

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Classes and mechanism
Vinca alkaloids (e.g., vincristine, vinblastine) disrupt microtubules; taxanes (e.g., paclitaxel, docetaxel) stabilize microtubules and inhibit cell division. They are distinct classes despite both affecting microtubules.
## Key toxicities
Vincristine is strongly associated with peripheral/autonomic neuropathy, constipation and ileus; it is for IV use only—intrathecal administration is fatal. Taxanes can cause peripheral neuropathy, myelosuppression, hypersensitivity and agent-specific fluid retention or other toxicities. Extravasation risk and vesicant classification vary by agent and policy.
## Nursing care
Independently verify drug, route and line before administration. Apply strict safeguards preventing any intrathecal route error with vincristine, including institution-mandated segregated preparation/labeling. Monitor neurologic function, bowel pattern, CBC and infusion reactions; stop and respond to suspected severe hypersensitivity or extravasation according to protocol.
## Patient teaching
Report numbness, weakness, gait changes, severe constipation/abdominal distention, fever, rash, dyspnea or infusion-site pain immediately. Avoid assuming neuropathy is a minor expected effect when it impairs function.
## PNLE traps
Vincristine must never be administered intrathecally. Distinguish neuropathy/ileus risk from myelosuppression and hypersensitivity risks of other agents; use drug-specific references.
