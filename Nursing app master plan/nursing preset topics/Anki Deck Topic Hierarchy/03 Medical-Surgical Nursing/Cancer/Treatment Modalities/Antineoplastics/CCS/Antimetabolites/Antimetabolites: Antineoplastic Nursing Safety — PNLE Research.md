# Antimetabolites: Antineoplastic Nursing Safety

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Mechanism and examples
Antimetabolites resemble normal metabolites and interfere with DNA/RNA synthesis. Examples include methotrexate, fluorouracil/capecitabine, cytarabine and gemcitabine. Indications, schedules and toxicities vary substantially.
## Major adverse effects
Myelosuppression, mucositis/diarrhea, infection and bleeding are important class concerns. Methotrexate may cause hepatotoxicity, nephrotoxicity and pulmonary toxicity; high-dose regimens require protocol-specific hydration, urine alkalinization and leucovorin rescue. Fluoropyrimidines can cause severe diarrhea, mucositis and hand-foot syndrome; rare DPD deficiency can lead to life-threatening toxicity. Cytarabine may cause cerebellar toxicity at high doses.
## Nursing checks
Verify exact regimen, route, timing, dose calculation and required rescue/supportive therapy. Monitor CBC, renal/hepatic function, oral mucosa, stool pattern, hydration and neurologic status as indicated. For oral agents, teach adherence and safe handling; never double a missed dose unless explicitly directed.
## Red flags
Fever, severe diarrhea, mouth sores preventing intake, bleeding, jaundice, reduced urine output or new neurologic changes require prompt clinical contact. Extravasation precautions depend on agent and formulation.
## PNLE traps
Methotrexate rescue and monitoring are dose/regimen-specific. Do not assume all drugs in the class have identical precautions. Cytotoxic handling requires institutional hazardous-drug procedures.
