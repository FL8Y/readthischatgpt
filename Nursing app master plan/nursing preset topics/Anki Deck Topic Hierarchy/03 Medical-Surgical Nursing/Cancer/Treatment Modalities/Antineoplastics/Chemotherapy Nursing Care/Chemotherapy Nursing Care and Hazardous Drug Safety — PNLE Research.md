# Chemotherapy Nursing Care and Hazardous Drug Safety

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Before administration
Verify patient identity, regimen, drug, dose calculation, route, sequence, cycle/day, allergies, pregnancy/fertility considerations, consent, required labs and treatment parameters against the current oncology protocol. Independent double checks are used where policy requires. Clarify any mismatch before administration; never infer a dose from a previous cycle without verification.
## During treatment
Use trained staff and current hazardous-drug precautions, PPE, engineering controls, spill procedures and disposal policy. Confirm vascular access patency; follow vesicant-specific administration requirements. Monitor for hypersensitivity, extravasation, pain, dyspnea, hypotension and other acute reactions. Stop the infusion and activate the drug-specific response protocol for suspected severe reaction/extravasation; do not flush a suspected extravasation site unless the protocol directs.
## After treatment
Monitor CBC and drug-specific organ function, infection, bleeding, mucositis, nausea/vomiting, diarrhea, hydration and nutrition. Give prescribed antiemetics and supportive medicines. Teach safe handling of oral antineoplastics, adherence, missed-dose instructions, reproductive precautions and urgent reporting of fever, bleeding, severe diarrhea, dyspnea or reduced urine output.
## Safety and Philippine context
Follow institutional hazardous-drug policy, applicable DOH/FDA Philippines requirements, occupational health guidance and scope rules. Do not improvise mixing, dose adjustments or disposal procedures.
## PNLE traps
Fever during chemotherapy may be neutropenic sepsis; it is urgent even if the patient looks well. Vesicant extravasation requires immediate protocol action. A medication order that does not match regimen/labs must be clarified before giving.
