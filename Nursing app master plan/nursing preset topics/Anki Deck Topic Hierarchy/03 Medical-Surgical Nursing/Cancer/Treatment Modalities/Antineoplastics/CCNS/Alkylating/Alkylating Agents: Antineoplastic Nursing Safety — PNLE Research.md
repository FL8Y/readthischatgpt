# Alkylating Agents: Antineoplastic Nursing Safety

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Class and mechanism
Alkylating agents damage DNA by forming covalent bonds/cross-links, impairing replication and cell division. Examples include cyclophosphamide, ifosfamide, melphalan and selected nitrosoureas. Exact toxicity differs by drug and regimen.
## Major risks and monitoring
Common high-priority risks include myelosuppression/infection, nausea, mucositis, infertility and secondary malignancy; cyclophosphamide/ifosfamide can cause hemorrhagic cystitis, while ifosfamide may cause encephalopathy and nephrotoxicity. Monitor CBC, renal/hepatic function, hydration, urine findings and neurologic status according to regimen.
## Nursing responsibilities
Verify protocol, patient identity, body-surface-area/weight-based dose and independent checks required for hazardous/high-alert chemotherapy. Use hazardous-drug PPE, closed-system/device and disposal procedures per current institutional policy. Assess baseline labs and infection/bleeding symptoms; teach urgent reporting of fever, hematuria, reduced urine output, confusion or uncontrolled vomiting.
## Drug-specific precautions
Mesna and hydration may be prescribed with ifosfamide and selected cyclophosphamide regimens to reduce urotoxicity; never assume the same prophylaxis is required for every agent/dose. Dosing and monitoring are regimen-specific. Review pregnancy/fertility concerns and drug interactions before treatment.
## PNLE traps
Class effects do not replace drug-specific checks. Hematuria during a urotoxic regimen requires prompt reporting. Do not handle hazardous antineoplastic drugs without training/PPE.
