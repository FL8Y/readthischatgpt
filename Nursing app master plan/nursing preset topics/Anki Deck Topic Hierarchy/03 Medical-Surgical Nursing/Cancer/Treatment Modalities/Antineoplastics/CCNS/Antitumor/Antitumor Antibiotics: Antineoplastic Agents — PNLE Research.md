# Antitumor Antibiotics: Antineoplastic Agents

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Overview
Antitumor antibiotics are cytotoxic agents derived from microbial products but are used as cancer drugs, not as routine infection antibiotics. Anthracyclines include doxorubicin and daunorubicin; other agents have different mechanisms and toxicities. They interfere with DNA function and cell replication.
## Safety priorities
Anthracyclines can cause cumulative dose-related cardiomyopathy and severe tissue injury if extravasated. Other risks include myelosuppression, mucositis, nausea and alopecia. Monitor CBC, cardiac history/left ventricular function when indicated, cumulative lifetime dose, infusion site and symptoms of heart failure.
## Nursing care
Verify drug/regimen and cumulative dose; assess IV patency and follow vesicant administration policy. Stop infusion and follow extravasation protocol immediately for burning, pain, swelling or suspected leakage—do not flush the line unless protocol directs. Report dyspnea, edema, orthopnea, palpitations or fever promptly.
## PNLE traps
Doxorubicin is associated with cardiotoxicity and is a vesicant. Routine antibiotic allergy assumptions do not explain this drug class; focus on cytotoxic and agent-specific toxicity. Never continue an infusion through suspected extravasation.
