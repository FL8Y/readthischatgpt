# Hormonal Therapy in Cancer

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Purpose and mechanism
Hormonal therapies slow hormone-sensitive cancers by reducing hormone production or blocking receptor signaling. Examples include tamoxifen and aromatase inhibitors in selected breast cancers, androgen-deprivation therapies in prostate cancer, and glucocorticoids in selected hematologic malignancies. They are not interchangeable; treatment depends on tumor biology and stage.
## Adverse effects and monitoring
Tamoxifen can increase venous thromboembolism and endometrial cancer risk; report unilateral leg swelling, chest pain, dyspnea or abnormal uterine bleeding. Aromatase inhibitors can cause arthralgia and bone loss; assess bone health. Androgen deprivation can cause hot flashes, sexual dysfunction, metabolic changes and bone loss. Glucocorticoids can cause hyperglycemia, infection risk, mood changes and adrenal suppression.
## Nursing care
Teach adherence, monitoring appointments and symptom reporting. Review pregnancy potential, drug interactions and comorbidities. Do not stop endocrine cancer treatment or chronic steroids without prescriber guidance. Provide supportive care for vasomotor symptoms, bone health, sexual health and psychosocial concerns.
## PNLE traps
“Hormonal therapy” is not one drug class with one adverse-effect profile. Match risk to the specific medicine and patient. New VTE symptoms during tamoxifen or androgen-deprivation complications require timely assessment.
