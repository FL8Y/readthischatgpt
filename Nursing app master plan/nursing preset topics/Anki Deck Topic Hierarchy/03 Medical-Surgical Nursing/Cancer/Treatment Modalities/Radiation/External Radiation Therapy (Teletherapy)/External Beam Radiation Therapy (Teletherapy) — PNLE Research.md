# External Beam Radiation Therapy (Teletherapy)

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Principles
External beam radiation directs ionizing radiation from a machine to a defined target. Treatment is planned and fractionated to balance tumor control with normal-tissue protection. The patient does not become radioactive from standard external beam treatment.
## Nursing care
Assess baseline skin, fatigue, nutrition, pain, swallowing/bowel/bladder function depending on treatment field, and psychosocial concerns. Keep alignment/skin markings intact; do not scrub or redraw markings. Follow the radiation team's skin-care instructions; use gentle washing and avoid unapproved creams, heat, ice, adhesive tape or friction over the field.
## Expected effects and monitoring
Effects depend on field and dose: fatigue and localized dermatitis are common; head/neck treatment may cause mucositis/xerostomia, chest treatment esophagitis, and pelvic treatment bowel/bladder symptoms. Report severe pain, inability to hydrate/eat, fever, skin breakdown or unexpected neurologic symptoms.
## Teaching
Explain treatment schedule, skin care, nutrition/hydration, fatigue pacing and follow-up. Avoid implying every radiation patient needs isolation; precautions depend on modality.
## PNLE traps
External beam therapy does not make the patient radioactive. Skin care is gentle and field-specific; preserve markings and avoid unapproved topical products.
