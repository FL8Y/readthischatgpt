# Radiation Protection Principles: Time, Distance and Shielding

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Three core principles
- Time: reduce time near a source when clinically safe.
- Distance: maximize distance from the source when possible.
- Shielding: use approved barriers/equipment appropriate to source energy and radiation type.
Use required personal dosimeters and follow the facility radiation safety plan. The exact precautions depend on source, dose, modality, and whether the source is sealed/unsealed.
## Clinical application
External beam treatment generally does not leave the patient radioactive. Temporary sealed-source brachytherapy requires specific precautions while the source is in place. Unsealed radionuclide therapy can require different contact, body-fluid and waste precautions. Do not apply one blanket isolation rule to every modality.
## Nursing actions
Confirm written instructions with radiation oncology/radiation safety staff, post required signage, educate patient/visitors, document exposure precautions and follow local regulations. If source integrity is compromised or a source is lost, secure the area and notify radiation safety immediately.
## PNLE traps
Time-distance-shielding is a framework, not a substitute for source-specific policy. Never touch a lost/dislodged source directly. Patient radiation precautions depend on the modality.
