# Types of Internal Radiation Therapy

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Brachytherapy
Brachytherapy places a radiation source inside a body cavity or tissue near the tumor. It may be temporary (source removed after treatment) or permanent (small seeds remain and decay over time). Delivery may be low-dose-rate or high-dose-rate, depending on the protocol.
## Systemic radionuclide therapy
Some treatments administer radioactive substances orally or intravenously, targeting specific tissues. These may require special instructions for body fluids, visitors, pregnancy and household contact; requirements depend on isotope, activity and local radiation-safety orders.
## Nursing assessment and precautions
Identify the exact treatment modality and source, confirm written precautions, assess pain/bleeding/elimination and provide patient-specific teaching. Use appropriate shielding and dosimetry as directed. Never assume all internal radiation uses the same isolation period or that external beam patients are radioactive.
## PNLE traps
Temporary sealed-source brachytherapy, permanent seeds and systemic radionuclide therapy are not interchangeable. Always use source-specific instructions and contact radiation safety if uncertain.
