# Nursing Management of Internal Radiation (Brachytherapy)

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Core principle
Brachytherapy places a sealed radiation source in or near a tumor. Exposure precautions depend on whether the source is temporary or permanent and on the specific isotope/device and institutional radiation-safety plan.
## Nursing responsibilities
Verify prescribed radiation precautions with the radiation safety officer/team. For temporary sources, use time, distance and shielding; wear the required dosimeter and use room signage/equipment as directed. Restrict visitors and exposure according to the written plan, not a memorized universal distance/time rule. Pregnant staff/visitors and children may require additional restrictions under policy.
## Source displacement or loss
If a temporary source becomes dislodged, do not touch it with bare hands. Follow the facility's emergency source-recovery protocol using designated long-handled forceps/shielded container only if trained, clear the area and immediately notify radiation safety/radiation oncology. Do not send the source through routine waste or linen.
## Patient care
Provide ordinary compassionate care while minimizing unnecessary exposure. Monitor pain, bleeding, urinary/bowel symptoms and treatment-specific complications. Clarify whether the patient remains radioactive after source removal; temporary source removal generally ends the source exposure, whereas permanent implants have their own instructions.
## PNLE traps
Brachytherapy precautions differ from external beam therapy. Never handle a dislodged source directly; activate the radiation safety protocol.
