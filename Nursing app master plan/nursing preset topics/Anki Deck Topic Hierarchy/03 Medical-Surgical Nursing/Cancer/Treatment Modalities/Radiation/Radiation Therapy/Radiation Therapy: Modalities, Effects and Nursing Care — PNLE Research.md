# Radiation Therapy: Modalities, Effects and Nursing Care

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Purpose and modalities
Radiation uses ionizing energy to damage tumor-cell DNA. External beam therapy directs radiation from outside the body; brachytherapy places a source in/near the target; systemic radionuclide therapy delivers a radioactive agent. Treatment may be curative, adjuvant, neoadjuvant or palliative.
## Assessment and common effects
Adverse effects depend on field, dose, fractionation and patient factors. Fatigue and localized skin changes are common; head/neck fields may cause mucositis and xerostomia, thoracic fields esophagitis, and abdominal/pelvic fields nausea, diarrhea or urinary symptoms. Late effects can occur after treatment and require follow-up.
## Nursing care
Assess baseline symptoms, nutrition, hydration, skin, pain and function. Teach gentle field-specific skin care, fatigue pacing, symptom reporting and adherence to appointments. Do not apply unapproved topical products or remove treatment markings. Escalate severe mucositis, dehydration, fever, uncontrolled pain, bleeding or neurologic deterioration.
## Safety
External beam patients are not radioactive after a session. Internal/systemic therapies may require source-specific radiation precautions; consult radiation oncology and radiation safety instructions.
## PNLE traps
Precautions depend on modality. Avoid blanket isolation recommendations. The irradiated field predicts many acute toxicities, but not all effects are immediate.
