# Cancer Surgery: Perioperative and Oncologic Nursing

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Purpose and planning
Cancer surgery may diagnose, stage, remove, debulk, reconstruct or palliate disease. The plan depends on tumor type, extent, patient condition and multimodal treatment. Verify procedure, site, consent, allergies, medication/anticoagulant plan and required preparation.
## Preoperative nursing
Assess nutrition, functional status, comorbidities, infection risk, blood counts/coagulation and patient understanding. Confirm informed consent is completed by the authorized clinician and that questions are addressed; nursing verification does not replace the clinician's consent discussion. Follow fasting, skin preparation, prophylaxis and medication instructions.
## Postoperative care
Prioritize airway, breathing, circulation, bleeding, pain, temperature, wound/drain output, urine output, mobility and VTE prevention. Monitor for procedure-specific complications, infection, anastomotic leak, fistula, impaired healing and functional changes. Encourage pulmonary hygiene and early mobility as ordered.
## Oncologic considerations
Surgical specimens require correct labeling and chain-of-custody handling for pathology. Teach wound care, activity restrictions, drain management, symptom red flags and follow-up. Discuss body image, fertility, sexuality, lymphedema or ostomy needs when relevant.
## PNLE traps
New bleeding, respiratory compromise or shock outranks routine pain/teaching. Do not promise cure based on surgery alone; staging/pathology and adjuvant plans determine next steps.
