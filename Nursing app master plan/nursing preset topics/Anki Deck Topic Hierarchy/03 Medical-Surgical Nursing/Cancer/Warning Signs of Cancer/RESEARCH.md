# Warning Signs of Cancer: Assessment and Referral

## General principle
Cancer can cause persistent symptoms, but most individual symptoms have benign causes. Warning signs are not diagnostic; their significance depends on duration, severity, risk factors, age and associated findings. Persistent, unexplained or progressive symptoms deserve timely clinical evaluation.

## Findings that warrant assessment
Potential warning signs include a new or enlarging lump; unexplained weight loss or persistent loss of appetite; unusual bleeding/bruising; blood in stool or urine; persistent change in bowel or bladder habits; a sore that does not heal; persistent cough/hoarseness; difficulty swallowing; unexplained anemia/fatigue; persistent or progressive pain; a changing pigmented skin lesion; or unexplained fever/night sweats. Organ-specific symptoms and formal screening recommendations vary.

## Nursing assessment
Obtain onset, progression, associated symptoms, medication and anticoagulant history, tobacco/alcohol/occupational exposures, family/personal cancer history, screening status and impact on daily function. Assess red flags such as hemodynamic instability, significant bleeding, airway compromise, severe pain, neurologic deficit or obstruction; these may require emergency care rather than routine referral. Do not reassure solely based on absence of pain—many cancers are painless early.

## Diagnostic pathway and education
A clinician may perform examination, laboratory tests, imaging, endoscopy or biopsy according to the suspected cause. Biopsy/pathology often establishes diagnosis; tumor markers alone generally do not diagnose most cancers. Teach the patient what symptoms warrant urgent evaluation and help arrange appropriate referral/follow-up. Avoid stigmatizing language and acknowledge anxiety, financial barriers and fear of diagnosis.

## PNLE distinctions
- Warning signs justify evaluation, not a presumptive cancer diagnosis.
- Persistent/progressive symptoms and abnormal bleeding require assessment even when a recent screen was normal.
- Unstable bleeding, airway obstruction or acute neurologic deficit requires emergency stabilization.
- Screening applies to eligible asymptomatic populations; symptomatic patients need diagnostic evaluation.
