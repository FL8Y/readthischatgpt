# Warning Signs of Cancer and When to Refer

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Possible warning signs
Persistent unexplained lump or swelling; nonhealing sore; unusual bleeding/discharge; change in bowel/bladder habits; persistent cough/hoarseness; difficulty swallowing; change in mole/skin lesion; unexplained weight loss; persistent fatigue or pain can warrant evaluation. These symptoms are nonspecific and often have benign causes; persistence, progression, risk factors and examination determine urgency.
## Nursing assessment
Clarify onset, duration, progression, associated symptoms, family history, exposures, screening history and red flags. Arrange timely clinical evaluation and avoid reassuring solely because pain is absent. Use age- and risk-appropriate screening recommendations.
## Urgent findings
Major bleeding, airway compromise, severe new neurologic deficit, spinal pain with weakness/bladder dysfunction, febrile neutropenia risk or rapidly worsening dyspnea require emergency escalation rather than routine referral.
## Patient education
Encourage reporting persistent changes and attending recommended screening/follow-up. Avoid promising that a symptom means cancer or that absence of symptoms excludes cancer.
## PNLE traps
Warning signs are prompts for assessment, not diagnostic criteria. Do not delay emergency response for oncologic red flags.
