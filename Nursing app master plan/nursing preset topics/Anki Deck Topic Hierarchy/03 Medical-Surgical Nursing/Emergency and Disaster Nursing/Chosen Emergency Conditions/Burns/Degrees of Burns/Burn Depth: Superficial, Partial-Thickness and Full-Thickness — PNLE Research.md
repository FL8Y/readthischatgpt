# Burn Depth: Superficial, Partial-Thickness and Full-Thickness

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Depth classification
- Superficial (epidermal): red, dry, painful, blanching; no blisters; typically heals without scarring.
- Partial-thickness: involves epidermis and part of dermis; often moist, blistered and very painful with blanching variable by depth.
- Full-thickness: destroys epidermis and dermis; may appear white, brown, leathery or charred and may be less painful centrally because nerve endings are destroyed.
Deep burns can extend into muscle/bone and are classified separately in specialist care.
## Assessment cautions
Depth can evolve over days, and appearance is affected by perfusion, contamination and skin tone. Document location, size, appearance, pain/sensation and circumferential involvement; use a trained assessment method and reassess.
## Management principles
Stop exposure, provide appropriate cooling without hypothermia, cover with clean dry material, manage pain and arrange referral based on depth, extent, site, mechanism and patient factors. Do not break blisters or apply unprescribed home remedies.
## PNLE traps
A painless, leathery center may indicate full-thickness injury, not a minor burn. Depth alone is not enough to triage; inhalation injury, extent, location and comorbidities matter.
