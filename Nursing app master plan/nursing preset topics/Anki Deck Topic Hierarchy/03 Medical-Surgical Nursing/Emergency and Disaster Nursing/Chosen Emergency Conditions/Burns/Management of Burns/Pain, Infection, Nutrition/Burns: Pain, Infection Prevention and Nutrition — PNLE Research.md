# Burns: Pain, Infection Prevention and Nutrition

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Pain
Burn pain may include background, procedural and breakthrough pain. Assess pain and sedation repeatedly; use prescribed multimodal analgesia and plan analgesia before wound care/therapy. Anxiety, positioning and nonpharmacologic support can help but do not replace needed medication.
## Infection prevention
Burn wounds lose the skin barrier and are vulnerable to infection. Use hand hygiene, aseptic technique, appropriate dressings and topical/systemic antimicrobials only as indicated. Monitor wound changes, fever/hypothermia, hemodynamics, mental status and laboratory trends; systemic prophylactic antibiotics are not routine for every burn and should follow specialist guidance.
## Nutrition
Major burns increase metabolic demand and protein needs. Early enteral nutrition is often preferred when feasible for significant burns; coordinate dietitian input, glucose monitoring, fluid balance and electrolyte monitoring. Assess weight, intake, wound healing and feeding tolerance.
## Nursing priorities
Recognize invasive wound infection/sepsis early, protect temperature, reassess analgesic effect and respiratory status, and support mobility and rehabilitation. Adjust care to burn size, age, comorbidity and local burn-center protocol.
## PNLE traps
Pain relief should be planned around dressing changes. Infection prevention is not synonymous with giving prophylactic systemic antibiotics to all patients. Large burns require nutrition support and ongoing fluid/electrolyte reassessment.
