# Burn Wound Cleansing, Hydrotherapy and Debridement

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Purpose and principles
Wound cleansing removes contaminants and loose debris; debridement removes devitalized tissue. Methods include gentle cleansing, selective sharp/surgical debridement, enzymatic approaches and other specialist-directed techniques. Choice depends on burn depth, extent, infection, patient stability and available expertise.
## Nursing preparation and care
Explain the procedure, assess pain/anxiety, provide prescribed analgesia before care, prepare supplies and maintain asepsis. Monitor temperature, hemodynamics and pain; large wounds and prolonged exposure increase heat loss. Use solution, technique and dressing plan prescribed by the burn team.
## Safety
Hydrotherapy is not automatically required and may cause hypothermia, cross-contamination or physiologic stress if poorly controlled. Do not debride adherent tissue or perform sharp debridement outside training/order/scope. Follow infection-control and environmental-cleaning protocols.
## Aftercare
Reassess wound appearance, bleeding, pain, temperature, hydration and dressing integrity. Document tissue removed, wound findings, tolerance and complications; escalate increasing erythema, purulence, odor, systemic deterioration or uncontrolled pain.
## PNLE traps
Pain control, asepsis and prevention of hypothermia are essential. Debridement method is selected according to clinical condition and expertise, not a universal routine.
