# Other Burn Management: Escharotomy, Grafting and Complications

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Definitive and adjunctive care
Major burns may require excision of devitalized tissue, skin grafting, specialized dressings and rehabilitation. Circumferential full-thickness burns can form restrictive eschar; chest restriction may impair ventilation and limb restriction may compromise perfusion. Escharotomy is a specialist emergency procedure when indicated, not a routine nursing intervention.
## Monitoring
Assess distal pulses, capillary refill, sensation, movement, skin temperature and pain in circumferential limb burns; assess chest expansion and ventilation for circumferential torso burns. Doppler assessment and compartment pressure evaluation may be ordered. Pulses can persist despite evolving compartment compromise, so trends matter.
## Nursing responsibilities
Escalate worsening pain, paresthesia, pallor, coolness, reduced movement, deteriorating ventilation or perfusion immediately. Prepare for surgical evaluation; maintain wound asepsis, pain control, temperature and fluid monitoring. After grafting, protect the graft and follow the ordered immobilization/dressing plan.
## PNLE traps
Loss of pulse is a late sign of vascular compromise. Do not wait for absent pulses if neurovascular status is worsening. Escharotomy is distinct from fasciotomy and requires specialist judgment.
