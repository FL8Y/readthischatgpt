# Rule of Nines and Burn Surface Area Estimation

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Purpose
Total body surface area (TBSA) estimation guides burn resuscitation and referral. Only partial-thickness and deeper burns are usually included; simple erythema/superficial burns are excluded. Adult Rule of Nines is a rapid estimate, not a substitute for a more accurate chart in children or irregular burns.
## Adult estimate (approximate)
Head/neck 9%; each arm 9%; each leg 18%; anterior trunk 18%; posterior trunk 18%; perineum 1%. The patient's entire palmar surface including fingers is often approximated as about 1% TBSA, but the exact method should follow the chart and local protocol.
## Special populations and limitations
Children have proportionally larger heads and smaller legs; use a pediatric Lund–Browder chart. Obesity, unusual body proportions and scattered burns may reduce accuracy. Reassess as wounds are cleaned and depth clarified.
## Nursing application
Document method, estimated TBSA, burn depth and time. Report major burns promptly; fluid resuscitation is calculated from burn size, weight and protocol and then titrated to response (e.g., urine output and perfusion). Do not use a formula as a fixed volume without reassessment.
## PNLE traps
Count partial/full-thickness burns, not simple superficial erythema. Use pediatric chart for children. Estimates guide initial treatment but do not replace serial assessment.
