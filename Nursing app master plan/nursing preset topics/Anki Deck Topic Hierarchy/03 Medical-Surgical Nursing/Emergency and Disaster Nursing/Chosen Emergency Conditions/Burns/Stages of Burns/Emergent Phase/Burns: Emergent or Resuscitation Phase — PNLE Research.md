# Burns: Emergent or Resuscitation Phase

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Time frame and priorities
The emergent/resuscitation phase begins at injury and continues through initial stabilization and fluid resuscitation; exact phase definitions vary by textbook and burn-center protocol. Priorities are scene safety, airway/inhalation injury, breathing, circulation, burn extent/depth, hypothermia prevention and associated trauma.
## Airway and breathing
Facial/neck burns, soot, hoarseness, stridor, enclosed-space smoke exposure or progressive edema raise concern for inhalation injury. Early expert airway assessment is critical because edema can progress. Monitor respiratory status and oxygenation; pulse oximetry may be misleading with carbon monoxide exposure.
## Circulation and fluids
Assess perfusion, pulse, BP, urine output, weight and burn size. Major burns may require IV fluid resuscitation using a burn-center formula as a starting estimate, then titration to response and protocol. Do not treat calculated volume as fixed; excessive fluids can cause edema/compartment complications.
## Nursing care
Obtain mechanism/time, estimate TBSA/depth, monitor temperature and pain, cover wounds cleanly, keep warm and prepare for transfer to a burn center when criteria are met. Avoid ice and hypothermia. Tetanus status and analgesia are reviewed per protocol.
## PNLE traps
Airway threat can worsen after initial presentation. Carbon monoxide can make pulse oximetry falsely reassuring. Fluid formulas require reassessment and do not replace clinical monitoring.
