# Burns: Acute Phase and Wound Closure

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Focus of acute phase
After initial resuscitation, care focuses on wound assessment/closure, infection prevention, pain control, nutrition, mobility, psychosocial support and prevention of complications. Phase boundaries vary with burn severity and treatment.
## Assessment and management
Monitor wound depth/appearance, perfusion, infection, temperature, fluid/electrolytes, renal function, nutrition, pain and function. Dressing selection, topical agents, excision/grafting and antimicrobial use are burn-team decisions. Systemic antibiotics are not routinely indicated for every burn absent a specific indication.
## Nursing responsibilities
Use aseptic wound care, provide analgesia before procedures, maintain temperature, support high-protein/high-energy nutrition for significant burns, and coordinate physiotherapy/occupational therapy. Monitor for sepsis, wound conversion, contractures, pressure injuries, delirium and VTE according to risk.
## Education and rehabilitation
Prepare patient/family for dressing changes, graft care, scar management, range-of-motion exercises and emotional support. Escalate worsening wound appearance, new systemic symptoms, reduced urine output or uncontrolled pain.
## PNLE traps
The acute phase is not just dressing changes: infection, nutrition, function and psychological support matter. Avoid routine prophylactic systemic antibiotics without indication.
