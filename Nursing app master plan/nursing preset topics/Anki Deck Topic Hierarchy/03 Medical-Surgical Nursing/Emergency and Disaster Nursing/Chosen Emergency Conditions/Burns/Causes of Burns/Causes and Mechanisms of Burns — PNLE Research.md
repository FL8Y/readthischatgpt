# Causes and Mechanisms of Burns

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Types of injury
Thermal burns result from flame, hot liquid/steam or contact; chemical burns from corrosive substances; electrical injury from current/lightning; radiation injury from ionizing or ultraviolet exposure. Injury depth and extent depend on temperature/concentration, contact duration, voltage/current path, tissue resistance and exposure circumstances.
## First actions
Ensure scene safety and stop the burning process. For thermal burns, remove hot/wet clothing and jewelry unless adherent; cool the burn with cool running water when feasible, while preventing hypothermia in large burns. Do not apply ice, butter, toothpaste or unverified topical remedies. For chemical exposure, protect rescuers, remove contaminated clothing and brush off dry chemical before irrigation when appropriate; follow substance-specific safety data and poison-center/emergency advice. Electrical injury requires power-source safety and assessment for dysrhythmia/deep injury.
## Assessment
Assess airway/inhalation injury, breathing, circulation, burn mechanism, location, depth, extent, time and associated trauma. Circumferential burns, face/neck burns, chemical/electrical burns, major burns and burns in vulnerable patients need urgent specialist evaluation.
## PNLE traps
Burn appearance can underestimate electrical/deep injury. Do not risk rescuer safety with chemicals/electricity. Avoid ice and hypothermia; airway injury can progress after initial exposure.
