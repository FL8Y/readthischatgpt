# Primary vs Secondary Survey in Trauma

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Primary survey: immediate threats
Use a systematic ABCDE approach to identify and treat life threats: airway with cervical-spine protection when indicated; breathing/ventilation; circulation and hemorrhage control; disability/rapid neurologic check; exposure/environmental control. Treat threats as they are found and reassess from the beginning after deterioration.
## Secondary survey
Once immediate threats are managed and the patient is sufficiently stabilized, obtain a more complete history (e.g., SAMPLE/AMPLE), head-to-toe examination, pain assessment, targeted imaging/labs and reassessment of all injuries. The secondary survey is not a reason to defer treatment of a newly discovered life threat.
## Nursing coordination
Communicate findings in a structured handoff, document times/trends/interventions, verify identity/allergies/medications when feasible, and preserve evidence/specimens according to policy without compromising care.
## PNLE traps
Primary survey is rapid and life-threat focused; secondary survey is comprehensive. If the patient deteriorates during secondary survey, return to ABCDE. Do not complete a checklist mechanically while ignoring clinical change.
