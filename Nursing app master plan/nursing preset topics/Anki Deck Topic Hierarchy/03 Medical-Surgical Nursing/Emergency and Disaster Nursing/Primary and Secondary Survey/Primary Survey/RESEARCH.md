# Primary Survey: ABCDE and Immediate Threats

## Purpose
The primary survey is a rapid, systematic search for immediate life threats, commonly organized as Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability and Exposure (ABCDE). It is performed rapidly and repeated after interventions because the patient's condition can change. Treat life threats as found; do not postpone intervention until the whole checklist is complete.

## ABCDE framework
- **A — Airway with cervical-spine consideration when indicated:** assess speech, obstruction, secretions, stridor, swelling and ability to protect the airway; call for skilled help early.
- **B — Breathing:** assess rate/effort, chest movement, breath sounds, oxygenation and severe chest injury; support ventilation/oxygen per protocol.
- **C — Circulation and hemorrhage:** assess pulse, BP trend, perfusion, bleeding, skin and mental status; control catastrophic external bleeding and initiate the relevant shock/trauma pathway.
- **D — Disability:** assess AVPU/GCS, pupils, motor deficit and glucose when indicated; identify stroke, seizure, head injury and reversible causes of altered consciousness.
- **E — Exposure/environment:** inspect for hidden injury, rash/burns/bleeding while preventing hypothermia and preserving dignity.

## Nursing actions and documentation
Call appropriate help, monitor vital signs, obtain relevant history only as it does not delay stabilization, and reassess the effect of each intervention. Note onset, mechanism, findings, intervention times and escalation. After primary threats are treated, undertake a secondary survey with focused history, head-to-toe examination and diagnostics as indicated.

## Special considerations
The sequence is a reasoning aid, not a rigid rule that prevents simultaneous interventions. Catastrophic bleeding may need immediate control while airway care continues. Pediatric, pregnant, trauma, burn and disaster contexts may require modified protocols. Scope of practice and local policy govern procedures.

## PNLE traps
- ABCDE is repeated, not completed once.
- Treat immediate threats rather than spending excessive time on a full history.
- A normal single vital sign does not exclude evolving shock.
- The secondary survey follows initial stabilization but returns to ABCDE if deterioration occurs.
