# Primary Survey: Disability and Neurologic Status

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Assessment
Disability is a rapid neurologic screen after airway, breathing and circulation. Assess level of consciousness (AVPU or GCS), pupils, limb movement, glucose when indicated, and focal deficits. Check for reversible causes such as hypoxia, hypoglycemia, intoxication and shock.
## Actions
Protect airway if consciousness is impaired, prevent falls/aspiration, check point-of-care glucose in altered mental status, and activate stroke/trauma pathways for sudden focal deficits or significant injury. Maintain spinal precautions when indicated. Repeat neurologic observations and document trends.
## Red flags
Sudden unilateral weakness, facial droop, speech change, seizure without recovery, declining GCS, unequal pupils, new severe headache or post-trauma deterioration requires immediate escalation. Do not delay stroke assessment for nonessential tasks.
## Nursing safety
A low GCS is a severity marker, not a diagnosis. Sedatives, paralysis, intoxication, language barriers and hearing impairment may confound scoring; document factors and reassess. Hypoglycemia can mimic stroke and should be checked promptly without delaying stroke-team activation.
## PNLE traps
ABCs precede disability. New neurologic deficit is time-critical. A single score is less informative than trend and context.
