# Primary Survey: Disability and Rapid Neurologic Check

## Purpose
“Disability” in the ABCDE primary survey is a rapid neurologic assessment to identify immediately life-threatening neurologic dysfunction. It does not replace a full neurologic exam. Consciousness can deteriorate because of hypoxia, hypoglycemia, shock, stroke, seizure, trauma, intoxication or metabolic derangement.

## Rapid assessment
Assess level of consciousness using AVPU (Alert, responds to Voice, responds to Pain, Unresponsive) or the locally required Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS). Check pupils, limb movement, obvious focal deficit and blood glucose when available/indicated. In trauma, consider cervical spine injury and the mechanism. Establish baseline and exact symptom onset/last-known-well when stroke is possible.

## Immediate priorities
- If consciousness is impaired, reassess airway/breathing and protect airway/aspiration risk.
- Check glucose promptly when altered mental status is unexplained; hypoglycemia is a reversible stroke mimic and emergency.
- Activate stroke, trauma, seizure or emergency pathways for focal deficit, seizure, severe headache after trauma, rapid decline or unequal pupils with deterioration.
- Avoid oral intake/medicines when swallowing or airway protection is unsafe.
- Repeat neurologic checks after intervention and communicate any change immediately.

## Documentation and nursing care
Record time, baseline versus current mental state, GCS/AVPU, pupils, motor findings, blood glucose, medications/toxins and onset history. Prevent falls, aspiration and secondary injury. Do not attribute confusion to age, dementia, pain medication or fatigue without assessing reversible causes. A single normal assessment does not exclude evolving stroke or intracranial injury.

## PNLE distinctions
- The primary survey looks for immediate threats; detailed differential diagnosis follows stabilization.
- New focal deficit requires stroke assessment even if symptoms improve.
- In an altered patient, assess ABCs and glucose rather than assuming a psychiatric cause.
- Declining GCS is a trend requiring escalation, not merely documentation.
