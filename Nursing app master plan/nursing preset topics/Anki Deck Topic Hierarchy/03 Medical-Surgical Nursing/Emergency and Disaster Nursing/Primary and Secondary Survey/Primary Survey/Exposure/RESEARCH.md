# Primary Survey: Exposure and Environmental Control

## Purpose
Exposure allows the team to identify hidden injuries, bleeding, rashes, burns, deformities, medical devices and environmental causes while protecting privacy and preventing hypothermia. It is the “E” in ABCDE, but it should occur in parallel with warming and monitoring—not as prolonged, uncontrolled undressing.

## Nursing actions
- Expose the body area needed for a systematic examination; inspect front and back when safe, using trained team coordination and spinal precautions where indicated.
- Identify wounds, bleeding, burns, deformity, bruising, medical alert identifiers, medication patches or devices, and signs of infection/allergic reaction.
- Remove wet clothing and jewelry when safe; avoid removing adherent material from burns or impaled objects unless the protocol directs it.
- Preserve body temperature with warm blankets, warmed environment/fluids when ordered and prompt covering after assessment. Trauma patients are vulnerable to hypothermia, which can worsen coagulopathy.
- Maintain dignity, privacy, consent where possible, culturally respectful communication and a chaperone when required.
- Document location, size, appearance and time of findings; do not delay stabilization for detailed wound description.

## Clinical reasoning
Exposure may reveal a cause of shock (external bleeding), anaphylaxis, burns, injury or concealed devices. Hypothermia prevention is essential during major trauma, burns and prolonged resuscitation. Assess for pressure injuries and safeguarding concerns as relevant once immediate stabilization is underway.

## PNLE distinctions
- Do not let the patient become cold while searching for injuries.
- Preserve dignity and cover the patient after inspection.
- In trauma, the back exam must be coordinated to protect the spine and avoid unnecessary movement.
- Life-threatening airway, breathing and circulation threats remain priorities throughout the primary survey.
