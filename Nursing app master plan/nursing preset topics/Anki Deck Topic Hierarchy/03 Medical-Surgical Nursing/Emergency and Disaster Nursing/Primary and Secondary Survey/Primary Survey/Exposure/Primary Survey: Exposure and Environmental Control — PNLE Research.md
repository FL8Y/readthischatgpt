# Primary Survey: Exposure and Environmental Control

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Purpose
Exposure means adequately inspecting the patient for injury, bleeding, rash, burns, devices and hidden threats while preventing hypothermia and preserving dignity. In trauma, remove or cut clothing as needed, inspect front and back using safe coordinated movement and spinal precautions where indicated.
## Nursing actions
Expose enough to complete assessment; use warm blankets, warmed environment/fluids when ordered, and promptly cover the patient after inspection. Remove wet clothing in hypothermia risk, monitor temperature and avoid unnecessary prolonged exposure. Maintain privacy and explain actions when possible.
## Special considerations
Hypothermia worsens trauma-associated coagulopathy and can complicate resuscitation. Burns require exposure to assess extent but rapid prevention of heat loss. Look for medical identification, wounds and environmental exposures without delaying immediate lifesaving care.
## PNLE traps
Exposure is not permission to leave a patient uncovered. Prevent hypothermia, preserve dignity, and continue reassessing after turning or repositioning. Control major bleeding and airway/breathing threats first.
