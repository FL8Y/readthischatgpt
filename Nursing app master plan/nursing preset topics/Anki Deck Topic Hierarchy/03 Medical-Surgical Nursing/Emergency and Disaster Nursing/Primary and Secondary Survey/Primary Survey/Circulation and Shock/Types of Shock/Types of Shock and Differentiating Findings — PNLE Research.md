# Types of Shock and Differentiating Findings

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Classification
| Type | Core mechanism | Common clues | Priority focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypovolemic | Low circulating volume | Hemorrhage, dehydration, tachycardia, cool skin | Control loss; restore volume/blood per cause |
| Cardiogenic | Pump failure | MI, pulmonary edema, JVD may occur, poor perfusion | Cardiac support; avoid indiscriminate fluids |
| Distributive—septic | Vasodilation/capillary leak | Infection, fever or hypothermia, altered mentation; warm early possible | Prompt source control, antimicrobials, hemodynamic support |
| Distributive—anaphylactic | Mediator-driven vasodilation/airway edema | Sudden exposure, urticaria, wheeze/stridor, hypotension | IM epinephrine and airway support |
| Distributive—neurogenic | Loss of sympathetic tone | Spinal injury, hypotension with relative bradycardia, warm/dry skin | Spinal precautions and hemodynamic support |
| Obstructive | Mechanical impediment to filling/output | Tension pneumothorax, tamponade, massive PE | Urgent correction of obstruction |
## Assessment caveats
Patterns overlap; older adults, medications, pregnancy and comorbidities alter signs. Do not use skin temperature or heart rate alone to exclude shock. Trends, perfusion, cause and response to intervention are essential.
## Nursing priorities
ABCs, rapid escalation, monitoring, access, cause-directed preparation and frequent reassessment. Distinguish anaphylaxis from simple allergy: respiratory or circulatory compromise is an emergency.
## PNLE traps
Neurogenic shock can produce hypotension with bradycardia, unlike typical compensatory tachycardia. Septic shock may be warm early. Obstructive shock requires treating the obstruction, not fluids alone.
