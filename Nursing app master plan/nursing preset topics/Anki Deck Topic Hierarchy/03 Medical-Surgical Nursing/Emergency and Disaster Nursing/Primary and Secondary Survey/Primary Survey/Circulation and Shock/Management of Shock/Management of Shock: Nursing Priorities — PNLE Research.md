# Management of Shock: Nursing Priorities

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## First priorities
Recognize shock early, summon the emergency/rapid-response team, assess ABCs, monitor ECG/SpO₂/BP/temperature, establish access, and identify the cause. Control external bleeding immediately with direct pressure and appropriate hemorrhage-control measures. Keep the patient warm and reassess frequently.
## Cause-specific care
- Hypovolemic/hemorrhagic: control source; prepare blood products and balanced resuscitation per trauma protocol. Avoid delaying hemorrhage control for repeated crystalloid boluses.
- Septic/distributive: obtain cultures when feasible without materially delaying antibiotics, administer timely antimicrobials and fluids/vasopressors per current sepsis protocol, and reassess perfusion.
- Cardiogenic: urgent cardiac evaluation; avoid indiscriminate fluids if pulmonary edema or pump failure is suspected.
- Obstructive: urgent correction of cause (e.g., tension pneumothorax or tamponade).
- Anaphylactic: intramuscular epinephrine is first-line for anaphylaxis; do not substitute antihistamines for epinephrine in airway/circulatory compromise.
## Monitoring
Track mental state, BP/MAP, capillary refill, skin temperature, urine output, lactate trends, respiratory status and response to therapy. Document timing of recognition, interventions, communication and reassessment.
## PNLE traps
Treatment depends on shock type. Hypotension is not required for early shock. Epinephrine is first-line for anaphylaxis; fluids alone do not reverse septic source or obstructive causes.
