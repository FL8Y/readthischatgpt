# Primary Survey: Breathing and Ventilation

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Assessment
After airway, assess respiratory rate/effort, chest rise, symmetry, breath sounds, SpO₂, skin color, mental status and chest injury. Life-threatening problems include tension pneumothorax, open pneumothorax, massive hemothorax, flail chest and respiratory failure.
## Immediate management
Call for help, support oxygenation/ventilation, expose the chest enough to assess while preventing hypothermia, and prepare for emergency interventions per protocol. Treat the patient rather than waiting for imaging when tension pneumothorax is clinically suspected with instability. Assisted ventilation may be needed for apnea or inadequate breathing; monitor closely for deterioration.
## Nursing safety
Reassess after every intervention. High-flow oxygen is indicated in critical hypoxemia, but titrate to prescribed targets when feasible, especially in patients at risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure. Do not delay emergency escalation for complete history or routine diagnostics.
## Red flags and PNLE
Severe distress, silent/absent unilateral breath sounds after trauma, cyanosis, exhaustion, altered mentation or hypotension demand urgent action. Tracheal deviation is late and may be absent in tension pneumothorax. Airway and breathing threats take priority over disability scoring or detailed secondary survey.
