# Primary Survey: Airway Assessment and Management

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Purpose and assessment
Airway is the first component of the primary survey because obstruction rapidly causes hypoxia. Assess ability to speak, voice changes, stridor, snoring/gurgling, secretions, facial/neck trauma, burns/soot, swelling and level of consciousness. Assume cervical-spine injury when mechanism indicates it; use jaw-thrust and spinal motion restriction per protocol rather than head-tilt/chin-lift when trauma is suspected.
## Immediate actions
Call for help; open/maintain airway using trained techniques, suction visible secretions, provide oxygen/ventilatory support as indicated, and prepare for advanced airway intervention if obstruction or fatigue progresses. Use appropriate airway adjuncts only when trained and contraindications are considered. Suspected inhalation injury, progressive swelling, stridor or inability to protect airway requires early expert escalation.
## Nursing responsibilities
Continuously reassess patency, respiratory effort, SpO₂, mental status and response. Do not leave a threatened airway unattended. Avoid blind finger sweeps; remove only visible objects when appropriate. If complete foreign-body obstruction occurs, follow current age-appropriate choking/CPR algorithm.
## PNLE priorities
Stridor, inability to speak, cyanosis, reduced consciousness or rapidly progressive facial/neck swelling are emergencies. Airway obstruction outranks routine assessment, pain control and documentation. A normal SpO₂ early does not exclude evolving upper-airway obstruction.
