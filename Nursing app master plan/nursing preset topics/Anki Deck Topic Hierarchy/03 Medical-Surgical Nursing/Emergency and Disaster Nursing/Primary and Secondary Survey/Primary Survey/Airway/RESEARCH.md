# Primary Survey: Airway Assessment and Initial Nursing Actions

## Why airway comes first
The airway must remain patent for oxygen to reach the lungs. Obstruction can progress rapidly to hypoxia, respiratory arrest and cardiac arrest. Use the local emergency/trauma algorithm and call for appropriately trained help early; nurses should work within current training and scope.

## Recognition
Assess whether the patient can speak normally, the quality of voice, work of breathing, air movement and visible obstruction. Red flags include stridor, gurgling, choking, hoarseness after neck/airway injury, facial or neck burns, blood/vomit/secretions, rapidly increasing swelling, reduced consciousness and inability to speak. Snoring in a patient with reduced consciousness may indicate tongue-related obstruction. A patient can have partial obstruction while still moving some air.

## Initial actions
- Ensure scene safety and summon emergency response.
- Assess responsiveness and airway while immediately addressing catastrophic bleeding or simultaneous life threats as indicated by protocol.
- If trauma with possible cervical injury, use trained jaw-thrust/manual stabilization rather than routine head-tilt when appropriate; do not delay lifesaving airway maneuvers if ventilation cannot otherwise be achieved.
- Remove only visible, accessible material; suction blood/secretions when trained and equipped. Do not perform blind finger sweeps.
- Use appropriate positioning and basic airway adjuncts only within training/protocol. Prepare bag-mask ventilation, advanced airway support and transfer to a higher level of care if airway patency or protection is failing.
- Reassess continuously after each intervention.

## Nursing assessment and safety
Monitor oxygenation, respiratory effort, voice, consciousness, pulse and response. Consider anaphylaxis, inhalation injury, foreign body, aspiration, trauma, seizure/postictal state, overdose and stroke as potential causes. Do not give oral fluids/medications to a patient with impaired consciousness or unsafe swallowing. Document onset, observed obstruction, interventions and response.

## PNLE priorities
- Stridor, silent/ineffective airflow, facial/neck burn with progressive edema, or decreasing consciousness are immediate threats.
- Airway patency and airway protection are not identical: an obtunded patient may have an open airway but cannot protect it.
- Avoid blind finger sweeps; remove visible objects only.
- Cervical spine precautions matter in trauma, but must not prevent effective life-saving airway/ventilation support.
