# Three-Level Triage Systems: Emergency Nursing Overview

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## General structure
Three-level systems commonly group patients as emergent/immediate, urgent, or non-urgent, but labels and operational definitions vary by jurisdiction and institution. They should not be treated as interchangeable with five-level ESI or CTAS categories.
## Assessment and action
Identify immediate ABC threats, shock, severe altered consciousness, major bleeding, stroke symptoms and severe allergic reactions first. Apply the locally approved algorithm, document rationale, and reassess according to policy and clinical change.
## Safety and context
Do not assign a universal triage time target unless it is specified by the applicable system. Use local protocol, training and escalation rules. Triage must be equitable and based on acuity.
## PNLE traps
The number of levels differs across triage systems. Do not map a three-level category directly to ESI/CTAS without a validated local crosswalk. Clinical deterioration requires reassessment and reprioritization.
