# Emergency Department Triage: Acuity and Reassessment

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Purpose
Triage rapidly prioritizes patients by urgency, not arrival order, diagnosis certainty or social status. Immediate life threats require immediate resuscitation; unstable patients must be reassessed as conditions change.
## Assessment
Use the facility's adopted validated triage system and trained protocol. Assess chief complaint, ABCs, vital signs, mental status, pain, mechanism, high-risk symptoms and special populations. Chest pain, stroke signs, respiratory distress, shock, severe allergic reaction, major bleeding and altered consciousness are high-risk presentations.
## Nursing responsibilities
Document triage category and rationale, promptly communicate red flags, initiate permitted protocol-based actions, protect privacy, and reassess waiting patients at intervals defined by local policy and whenever symptoms change. Triage is dynamic; do not assume a patient remains stable because an initial category was low.
## Safety
Use objective findings and clinical concern; avoid bias based on age, disability, language, behavior or ability to pay. Follow local emergency consent and escalation policy.
## PNLE traps
Triage is prioritization, not definitive diagnosis. A patient with airway compromise or shock outranks a stable patient with a painful but non-life-threatening condition. Deterioration requires re-triage.
