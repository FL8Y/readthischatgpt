# Emergency Department Triage

## Purpose
Triage sorts patients by acuity and urgency so those who need immediate life-saving intervention are identified before patients who can safely wait. It is not a first-come, first-served queue and is not a complete diagnostic assessment. Use the hospital's adopted validated triage system and local rules.

## Immediate red flags
Immediate resuscitation-level threats can include airway obstruction/stridor, severe respiratory distress, absent/ineffective breathing, shock or major hemorrhage, ongoing seizure, unresponsiveness, sudden focal neurologic deficit, acute chest pain with instability, severe anaphylaxis or rapidly deteriorating status. The specific category depends on the adopted system and trained triage assessment.

## Core triage approach
- Perform a rapid first look: appearance, airway, breathing, circulation, mental status and obvious catastrophic bleeding.
- Identify high-risk mechanism, symptoms, vulnerable populations and abnormal vital signs; measure and document promptly.
- Use a standardized system (for example, a locally adopted five-level emergency severity index or CTAS protocol) consistently. Do not mix category definitions between systems.
- Reassess waiting patients at the interval and triggers required by local protocol; acuity can change.
- Escalate when new symptoms, abnormal vital signs, deterioration or a patient's/family's concern warrants reassessment.
- Document reason for category, vital signs, time, reassessments and escalation.

## Ethics and safety
Triage must be impartial, evidence-informed and respectful; communicate delays and warning signs. Use interpreters/accessibility support when available, preserve privacy and observe infection-prevention precautions. Disaster triage during resource scarcity differs from routine ED triage; use the incident command plan and the specific disaster protocol.

## PNLE distinctions
- Triage prioritizes urgency, not arrival time, social status or who complains loudest.
- A patient with an airway/breathing problem or shock outranks a stable patient seeking routine care.
- A triage score is a snapshot; repeated assessment is essential.
- CTAS, ESI, three-level systems and local Philippine Emergency Nurses Association (PENA) materials may have different definitions. Never transfer exact category rules between frameworks without checking the applicable standard.
