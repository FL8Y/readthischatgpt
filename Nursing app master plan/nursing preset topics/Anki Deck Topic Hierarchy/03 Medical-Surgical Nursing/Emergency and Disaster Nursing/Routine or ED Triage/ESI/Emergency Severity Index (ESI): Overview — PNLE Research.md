# Emergency Severity Index (ESI): Overview

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Overview
The Emergency Severity Index is a five-level ED triage algorithm that combines immediate life-saving intervention needs, high-risk status, mental status/vital-sign concerns and anticipated resource use. Level 1 requires immediate life-saving intervention; level 2 is high risk or otherwise should not wait; levels 3–5 use expected resources with vital-sign reassessment and clinical judgment.
## Safe use
Only trained staff should apply the current ESI handbook. Do not assign levels from resource count alone when high-risk features or abnormal vital signs suggest instability. Pediatric patients and special populations require careful application of the current algorithm.
## Nursing duties
Assess ABCs and high-risk complaints first, record rationale, escalate abnormal vital signs or clinical concern, and reassess patients in the waiting room. Local policy may require additional screening pathways for sepsis, stroke, chest pain or trauma.
## PNLE traps
ESI is not simply a pain scale or first-come-first-served list. A high-risk patient can require level 2 even before resource counting; instability overrides routine queue order.
