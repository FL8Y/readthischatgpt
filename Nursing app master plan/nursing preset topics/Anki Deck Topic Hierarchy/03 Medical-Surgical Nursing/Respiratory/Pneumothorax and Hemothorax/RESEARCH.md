# Pneumothorax and Hemothorax

## Mechanism
Pneumothorax is air in the pleural space, impairing lung expansion. Hemothorax is blood in the pleural cavity, often after trauma or procedures. Tension pneumothorax develops when pressure rises and compromises venous return, causing obstructive shock; it is an immediate clinical emergency.

## Recognition
Symptoms include sudden pleuritic chest pain, dyspnea, tachypnea, reduced breath sounds and hypoxemia. Tension physiology may cause severe distress, hypotension, tachycardia, cyanosis or collapse; tracheal deviation and distended neck veins may be absent, especially early. Hemothorax can cause hemorrhagic shock with reduced breath sounds and dullness to percussion. No single sign is definitive.

## Diagnostics and management
Stable patients may undergo chest radiography, ultrasound or CT according to context. In suspected tension pneumothorax with instability, emergency decompression by trained clinicians should not wait for imaging. Chest-tube drainage is used for significant cases; massive hemothorax may need blood products and surgery. Avoid treating suspected tension physiology as a routine imaging problem.

## Nursing management
Activate emergency response for sudden respiratory distress, hypoxemia or shock, especially after trauma/procedure. Assess ABCs, respiratory effort, chest expansion, breath sounds, SpO2, pulse/BP and mental status. Prepare monitoring, oxygen/ventilatory support and drainage equipment per protocol and clinician direction. If a chest tube is present, keep the unit upright and below chest level, secure connections and avoid routine clamping/stripping. Watch for bleeding, air leak, subcutaneous emphysema, infection and recurrence.

## PNLE distinctions
- Tension pneumothorax is a clinical emergency; classic signs may be incomplete.
- Hemothorax may cause hemorrhagic shock, so assess circulation as well as breathing.
- Deterioration after central-line insertion or positive-pressure ventilation is concerning.
- Do not delay escalation while collecting a full history.
