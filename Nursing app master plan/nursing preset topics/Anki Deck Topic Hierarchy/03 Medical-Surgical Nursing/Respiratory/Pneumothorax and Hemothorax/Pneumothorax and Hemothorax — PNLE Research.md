# Pneumothorax and Hemothorax

**Status:** Researched draft; independent source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Definitions
Pneumothorax is air in the pleural space; hemothorax is blood in the pleural space. Both can impair ventilation; hemothorax can also cause major blood loss. Tension pneumothorax occurs when pressure builds and compromises venous return, causing obstructive shock.
## Findings
Pneumothorax may cause sudden pleuritic chest pain, dyspnea and reduced breath sounds on the affected side. Tension physiology may produce severe distress, hypotension, distended neck veins (not always), tracheal deviation (late/unreliable) and rapid deterioration. Hemothorax may add signs of hypovolemia and dullness to percussion.
## Emergency priorities
Assess ABCs and activate emergency response. Suspected tension pneumothorax in an unstable patient is a clinical emergency; treatment must not wait for imaging. Clinicians perform immediate decompression followed by definitive pleural drainage per protocol. Support oxygenation/ventilation and circulation, establish access and prepare for chest tube and blood products when indicated.
## Nursing care
Monitor respiratory status, hemodynamics and response; prepare equipment; maintain chest-drain safety; quantify bleeding; and report sudden deterioration. Avoid unnecessary positive-pressure ventilation before decompression when tension pneumothorax is suspected if this can be safely avoided while urgent treatment is mobilized.
## PNLE traps
Tracheal deviation is a late sign and its absence does not exclude tension pneumothorax. Hypotension with unilateral absent breath sounds after trauma or a procedure is an emergency. Hemothorax can cause hemorrhagic shock.
