# Tonsillectomy and Adenoidectomy: Perioperative Care

**Status:** Researched draft; independent source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Indications and risks
Tonsillectomy/adenoidectomy may be performed for selected recurrent infections, obstructive sleep-disordered breathing or other specialist indications. Important risks include hemorrhage, airway obstruction, dehydration, pain and anesthesia complications.
## Postoperative nursing priorities
Assess airway, respiratory effort, oxygenation, level of consciousness and bleeding. Position according to recovery status and local protocol; maintain suction and emergency equipment availability. Encourage prescribed analgesia and fluids as tolerated once fully awake and safe to swallow. Monitor intake/output and dehydration.
## Hemorrhage red flags
Frequent swallowing, throat clearing, restlessness, pallor, tachycardia, hematemesis or bright-red oral/nasal blood can signal bleeding. Bleeding after tonsillectomy is an emergency: notify the surgical team, keep NPO as directed, assess ABCs, prepare for urgent intervention and do not leave the patient alone. Avoid blind probing of the surgical site.
## Teaching
Follow surgeon's diet/activity and analgesic instructions; encourage hydration and recognize bleeding, respiratory distress or dehydration warning signs. Avoid aspirin unless specifically directed because of bleeding risk; medication choices should follow age-specific orders.
## PNLE traps
Frequent swallowing in a child after tonsillectomy can be an early hemorrhage clue. Airway and bleeding outrank routine pain assessment.
