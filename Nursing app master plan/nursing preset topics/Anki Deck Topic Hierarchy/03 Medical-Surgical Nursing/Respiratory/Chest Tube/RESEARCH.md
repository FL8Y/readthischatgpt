# Chest Tube Drainage: Nursing Management

## Purpose and assessment
A chest tube drains air, blood, pus or fluid from the pleural space to support lung re-expansion or remove pleural contents. Indications include pneumothorax, hemothorax, pleural effusion and selected postoperative conditions. Assess respiratory effort, SpO2, breath sounds, chest expansion, vital signs, pain, insertion site, dressing, tube connections and drainage amount/character per device and policy.

## System safety
Keep the drainage unit upright and below chest level. Secure connections, avoid kinks/dependent loops, and ensure prescribed suction is correctly configured. Water-seal tidaling may occur with respiration; its absence can mean lung re-expansion or obstruction/disconnection, so interpret it alongside the patient's condition and system type. Continuous bubbling in a water-seal chamber may indicate an air leak. Follow device-specific protocol to localize/report an air leak.

Do not routinely clamp or strip/milk a chest tube; clamping can trap air and stripping may cause unsafe pressure changes. If the system disconnects, call for help and follow the unit's emergency reconnection procedure rather than improvising. Maintain insertion-site asepsis and the prescribed dressing.

## Deterioration and interventions
Sudden severe dyspnea, hypoxemia, hypotension, tracheal deviation, rapid drainage change, new subcutaneous emphysema or diminished breath sounds may indicate tension pneumothorax, hemorrhage, tube displacement or obstruction. Escalate immediately and assess ABCs; do not prioritize a chamber observation over a deteriorating patient. Provide prescribed analgesia to support deep breathing/mobility. Encourage coughing, deep breathing and mobilization as tolerated. Monitor for infection, bleeding, re-expansion pulmonary edema and complications of immobility.

## If the tube is pulled out
Call for immediate assistance, assess ABCs, and use the facility's prescribed dressing protocol while preparing for urgent evaluation. Dressing approach depends on local protocol; do not delay escalation to search for supplies. Activate emergency response if deterioration occurs.

## PNLE distinctions
- Keep the drainage system upright and below chest level.
- Do not routinely clamp or strip a chest tube.
- Continuous bubbling in the water seal can suggest an air leak.
- Sudden respiratory distress or hemodynamic compromise is an emergency.
