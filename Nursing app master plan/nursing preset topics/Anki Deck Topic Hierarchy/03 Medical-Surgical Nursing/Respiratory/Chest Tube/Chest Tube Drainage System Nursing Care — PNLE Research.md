# Chest Tube Drainage System Nursing Care

**Status:** Researched draft; independent source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Purpose and system principles
Chest tubes drain air, blood, or fluid from the pleural space to help re-establish negative intrapleural pressure and lung expansion. Systems may use water seal and prescribed suction; understand the specific device and institutional protocol.
## Assessment
Check respiratory status, bilateral breath sounds, tube insertion site/dressing, tubing patency and kinks, secure connections, drainage amount/color/trend, and the water-seal/suction chamber if present. Tidaling may reflect pressure changes; absence can mean re-expansion or obstruction, so correlate with clinical status. Continuous bubbling in the water-seal chamber may indicate an air leak; intermittent bubbling can occur with air evacuation depending on context/device.
## Safety
Keep the drainage system upright and below chest level. Avoid dependent loops and accidental disconnection. Do not routinely clamp a chest tube; clamping can trap air and cause tension pneumothorax. If the tube disconnects, follow institutional emergency procedure to restore a safe water seal/connection and summon help. If tube dislodges from the chest, apply an appropriate sterile occlusive dressing per protocol and get immediate assistance; local protocols differ on dressing technique.
## Escalation
Sudden dyspnea, hypoxemia, hypotension, tracheal deviation, rapidly increasing subcutaneous emphysema, large/rapid bleeding, or abrupt cessation of drainage with deterioration requires urgent assessment. Report unexpected large output according to surgical orders and policy.
## PNLE traps
Do not strip or milk tubing routinely unless specifically ordered by protocol; it can create high negative pressure. Do not clamp casually for transport or troubleshooting.
