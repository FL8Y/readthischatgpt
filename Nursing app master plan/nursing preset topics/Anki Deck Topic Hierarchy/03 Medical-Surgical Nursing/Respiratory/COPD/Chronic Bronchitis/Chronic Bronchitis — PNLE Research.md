# Chronic Bronchitis

**Status:** Researched draft; independent source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Definition and mechanism
Chronic bronchitis is classically productive cough for at least 3 months in each of 2 consecutive years after excluding other causes. It is a clinical phenotype commonly associated with COPD, mucus hypersecretion and airway inflammation; COPD diagnosis requires spirometry demonstrating persistent airflow obstruction.
## Findings and assessment
Productive cough, sputum, dyspnea, wheeze and recurrent exacerbations may occur. Assess baseline respiratory status, sputum change, fever, work of breathing, SpO₂, mental status and signs of CO₂ retention or right-sided heart failure.
## Management and nursing care
Smoking cessation is the most important disease-modifying intervention. Management may include inhaled long-acting bronchodilators, selected inhaled corticosteroid combinations, vaccinations, pulmonary rehabilitation and oxygen for qualifying chronic hypoxemia. During exacerbation, assess for infection, use prescribed short-acting bronchodilators, systemic corticosteroids and antibiotics when indicated; support ventilation if respiratory failure develops.
## Oxygen and safety
In patients at risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure, controlled oxygen and blood gas reassessment may be needed; a common acute-care target is SpO₂ 88–92% pending individualized assessment/protocol. Do not withhold oxygen from a hypoxemic patient; titrate and monitor.
## PNLE traps
COPD is not diagnosed by symptoms alone; spirometry confirms obstruction. Sputum purulence may inform exacerbation assessment but does not alone prove bacterial infection. Smoking cessation and inhaler technique are essential.
