# Sinusitis (Rhinosinusitis)

**Status:** Researched draft; independent source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Definition and causes
Rhinosinusitis is inflammation of nasal and paranasal sinus mucosa, most often viral. Bacterial disease is more likely with persistent symptoms without improvement, severe onset with high fever/purulent discharge/facial pain, or worsening after initial improvement; duration criteria vary by guideline.
## Assessment
Symptoms include nasal obstruction/discharge, facial pressure/pain, reduced smell and cough. Assess duration, trajectory, fever, unilateral swelling, severe headache, eye symptoms, immune status and medication history.
## Management
Most uncomplicated acute cases need symptomatic care such as analgesia, saline irrigation with safe water, and selected intranasal corticosteroids. Antibiotics are reserved for cases meeting clinical criteria or complications and should follow local guidance. Avoid routine antibiotics for likely viral disease. Decongestants have contraindications/precautions, including some cardiovascular conditions and drug interactions; topical agents can cause rebound congestion if used too long.
## Red flags
Periorbital swelling, visual change, ophthalmoplegia, severe frontal headache, meningismus, altered mental status or focal neurologic findings may signal orbital/intracranial complications and need urgent assessment.
## PNLE traps
Purulent mucus alone does not prove bacterial infection. Consider duration and symptom trajectory. Orbital/neurologic signs are not routine sinusitis and require urgent escalation.
