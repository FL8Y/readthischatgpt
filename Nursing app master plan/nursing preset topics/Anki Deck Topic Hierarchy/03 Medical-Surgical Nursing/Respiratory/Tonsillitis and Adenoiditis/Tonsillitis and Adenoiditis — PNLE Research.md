# Tonsillitis and Adenoiditis

**Status:** Researched draft; independent source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Causes and assessment
Inflammation of tonsils/adenoids is commonly viral; group A streptococcal pharyngitis is an important bacterial cause. Findings include sore throat, fever, odynophagia, tonsillar erythema/exudate, tender anterior cervical nodes, halitosis or nasal obstruction/mouth breathing with adenoid enlargement. Exudate alone does not prove bacterial disease.
## Diagnosis and management
Use clinical criteria and appropriate rapid antigen testing/culture according to age and local guideline. Antibiotics are indicated for confirmed or appropriately diagnosed bacterial infection, not routine viral illness. Supportive care includes fluids, rest and age-appropriate analgesia. Complete prescribed antibiotics; avoid sharing medicines.
## Red flags
Drooling, stridor, respiratory distress, inability to swallow fluids, muffled “hot potato” voice, trismus, unilateral swelling or neck stiffness may indicate airway compromise or peritonsillar/deep neck infection. Urgent assessment is required.
## Nursing and teaching
Assess hydration, airway, swallowing, pain and fever. Use hand hygiene and appropriate infection precautions. Provide medication education and advise urgent care for breathing/swallowing difficulty or worsening symptoms.
## PNLE traps
A threatened airway outranks oral medication or throat inspection. Antibiotics do not treat viral tonsillitis; testing and clinical context guide treatment.
