# Calcium Imbalances

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Physiology and assessment
Calcium supports neuromuscular function, coagulation and bone integrity. Interpret total calcium with albumin and consider ionized calcium in critical illness. Symptoms depend on severity, rate of change and magnesium status.
## Hypocalcemia
Perioral/finger tingling, cramps, tetany, hyperreflexia, seizures, prolonged QT and positive Chvostek/Trousseau signs may occur. Causes include hypoparathyroidism, vitamin D deficiency, CKD, pancreatitis and low magnesium. Severe symptomatic hypocalcemia is an emergency; IV calcium gluconate may be ordered with ECG monitoring and secure IV access because extravasation can injure tissue.
## Hypercalcemia
Constipation, nausea, polyuria, dehydration, weakness, confusion, shortened QT and renal stones may occur. Causes include hyperparathyroidism, malignancy and some medicines. Management may include isotonic fluids when appropriate, treatment of cause and selected antiresorptive agents. Use caution with fluid therapy in heart/renal failure.
## Nursing priorities and medication safety
Monitor ECG, mental status, neuromuscular findings, renal function and associated Mg/phosphate. Correct magnesium deficiency when relevant because it can impair calcium correction. Avoid giving calcium through incompatible lines/solutions; follow compatibility and extravasation protocols.
## PNLE traps
Hypocalcemia increases neuromuscular excitability; hypercalcemia often causes weakness, constipation and stones. Do not rely on Chvostek/Trousseau alone. Consider albumin and ionized calcium in critically ill patients.
