# Sodium Imbalances

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Physiology and interpretation
Serum sodium mainly reflects water balance, not total-body sodium alone. Hyponatremia is usually <135 mEq/L; hypernatremia is usually >145 mEq/L, but use the local laboratory range. Symptoms depend on severity and rate of change.
## Hyponatremia
Causes include SIADH, diuretics, GI losses with replacement by free water, heart/liver failure and adrenal insufficiency. Headache, nausea, confusion, seizures or coma may occur. Assess volume status, serum/urine osmolality, urine sodium and cause. Severe neurologic symptoms may require hypertonic saline under a strict protocol; avoid overcorrection because osmotic demyelination can occur.
## Hypernatremia
Usually reflects water deficit or less commonly sodium gain. Thirst, weakness, irritability, confusion, seizures and coma may occur. Treat cause and replace water carefully; route/rate depends on hemodynamics and chronicity. Too-rapid correction of chronic hypernatremia can cause cerebral edema.
## Nursing priorities
Frequent neurologic checks, strict intake/output, daily weights and serial sodium monitoring. Review medicines and fluid intake. Escalate seizures, declining consciousness, shock or severe symptomatic dysnatremia.
## PNLE traps
Both low and high sodium can cause neurologic emergencies. Correction rate depends on acuity/chronicity and patient risk; do not memorize a universal rate. Treat shock first when present, then correct water/sodium deficit in a monitored plan.
