# Intravenous Fluids: Tonicity and Nursing Safety

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Categories
- Isotonic crystalloid (e.g., 0.9% sodium chloride, balanced crystalloids): expands extracellular volume; used for selected hypovolemia/shock.
- Hypotonic fluid (e.g., 0.45% sodium chloride): supplies relatively more free water; can worsen cerebral edema or hyponatremia in inappropriate contexts.
- Hypertonic saline (e.g., 3% NaCl): selected severe symptomatic hyponatremia or specific indications with close monitoring; rapid/unsupervised administration risks serious harm.
Dextrose-containing fluids change effective tonicity after glucose metabolism; classify based on actual solution and clinical purpose, not shorthand alone.
## Assessment and monitoring
Assess BP, HR, perfusion, lungs, edema, urine output, weight, sodium, glucose and renal/cardiac function. Reassess response after boluses; use ordered rate and protocol. Watch for pulmonary edema, infiltration/extravasation, phlebitis and electrolyte shifts.
## Safety
Fluid choice depends on volume status, sodium, renal/cardiac function, neurologic condition and the reason for treatment. Do not automatically give large boluses to patients with heart failure or renal failure. Hypertonic saline and severe dysnatremia correction require protocolized monitoring.
## PNLE traps
Treat shock/perfusion with appropriate isotonic crystalloid when indicated, but reassess frequently. Do not use hypotonic fluid for initial resuscitation of shock. Never correct chronic hyponatremia rapidly without a prescribed protocol.
