# Magnesium Imbalance: Hypomagnesemia and Hypermagnesemia

## Physiologic role
Magnesium supports neuromuscular function, ATP-dependent reactions and cardiac electrical stability. It is influenced by GI absorption, renal excretion, alcohol use, nutrition, medications and critical illness. Interpret serum magnesium alongside potassium, calcium, renal function and clinical status.

## Hypomagnesemia
Causes include diarrhea/malabsorption, poor nutrition/alcohol use, diuretics, some nephrotoxic drugs and renal wasting. Findings may include tremor, hyperreflexia, muscle cramps, weakness, seizures, prolonged QT and dysrhythmias (including risk of torsades de pointes). It can coexist with hypokalemia and hypocalcemia; potassium may be difficult to correct until magnesium is addressed. Check magnesium and associated electrolytes/renal function, ECG when indicated and medication history.

Treatment ranges from oral replacement to IV magnesium for severe symptoms or arrhythmia under monitored protocol. Monitor BP, respiratory status, reflexes, renal function and infusion safety. IV administration rate and monitoring must follow order/protocol; excessive or rapid administration can cause hypotension and toxicity.

## Hypermagnesemia
Usually occurs with renal impairment plus magnesium-containing laxatives/antacids or excessive replacement. Findings can include nausea/flushing, hypotension, diminished reflexes, weakness, bradycardia, respiratory depression and cardiac arrest at severe levels. Stop further magnesium exposure under clinician direction and escalate symptomatic cases. Treatment may include supportive care, IV calcium for severe cardiac/neuromuscular effects and dialysis when needed; choices depend on kidney function and severity.

## Nursing priorities
- Report severe weakness, seizures, dysrhythmia, hypotension, reduced reflexes or respiratory depression immediately.
- Trend magnesium, potassium, calcium, creatinine/eGFR, urine output and ECG when indicated.
- Use caution with magnesium replacement in renal impairment; verify product, dose, rate and monitoring.
- Review OTC magnesium laxatives/antacids and educate patients with CKD not to self-medicate without advice.

## PNLE distinctions
- Low magnesium can cause neuromuscular irritability and refractory hypokalemia.
- High magnesium can suppress reflexes and respiration.
- Renal impairment raises toxicity risk.
- Treatment depends on symptoms and cause; never guess replacement dose/rate.
