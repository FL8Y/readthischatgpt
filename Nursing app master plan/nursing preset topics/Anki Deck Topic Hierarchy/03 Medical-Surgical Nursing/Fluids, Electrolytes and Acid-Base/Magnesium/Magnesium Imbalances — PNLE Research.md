# Magnesium Imbalances

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Role and causes
Magnesium supports neuromuscular function and cardiac conduction and affects potassium/calcium regulation. Hypomagnesemia may result from poor intake, diarrhea, alcohol use, diuretics or renal losses; hypermagnesemia is most likely with renal impairment plus magnesium-containing medicines/supplements.
## Hypomagnesemia
Tremor, hyperreflexia, cramps, seizures, QT prolongation and torsades de pointes may occur; hypokalemia/hypocalcemia can coexist. Replace oral or IV magnesium according to severity and renal function. IV replacement requires monitoring for hypotension, flushing, bradycardia and respiratory depression.
## Hypermagnesemia
Nausea, flushing, hypotension, diminished reflexes, bradycardia, respiratory depression and cardiac arrest may occur. Stop magnesium sources and urgently escalate severe symptoms; treatment may include IV calcium as antagonist, supportive care and dialysis in selected renal failure.
## Nursing priorities
Monitor ECG, BP, respiratory rate, deep tendon reflexes, renal function and associated electrolytes. Use caution with magnesium sulfate in renal impairment; assess toxicity signs and follow the prescribed protocol. Calcium gluconate is a potential antidote for serious magnesium toxicity under emergency orders.
## PNLE traps
Low magnesium predisposes to dysrhythmias and refractory hypokalemia. High magnesium causes depressed reflexes and respiratory/cardiac depression. Never continue magnesium infusion blindly when toxicity signs develop.
