# Phosphate Imbalances

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Physiology
Phosphate is required for ATP, cell membranes, bone and oxygen delivery. Interpret phosphate with calcium, magnesium, renal function, nutrition and acid-base status.
## Hypophosphatemia
Causes include refeeding after malnutrition, alcohol use, malabsorption, hyperparathyroidism and shifts during treatment of DKA. Severe deficiency may cause weakness, respiratory muscle failure, rhabdomyolysis, hemolysis, confusion or cardiac dysfunction. Replace orally or IV depending on severity and protocol; IV phosphate can cause hypocalcemia, arrhythmia and calcium-phosphate precipitation, especially in renal dysfunction.
## Hyperphosphatemia
Often occurs in CKD, tumor lysis, rhabdomyolysis or phosphate load. It can lower calcium and cause tetany/soft tissue calcification. Management treats cause, limits phosphate intake, uses binders with meals when prescribed, and may require dialysis in severe renal failure.
## Nursing priorities
Monitor phosphate, calcium, potassium, renal function, ECG and respiratory muscle strength in severe deficiency. In refeeding-risk patients, monitor phosphate closely when nutrition is restarted and replace per protocol.
## PNLE traps
Refeeding syndrome can cause a rapid phosphate drop and respiratory/cardiac compromise. Renal impairment increases risk of phosphate accumulation and replacement toxicity.
