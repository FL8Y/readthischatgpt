# Phosphate Imbalance: Hypophosphatemia and Hyperphosphatemia

## Role and interpretation
Phosphate supports ATP/energy production, cell membranes, oxygen delivery via 2,3-DPG and bone mineralization. Most phosphate is intracellular or in bone; serum concentration can shift with nutrition, insulin, acid-base state and renal function. Reference intervals vary by age and laboratory, and children normally have higher values than adults.

## Hypophosphatemia
Causes include malnutrition/refeeding, alcohol use, malabsorption, hyperparathyroidism, renal phosphate wasting and intracellular shifts (including after insulin treatment of DKA). Severe depletion can cause muscle weakness, respiratory muscle failure, confusion, seizures, hemolysis, impaired myocardial function and rhabdomyolysis. Evaluate phosphate with calcium, magnesium, potassium, renal function and the clinical context. Repletion route and monitoring depend on severity; IV phosphate can cause hypocalcemia, arrhythmia and calcium-phosphate precipitation, so it requires a verified order/protocol.

## Hyperphosphatemia
Common causes include CKD, hypoparathyroidism, cell breakdown/tumor lysis and acidosis. Symptoms may reflect hypocalcemia, including tingling, tetany or seizures; chronic phosphate imbalance contributes to CKD mineral-bone disease and vascular/soft-tissue calcification. Treatment addresses the cause and may involve individualized dietary phosphate guidance, phosphate binders with meals, renal replacement therapy or other cause-directed care. Binders differ, and calcium-containing products may be unsafe for some patients.

## Nursing responsibilities
- Monitor phosphate, calcium, magnesium, potassium and renal function as ordered; trend after repletion/treatment.
- Assess weakness, respiratory effort, mental status, muscle pain and signs of hypocalcemia.
- During refeeding or DKA treatment, watch for clinically significant intracellular shifts.
- Verify whether binders must be taken with meals and review interactions with other medicines.
- Do not initiate phosphate or calcium supplements or fluid/diet changes without checking renal function and the current plan.

## PNLE distinctions
- Severe hypophosphatemia can impair respiratory muscles and cardiac function.
- Hyperphosphatemia often accompanies renal impairment and may lower calcium through binding/precipitation.
- Treat the cause and monitor calcium; replacement itself can be dangerous if excessive/rapid.
