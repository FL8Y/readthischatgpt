# Acid–Base Imbalance: Acidosis and Alkalosis

## Core framework
Arterial pH is commonly referenced as 7.35–7.45; pH below this is acidemia and above this is alkalemia. The terms **acidosis** and **alkalosis** refer to processes causing the pH change. The lungs regulate carbon dioxide (CO2), while kidneys regulate bicarbonate and hydrogen-ion handling. Reference intervals differ somewhat by lab and specimen.

## Four primary disorders
| Disorder | Primary change | Common causes / clues |
|---|---|---|
| Respiratory acidosis | Increased PaCO2 from inadequate alveolar ventilation | COPD exacerbation, respiratory depression, airway obstruction, neuromuscular weakness |
| Respiratory alkalosis | Decreased PaCO2 from increased ventilation | Pain/anxiety, hypoxemia, sepsis, pregnancy, some CNS or pulmonary disorders |
| Metabolic acidosis | Decreased HCO3− or added acid | DKA, lactic acidosis/shock, renal failure, diarrhea, toxins |
| Metabolic alkalosis | Increased HCO3− or acid loss | Vomiting/NG suction, diuretics, hypokalemia, selected mineralocorticoid states |

## ABG interpretation
1. Check pH to identify acidemia/alkalemia.
2. Check PaCO2 (respiratory component) and HCO3− (metabolic component) for the primary process.
3. Determine whether compensation is appropriate; compensation does not normally overshoot to make pH move past normal in a simple single disorder.
4. Consider mixed disorders, oxygenation, clinical status, electrolytes and anion gap.
5. Compare with baseline, especially in chronic respiratory disease. Do not diagnose from pH alone.

Typical adult arterial reference intervals are pH 7.35–7.45, PaCO2 35–45 mmHg and HCO3− about 22–26 mmol/L, but use the reporting laboratory's ranges and interpret alongside clinical context.

## Nursing priorities
Treat the underlying cause: restore perfusion and treat sepsis/DKA as prescribed; assess ventilation and airway in respiratory acidosis; control vomiting/NG losses and review diuretics in metabolic alkalosis; evaluate for renal failure or toxin exposure when indicated. Monitor ECG, potassium, calcium, respiratory pattern, mental status, lactate, renal function and repeat blood gases as ordered. Escalate severe acidemia, arrhythmia, altered consciousness, respiratory fatigue or hemodynamic instability urgently. Do not give bicarbonate routinely for every acidosis; its role depends on cause/severity and clinician assessment.

## PNLE traps
- Low pH is acidemia; it does not alone tell you whether the primary cause is respiratory or metabolic.
- Compensation should be plausible for the underlying disorder; unexplained values may indicate a mixed process.
- Vomiting tends toward metabolic alkalosis; diarrhea commonly causes bicarbonate loss and metabolic acidosis.
- Severe respiratory fatigue can make a previously anxious tachypneic patient slow down—this may indicate deterioration, not recovery.
