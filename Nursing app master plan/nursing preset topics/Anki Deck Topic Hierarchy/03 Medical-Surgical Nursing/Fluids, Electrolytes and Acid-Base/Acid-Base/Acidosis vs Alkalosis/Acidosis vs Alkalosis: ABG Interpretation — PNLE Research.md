# Acidosis vs Alkalosis: ABG Interpretation

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## ABG framework
Typical adult reference ranges (laboratory dependent): pH 7.35–7.45, PaCO₂ 35–45 mm Hg, HCO₃⁻ 22–26 mEq/L. Verify the local lab range. pH <7.35 indicates acidemia; >7.45 alkalemia. Identify the primary process from PaCO₂ (respiratory) or HCO₃⁻ (metabolic), then assess compensation and clinical context.
| Primary disorder | pH | Primary change |
|---|---|---|
| Respiratory acidosis | ↓ | PaCO₂ ↑ (hypoventilation) |
| Respiratory alkalosis | ↑ | PaCO₂ ↓ (hyperventilation) |
| Metabolic acidosis | ↓ | HCO₃⁻ ↓ |
| Metabolic alkalosis | ↑ | HCO₃⁻ ↑ |
Compensation usually moves pH toward normal; it does not typically fully normalize it in a simple disorder. Mixed disorders occur, especially in critically ill patients.
## Causes and nursing priorities
Respiratory acidosis: COPD exacerbation, sedative/opioid effect, neuromuscular weakness or airway failure—assess ventilation and mental status. Respiratory alkalosis: pain, anxiety, hypoxemia, sepsis or PE—treat cause, do not assume anxiety. Metabolic acidosis: DKA, lactic acidosis/shock, renal failure or diarrhea. Metabolic alkalosis: vomiting/NG suction, diuretics, volume/chloride depletion.
Prioritize ABCs, perfusion, glucose, electrolytes and the cause; trend ABGs with clinical status. Escalate severe acidemia/alkalemia, arrhythmia, shock or altered consciousness.
## PNLE traps
Do not treat the number alone; identify the underlying cause. Normal pH does not exclude a mixed disorder. In suspected opioid hypoventilation, respiratory assessment and emergency treatment take priority.
