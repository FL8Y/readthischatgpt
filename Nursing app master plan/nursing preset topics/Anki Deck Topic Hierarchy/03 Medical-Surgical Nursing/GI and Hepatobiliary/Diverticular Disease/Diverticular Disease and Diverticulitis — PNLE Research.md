# Diverticular Disease and Diverticulitis

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Definitions
Diverticulosis is the presence of colonic diverticula; diverticulitis is inflammation, sometimes with microperforation. Left lower quadrant pain, fever and altered bowel habits are common in Western populations, though location varies with anatomy and population.
## Assessment and diagnosis
Assess pain, fever, bowel changes, bleeding, hydration, peritoneal signs and immunosuppression. CT is often used for suspected acute diverticulitis and complications. Rectal bleeding can occur with diverticular disease but requires assessment for other causes.
## Management and nursing care
Uncomplicated disease may be managed with diet adjustment and symptom control; antibiotic use depends on severity, immune status and current guidance. Complicated disease (abscess, obstruction, fistula, perforation/peritonitis) may need antibiotics, drainage or surgery. During severe acute illness, follow ordered diet/IV fluid/analgesia plan; advance diet as tolerated per clinical plan.
## Emergency signs
Rigidity, rebound, severe worsening pain, persistent vomiting, fever with hypotension or altered mental status suggest complication/sepsis. Escalate urgently.
## PNLE traps
Do not assume every uncomplicated case requires antibiotics; treatment is individualized. Do not recommend colonoscopy during acute inflammation; timing after recovery depends on history and guideline.
