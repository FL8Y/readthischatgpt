# Cholecystitis: Medical-Surgical Nursing

## Definition and mechanism
Cholecystitis is gallbladder inflammation, most often associated with cystic-duct obstruction by gallstones (calculous cholecystitis). Acalculous cholecystitis can occur in critically ill patients without stones. Prolonged obstruction may cause infection, gangrene, perforation or biliary complications.

## Findings and diagnostic studies
Typical symptoms include persistent right-upper-quadrant/epigastric pain, tenderness or Murphy sign, nausea/vomiting and fever. Pain can radiate to the right shoulder/back. Jaundice, dark urine or pale stools raises concern for common bile duct obstruction or other hepatobiliary disease. Assess vitals, abdominal findings, hydration, pain duration and sepsis signs. Tests may include CBC, liver chemistry, bilirubin, lipase and right-upper-quadrant ultrasound; further imaging depends on the clinical question.

## Management and nursing care
Treatment may include NPO during acute evaluation, IV fluids when indicated, analgesia/antiemetics, antibiotics for suspected infection and early laparoscopic cholecystectomy for appropriate patients. Biliary obstruction/cholangitis or pancreatitis requires its own urgent assessment. Monitor for fever, worsening pain, jaundice, hypotension, confusion or peritoneal signs.

After cholecystectomy, assess wound, pain, nausea, bowel function and signs of bile leak/bleeding/infection. Diet is advanced as tolerated according to orders; some patients need temporary fat-intake adjustments. Teach wound care, activity guidance and warning signs.

## PNLE distinctions
- Persistent RUQ pain with fever supports cholecystitis; jaundice/fever raises concern for bile-duct involvement.
- Murphy sign supports but does not independently establish diagnosis.
- Hypotension, confusion, jaundice with fever or generalized peritonitis needs urgent escalation.
