# Cholecystitis and Biliary Disease

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Mechanism and manifestations
Acute cholecystitis most often follows cystic duct obstruction by a gallstone, causing gallbladder inflammation. Right upper quadrant/epigastric pain after meals, radiation to right shoulder/back, nausea, vomiting, fever and Murphy sign may occur. Jaundice, dark urine, pale stools or severe systemic illness suggest possible common bile duct obstruction/cholangitis.
## Diagnostics
Ultrasound is usually first-line; labs may include CBC, bilirubin, liver enzymes and lipase to assess complications/differentials. HIDA scanning may be used if diagnosis remains unclear. Do not assume all RUQ pain is gallbladder disease.
## Nursing care
Assess pain, fever, hydration, jaundice and sepsis signs. NPO during acute evaluation as ordered, IV fluids, analgesia/antiemetics and prescribed antibiotics when indicated. Prepare for laparoscopic cholecystectomy or other intervention. Monitor after procedure for bleeding, bile leak, infection and respiratory complications.
## Red flags
Fever plus jaundice and RUQ pain suggests cholangitis; hypotension/confusion are emergency signs. Persistent severe pain, peritonitis or shock requires urgent escalation.
## PNLE traps
A fatty meal may precipitate symptoms but is not diagnostic. Charcot triad suggests ascending cholangitis; shock/confusion indicate severe disease.
