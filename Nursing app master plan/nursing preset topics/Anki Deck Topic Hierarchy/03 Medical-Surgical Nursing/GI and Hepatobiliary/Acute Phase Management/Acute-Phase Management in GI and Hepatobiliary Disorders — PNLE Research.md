# Acute-Phase Management in GI and Hepatobiliary Disorders

**Status:** Substantive research draft. Source-to-claim and Philippine-context verification pending. Reviewed 2026-10-17.

## Core concepts, assessment, management and nursing care
Assess ABCs, vital signs, pain onset/location, guarding/rigidity, vomiting, bleeding, hydration, urine output, pregnancy possibility and anticoagulant/NSAID use. Red flags include shock, rigid abdomen, sudden severe pain, hematemesis/melena with instability, persistent vomiting/dehydration, jaundice with fever/right-upper-quadrant pain, or altered mental status in liver disease. Escalate promptly; prepare access, labs/imaging and NPO status when urgent procedure/surgery is suspected, following orders. Reassess after analgesia/antiemetics and monitor aspiration and electrolyte risk. Avoid laxatives/enemas or oral intake when obstruction/perforation is suspected unless directed. NSAIDs may worsen ulcers, bleeding and kidney injury. Sudden pain change can indicate perforation or ischemia. Sources: Merck Manual acute abdomen https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/gastrointestinal-disorders/acute-abdomen-and-surgical-gastroenterology/acute-abdominal-pain ; NICE upper GI bleeding https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg141

## PNLE priorities and safety
Prioritize immediate threats, reassess after interventions, use trends and patient presentation rather than isolated findings, and follow current orders, local protocols and nursing scope.
