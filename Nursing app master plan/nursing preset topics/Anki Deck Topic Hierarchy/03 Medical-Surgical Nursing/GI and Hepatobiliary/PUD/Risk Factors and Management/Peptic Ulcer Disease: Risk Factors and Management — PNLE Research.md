# Peptic Ulcer Disease: Risk Factors and Management

**Status:** Substantive research draft. Source-to-claim and Philippine-context verification pending. Reviewed 2026-10-17.

## Core concepts, assessment, management and nursing care
Peptic ulcer disease is a gastric or duodenal mucosal break commonly associated with H. pylori infection or NSAID exposure. Symptoms may include epigastric pain, nausea or dyspepsia but cannot reliably distinguish gastric from duodenal ulcers. Diagnosis uses H. pylori testing and/or endoscopy as indicated; PPIs, antibiotics and bismuth can affect test accuracy, so follow test-specific instructions. Treat H. pylori with current locally appropriate eradication therapy and confirm eradication afterward. Avoid unprescribed NSAIDs; if essential, the prescriber should assess alternatives and gastroprotection. Complete the regimen. Hematemesis, melena with syncope/weakness, sudden severe pain/rigidity or shock suggests bleeding/perforation and requires emergency care. Do not independently stop prescribed anticoagulants/antiplatelets; urgent clinician review is needed. Sources: ACG guideline resources https://gi.org/guidelines/ ; NICE dyspepsia/PUD https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg184

## PNLE priorities and safety
Prioritize immediate threats, reassess after interventions, use trends and patient presentation rather than isolated findings, and follow current orders, local protocols and nursing scope.
