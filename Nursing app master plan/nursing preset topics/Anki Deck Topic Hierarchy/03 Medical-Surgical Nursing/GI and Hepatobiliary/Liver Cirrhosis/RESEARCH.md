# Liver Cirrhosis: Assessment and Nursing Management

## Pathophysiology
Cirrhosis is advanced hepatic fibrosis with regenerative nodules and distorted architecture, impairing hepatic function and portal blood flow. Causes include chronic viral hepatitis, alcohol-associated disease, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease and other chronic liver disorders. Complications arise from portal hypertension, reduced synthetic function and altered metabolism.

## Assessment and diagnostics
Findings can include fatigue, anorexia, jaundice, ascites, peripheral edema, muscle wasting, bruising and pruritus. Decompensation includes ascites, variceal bleeding, hepatic encephalopathy or jaundice. Assess mental status, abdominal girth/weight, edema, bleeding, medication exposure and infection signs. Tests may include CBC, liver enzymes, bilirubin, albumin, INR/PT, renal function, sodium, ultrasound and endoscopy for varices. Aminotransferase values alone do not describe functional severity.

## Major complications and nursing priorities
- **Variceal bleeding:** hematemesis/melena, tachycardia, hypotension or syncope requires emergency response, airway/aspiration precautions and urgent resuscitation/endoscopic care.
- **Ascites:** trend weight/girth, respiratory comfort, renal function and sodium; diuretics/paracentesis are clinician-directed. Fever, abdominal tenderness or confusion can signal spontaneous bacterial peritonitis.
- **Hepatic encephalopathy:** assess confusion, sleep changes, asterixis and consciousness. Evaluate triggers such as infection, GI bleeding, constipation, dehydration, electrolyte changes or sedatives. Lactulose/rifaximin may be prescribed; monitor per treatment plan.
- **Renal dysfunction/hypotension:** monitor urine output, BP and creatinine; avoid unreviewed nephrotoxic medicines and NSAIDs in many patients with decompensated cirrhosis.

## Education
Avoid alcohol, follow individualized sodium/fluid guidance, adhere to prescribed therapies and surveillance, and consult clinicians before OTC/herbal medicines. Teach warning signs: blood in vomit/stool, increasing confusion, fever/abdominal pain, rapid abdominal growth, reduced urine output or dyspnea.

## PNLE distinctions
- New confusion may be encephalopathy; assess precipitants and other urgent causes.
- Ascites with fever/abdominal pain requires assessment for infection.
- GI bleeding with altered consciousness is an ABC emergency.
