# Appendicitis

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Pathophysiology and findings
Appendicitis usually follows luminal obstruction with inflammation and risk of ischemia/perforation. Typical pain begins periumbilically and migrates to right lower quadrant, with anorexia, nausea, low-grade fever and localized tenderness; presentations can be atypical, especially in pregnancy, children and older adults.
## Assessment and diagnostics
Assess pain migration, guarding/rebound, fever, vomiting, bowel function and hemodynamic status. CBC/CRP and ultrasound or CT may support diagnosis; pregnancy changes imaging choices. Perforation can cause generalized peritonitis and sepsis.
## Nursing priorities
Keep NPO when surgery is possible, establish access, monitor vitals/pain, administer prescribed fluids/analgesia/antibiotics and prepare for surgical evaluation. Do not apply heat, give laxatives/enemas or repeatedly palpate the abdomen. Sudden pain relief followed by generalized pain, rigidity, fever or shock may indicate perforation.
## PNLE traps
Avoid laxatives/enemas and heat because they may worsen risk/delay treatment. Atypical pain does not exclude appendicitis. Peritonitis/shock outrank routine pain control.
