# Appendicitis: Assessment and Perioperative Nursing

## Pathophysiology
Appendicitis is acute inflammation of the appendix, often associated with luminal obstruction. Rising pressure, impaired venous drainage and bacterial proliferation can progress to ischemia, perforation, abscess or peritonitis.

## Assessment and diagnosis
Pain may begin near the umbilicus and migrate to the right lower quadrant, with anorexia, nausea/vomiting and fever. Presentations can be atypical, particularly in children, older adults and pregnancy. Assess onset/migration of pain, guarding/rebound, fever, bowel/urinary symptoms, pregnancy possibility and vital-sign trends. Diagnostic evaluation may include CBC, urinalysis, pregnancy testing when relevant, and ultrasound or CT based on patient group and local pathway. No isolated sign or normal white count reliably excludes appendicitis.

## Nursing priorities
Suspected appendicitis requires timely evaluation. Keep NPO in anticipation of surgery, establish ordered IV access/fluids, monitor vitals and pain, administer prescribed analgesia/antiemetics/antibiotics, and prepare for imaging/surgical review. Do not apply heat, give laxatives/enemas or encourage oral intake while an acute surgical abdomen is evaluated. Sudden pain relief followed by generalized pain, rigidity, fever or shock can indicate perforation rather than recovery.

## Postoperative care
Monitor airway/recovery, vital signs, pain, wound/drain, bowel function, infection signs and mobility. Encourage mobilization and respiratory hygiene as prescribed. Teach wound care, activity instructions, medicines and warning signs such as fever, increasing pain, persistent vomiting, redness/drainage or abdominal distension.

## PNLE distinctions
- Migratory pain and right-lower-quadrant tenderness are classic but not universal.
- Avoid heat, laxatives/enemas and oral intake during evaluation for suspected acute appendicitis.
- Generalized guarding, rigidity, fever or hypotension can indicate perforation/peritonitis.
