# Acute Pancreatitis

## Pathophysiology and causes
Acute pancreatitis is acute pancreatic inflammation caused by premature enzyme activation and tissue injury. Common causes include gallstones and alcohol; other causes include severe hypertriglyceridemia, selected medicines, ERCP and metabolic conditions. Severity ranges from mild self-limited inflammation to persistent organ failure and necrosis.

## Assessment and diagnostics
Typical pain is severe epigastric pain, often radiating to the back, with nausea/vomiting and abdominal tenderness. Assess vital signs, hydration, urine output, mental status, respiratory status and risk factors. Diagnosis generally requires two of three: characteristic pain, amylase/lipase above the diagnostic threshold (often ≥3 times the upper limit of normal), or characteristic imaging. Lipase is generally preferred; enzyme level does not alone determine severity. Tests may include CBC, electrolytes, renal function, glucose, liver tests, triglycerides and ultrasound. CT is selected when diagnosis/severity/complications require it, not automatically for every uncomplicated case.

## Treatment and nursing care
Early care includes individualized IV crystalloid resuscitation with frequent reassessment, analgesia, antiemetics, early oral/enteral nutrition as tolerated, oxygen when indicated and cause-directed treatment. Avoid excessive fluids in heart/kidney impairment or overload. Antibiotics are not routine for sterile pancreatitis; use for a defined infection indication. ERCP is reserved for selected biliary cases (e.g., cholangitis/ongoing obstruction), not every gallstone pancreatitis case.

Monitor pain, vitals, fluid balance, renal function, oxygenation, glucose, electrolytes and signs of systemic inflammation or organ failure. Watch for hypotension, oliguria, hypoxemia, confusion, fever and abdominal distension. Teach alcohol abstinence where relevant and management/follow-up for gallstones or hypertriglyceridemia.

## PNLE distinctions
- Diagnosis requires clinical, biochemical and/or imaging criteria together—not enzyme level alone.
- Antibiotics do not treat sterile inflammation routinely.
- Monitor for shock, respiratory failure, acute kidney injury and infected necrosis.
- Fluid resuscitation is individualized and repeatedly reassessed.
