# Acute Pancreatitis

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Pathophysiology and causes
Acute pancreatitis is acute pancreatic inflammation, commonly due to gallstones or alcohol; hypertriglyceridemia, medicines and procedures are other causes. Activated enzymes cause local injury and systemic inflammatory response.
## Findings and diagnosis
Severe persistent epigastric pain radiating to the back, nausea/vomiting and abdominal tenderness are common. Diagnosis generally requires two of three: characteristic pain, lipase/amylase >3 times upper limit, or characteristic imaging. Lipase is generally preferred. Assess vitals, oxygenation, urine output, BUN/creatinine, hematocrit, glucose, calcium, liver tests and systemic complications.
## Nursing care and treatment
Early supportive care includes appropriately titrated IV fluids (often balanced crystalloid), analgesia, antiemetics, monitoring and early oral/enteral feeding as tolerated rather than prolonged routine fasting in mild disease. Treat gallstone obstruction/cholangitis or other cause. Antibiotics are not routine for sterile pancreatitis; use for confirmed/suspected infection as directed.
## Red flags
Hypotension, hypoxemia, oliguria, confusion, fever/sepsis or worsening organ dysfunction may indicate severe disease. Monitor for ARDS, shock, acute kidney injury, necrosis and hypocalcemia.
## PNLE traps
Do not give routine prophylactic antibiotics for sterile pancreatitis. Lipase elevation alone is not enough without clinical context. Avoid unmonitored aggressive fluids in heart/renal failure; reassess frequently.
