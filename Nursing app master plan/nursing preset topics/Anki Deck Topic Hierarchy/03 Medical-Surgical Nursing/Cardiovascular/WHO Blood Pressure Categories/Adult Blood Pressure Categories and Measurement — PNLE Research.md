# Adult Blood Pressure Categories and Measurement

**Status:** Researched draft; independent source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Adult classification (2025 AHA/ACC framework)
| Category | Systolic | Diastolic |
|---|---:|---:|
| Normal | <120 mm Hg | and <80 |
| Elevated | 120–129 | and <80 |
| Stage 1 hypertension | 130–139 | or 80–89 |
| Stage 2 hypertension | ≥140 | or ≥90 |
When systolic and diastolic values fall into different categories, use the higher category. This is the US AHA/ACC framework; do not label it as a WHO or Philippine classification without checking the specific standard required by an exam or local guidance.
## Accurate measurement
Use validated equipment and a correct cuff on a bare arm; rest quietly, support back/feet/arm, keep arm at heart level, avoid talking, and repeat readings. Diagnosis generally uses averages of careful readings on more than one occasion; home/ambulatory monitoring may confirm. Pain, anxiety, recent activity, caffeine/nicotine and wrong cuff size can distort readings.
## Emergency distinction
A very high number alone does not define hypertensive emergency; acute target-organ injury (e.g., encephalopathy, stroke, ACS, pulmonary edema, aortic dissection or acute kidney injury) is central. Severe BP with chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic deficit, confusion or vision change requires emergency assessment. Do not rapidly lower BP without a clinical plan.
## Education and traps
Teach correct home monitoring, adherence, activity, healthy weight, dietary sodium reduction and follow-up. Potassium advice needs caution in CKD or with medicines that raise potassium. Stage 1/2 uses SBP OR DBP thresholds; one isolated reading is not enough for routine diagnosis.
