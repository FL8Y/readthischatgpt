# Blood Pressure Categories: WHO Guidance and Clinical Interpretation

## Measurement
BP varies with technique, pain, anxiety, activity, medication, volume status and cuff size. Use a validated device and correct cuff; allow rest where clinically feasible, position appropriately, support the arm at heart level and repeat readings. Do not delay emergency assessment when the patient is unstable.

## WHO threshold versus other classification systems
WHO defines hypertension in adults as BP **140/90 mmHg or higher**, diagnosed when systolic pressure is ≥140 and/or diastolic pressure is ≥90 on measurements taken on two different days. This is a diagnostic threshold, not a complete multi-level category chart. Other professional societies may use different labels or lower thresholds; a question specifically asking for WHO guidance should not automatically be answered using another society's staging.

Severely elevated BP with acute neurologic deficits, chest pain, pulmonary edema, aortic-type pain, visual change, seizure or acute kidney injury may be a hypertensive emergency. Urgency is driven by suspected acute target-organ injury—not a number alone. An asymptomatic high reading still requires correct remeasurement and follow-up, but does not automatically call for rapid IV reduction.

## Nursing response
Verify technique, symptoms, baseline and medicine history. Assess neurologic status, chest/back pain, breathing, vision, renal symptoms and pregnancy-related risk as relevant. Escalate concerning symptoms immediately; avoid rapid unsupervised BP lowering because it can impair organ perfusion. Teach home BP technique, medication adherence and follow-up; reinforce individualized salt/activity/weight/tobacco guidance.

## PNLE distinctions
- Do not confuse the diagnostic threshold with an individualized treatment target.
- WHO criteria are not identical to every national professional society's stages.
- Hypertensive emergency requires concern for acute target-organ injury.
- One elevated measurement in a stable asymptomatic person is not sufficient for routine diagnosis.
