# Peripheral Vascular Disease: Arterial vs Venous Problems

## Arterial insufficiency (PAD)
Peripheral artery disease commonly results from atherosclerosis and reduced limb perfusion. Risk factors include tobacco use, diabetes, dyslipidemia, hypertension, older age and established vascular disease. Intermittent claudication is exertional leg discomfort relieved by rest; advanced disease can cause rest pain, ulcers, gangrene and chronic limb-threatening ischemia. Feet may be cool, pale, hairless and have diminished pulses. An ankle-brachial index (ABI) ≤0.90 supports lower-extremity PAD; interpretation can be affected by noncompressible calcified arteries, especially in diabetes/CKD.

Nursing care includes pulse/skin/sensation assessment, foot protection, smoking cessation support, prescribed antiplatelet/statin/risk-factor management and structured exercise. Avoid direct heat and injury to an ischemic limb. Sudden severe pain, pallor, pulselessness, paresthesia, paralysis or coldness can indicate acute limb ischemia and is a vascular emergency.

## Venous disease
Chronic venous insufficiency causes venous hypertension, edema, heaviness, varicosities and often shallow irregular ulcers near the medial ankle with surrounding skin changes. Elevation, mobility, skin care and clinician-directed compression may help after arterial supply is assessed. Do not apply compression blindly when severe arterial disease is suspected.

Deep-vein thrombosis (DVT) may present with unilateral swelling, warmth, pain or tenderness, but symptoms are nonspecific and some cases are silent. Sudden dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, syncope or hypoxemia may indicate pulmonary embolism and requires emergency response. Diagnosis requires clinical evaluation and testing; do not massage a suspected DVT.

## Nursing assessment and education
Compare both limbs, temperature, color, edema, wounds, pulses, sensation and pain. Ask about walking-related symptoms, rest pain, immobility, surgery, cancer, pregnancy/postpartum status and prior thrombosis. Give anticoagulants only as ordered and monitor bleeding. Reinforce movement/VTE prevention as appropriate to patient condition and orders.

## PNLE comparison
| Feature | Arterial disease | Venous disease |
|---|---|---|
| Pain | Claudication/rest pain | Aching/heaviness, often worse with standing |
| Skin/temp | Cool, pale, shiny, reduced hair | Warm, edema, stasis discoloration |
| Pulses | May be reduced | Usually present unless mixed disease |
| Ulcer | Distal toes/pressure points, often painful | Gaiter/medial ankle, shallow/irregular |
| Position | Dependent position may relieve severe ischemic rest pain | Elevation often reduces edema |
