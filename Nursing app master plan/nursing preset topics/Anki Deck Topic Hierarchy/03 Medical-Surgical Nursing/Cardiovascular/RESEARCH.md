# Cardiovascular Nursing: Core Assessment and Clinical Priorities

> Purpose: PNLE-focused synthesis for cardiovascular assessment and safe prioritization. This is educational guidance; follow current orders, institutional protocols, and the patient's presentation.

## Foundations and pathophysiology
Cardiovascular nursing focuses on cardiac output, tissue perfusion, vascular resistance, coronary blood flow, and fluid balance. Cardiac output equals heart rate multiplied by stroke volume. Perfusion may deteriorate because of pump failure, low circulating volume, vascular obstruction, dysrhythmia, or altered vascular tone. A normal blood pressure does not exclude early shock or acute coronary syndrome.

## Focused assessment
Start with immediate threats: airway and breathing, mental status, skin temperature/color, pulse quality, capillary refill, blood pressure trend, oxygen saturation, chest discomfort, and urine output when available. Assess pain onset, location, quality, radiation, duration, associated dyspnea/diaphoresis/nausea, precipitating factors, and prior cardiac history. Compare both sides when assessing pulses or suspected vascular compromise. New confusion, syncope, cyanosis, severe dyspnea, hypotension, chest pain, or rapidly changing rhythm requires urgent reassessment and escalation.

A 12-lead ECG is an early key test for suspected acute coronary syndrome; cardiac troponin is interpreted serially with symptoms, timing, ECG and the local assay's reference limit. Other testing may include CBC, electrolytes, renal function, glucose, chest imaging, echocardiography and lipid testing according to the clinical question. Laboratory reference ranges and ECG interpretation depend on the laboratory, equipment, clinical context and prior results.

## Common nursing priorities
- **Chest pain or suspected ACS:** activate the facility's emergency pathway, obtain ECG promptly, assess vital signs and contraindications, establish monitoring/access as indicated, and prepare for time-critical treatment. Do not delay escalation while completing a lengthy history.
- **Acute pulmonary edema:** sit upright if tolerated, rapidly assess oxygenation/work of breathing, summon urgent assistance, monitor ECG and vital signs, and prepare prescribed oxygen/ventilatory support and treatment.
- **Unstable dysrhythmia:** distinguish a pulse-present patient from cardiac arrest; begin CPR/AED pathway for cardiac arrest and urgent rhythm-specific care for instability.
- **Suspected shock:** look for impaired perfusion rather than relying on one blood-pressure reading. Identify bleeding, infection, cardiac failure, anaphylaxis or obstruction and escalate immediately.
- **Peripheral vascular compromise:** compare color, temperature, pain, sensation, movement and pulses. Sudden pain, pallor, paresthesia, paralysis, pulselessness or poikilothermia is an emergency.

## Monitoring, safety and education
Trend symptoms, vital signs, mental status, fluid balance, weight where relevant, medication response and laboratory changes. Reconcile prescribed drugs and allergies; assess risks before administering medicines that alter blood pressure, heart rate, coagulation or volume status. Patient education may include medication adherence, smoking cessation, individualized activity, diet and sodium guidance, BP/lipid/glucose control, and urgent warning signs. Tailor teaching to health literacy, affordability, access to follow-up and local resources.

## PNLE distinctions
- A deteriorating patient with new confusion or respiratory distress takes priority over routine teaching or stable chronic findings.
- Do not assume all chest pain is cardiac, but do not dismiss atypical symptoms, including nausea, dyspnea, fatigue or discomfort without classic crushing pain.
- A single normal ECG or initial troponin does not always exclude ACS; testing is interpreted in the whole clinical timeline.
- Oxygen is used for hypoxemia or respiratory distress according to protocol, not reflexively for every chest-pain patient with normal oxygenation.
