# Myocardial Infarction (MI) — PNLE-Focused Research

**Reviewed:** 17 October 2026. **Scope:** adult acute MI within acute coronary syndrome (ACS). Follow current facility protocols and authorized orders; this note is educational, not a substitute for emergency care.

## Definition and pathophysiology
MI is myocardial necrosis caused by acute ischemia. Type 1 MI most often follows atherosclerotic plaque disruption with coronary thrombosis. ACS includes unstable angina, NSTEMI, and STEMI. STEMI generally has qualifying ST elevation or an equivalent occlusion pattern and requires urgent reperfusion assessment; NSTEMI has acute myocardial injury plus evidence of ischemia without persistent qualifying ST elevation. Unstable angina does not meet MI biomarker criteria. Elevated troponin alone means myocardial injury, not automatically type 1 MI; interpret rise/fall, symptoms, ECG, renal function, and assay-specific thresholds.

## Risk factors and manifestations
Risk factors include coronary atherosclerosis, hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes, smoking, chronic kidney disease, age, family history, obesity, inactivity, and prior vascular disease. Symptoms may include central pressure/heaviness, pain radiating to arms/jaw/back/neck/upper abdomen, dyspnea, diaphoresis, nausea, pallor, palpitations, fatigue, lightheadedness, or syncope. Presentations can be subtle, especially in older adults and people with diabetes. Women may also have typical chest discomfort; do not dismiss symptoms as atypical.

## Assessment and diagnostics
- Treat suspected ACS as time-critical. Rapidly assess ABCs, mental status, pain onset/character, vital signs, oxygen saturation, perfusion, heart/lung sounds, and heart-failure/shock signs.
- Obtain a 12-lead ECG promptly (commonly within 10 minutes of first medical contact) and repeat if symptoms persist or the first ECG is nondiagnostic. One normal ECG does not exclude ACS.
- Obtain high-sensitivity cardiac troponin at presentation and repeat using the locally validated assay pathway. Use the clinical picture plus rise/fall and evidence of ischemia; do not use one universal threshold for all assays.
- Other tests may include CBC, electrolytes, renal function, glucose, coagulation tests when relevant, lipid profile, chest radiograph for complications/differential diagnosis, echocardiography, and coronary angiography. Do not delay indicated reperfusion for nonessential tests.

## Immediate nursing priorities
1. Activate the emergency/ACS pathway, notify the responsible team, document symptom onset, and expedite ECG and reperfusion evaluation.
2. Rest the patient; monitor rhythm, vital signs, oxygen saturation, pain, perfusion, and trends. Establish IV access and prepare for transfer/procedures per protocol.
3. Rapidly check allergy, active bleeding, blood pressure, anticoagulant use, recent PDE5 inhibitors, prior stroke/TIA, renal function, and planned procedures before medication administration.
4. Watch for recurrent pain, dysrhythmia, hypotension, cool clammy skin, altered mentation, oliguria, worsening dyspnea, or new murmur; escalate immediately.
5. STEMI requires urgent reperfusion assessment. Primary PCI is preferred when available within guideline time targets. Fibrinolysis is only for eligible STEMI when timely PCI is unavailable, after formal contraindication screening and an authorized order; never use fibrinolytics for NSTEMI.

## Medication safety

| Therapy | Nursing implications and contraindication traps |
|---|---|
| Aspirin | Early antiplatelet therapy is recommended for ACS when not contraindicated. Check allergy and active major bleeding; follow loading/maintenance orders and protocol. |
| P2Y12 inhibitor (ticagrelor, prasugrel, clopidogrel) | Often combined with aspirin as DAPT. Check bleeding risk, interactions, procedure plans, and adherence. Prasugrel is contraindicated with prior stroke/TIA; agent choice depends on patient and strategy. |
| Nitroglycerin | Check BP and response. Avoid in hypotension (guideline cautions SBP <90 mm Hg or >30 mm Hg below baseline), suspected right-ventricular infarction, or recent PDE5 inhibitor use. Avoid nitrates within 12 h of avanafil, 24 h of sildenafil/vardenafil, or 48 h of tadalafil. Never let it delay reperfusion. |
| Oxygen | The 2025 ACS guideline recommends oxygen for confirmed hypoxia (SpO₂ <90%) to raise saturation to ≥90%; routine oxygen is not recommended for ACS patients with SpO₂ ≥90% absent another indication. |
| Beta blocker | Early oral therapy may be appropriate if no contraindications. Avoid automatic administration with shock, acute decompensated HF, significant bradycardia, high-grade AV block, or other contraindications. |
| Anticoagulants | Use agent-specific protocols; monitor bleeding, CBC/platelets and renal function as indicated. Verify renal adjustments, timing, and concurrent antithrombotics. |
| High-intensity statin | Recommended for ACS unless contraindicated; review interactions, tolerance, and relevant hepatic/muscle concerns. |
| Opioid analgesia | Reserve for pain resistant to other maximally tolerated anti-ischemic treatment; monitor respiratory status/BP. Opioids can delay oral P2Y12 inhibitor effects. |
| Fibrinolytics | Major bleeding/intracranial hemorrhage risk. Screen using current protocol for prior intracranial hemorrhage, active bleeding, suspected aortic dissection, recent major head trauma/intracranial surgery, and other listed contraindications. |

Avoid non-aspirin NSAIDs for ischemic pain in suspected/known ACS when possible because of cardiovascular risk and lack of benefit for this indication.

## Complications and deterioration
- VT/VF, bradyarrhythmias, or conduction block.
- Acute left ventricular failure/pulmonary edema.
- Cardiogenic shock: hypotension with impaired perfusion, confusion, cool extremities, and reduced urine output.
- Mechanical complications: papillary muscle rupture/acute mitral regurgitation, ventricular septal rupture, or free-wall rupture/tamponade. Sudden deterioration or a new murmur is urgent.
- Recurrent ischemia/reinfarction, pericarditis, ventricular thrombus, and later heart failure.

## Nursing process and PNLE reasoning
**Assessment:** ABCs, hemodynamic stability, symptoms, ECG, SpO₂, perfusion, and medication contraindications.  
**Priority:** immediate threats (airway/respiratory failure, shock, lethal rhythm, ongoing ischemia) outrank routine comfort, education, and documentation. Use Maslow and the nursing process as reasoning frameworks, not mechanical rules that override acute threats.  
**Implementation:** minimize ECG/reperfusion delays, monitor continuously as indicated, administer ordered therapy safely, and reassess response.  
**Evaluation:** symptoms and ECG trend, stable hemodynamics/perfusion, no new dysrhythmia or major bleeding, and timely reperfusion when indicated.

### Common PNLE traps
- Do not give oxygen routinely to every normoxic ACS patient.
- A normal first ECG or early negative troponin does not rule out MI.
- Check BP, suspected RV infarction, and PDE5 inhibitor timing before nitrates.
- Morphine is not mandatory for all chest pain and must not delay reperfusion.
- Fibrinolysis is not treatment for NSTEMI.
- Troponin elevation indicates myocardial injury; clinical evidence is needed to classify MI and mechanism.
- ABCs and immediate threats take priority over routine tasks.

## Patient education
Teach medication adherence and bleeding precautions; smoking cessation; individualized heart-healthy diet/activity; BP, diabetes and lipid control; cardiac rehabilitation; follow-up; and a clear emergency action plan. Recurrent chest pressure, dyspnea, diaphoresis, syncope, or stroke symptoms require immediate emergency services, not waiting to see if they resolve. Do not stop antiplatelet therapy after stenting without the treating team's direction.

## Philippine practice context
Follow the facility's current ACS pathway, local referral/reperfusion network, formulary, standing orders, and Philippine nursing scope/institutional policy.
