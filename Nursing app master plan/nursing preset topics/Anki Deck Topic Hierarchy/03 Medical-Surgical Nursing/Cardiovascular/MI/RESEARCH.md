# Myocardial Infarction (MI) — PNLE Medical-Surgical Nursing

> Educational reference only. Suspected acute coronary syndrome (ACS) is an emergency; activate the local emergency/ACS pathway.

## Definition and pathophysiology
Myocardial infarction is myocardial necrosis due to ischemia, commonly from atherosclerotic plaque disruption and coronary thrombosis. ACS includes unstable angina, NSTEMI, and STEMI; these terms are not interchangeable. Diagnosis integrates a rise/fall in cardiac troponin with evidence of ischemia. Troponin elevation alone may indicate myocardial injury from causes other than type 1 MI.

## Risk factors
Modifiable: tobacco, hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes, inactivity, and cardiometabolic risks. Other factors include age, family history, chronic kidney disease, and established vascular disease.

## Assessment and recognition
Possible symptoms include chest pressure/discomfort, pain in the arm, shoulder, jaw, back or upper abdomen, dyspnea, diaphoresis, nausea, lightheadedness, palpitations, or unusual fatigue. Atypical or less prominent chest pain does not exclude MI. Rapidly assess symptom onset, vital signs, perfusion, mental status, oxygen saturation, allergies, bleeding risks, and medications including anticoagulants and PDE-5 inhibitors.

Obtain a 12-lead ECG promptly according to the ACS pathway (commonly within 10 minutes of first medical contact); repeat if symptoms continue and the first ECG is nondiagnostic. High-sensitivity cardiac troponin is preferred and should be interpreted using the institution's serial-testing pathway and assay-specific cutoffs. A normal initial ECG or early biomarker does not necessarily exclude ACS.

## Immediate management and nursing priorities
1. Activate emergency response/ACS protocol; reassess ABCs and hemodynamic stability.
2. Obtain ECG, establish monitoring and vascular access as ordered/protocolized, and communicate symptom-onset time.
3. Give aspirin promptly when indicated and no true allergy, active bleeding, or other contraindication exists; follow local protocol.
4. Give oxygen for hypoxemia or respiratory distress as indicated; routine oxygen in a normoxemic patient is not automatically beneficial.
5. Nitroglycerin may be used for ischemic discomfort if prescribed and hemodynamically safe. Avoid with hypotension, suspected right-ventricular infarction/preload dependence, or recent PDE-5 inhibitor exposure (interval depends on the agent).
6. Eligible STEMI requires time-critical reperfusion. PCI versus fibrinolysis depends on timing, availability, bleeding risk, and current protocol. Fibrinolytics require formal contraindication screening.
7. Monitor for ventricular arrhythmias, heart block, acute heart failure/pulmonary edema, cardiogenic shock, recurrent ischemia, mechanical complications, and bleeding.

## Medication safety
- Antiplatelets/anticoagulants: check allergy, bleeding, and patient-specific contraindications.
- Beta blockers may be unsuitable with shock, acute decompensated heart failure, significant bradycardia, high-grade AV block, or severe bronchospasm.
- Fibrinolytic contraindications are high stakes and agent/protocol-specific; use the current checklist.
- “MONA” is not a mandatory sequence: oxygen, nitrates, and morphine are not universally indicated.
Doses are omitted because they depend on protocol, patient factors, and local formulary.

## Nursing interventions
Continuously trend rhythm, BP, oxygenation, perfusion, pain, mental status, and response to interventions. Document onset, ECG times, medications, reassessment, escalation, and response. Prepare for emergency defibrillation/resuscitation if indicated. After stabilization, support medication adherence, risk-factor reduction, smoking cessation, cardiac rehabilitation, and clear instructions for recurrent symptoms.

## PNLE priorities and traps
- Unstable ABCs, shock, pulmonary edema, altered consciousness, or malignant arrhythmia outrank routine comfort measures and teaching.
- Do not exclude MI because symptoms are atypical or initial ECG is normal.
- Interpret troponin in clinical context and serial trend.
- Do not give nitrates without checking BP and PDE-5 inhibitor exposure.
- Do not delay reperfusion decisions for nonessential tests.
- Use Maslow as a reasoning aid, not a rule that overrides immediate life threats.

## Philippine practice
Follow current facility ACS, referral, and emergency transport pathways and applicable Philippine DOH guidance. Local capacity affects transfer and reperfusion planning. Nurses must work within authorized scope, verify orders, document accurately, and promptly escalate deterioration.
