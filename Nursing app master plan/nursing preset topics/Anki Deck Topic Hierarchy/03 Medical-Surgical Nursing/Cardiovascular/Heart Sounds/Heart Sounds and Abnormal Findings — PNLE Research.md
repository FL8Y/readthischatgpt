# Heart Sounds and Abnormal Findings

**Status:** Researched draft; independent source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Normal sounds
S1 is mainly mitral/tricuspid closure at systole onset; S2 is aortic/pulmonic closure at systole end. S3 occurs during early diastolic filling; it may be normal in children/young adults or pregnancy, but in older adults with dyspnea may suggest volume overload/ventricular dysfunction. S4 reflects atrial contraction into a stiff ventricle and is generally absent in atrial fibrillation.
## Abnormal findings and red flags
A murmur suggests turbulent flow from stenosis, regurgitation or abnormal flow. A scratchy friction rub may occur with pericarditis. A new murmur after MI with sudden dyspnea/shock may indicate acute mitral regurgitation or ventricular septal rupture. Muffled sounds with hypotension and JVD may suggest tamponade.
## Assessment
Auscultate systematically at aortic (right 2nd intercostal space), pulmonic (left 2nd), Erb point (left 3rd), tricuspid (left lower sternal border) and mitral/apical (usually 5th intercostal space, midclavicular line). Correlate with pulse, BP, respiratory status, edema, JVP and symptoms.
## Priority
New chest pain, hypotension, syncope, acute dyspnea or poor perfusion requires immediate ABC assessment and escalation. Do not diagnose structural disease from one sound alone; echo and clinical assessment may be needed.
## PNLE traps
S3 is not always pathologic in younger people; S4 requires atrial contraction. New murmur plus shock after MI is a mechanical-complication emergency.
