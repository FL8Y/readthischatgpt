# ECG Leads: Placement and Territories

**Status:** Researched draft; independent source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## What a 12-lead ECG shows
Ten electrodes generate 12 electrical views: limb leads I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF and chest leads V1–V6. It records electrical activity, not direct mechanical pumping or definitive coronary anatomy.
## Chest electrode landmarks
- V1: fourth intercostal space, right sternal border.
- V2: fourth intercostal space, left sternal border.
- V4: fifth intercostal space, midclavicular line.
- V3: midway between V2 and V4.
- V5: level with V4 at anterior axillary line.
- V6: level with V4 at midaxillary line.
Accurate placement reduces false patterns; follow local policy and document adaptations.
## Territory clues
Septal V1–V2; anterior V3–V4; lateral I/aVL/V5–V6; inferior II/III/aVF. Posterior infarction may show ST depression in V1–V3 and require V7–V9; right-sided leads may be needed if RV infarction is suspected.
## Nursing workflow
Obtain ECG promptly for suspected ACS (commonly within 10 minutes of first medical contact), verify patient identity, reduce artifact, document symptom/time, and urgently report concerning changes. Repeat if symptoms persist/change despite a nondiagnostic initial tracing.
## PNLE traps
Do not delay ECG for laboratory results. Automated interpretation is not definitive. One normal ECG does not rule out ACS. ST elevation criteria depend on lead and context; use current protocol and clinician interpretation.
