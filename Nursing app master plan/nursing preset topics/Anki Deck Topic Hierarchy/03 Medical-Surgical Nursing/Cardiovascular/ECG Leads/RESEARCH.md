# ECG Leads: Practical Nursing Interpretation

## What the leads show
A standard 12-lead ECG uses 10 electrodes to create 12 views of cardiac electrical activity. Limb leads I, II and III are bipolar; aVR, aVL and aVF are augmented limb leads; V1–V6 are precordial leads. The ECG records electrical activity—not mechanical pumping or perfusion directly—so correlate it with pulse, symptoms, BP and consciousness.

## Approximate territories
| Leads | Commonly viewed area | Typical artery association (variable) |
|---|---|---|
| V1–V2 | Septal/anterior-septal | LAD/septal branches |
| V3–V4 | Anterior | LAD |
| I, aVL, V5–V6 | Lateral | LCx or diagonal branches |
| II, III, aVF | Inferior | RCA commonly; LCx in some patients |
| V7–V9 | Posterior views when indicated | Circumflex or RCA branches depending on anatomy |
| V3R–V4R | Right ventricle when indicated | Often RCA in right-ventricular MI |

These are teaching patterns, not a replacement for expert interpretation; coronary dominance and branch anatomy vary. ST-segment elevation/depression, T-wave changes and new conduction abnormalities must be interpreted using formal criteria and clinical context.

## Correct acquisition
Verify patient identity, explain briefly, prepare/clean/dry skin, place electrodes correctly, minimize movement and artifact, and label the tracing with date/time and symptoms. Misplaced precordial leads can create misleading patterns. Repeat a tracing if artifact or placement error is suspected, but do not delay emergency escalation in an unstable patient.

## Nursing responsibilities
For chest pain or possible ACS, obtain an ECG promptly according to the emergency pathway, compare with prior tracings, report concerning changes, and ensure continuous monitoring when indicated. A rhythm strip or monitor alarm does not replace a 12-lead ECG. If an ECG displays a dangerous rhythm, immediately assess the patient and pulse—treat the patient, not just the monitor. Document symptoms, onset, ECG timing and communication.

## PNLE traps
- A 12-lead ECG has 12 electrical views but typically 10 electrodes.
- Electrical rhythm does not prove adequate cardiac output; assess a pulse and perfusion.
- One normal ECG does not rule out ACS.
- Lead placement error can imitate pathology; check placement when the pattern conflicts with clinical findings.
