# Pericardial Effusion and Cardiac Tamponade

## Definitions and mechanism
Pericardial effusion is excess fluid in the pericardial sac. Causes include malignancy, infection/inflammation, renal disease, trauma, post-cardiac injury and selected medicines. Cardiac tamponade occurs when intrapericardial pressure limits diastolic filling, reducing stroke volume and cardiac output. Rate of accumulation matters: a relatively small rapid bleed may be more dangerous than a larger slowly accumulating effusion.

## Recognition
Symptoms may include dyspnea, chest discomfort, tachycardia and fatigue. Tamponade can cause hypotension, tachycardia, distended neck veins, muffled heart sounds, pulsus paradoxus, narrow pulse pressure, oliguria and shock. Beck's triad (hypotension, distended neck veins, muffled heart sounds) is helpful but may be incomplete. Trauma or a recent cardiac procedure followed by sudden deterioration raises concern.

## Diagnostics and management
Urgent echocardiography assesses effusion and hemodynamic effects; ECG and additional tests investigate cause. Tamponade is a clinical emergency supported by imaging, not an imaging finding alone. Hemodynamically significant tamponade generally requires urgent drainage by the appropriate specialist team (pericardiocentesis or surgical drainage) and cause-directed management. Do not delay escalation for a complete diagnostic workup in an unstable patient.

## Nursing priorities
- Assess ABCs, BP trends, pulse, neck veins, mental status, urine output and respiratory distress.
- Activate emergency response for suspected tamponade/shock; prepare for urgent bedside evaluation and possible drainage.
- Monitor ECG, perfusion and post-procedure bleeding/recurrence according to protocol.
- Explain procedures and provide support without delaying life-saving intervention.
- Document onset, findings, deterioration and team notification.

## PNLE distinctions
- Effusion is fluid accumulation; tamponade is impaired filling with hemodynamic compromise.
- Beck's triad may be incomplete; absence does not exclude tamponade.
- Rapid fluid accumulation can cause tamponade with a relatively small volume.
- New hypotension plus distended neck veins and muffled heart sounds requires urgent response.
