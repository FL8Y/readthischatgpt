# Pericardial Effusion and Cardiac Tamponade

**Status:** Researched draft; independent source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Definitions and mechanism
Pericardial effusion is excess fluid in the pericardial space. Tamponade occurs when intrapericardial pressure impairs filling, reducing stroke volume/cardiac output. Rate of accumulation matters: rapidly accumulating smaller effusions can cause tamponade.
## Causes and manifestations
Causes include malignancy, infection/inflammation, uremia, trauma, post-cardiac procedure complications and post-MI mechanical complications. Findings can include dyspnea, tachycardia, hypotension, JVD, muffled sounds, pulsus paradoxus, cool skin, confusion and oliguria. Beck triad (hypotension, JVD, muffled sounds) is classic but not always present.
## Diagnostics
Echocardiography is the key bedside test. ECG may show low voltage or electrical alternans with large effusion; chest radiography cannot rule out acute tamponade. Do not wait for the full triad if the patient is deteriorating.
## Emergency nursing actions
Assess ABCs/perfusion, monitor continuously, establish access, notify emergency/cardiology team, and prepare for urgent echo and drainage (pericardiocentesis or surgery). Support oxygenation if hypoxemic and follow resuscitation protocols. Fluids/vasopressors may be a bridge under expert direction, not definitive treatment.
## PNLE traps
Effusion is not synonymous with tamponade. Absence of Beck triad does not exclude it. Sudden hypotension/JVD after a cardiac procedure or MI requires urgent escalation.
