# Coronary Arteries and Myocardial Perfusion

## Anatomy and function
The coronary arteries arise from the aortic root and supply the myocardium. The left main coronary artery commonly divides into the left anterior descending (LAD) and left circumflex (LCx) arteries; the right coronary artery (RCA) supplies much of the right heart and, in many people, the inferior wall and AV node. Coronary dominance depends on which artery gives rise to the posterior descending artery and varies between individuals, so ECG territory patterns are useful clues rather than absolute anatomical rules.

## Ischemia and infarction
Myocardial oxygen supply can become inadequate because of atherosclerotic stenosis, acute thrombus, vasospasm, embolism, or an imbalance between oxygen supply and demand (for example, severe anemia, tachyarrhythmia or shock). Plaque rupture/erosion with thrombosis is a common mechanism of type 1 MI. Persistent ischemia can cause infarction and loss of contractile tissue.

## Assessment and diagnostic clues
Assess chest discomfort, dyspnea, diaphoresis, nausea, fatigue, syncope, vital signs and perfusion. A 12-lead ECG helps localize ischemia; troponin and clinical evidence help diagnose myocardial injury/infarction. Approximate ECG associations include anterior/septal changes with LAD, lateral changes with LCx or diagonal branches, and inferior changes with RCA or LCx depending on dominance. Posterior and right-ventricular infarction may require additional leads when suspected. A normal initial ECG does not exclude ACS; correlate serial ECGs, symptoms and biomarkers.

## Nursing priorities
Suspected ACS requires urgent escalation, prompt ECG, monitoring and preparation for time-critical reperfusion as ordered. Watch for dysrhythmias, AV block, acute heart failure and shock. Report inferior MI with hypotension/bradycardia or suspected right-ventricular involvement because preload-sensitive physiology can affect nitrate/fluid decisions; medications must follow clinician assessment and protocol. Prevent avoidable delays and accurately communicate symptom onset, ECG times, medication exposure and changes in status.

## PNLE high yield
- Coronary territory patterns are approximate; anatomy varies.
- Coronary ischemia may present without classic chest pain.
- Inferior MI may involve bradycardia/AV-node effects, especially with RCA involvement.
- ECG localization helps guide care but does not replace clinical interpretation and biomarkers.
