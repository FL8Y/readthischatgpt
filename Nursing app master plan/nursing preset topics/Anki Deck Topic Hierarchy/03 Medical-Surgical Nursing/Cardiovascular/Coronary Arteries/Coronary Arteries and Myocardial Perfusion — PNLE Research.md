# Coronary Arteries and Myocardial Perfusion

**Status:** Researched draft; independent source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Anatomy and perfusion
The right and left coronary arteries arise from the aortic root. The left main usually divides into the left anterior descending (LAD) and circumflex (LCx). LAD commonly supplies anterior wall, anterior septum and apex; LCx commonly supplies lateral myocardium; RCA commonly supplies the right ventricle and inferior wall and often the AV node in right-dominant circulation. Coronary dominance is defined by the artery giving rise to the posterior descending artery and varies among patients.
## Clinical application
Plaque rupture with thrombosis can abruptly reduce perfusion and cause ACS/MI. ECG territory clues are approximate, not proof of a culprit vessel.
| Territory | Leads | Common vessel (variable) |
|---|---|---|
| Septal/anterior | V1–V4 | LAD |
| Lateral | I, aVL, V5–V6 | LCx/diagonal |
| Inferior | II, III, aVF | RCA more often; sometimes LCx |
| Posterior | ST depression/tall R in V1–V3; confirm with V7–V9 | RCA or LCx |
## Nursing priorities
For chest discomfort, diaphoresis, dyspnea, syncope or shock, assess ABCs/perfusion, obtain prompt ECG, activate ACS pathway, and avoid delays. Serial ECG/troponin may be required. Escalate persistent pain, new ST changes, hypotension, pulmonary edema or malignant dysrhythmia.
## PNLE traps
Inferior MI may involve the right ventricle; nitrates may worsen hypotension in suspected RV infarction. A normal first ECG does not exclude ACS. Prioritize immediate threats over routine teaching.
