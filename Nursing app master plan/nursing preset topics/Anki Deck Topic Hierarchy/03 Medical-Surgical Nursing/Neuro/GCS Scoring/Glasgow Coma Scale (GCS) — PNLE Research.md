# Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS)

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Components and scoring
GCS measures eye opening (E 1–4), verbal response (V 1–5) and motor response (M 1–6); total 3–15. Record components separately (e.g., E3 V4 M6) rather than total alone. If a component cannot be tested because of intubation, sedation, facial trauma or language barrier, document the limitation and local notation; do not assign an invented normal score.
## Interpretation
A declining score or new motor asymmetry can signal worsening brain injury. A GCS ≤8 is commonly associated with severe impairment and need for airway assessment, but airway decisions depend on the whole clinical picture, protective reflexes and trajectory.
## Nursing assessment
Assess serially using consistent technique; note pupils, limb movement, speech, oxygenation, glucose, medications/sedatives and baseline. Escalate a fall in score, new confusion, unequal pupils, seizure, vomiting or abnormal respiratory pattern immediately.
## PNLE traps
GCS is not a diagnosis and does not replace ABCs. Trend and components matter more than one isolated total. Intubation/sedation can confound the score.
