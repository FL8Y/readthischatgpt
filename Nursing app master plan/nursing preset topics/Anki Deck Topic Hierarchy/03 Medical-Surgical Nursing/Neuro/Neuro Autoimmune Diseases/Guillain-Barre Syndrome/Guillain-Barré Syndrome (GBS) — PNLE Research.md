# Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS)

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Pathophysiology and manifestations
GBS is an acute immune-mediated peripheral neuropathy, often following infection, with progressive symmetric weakness and reduced/absent reflexes. It may progress over hours/days to respiratory muscle failure and autonomic instability.
## Assessment and priorities
Monitor respiratory mechanics (e.g., vital capacity per protocol), cough/swallow, limb strength, cranial nerves, ECG, BP and heart rate. Do not rely on oxygen saturation alone to detect early ventilatory failure. Escalate bulbar weakness, weak cough, rapid progression, dyspnea or autonomic swings; ICU monitoring may be required.
## Treatment and nursing
IV immunoglobulin or plasma exchange are disease-modifying therapies; corticosteroids alone are not effective treatment for typical GBS. Provide respiratory support, DVT prevention, pain control, skin care, nutrition, bowel/bladder care and rehabilitation.
## PNLE traps
Ascending weakness with areflexia and recent infection suggests GBS, but progression/respiratory risk is the priority. Autonomic dysfunction can cause dangerous BP/heart-rate fluctuations.
