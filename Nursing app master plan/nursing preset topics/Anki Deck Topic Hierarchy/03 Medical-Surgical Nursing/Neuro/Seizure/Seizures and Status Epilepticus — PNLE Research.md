# Seizures and Status Epilepticus

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Types and assessment
Seizures may be focal or generalized and can affect awareness, movement, sensation or behavior. Assess onset, duration, movements, awareness, recovery, injury, fever, glucose, medication adherence and potential triggers. Protect privacy and safety.
## During a convulsive seizure
Stay with the patient, time the seizure, protect from injury, lower to a safe surface if possible, cushion the head, loosen restrictive clothing and turn to the side when feasible to support drainage/airway. Do not restrain, put objects/fingers in the mouth or give oral medication/fluids during the seizure.
## Emergency status
A convulsive seizure lasting 5 minutes or longer, or recurrent seizures without recovery, is status epilepticus and a medical emergency. Activate emergency response, support airway/oxygenation, check glucose, obtain access and administer prescribed benzodiazepine promptly per protocol; prepare second-line antiseizure medication and respiratory monitoring.
## Postictal care
Assess breathing, injuries, glucose and neurologic recovery; reorient calmly and document duration/observations. Escalate first seizure, pregnancy, injury, water-related seizure, breathing difficulty, prolonged seizure or failure to recover.
## Medication safety
Antiseizure medicines should not be stopped abruptly without prescriber direction. Review interactions, pregnancy risks and adverse effects for the specific agent. Some drugs require serum levels or CBC/liver monitoring.
## PNLE traps
Nothing goes in the mouth; do not restrain. Time the event. Five minutes or repeated seizures without recovery is emergency status.
