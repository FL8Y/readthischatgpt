# Alzheimer Disease and Dementia Nursing Care

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Overview
Alzheimer disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder and a common cause of dementia. Dementia impairs cognition and function beyond expected aging; delirium is acute/fluctuating and often reversible cause-related, so new confusion requires urgent assessment.
## Assessment
Assess baseline cognition/function, onset/course, medication burden, sleep, mood, sensory impairment, pain, hydration, infection, metabolic causes and caregiver stress. Use structured tools as appropriate. Screen for delirium when change is acute.
## Nursing interventions
Use consistent routines, simple instructions, orientation cues, hearing/vision aids, safe environment, hydration/nutrition support, mobility and meaningful activities. Identify triggers for agitation and use nonpharmacologic de-escalation first. Avoid restraints where possible and follow safety policy.
## Medication cautions
Cholinesterase inhibitors may cause nausea, bradycardia/syncope and weight loss; monitor pulse, falls and intake. Memantine requires renal dose consideration. Antipsychotics in dementia carry increased mortality risk and should be reserved for specific severe circumstances after risk-benefit review; avoid routine use for ordinary agitation.
## PNLE traps
Sudden confusion is not automatically Alzheimer progression; evaluate delirium, infection, hypoxia, hypoglycemia and medication effects. Prioritize safety and caregiver education.
