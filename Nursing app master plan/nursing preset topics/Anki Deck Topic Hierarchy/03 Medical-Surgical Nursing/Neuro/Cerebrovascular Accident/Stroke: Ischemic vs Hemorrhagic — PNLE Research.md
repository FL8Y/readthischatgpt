# Stroke: Ischemic vs Hemorrhagic

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Recognition and priority
Stroke is acute neurologic dysfunction from cerebral infarction or hemorrhage. Use FAST/BE-FAST for recognition but do not exclude stroke when a screen is negative. Document last-known-well time, symptom discovery time, anticoagulants, baseline function and glucose. Activate stroke pathway immediately.
## Initial assessment
ABCs, glucose, vital signs, focused neurologic exam/NIHSS by trained staff, rapid noncontrast head CT to distinguish hemorrhage from ischemia; CTA/advanced imaging may identify large-vessel occlusion. Do not give food, drink or oral medication until swallowing safety is assessed. Correct hypoglycemia and treat hypoxia; avoid unnecessary delays.
## Treatment distinctions
Eligible acute ischemic stroke may receive IV thrombolytic therapy (including tenecteplase in guideline-eligible patients) and/or endovascular thrombectomy depending on timing, imaging and criteria. The 2026 AHA/ASA guideline updates eligibility and systems of care; use current local stroke protocol. Hemorrhagic stroke requires BP/anticoagulant management and neurosurgical/neurocritical assessment as indicated. Never give thrombolytic until hemorrhage is excluded and contraindications reviewed.
## Nursing care
Frequent neuro checks, BP/glucose/temperature management per protocol, aspiration prevention, swallow screen before oral intake, mobility/skin/VTE prevention, communication support and early rehabilitation. Sudden headache, vomiting, declining consciousness or worsening deficit requires immediate escalation.
## PNLE traps
Record last-known-well, not just arrival time. CT excludes hemorrhage before thrombolytic decisions. Do not give oral intake before swallow assessment. Avoid routine aggressive BP reduction unless protocol/indication requires it.
