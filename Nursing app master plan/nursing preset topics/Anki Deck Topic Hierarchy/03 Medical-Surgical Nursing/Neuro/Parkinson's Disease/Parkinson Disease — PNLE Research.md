# Parkinson Disease

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Pathophysiology and manifestations
Parkinson disease involves dopaminergic neuron loss in the substantia nigra. Core motor features include bradykinesia plus rest tremor and/or rigidity; gait freezing, postural instability, constipation, sleep/mood changes and autonomic symptoms may occur.
## Nursing care
Assess swallowing/aspiration, mobility, falls, orthostatic BP, cognition, constipation and medication timing. Use cueing, assistive devices, exercise/physiotherapy, home safety and nutrition strategies. Refer for swallowing assessment when coughing/choking or wet voice occurs.
## Medication safety
Levodopa/carbidopa timing matters; delayed doses can cause severe immobility and complications. Protein can compete with levodopa absorption in some patients; coordinate protein timing without compromising nutrition. Dopamine agonists can cause sleep attacks, hallucinations, edema and impulse-control disorders. Do not abruptly stop dopaminergic therapy because severe rigidity/fever/autonomic instability can occur.
## Escalation
New fever, severe rigidity, altered mental status and autonomic instability after medication interruption may indicate parkinsonism-hyperpyrexia syndrome, an emergency. Aspiration and falls are major risks.
## PNLE traps
Give time-critical Parkinson medicines on schedule. Do not abruptly discontinue. Assess swallowing and fall risk.
