# Perioperative Nursing: Preoperative, Intraoperative and Postoperative Care

## Phases and goals
Perioperative nursing spans preoperative preparation, intraoperative safety and postoperative recovery. Goals include informed participation, prevention of infection and injury, safe anesthesia/surgery, effective pain control, restoration of physiologic function and early recognition of complications.

## Preoperative responsibilities
Confirm patient identity using approved identifiers, planned procedure/site, allergies, relevant history, medication/reaction history, baseline vital signs, required investigations and completion of the consent process by the authorized practitioner. The nurse may witness/document consent per policy but must not substitute for the proceduralist's explanation. Verify fasting instructions based on anesthetic orders, assess anticoagulants/diabetes medicines and other high-risk drugs against the specific perioperative plan, and ensure required site marking/checklist steps are complete. Do not independently direct patients to stop essential medicines; escalate uncertainty. Prepare skin according to protocol, remove/secure items as required, and verify pregnancy, implants, dentures, prostheses or other risk-specific considerations when relevant.

## Intraoperative safety
Use the surgical safety checklist and team time-out to confirm patient, procedure, site, positioning, antibiotic timing where indicated, anticipated risks and equipment. Maintain aseptic technique, sterile-field integrity, counts and specimen labeling. Prevent pressure/nerve injury, hypothermia, burns, fire, retained items and medication errors. Document counts, implants, specimens, positioning, fluids, blood products and relevant events according to policy.

## Postoperative priorities
Assess airway patency, ventilation, oxygenation, circulation, consciousness, temperature, pain, nausea, wound/dressing, drains and urine output. Early complications include airway obstruction, respiratory depression, hemorrhage, shock, anaphylaxis, dysrhythmia, hypothermia, aspiration and surgical-site problems. Treat immediate ABC threats first, call for help and follow PACU/post-anesthesia criteria. Monitor for opioid/sedative effects and use multimodal pain management when ordered. Encourage pulmonary hygiene, early mobilization, hydration/nutrition and VTE prevention when safe and prescribed.

## Medication and consent safety
Match drug, dose, route, time and patient; clarify allergies and perioperative medication holds. Antibiotic prophylaxis timing and redosing are procedure-specific. Anticoagulant interruption and resumption depend on indication, bleeding risk, renal function and anesthetic plan. Consent requires voluntary informed agreement with capacity and adequate explanation by the responsible clinician, subject to applicable Philippine law and institutional policy.

## Patient teaching and PNLE priorities
Teach deep breathing/coughing or incentive spirometry when ordered, splinting the incision, safe mobilization, wound care, medication use and warning signs (increasing bleeding, fever, shortness of breath, calf swelling/pain or uncontrolled pain). New stridor, decreasing consciousness, rapidly saturating dressing or hemodynamic instability takes priority over routine comfort measures.
