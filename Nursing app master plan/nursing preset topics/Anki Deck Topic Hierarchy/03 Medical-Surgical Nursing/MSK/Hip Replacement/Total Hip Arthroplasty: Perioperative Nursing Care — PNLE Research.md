# Total Hip Arthroplasty: Perioperative Nursing Care

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Purpose and assessment
Total hip arthroplasty treats selected severe joint disease/fracture. Assess baseline mobility, cognition, skin, nutrition, pain, fall risk, anticoagulants, infection risks and home support. Surgical approach and implant influence precautions.
## Postoperative priorities
Monitor ABCs, bleeding, pain, wound/drain, distal neurovascular status, urine output, delirium and signs of VTE/infection. Encourage early mobilization and exercises as ordered; coordinate physiotherapy. Use prescribed VTE prophylaxis and assess bleeding risk.
## Dislocation and precautions
Traditional precautions (avoid certain flexion/adduction/internal rotation) vary with surgical approach and surgeon protocol; do not teach one universal restriction for every patient. New sudden hip pain, deformity, shortening/rotation or inability to bear weight suggests dislocation/fracture and requires urgent evaluation. Do not attempt to reduce.
## Patient education
Teach safe transfers, prescribed mobility aid, home fall prevention, wound care, medication adherence and signs of infection/VTE. Follow current guidance on dental/infection questions rather than giving blanket antibiotic prophylaxis advice.
## PNLE traps
Neurovascular checks and early safe mobilization matter. Precautions are approach-specific; sudden deformity and severe pain after arthroplasty is urgent.
