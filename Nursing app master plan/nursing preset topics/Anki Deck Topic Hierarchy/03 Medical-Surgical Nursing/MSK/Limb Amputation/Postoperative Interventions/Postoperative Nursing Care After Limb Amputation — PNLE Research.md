# Postoperative Nursing Care After Limb Amputation

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Immediate priorities
Assess ABCs, bleeding, hemodynamics, pain, wound/dressing, residual-limb perfusion and signs of infection. Monitor for hemorrhage, hematoma, ischemia, wound breakdown, VTE and phantom limb pain. Use prescribed analgesia and reassess response.
## Residual-limb care
Follow surgeon's dressing, compression/shaping and positioning instructions. Avoid prolonged hip/knee flexion where contracture risk exists; positioning and exercises are individualized to amputation level and surgical plan. Inspect skin and protect the limb from pressure/injury.
## Rehabilitation and psychosocial care
Coordinate physiotherapy, occupational therapy, prosthetic planning, mobility safety and fall prevention. Address grief, body image, phantom sensation/pain and mental health. Phantom pain is real and should be assessed and treated rather than dismissed.
## Teaching
Teach wound hygiene, signs of infection/ischemia, safe transfers, medication use, follow-up and rehabilitation participation. Sudden bleeding, fever with deterioration, increasing pain/coolness or new respiratory symptoms require urgent review.
## PNLE traps
Do not place pillows persistently under a residual limb without protocol because contracture may develop. Phantom pain differs from residual-limb wound pain but both require assessment.
