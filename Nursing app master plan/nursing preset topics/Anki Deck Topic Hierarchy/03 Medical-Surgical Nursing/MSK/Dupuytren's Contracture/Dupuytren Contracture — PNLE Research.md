# Dupuytren Contracture

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Mechanism and manifestations
Dupuytren contracture is progressive thickening of palmar fascia causing nodules and cords that flex one or more fingers, commonly ring and little fingers. It is usually painless but can impair hand function. Risks include family history, increasing age, diabetes, smoking and alcohol use.
## Assessment
Assess contracture, ability to place palm flat, grip/function, skin integrity and daily activity impact. Distinguish from trigger finger, tendon injury and joint contracture.
## Management
Observation is reasonable for mild disease without functional limitation. Options for functionally significant disease include needle fasciotomy, collagenase injection where available, or surgery; recurrence is common and choice depends on severity, availability and specialist judgment.
## Nursing care
Teach that exercises do not reliably reverse established cords; refer for assessment when function declines. After intervention, monitor wound, swelling, sensation, perfusion and therapy adherence; splinting/hand therapy is procedure-specific.
## PNLE traps
Dupuytren is a palmar fascial disorder, not primarily a tendon sheath or median nerve disorder. Treatment depends on functional limitation; recurrence can occur.
