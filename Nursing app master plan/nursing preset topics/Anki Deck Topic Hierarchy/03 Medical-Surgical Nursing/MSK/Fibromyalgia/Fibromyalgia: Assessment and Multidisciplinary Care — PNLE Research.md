# Fibromyalgia: Assessment and Multidisciplinary Care

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Overview
Fibromyalgia is a chronic pain syndrome characterized by widespread pain, fatigue, unrefreshing sleep and cognitive symptoms. It is not explained by tissue inflammation alone; symptoms are real and may coexist with other conditions.
## Assessment
Assess pain distribution, sleep, fatigue, function, mood, medication use and comorbidities. Evaluate red flags or findings suggesting alternative disease (e.g., objective weakness, fever, joint swelling, weight loss or focal neurologic deficit) rather than attributing every symptom to fibromyalgia.
## Management
A multimodal plan may include graded aerobic/strength activity, pacing, sleep interventions, education, psychological therapies and selected medicines (e.g., duloxetine, milnacipran, pregabalin where approved/appropriate). Medication choices depend on comorbidities and adverse effects. Opioids are generally discouraged for chronic fibromyalgia because benefit is limited and harms can be substantial.
## Nursing care
Validate symptoms, set functional goals, support gradual activity and sleep hygiene, monitor mood and adverse effects, and coordinate interdisciplinary care. Avoid stigmatizing language or promising a cure.
## PNLE traps
Normal routine inflammatory tests do not mean symptoms are fabricated. New focal/red-flag findings require evaluation; do not attribute them automatically to fibromyalgia.
