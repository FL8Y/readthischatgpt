# Carpal Tunnel Syndrome

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Mechanism and findings
Median nerve compression in the carpal tunnel causes numbness/tingling in the thumb, index, middle and radial half of the ring finger, often worse at night; weakness or thenar wasting may occur in advanced disease. Risks include repetitive wrist activity, pregnancy, diabetes, hypothyroidism and inflammatory arthritis.
## Assessment and diagnosis
Assess distribution, grip/pinch strength, fine motor function, nocturnal symptoms, work/ergonomic exposures and neurovascular status. Provocative tests can support but do not independently prove diagnosis; nerve-conduction/electrodiagnostic testing is used when diagnosis is uncertain or surgery is considered.
## Management
Activity modification, neutral-position wrist splinting, ergonomic changes and selected corticosteroid injection may relieve symptoms. Persistent/progressive weakness, thenar atrophy or severe symptoms may require surgical decompression. Avoid prolonged wrist flexion/extension.
## Nursing education
Teach neutral wrist positioning at night, breaks/ergonomic changes and adherence to prescribed splint. After surgery, monitor wound, sensation, movement, pain and signs of infection; report worsening weakness or neurovascular change.
## PNLE traps
Median nerve symptoms differ from ulnar nerve distribution. Progressive weakness or muscle wasting warrants timely evaluation rather than indefinite self-care.
