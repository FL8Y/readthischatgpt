# Herniated Nucleus Pulposus and Radiculopathy

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Mechanism and symptoms
A herniated intervertebral disc can compress/irritate nerve roots, causing radicular pain, paresthesia, sensory change or weakness. Lumbar disease may cause sciatica; cervical disease can cause arm symptoms. Many disc findings on imaging are incidental, so correlate with symptoms and exam.
## Assessment and red flags
Assess pain pattern, strength, sensation, reflexes, gait, bowel/bladder function and saddle sensation. Cauda equina red flags include new urinary retention/incontinence, saddle anesthesia, bilateral symptoms or progressive weakness and require emergency assessment. Fever, cancer history, major trauma or unexplained weight loss may suggest other serious pathology.
## Management
Most uncomplicated cases initially use activity as tolerated, analgesia/anti-inflammatory therapy if safe, physiotherapy and follow-up. Avoid prolonged bed rest. Imaging is guided by red flags, progressive deficit or persistent symptoms where results affect management. Surgery may be needed for severe/progressive neurologic deficit or selected refractory cases.
## Nursing care
Promote safe movement, posture and medication safety; assess sedation/constipation with analgesics. Escalate new weakness, saddle numbness or bowel/bladder change immediately.
## PNLE traps
Cauda equina is a time-critical emergency. Do not rely on imaging alone; symptoms and neurologic examination determine significance.
