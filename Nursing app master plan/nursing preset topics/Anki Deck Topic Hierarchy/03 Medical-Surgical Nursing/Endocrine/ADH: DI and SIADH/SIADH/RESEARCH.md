# Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuretic Hormone Secretion (SIADH)

## Mechanism
SIADH is persistent ADH/vasopressin activity despite low plasma osmolality, causing renal water retention and dilutional hyponatremia. Causes include CNS disease/injury, pulmonary disease, malignancy (classically small-cell lung cancer), postoperative stress and medicines. Assess the whole clinical picture; other causes of hyponatremia must be excluded.

## Findings and diagnostic approach
Patients may be asymptomatic or develop nausea, headache, weakness, gait change, confusion, seizure or coma. Severity depends on sodium concentration and the speed of decline. Typical evaluation includes serum sodium/osmolality, urine osmolality, urine sodium, kidney function, glucose, volume status and medication review; adrenal and thyroid disease may need exclusion. SIADH classically has hypotonic plasma with inappropriately concentrated urine and no clear edema/hypovolemia, but diagnostic criteria are contextual.

## Treatment and nursing care
Management depends on symptoms, sodium level, cause and setting. Fluid restriction is common for stable patients; severe neurologic symptoms may require controlled hypertonic saline in a closely monitored protocol. Stop/adjust causative medicines and treat the underlying condition when directed. Avoid overly rapid sodium correction because osmotic demyelination can cause devastating neurologic injury. Vaptans and other therapies require specialist selection; they are not generic first-line nursing interventions.

- Trend neurologic status, seizure activity, sodium/osmolality and intake/output.
- Use prescribed fluid restriction consistently; clarify whether oral medications/flushes count toward the total.
- Initiate seizure/fall precautions for neurologic symptoms and escalate headache, confusion or vomiting.
- Track response and sodium correction rate; report unexpected rapid changes immediately.
- Do not independently administer hypotonic fluid or correct sodium without orders/protocol.

## DI comparison / PNLE traps
| Feature | SIADH | DI |
|---|---|---|
| ADH activity | Excess/inappropriate | Deficient or kidney-resistant |
| Urine | Inappropriately concentrated | Very dilute |
| Serum sodium | Often low | May be high |
| Water balance | Retained | Lost |
| Key risk | Cerebral edema/seizures with acute hyponatremia | Dehydration/hypernatremia |
