# Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuretic Hormone (SIADH)

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Mechanism
Excess ADH activity causes renal water retention, dilutional hypotonic hyponatremia and inappropriately concentrated urine. Causes include CNS/pulmonary disease, malignancy (notably small-cell lung cancer), postoperative stress and medications.
## Findings and diagnosis
Symptoms depend on sodium level and speed of decline: nausea, headache, confusion, gait change, lethargy, seizures or coma. Typical findings include low serum osmolality, low sodium, urine that is not maximally dilute and often clinically euvolemic appearance. Exclude adrenal insufficiency, hypothyroidism, renal failure and diuretic effects as appropriate.
## Management and nursing
Treat cause and review implicated medicines. Fluid restriction is common in stable cases; selected patients may require salt/urea, loop diuretic or vasopressin antagonist under specialist direction. Severe neurologic symptoms require emergency management, sometimes carefully controlled hypertonic saline.
## Safety
Frequent sodium and neurologic monitoring is essential. Correct chronic hyponatremia slowly according to protocol; overly rapid correction can cause osmotic demyelination. Do not encourage free-water intake in established SIADH unless specifically directed.
## PNLE traps
SIADH usually means water retention with low serum sodium/osmolality and concentrated urine; DI means dilute polyuria. Seizures or declining consciousness are emergencies. Sodium correction targets depend on acuity and risk; avoid memorizing a universal rapid correction rate.
