# ADH and Water Balance: Core Concepts

**Status:** Substantive research draft. Source-to-claim and Philippine-context verification pending. Reviewed 2026-10-17.

## Core concepts, assessment and nursing priorities
ADH regulates renal water retention in response to plasma osmolality and effective circulating volume. DI is deficient ADH effect with polyuria and dilute urine, risking dehydration/hypernatremia; SIADH is inappropriate ADH effect with water retention, concentrated urine and dilutional hyponatremia. Assess intake/output, daily weight, mental status, sodium/osmolality and urine concentration. New confusion, seizures, hypotension or rapidly changing sodium requires urgent escalation. Never impose fluid restriction for suspected DI or encourage free water in SIADH without a clinical plan. Sodium correction must be controlled to avoid osmotic demyelination. Sources: NIDDK diabetes insipidus https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/diabetes-insipidus ; Merck Manual SIADH https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/electrolyte-disorders/syndrome-of-inappropriate-antidiuretic-hormone-secretion-siadh

## PNLE priorities and safety
Prioritize airway, breathing, circulation and acute neurologic changes; use trends and patient presentation rather than isolated laboratory values. Follow current orders, local protocols and nursing scope. Explain unexpected deterioration promptly.
