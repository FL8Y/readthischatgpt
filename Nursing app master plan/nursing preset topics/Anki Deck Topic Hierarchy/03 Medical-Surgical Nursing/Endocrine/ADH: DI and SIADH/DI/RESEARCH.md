# Diabetes Insipidus (DI)

## Definition and pathophysiology
Diabetes insipidus is impaired water conservation that produces large amounts of dilute urine and can cause hypernatremia/dehydration if water intake cannot match losses. Central DI results from inadequate vasopressin (ADH) release; nephrogenic DI occurs when kidneys respond poorly to ADH. Do not confuse DI with diabetes mellitus: DI is primarily a water-balance disorder, not a glucose disorder.

## Causes and assessment
Central DI may follow pituitary/hypothalamic injury, surgery, tumors or selected inflammatory/genetic causes. Nephrogenic DI may be associated with lithium, kidney disease, hypercalcemia, hypokalemia or inherited defects. Findings include polyuria, intense thirst, nocturia, dehydration, tachycardia, hypotension and increasing serum sodium/osmolality. Assess intake/output, daily weight, orthostatic vitals, mental status, urine specific gravity/osmolality, serum electrolytes and renal function.

## Diagnostics and treatment
Diagnosis requires assessment of urine and serum concentration and cause-specific testing; a water-deprivation test or desmopressin response may be performed only under supervised clinical protocols. Do not restrict water unsupervised in suspected DI. Central DI is commonly treated with desmopressin and reliable water access; nephrogenic DI treatment depends on cause and may include stopping the causative drug, dietary modification and selected medications. Treatment must account for fluid status, renal function and sodium trends.

## Nursing priorities
- Measure all intake/output and trend daily weight, serum sodium, osmolality and urine concentration.
- Ensure ready access to water when safe; report inability to drink, vomiting, confusion or hypotension urgently.
- Monitor response to desmopressin and the risk of water retention/hyponatremia; teach the prescribed dosing plan and when to seek advice.
- Assess for hypernatremia-related neurologic change and dehydration.
- For perioperative or reduced-consciousness patients, coordinate prescribed fluid replacement and monitoring closely.

## PNLE distinctions
- DI: polyuria with dilute urine and risk of hypernatremia.
- SIADH: water retention, concentrated urine and often hyponatremia.
- Desmopressin use requires monitoring; excess effect can cause water intoxication/hyponatremia.
- Serum sodium correction must be controlled, especially when the duration is chronic/unknown.
