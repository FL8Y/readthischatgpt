# Diabetes Insipidus (DI)

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Mechanism and causes
DI results from deficient antidiuretic hormone (central DI) or renal resistance to ADH (nephrogenic DI), causing inability to concentrate urine. Causes include pituitary/hypothalamic injury or surgery, lithium and renal disorders; confirm cause clinically.
## Findings and diagnostics
Large volumes of dilute urine, intense thirst, dehydration, tachycardia, hypotension and hypernatremia may occur. Assess intake/output, daily weight, orthostatic vitals, serum sodium/osmolality and urine osmolality/specific gravity. Water-deprivation testing is specialist-supervised; never perform unsupervised fluid restriction in suspected DI.
## Management and nursing
Restore fluid deficit carefully and treat cause. Central DI may respond to desmopressin; monitor urine output, sodium, weight and neurologic status to avoid overcorrection/water intoxication. Nephrogenic DI management may include treating cause, reducing dietary solute and selected thiazide/NSAID strategies under clinician supervision; NSAIDs have renal/GI risks.
## Emergency priorities
Hypotension, confusion, seizures or severe hypernatremia require urgent care. Track hourly output in unstable patients and promptly report abrupt changes.
## PNLE comparison
DI: polyuria, dilute urine, risk of dehydration/hypernatremia. SIADH: water retention, concentrated urine and hyponatremia. Do not confuse desmopressin treatment of central DI with SIADH, where it can worsen water retention.
