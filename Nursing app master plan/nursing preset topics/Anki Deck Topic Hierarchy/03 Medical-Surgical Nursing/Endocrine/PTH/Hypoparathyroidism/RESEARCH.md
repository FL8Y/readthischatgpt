# Hypoparathyroidism

## Definition and pathophysiology
Hypoparathyroidism is inadequate PTH secretion or action, reducing renal calcium reabsorption and calcitriol production while impairing phosphate excretion. The typical pattern is low calcium with low or inappropriately normal PTH and often high phosphate. A common acquired cause is parathyroid injury/removal during thyroid or neck surgery; autoimmune, genetic and infiltrative causes also occur.

## Assessment and diagnostics
Neuromuscular irritability can cause perioral/finger tingling, cramps, paresthesia, hyperreflexia, carpopedal spasm, tetany, laryngospasm, seizure or prolonged QT/arrhythmia. Assess voice, airway, swallowing, mental status and cardiac status. Tests may include total/ionized calcium, albumin, phosphate, magnesium, renal function, vitamin D and PTH. Severe hypomagnesemia may impair PTH secretion/action and contribute to refractory hypocalcemia.

## Management and nursing care
Acute severe symptomatic hypocalcemia requires emergency escalation and may need monitored IV calcium per prescribed protocol. Chronic disease often needs oral calcium and active vitamin D individualized by labs and specialist plan. Monitor persistent hypocalcemia and treatment-associated hypercalcemia/hypercalciuria, kidney stones and renal injury. After neck surgery, perioral numbness, tingling, cramps, stridor or seizure require prompt assessment; airway signs are an emergency.

## PNLE distinctions
- Hypoparathyroidism: low PTH with low calcium, often high phosphate.
- Hyperparathyroidism often gives elevated/inappropriately normal PTH and high calcium in primary disease.
- Post-neck-surgery tingling/tetany may indicate acute hypocalcemia.
- Stridor or seizure requires immediate response, not routine follow-up.
