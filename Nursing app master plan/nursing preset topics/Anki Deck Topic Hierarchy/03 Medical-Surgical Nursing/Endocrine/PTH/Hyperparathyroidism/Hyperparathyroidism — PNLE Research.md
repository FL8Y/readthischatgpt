# Hyperparathyroidism

**Status:** Substantive research draft. Source-to-claim and Philippine-context verification pending. Reviewed 2026-10-17.

## Core concepts, assessment and nursing priorities
Excess PTH raises serum calcium through renal, bone and vitamin-D-mediated effects. Primary disease often involves an adenoma; secondary hyperparathyroidism often accompanies CKD or vitamin D deficiency. Hypercalcemia may cause constipation, nausea, polyuria, thirst, weakness, confusion, renal stones and bone disease. Assess calcium (consider ionized calcium when indicated), phosphate, PTH, vitamin D, renal function, hydration and ECG when clinically appropriate. Treatment depends on cause and severity; primary disease may require surgery, while CKD-associated disease may require phosphate control, vitamin D strategies or calcimimetics. After parathyroidectomy monitor airway/neck swelling, voice and hypocalcemia symptoms. Avoid unprescribed calcium/vitamin D supplements until reviewed. Sources: NIDDK https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/primary-hyperparathyroidism ; Merck Manual https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/parathyroid-disorders/hyperparathyroidism

## PNLE priorities and safety
Prioritize airway, breathing, circulation and acute neurologic changes; use trends and patient presentation rather than isolated laboratory values. Follow current orders, local protocols and nursing scope. Explain unexpected deterioration promptly.
