# Parathyroid Hormone and Calcium Regulation

## Core concepts, assessment and nursing priorities
PTH is released when ionized calcium falls. It increases renal calcium reabsorption, promotes phosphate excretion, stimulates active vitamin D formation and indirectly increases intestinal calcium absorption. Typical patterns: primary hyperparathyroidism—high calcium, often low phosphate, PTH high or inappropriately normal; hypoparathyroidism—low calcium, high phosphate, low/inappropriately normal PTH; CKD-associated secondary hyperparathyroidism—PTH high with calcium variable and phosphate often high in advanced disease. Interpret PTH alongside calcium, phosphate, renal function, magnesium, albumin and vitamin D. Hypocalcemia can cause tingling, tetany, seizures or laryngospasm; hypercalcemia can cause dehydration, constipation, weakness and confusion. Sources: NIDDK https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/primary-hyperparathyroidism ; Merck Manual https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/electrolyte-disorders/hypocalcemia

## PNLE priorities and safety
Prioritize airway, breathing, circulation and acute neurologic changes; use trends and patient presentation rather than isolated laboratory values. Follow current orders, local protocols and nursing scope. Explain unexpected deterioration promptly.
