# Primary Aldosteronism (Conn Syndrome)

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Mechanism and manifestations
Primary aldosteronism is autonomous aldosterone production causing sodium retention, hypertension and increased renal potassium/hydrogen loss. Many patients have normal potassium; hypokalemia is not required for diagnosis.
## Assessment and diagnosis
Consider in resistant hypertension, hypertension with spontaneous/diuretic-associated hypokalemia, adrenal incidentaloma or relevant family history. Screening commonly uses aldosterone and renin with an aldosterone-renin ratio, interpreted with medications, potassium status and sampling conditions. Confirmatory testing and subtype evaluation are specialist-led.
## Management
Unilateral aldosterone-producing adenoma may be treated with adrenalectomy; bilateral disease is usually treated medically with mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (spironolactone/eplerenone). Monitor BP, potassium and renal function. Spironolactone may cause gynecomastia/sexual adverse effects; both agents can cause hyperkalemia, especially with CKD or interacting medicines.
## Nursing priorities and teaching
Teach adherence and BP monitoring. Do not recommend potassium supplements or salt substitutes without reviewing potassium/renal status and medicines. Report weakness, palpitations or severe BP elevation.
## PNLE traps
Aldosterone excess causes hypertension and may cause hypokalemia/metabolic alkalosis, but normal potassium does not exclude it. Mineralocorticoid antagonists can reverse potassium loss yet create hyperkalemia.
