# Adrenal Gland and Hormones

**Status:** Substantive research draft. Source-to-claim and Philippine-context verification pending. Reviewed 2026-10-17.

## Core concepts, assessment and nursing priorities
The adrenal cortex produces aldosterone (salt/potassium balance), cortisol (stress response, metabolism, vascular responsiveness) and adrenal androgens; the medulla releases epinephrine and norepinephrine. Cortisol is regulated by hypothalamic CRH and pituitary ACTH; aldosterone mainly by renin-angiotensin and potassium. Deficiency can cause hypotension and impaired stress response; primary adrenal failure may cause hyperkalemia/hyponatremia. Excess cortisol can cause hyperglycemia, hypertension, skin fragility and infection risk; excess aldosterone causes hypertension and sometimes hypokalemia. Long-term systemic corticosteroids must not be abruptly stopped without a prescribed taper. Suspected adrenal crisis is an emergency. Sources: NIDDK adrenal insufficiency https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/adrenal-insufficiency ; Endocrine Society clinical guidelines https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines

## PNLE priorities and safety
Prioritize airway, breathing, circulation and acute neurologic changes; use trends and patient presentation rather than isolated laboratory values. Follow current orders, local protocols and nursing scope. Explain unexpected deterioration promptly.
