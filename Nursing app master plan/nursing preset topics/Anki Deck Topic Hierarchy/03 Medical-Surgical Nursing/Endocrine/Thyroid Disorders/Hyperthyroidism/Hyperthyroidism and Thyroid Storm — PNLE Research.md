# Hyperthyroidism and Thyroid Storm

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Mechanism and findings
Hyperthyroidism is excess thyroid hormone action, commonly Graves disease, toxic nodules or thyroiditis. Findings include heat intolerance, weight loss despite appetite, tremor, anxiety, tachycardia, frequent stools, goiter and sometimes eye findings in Graves disease.
## Diagnosis and treatment
TSH is generally suppressed in primary hyperthyroidism, with elevated free T4 and/or T3. Treatment depends on cause and may include beta blocker for symptoms, antithyroid medication, radioactive iodine or surgery.
## Medication safety
Methimazole can cause agranulocytosis and liver injury; fever/sore throat requires urgent CBC/clinical review and the patient should follow instructions about withholding and contacting the prescriber. PTU has serious hepatotoxicity risk and is generally reserved for selected situations, including first-trimester pregnancy or thyroid storm under specialist guidance. Antithyroid medicines require monitoring and pregnancy-specific decisions. Beta blockers require caution with bradycardia, hypotension and certain obstructive airway conditions.
## Thyroid storm
Severe thyrotoxicosis with fever, marked tachycardia/arrhythmia, agitation/delirium, vomiting/diarrhea and heart failure is life-threatening. Activate emergency response; treatment typically combines supportive care, beta blockade if appropriate, thionamide, iodine after thionamide, corticosteroid and treatment of trigger under specialist orders.
## PNLE traps
Thionamide before iodine in thyroid storm prevents new hormone synthesis before iodine blocks release. Do not use aspirin for fever in thyroid storm if avoidable because it can increase free thyroid hormone; follow current emergency protocol.
