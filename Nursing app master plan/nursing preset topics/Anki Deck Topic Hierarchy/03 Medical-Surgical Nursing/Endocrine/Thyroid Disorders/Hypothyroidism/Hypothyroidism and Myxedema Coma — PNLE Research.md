# Hypothyroidism and Myxedema Coma

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Mechanism and findings
Hypothyroidism is insufficient thyroid hormone, often autoimmune thyroiditis or treatment-related. Findings include fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, constipation, dry skin, bradycardia, slowed cognition and delayed reflex relaxation. Primary hypothyroidism usually has elevated TSH and low free T4.
## Management
Levothyroxine is standard replacement. Take consistently as directed, commonly on an empty stomach; separate from iron/calcium and other absorption-interfering products according to prescribing information. Dose changes are gradual, and TSH is rechecked after an appropriate interval. Older adults and people with coronary disease often require cautious initiation/titration.
## Myxedema coma
Severe decompensated hypothyroidism can cause altered mental status, hypothermia, bradycardia, hypotension, hypoventilation, hyponatremia and hypoglycemia. It is an emergency requiring airway/ventilatory support, careful warming, IV thyroid hormone and empiric glucocorticoid until adrenal insufficiency is addressed, per specialist protocol.
## Nursing priorities
Monitor temperature, respiratory status, mental state, HR/BP, sodium and glucose. Avoid aggressive external warming in severe cases without medical direction; assess for precipitating infection, sedatives or cold exposure.
## PNLE traps
Do not stop levothyroxine when symptoms improve without medical direction. Myxedema coma is not merely sleepiness; hypothermia, hypoventilation and altered consciousness require emergency treatment.
