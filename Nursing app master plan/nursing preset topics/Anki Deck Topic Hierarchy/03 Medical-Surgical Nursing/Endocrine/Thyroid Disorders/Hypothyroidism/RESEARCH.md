# Hypothyroidism

## Pathophysiology and causes
Hypothyroidism is insufficient thyroid hormone action. Primary hypothyroidism originates in the thyroid, often from autoimmune thyroiditis, surgery, radioiodine or iodine-related causes; central hypothyroidism results from pituitary/hypothalamic dysfunction. Thyroid hormone deficiency slows metabolic processes.

## Assessment and diagnostics
Symptoms may include fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, constipation, dry skin, hair changes, hoarse voice, slowed thinking, low mood, bradycardia and menstrual changes. Older adults may present subtly. Primary disease commonly has elevated TSH and low free T4; central disease may have low or inappropriately normal TSH with low free T4. Interpret results in context of illness, pregnancy and medication history.

## Treatment and nursing education
Levothyroxine is standard replacement for many patients; dose is individualized and adjusted to labs, age, cardiac risk and symptoms. Consistent timing matters. Iron, calcium and some medicines/foods can reduce absorption; teach the patient's prescribed schedule and separation instructions. Do not stop, switch formulation or change dose without the treating clinician. TSH is reassessed after sufficient time following a dose change, according to protocol.

Monitor adherence, symptoms, pulse and lab trends. Older patients and those with coronary disease may need cautious initiation/titration because excess replacement can provoke angina or arrhythmias. Teach patients not to stop treatment when symptoms improve and to report palpitations, chest pain or signs of over-replacement.

## Myxedema coma
Severe decompensated hypothyroidism may cause hypothermia, altered consciousness, bradycardia, hypotension, hypoventilation and hyponatremia. This is an emergency requiring urgent support, airway/ventilation management and clinician-directed thyroid hormone/glucocorticoid treatment as appropriate. Avoid rapid unmonitored warming in unstable patients; follow emergency protocol.

## PNLE distinctions
- Primary hypothyroidism: TSH is usually high and free T4 low; central hypothyroidism may not elevate TSH.
- Replacement is commonly long-term; consistency matters.
- Hypothermia plus altered consciousness and hypoventilation suggests severe decompensation, not routine fatigue.
