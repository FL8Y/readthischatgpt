# Diabetes Mellitus: Type 1, Type 2, and Acute Complications

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Pathophysiology
Diabetes mellitus is chronic hyperglycemia from deficient insulin secretion, insulin resistance, or both. Type 1 typically involves autoimmune beta-cell destruction and absolute insulin deficiency; type 2 combines insulin resistance with progressive beta-cell dysfunction.
## Assessment and diagnosis
Classic symptoms include polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, weight change, blurred vision and recurrent infection. Diagnostic thresholds in nonpregnant adults include A1C ≥6.5%, fasting plasma glucose ≥126 mg/dL, 2-hour OGTT glucose ≥200 mg/dL, or random plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL with classic symptoms/hyperglycemic crisis; absent unequivocal hyperglycemia, confirm with repeat testing. Use current criteria and clinical context.
## Acute emergencies
Hypoglycemia: symptoms may include sweating, tremor, hunger, confusion, seizure or coma. If awake and able to swallow, give fast-acting carbohydrate per protocol and recheck; never give oral intake to an unconscious patient. Use prescribed IV dextrose or glucagon when unable to take oral carbohydrate.
DKA: ketones and metabolic acidosis, often with dehydration; may occur in type 1 and type 2. HHS: profound hyperglycemia/hyperosmolality and dehydration, often with little significant ketoacidosis. Both require urgent fluid/electrolyte/insulin management and close potassium monitoring.
## Medication safety
Insulin is high-alert. Verify product, dose, timing, glucose, meal availability and independent double-check policy. Never omit basal insulin in type 1 diabetes without a specific clinical plan. SGLT2 inhibitors may cause euglycemic DKA and are often withheld around surgery/acute illness according to prescribing guidance. Metformin may need temporary withholding in selected acute kidney injury, severe hypoxia or contrast-related situations according to current policy; do not apply blanket rules without assessing indication and protocol.
## Chronic nursing care
Foot/skin checks, eye/kidney screening, BP/lipid management, nutrition, activity, sick-day rules, medication adherence and hypoglycemia prevention are key. Teach when to check ketones and seek urgent care.
## PNLE traps
Never give oral food/drink to an unconscious hypoglycemic patient. In DKA, potassium can be depleted even if initial serum potassium is normal/high; insulin can rapidly lower serum potassium. Treat ABC threats and follow protocols.
