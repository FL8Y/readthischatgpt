# Pituitary Gland Hormones and Nursing Implications

**Status:** Substantive research draft. Source-to-claim and Philippine-context verification pending. Reviewed 2026-10-17.

## Core concepts, assessment, management and nursing care
The anterior pituitary secretes ACTH (stimulates cortisol), TSH (stimulates thyroid), LH/FSH (gonadal function), GH (growth/metabolism) and prolactin (lactation). The posterior pituitary releases hypothalamically synthesized ADH and oxytocin. Pituitary disorders can cause hormone deficiency/excess, headache and visual-field changes. Assess symptoms, medications, fluid balance, BP, sodium/osmolality, visual symptoms and ordered hormone tests; timing and stimulation/suppression testing matter. After transsphenoidal surgery monitor neurologic status, fluid balance and sodium, and report clear nasal drainage suspicious for CSF, worsening headache, visual change or altered consciousness. Central hypothyroidism may show low free T4 with low/inappropriately normal TSH. Sources: NIDDK pituitary/endocrine diseases https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases ; Merck Manual https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/pituitary-disorders
