# Hematology and Immune Function: Clinical Overview

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Core relationships
Hematology includes red cells/hemoglobin, white-cell defense, platelets and coagulation. Marrow disorders can cause anemia, infection risk and bleeding simultaneously. Immune dysfunction may be due to inherited defects, malignancy, infection, medication or treatment.
## Assessment
Interpret CBC with differential, reticulocyte count, smear and coagulation tests in clinical context. Review trends, medication exposures, transfusion history, infection/bleeding symptoms, nutrition and family history. Lab reference ranges vary by age, pregnancy, altitude and laboratory.
## Nursing priorities
Escalate fever with neutropenia, active major bleeding, suspected hemolysis/transfusion reaction, severe dyspnea, chest pain, neurologic deficits or hemodynamic instability. Use infection and bleeding precautions tailored to actual risks; avoid blanket restrictions without indication.
## Patient education
Explain medication safety, follow-up testing, vaccination or exposure precautions where relevant, and urgent symptoms. Use shared decision-making and preserve privacy around genetic/infectious diagnoses.
## PNLE traps
A single CBC value rarely explains the whole clinical picture. Evaluate trends and symptoms; anemia, infection and bleeding risks can coexist.
