# Sickle Cell Disease and Vaso-Occlusive Crisis

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Mechanism
Sickle cell disease is an inherited hemoglobin disorder in which deoxygenated HbS polymerizes, causing red-cell sickling, hemolysis and vaso-occlusion. Triggers include dehydration, infection, hypoxia, cold and acidosis, though crises may occur without a clear trigger.
## Assessment and complications
Pain crisis causes acute severe pain; assess oxygenation, fever, chest symptoms, neurologic status, hydration and usual baseline. Acute chest syndrome (new pulmonary infiltrate with respiratory symptoms/fever), stroke, splenic sequestration, aplastic crisis and sepsis are serious complications.
## Nursing care
Provide prompt individualized analgesia, hydration to euvolemia (avoid fluid overload), warmth, incentive spirometry for hospitalized pain episodes when prescribed, and reassessment. Oxygen is for hypoxemia or another indication, not routine solely because a pain crisis exists. Fever requires urgent evaluation for infection; follow local protocols.
## Medication safety
Hydroxyurea can reduce crises but requires CBC monitoring and pregnancy counseling. Transfusion is used for selected complications, not routinely for every uncomplicated pain episode; avoid hyperviscosity and alloimmunization risks through specialist protocols.
## PNLE traps
Chest pain, fever, dyspnea, hypoxemia or neurologic deficit may indicate acute chest syndrome/stroke and require emergency action. Do not dismiss pain or delay analgesia while completing routine paperwork.
