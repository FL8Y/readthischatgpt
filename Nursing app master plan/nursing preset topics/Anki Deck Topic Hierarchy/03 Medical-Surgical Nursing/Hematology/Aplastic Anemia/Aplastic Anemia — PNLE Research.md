# Aplastic Anemia

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Definition and mechanism
Aplastic anemia is bone-marrow failure causing pancytopenia: anemia, neutropenia and thrombocytopenia. Causes include immune-mediated marrow suppression, drugs/toxins, radiation, viral illness and inherited syndromes; many cases are idiopathic.
## Findings and diagnostics
Fatigue, pallor, dyspnea, infections/fever, petechiae, bruising and mucosal bleeding may occur. CBC shows reduced cell lines; reticulocytes are often low. Bone-marrow biopsy typically shows hypocellularity. Distinguish from leukemia or marrow infiltration through specialist evaluation.
## Nursing priorities
Fever in severe neutropenia is urgent; initiate local febrile-neutropenia pathway. Use bleeding precautions for thrombocytopenia, monitor CBC trends, assess oral/skin integrity and avoid unnecessary invasive procedures. Transfusions, immunosuppressive therapy or stem-cell transplantation are specialist-directed.
## Medication and safety
Review marrow-suppressive medicines and toxin exposure. Avoid rectal temperatures/suppositories and IM injections when bleeding risk is high unless specifically directed. Transfusion decisions follow clinical status and current thresholds, not a universal single number.
## PNLE traps
Pancytopenia creates simultaneous infection, bleeding and oxygen-delivery risks. Prioritize fever, active bleeding, dyspnea or hemodynamic instability.
