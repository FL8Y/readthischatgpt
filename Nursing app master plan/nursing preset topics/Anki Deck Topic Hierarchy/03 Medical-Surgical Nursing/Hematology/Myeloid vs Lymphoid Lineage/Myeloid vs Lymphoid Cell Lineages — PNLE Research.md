# Myeloid vs Lymphoid Cell Lineages

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Hematopoiesis overview
Hematopoietic stem cells produce myeloid and lymphoid lineages. Myeloid cells include erythrocytes, platelets (via megakaryocytes), neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils and monocytes. Lymphoid lineage includes B cells, T cells and natural killer cells.
## Clinical relevance
Acute/chronic leukemias are classified using lineage, maturation, genetics and clinical features—not cell count alone. AML arises from myeloid precursors; ALL from lymphoid precursors. Chronic myeloid leukemia involves a characteristic molecular abnormality in many cases; CLL is a mature B-cell neoplasm.
## Assessment
Interpret CBC differential, peripheral smear, flow cytometry, marrow findings and molecular testing in context. Blast percentages and classification criteria depend on current hematologic standards; avoid diagnosing leukemia from a CBC alone.
## Nursing implications
Patients may have infection, bleeding and anemia from marrow dysfunction or treatment. Fever, bleeding, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms or suspected leukostasis require urgent evaluation. Protect patient confidentiality and provide clear explanation of tests and treatment.
## PNLE traps
Myeloid/lymphoid lineage is not the same as acute/chronic classification. WBC count can be low, normal or high in leukemia. Classification requires specialized studies.
