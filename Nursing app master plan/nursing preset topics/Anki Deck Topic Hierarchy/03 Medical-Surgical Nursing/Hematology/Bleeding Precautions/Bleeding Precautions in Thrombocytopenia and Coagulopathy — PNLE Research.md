# Bleeding Precautions in Thrombocytopenia and Coagulopathy

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Risk assessment
Bleeding risk depends on platelet count/function, coagulation defects, medicines, active bleeding, procedures and patient factors. Assess petechiae, ecchymoses, gum/nose bleeding, hematuria, melena, severe headache, neurologic change and hemodynamic trends.
## Nursing precautions
Use gentle oral care with soft brush, electric razor, fall prevention, pressure after venipuncture, and avoid unnecessary IM injections or invasive procedures. Avoid rectal temperatures, suppositories and enemas in significant thrombocytopenia unless specifically ordered. Use stool-softening strategies when prescribed to reduce straining.
## Medication and procedure safety
Review anticoagulants, antiplatelets, NSAIDs and supplements that affect hemostasis. Do not independently stop essential antithrombotics; promptly escalate bleeding and obtain prescriber guidance. Platelet transfusion thresholds vary with cause, active bleeding and planned procedure.
## Emergency signs
Intracranial bleeding may present as sudden severe headache, confusion, vomiting, weakness or altered consciousness; GI bleeding can cause melena/hematemesis, tachycardia or shock. Escalate immediately.
## PNLE traps
Bleeding precautions are tailored to risk; avoid false universal platelet cutoffs. New neurologic symptoms in a bleeding-risk patient are an emergency.
