# Infection Precautions for Neutropenia and Immunosuppression

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Risk and recognition
Neutropenia and immunosuppression reduce ability to contain infection. Fever may be the only early sign, and inflammatory findings can be muted. Assess temperature trends, chills, mucositis, cough, dysuria, line sites, skin lesions, mental status and hemodynamics.
## Nursing actions
Perform hand hygiene, use standard precautions and add transmission-based precautions based on suspected/confirmed pathogen and policy. Follow local protective-environment/neutropenia procedures; avoid unnecessary exposure to ill visitors and unsafe food/water practices. Maintain meticulous central-line and oral care. Do not use rectal temperatures or procedures in severe neutropenia unless specifically directed.
## Fever emergency
Suspected febrile neutropenia requires urgent assessment, CBC/ANC, cultures and prompt empiric antimicrobials per protocol; cultures should not cause a material antibiotic delay. Follow local threshold definitions because they may specify fever pattern and ANC.
## Patient teaching
Report fever at the threshold specified by the oncology team immediately, even overnight; do not self-treat with antipyretics and wait. Teach hand hygiene, food safety, line care and when to seek emergency help.
## PNLE traps
Neutropenic sepsis may occur without dramatic redness or pus. Prompt escalation and antimicrobials are time-critical; avoid treating all immunocompromised patients as requiring identical isolation.
