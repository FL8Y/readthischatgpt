# Iron-Deficiency Anemia

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Causes and mechanism
Iron deficiency reduces hemoglobin synthesis and usually causes microcytic, hypochromic anemia. Causes include blood loss (GI or menstrual), pregnancy/increased demand, low intake and malabsorption. In adults, unexplained iron deficiency requires evaluation for the cause, not iron replacement alone.
## Findings and diagnostics
Fatigue, pallor, exertional dyspnea, palpitations, pica and restless legs may occur. CBC may show low MCV/MCH; ferritin is usually low but can be falsely normal/high with inflammation. Iron studies and clinical context guide diagnosis.
## Management and nursing care
Treat the cause and replace iron orally or intravenously depending on severity, tolerance, absorption and urgency. Oral iron commonly causes GI upset/constipation and dark stools; absorption may be affected by calcium, antacids and some medicines. Follow product-specific administration guidance and reassess hemoglobin/ferritin response.
## Safety
Keep iron products away from children; overdose can be fatal. IV iron can cause infusion reactions and requires monitored administration according to product instructions. Transfusion is not automatic for iron deficiency; it depends on clinical instability and severity.
## PNLE traps
Low ferritin strongly supports iron deficiency, but inflammation changes interpretation. Investigate occult blood loss in appropriate patients. Teach adherence and expected dark stools while distinguishing them from tarry melena with symptoms.
