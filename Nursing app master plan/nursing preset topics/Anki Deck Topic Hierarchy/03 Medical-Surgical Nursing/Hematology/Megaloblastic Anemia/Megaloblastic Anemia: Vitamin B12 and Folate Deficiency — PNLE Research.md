# Megaloblastic Anemia: Vitamin B12 and Folate Deficiency

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Mechanism
Vitamin B12 or folate deficiency impairs DNA synthesis and causes megaloblastic macrocytic anemia. B12 deficiency may also cause neurologic injury; folate deficiency does not typically cause the same neurologic syndrome.
## Assessment and diagnosis
Fatigue, pallor, glossitis and macrocytosis may occur. B12 deficiency can cause paresthesias, gait disturbance, cognitive changes and loss of vibration/position sense. Check CBC/smear, B12, folate and additional markers when needed; investigate pernicious anemia, diet, malabsorption, alcohol and medications.
## Management
Replace the deficient vitamin and address cause. B12 replacement route/duration depends on cause and severity; pernicious anemia often requires lifelong replacement. Folate can improve anemia while allowing B12-related neurologic injury to progress, so assess B12 before treating presumed folate deficiency alone.
## Nursing care
Monitor hematologic response and neurologic function, assess nutrition and medication history, and teach dietary sources and follow-up. Do not dismiss neurologic symptoms because anemia is mild.
## PNLE traps
Macrocytosis can be caused by multiple conditions. B12 deficiency can cause neurologic deficits; folate alone must not mask untreated B12 deficiency.
