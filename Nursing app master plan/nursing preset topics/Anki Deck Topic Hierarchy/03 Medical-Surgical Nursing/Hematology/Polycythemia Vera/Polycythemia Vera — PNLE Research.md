# Polycythemia Vera

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Pathophysiology
Polycythemia vera is a myeloproliferative neoplasm, commonly associated with JAK2 mutation, causing increased red-cell mass and often leukocytosis/thrombocytosis. Hyperviscosity and abnormal platelet function increase both thrombosis and bleeding risks.
## Findings and diagnostics
Headache, dizziness, visual disturbance, aquagenic pruritus, erythromelalgia, splenomegaly and thrombosis may occur. CBC shows elevated hemoglobin/hematocrit; evaluation may include JAK2 testing, erythropoietin level and marrow assessment. Dehydration and secondary erythrocytosis must be considered.
## Management and nursing
Specialist care may include phlebotomy to a target hematocrit, low-dose aspirin if appropriate, and cytoreductive therapy for selected risk groups. Monitor thrombotic/bleeding symptoms, CBC trends, medication effects and hydration. Aspirin is not automatic when bleeding risk or contraindications exist.
## Urgent symptoms
Sudden chest pain, dyspnea, unilateral weakness, severe headache, vision loss or limb ischemia requires emergency assessment.
## PNLE traps
PV raises both thrombosis and bleeding risk. Phlebotomy targets and cytoreductive choices are individualized; do not infer diagnosis from high hematocrit alone.
