# Hemophilia: Bleeding Disorder Nursing Care

**Status:** Draft researched under the updated full research standard; source-to-claim audit and file reopen check pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Pathophysiology
Hemophilia A is factor VIII deficiency; hemophilia B is factor IX deficiency. Inherited X-linked disorders commonly cause deep tissue, muscle and joint bleeding. Severity correlates with factor activity, but bleeding phenotype and inhibitor status also matter.
## Findings and diagnostics
Hemarthrosis causes pain, warmth, swelling and reduced range of motion; deep muscle or intracranial bleeding can be life-threatening. PT is often normal and aPTT may be prolonged, but results depend on assay and factor level. Confirm with factor assays and inhibitor testing.
## Management
Factor replacement or other hemostatic therapy is individualized by hemophilia team. Emicizumab is used for prophylaxis in hemophilia A in selected patients but is not acute factor replacement; concomitant bypassing agents require specialist oversight because of thrombotic/microangiopathic risk.
## Nursing responsibilities
For suspected joint bleed, rest/protect the joint and seek prompt factor-treatment guidance; avoid IM injections and unnecessary invasive procedures. Apply pressure to minor bleeding. Head injury, severe headache, neck/throat swelling, abdominal pain or uncontrolled bleeding requires emergency treatment even if external signs are minimal.
## Teaching and medication cautions
Avoid aspirin and nonselective NSAIDs unless the hemophilia team approves. Teach individualized home treatment, medical identification, safe activity and family/genetic counseling. Do not delay factor therapy for routine imaging when a serious bleed is suspected and a treatment plan exists.
## PNLE traps
Hemophilia causes deep joint/muscle bleeding rather than only petechiae. Head injury is urgent. Factor product and inhibitor treatment are specialist-specific.
