# Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

## Definition and staging
CKD is an abnormality of kidney structure or function present for at least three months with implications for health. It is classified by cause, glomerular filtration category (G1–G5) and albuminuria category (A1–A3), the combined “CGA” classification. An isolated eGFR below 60 or albuminuria may need repeat testing and clinical interpretation to establish chronicity; one result alone does not always prove CKD.

## Assessment and monitoring
Assess BP, edema, volume status, fatigue, pruritus, appetite, nausea, sleep, cognition, urine changes, medicines and cardiovascular risks. Monitor eGFR/creatinine, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, potassium, bicarbonate, hemoglobin, calcium/phosphate and other tests according to stage and plan. CKD may be asymptomatic early. Trends and risk category help guide follow-up and progression risk.

## Management principles
Management includes treating the underlying cause, BP and diabetes control, cardiovascular risk reduction, medication review and kidney-protective therapies when indicated. ACE inhibitors/ARBs and SGLT2 inhibitors are recommended for specific groups based on albuminuria, eGFR, diabetes, heart failure and contraindications; they are not automatically suitable for all patients. Monitor renal function, potassium, volume status and adverse effects after treatment changes. Avoid unnecessary nephrotoxins and use renal dosing where required. Diet/fluid recommendations should be individualized, not applied as a universal low-protein/low-potassium regimen without labs and nutrition assessment.

Advanced CKD may require preparation for dialysis, transplantation or conservative kidney care based on clinical status and shared decision-making. Dialysis initiation is based on symptoms, complications, preferences and overall context rather than eGFR number alone.

## Nursing care and education
- Trend BP, weight, edema, urine pattern, eGFR, albuminuria and electrolyte/acid-base complications.
- Review over-the-counter analgesics/herbals and contrast/exposure risk with the care team; patients should not start NSAIDs or supplements without advice.
- Explain medication purpose, monitoring and sick-day/acute illness instructions as prescribed.
- Encourage individualized nutrition, physical activity, smoking cessation and diabetes/BP control.
- For dialysis planning, support vascular-access protection education and coordinate nephrology, dietitian, social support and transplant services as appropriate.
- Escalate pulmonary edema, chest pain, severe weakness/palpitations, altered mental state or rapidly declining urine output.

## PNLE distinctions
- CKD is chronic (≥3 months); AKI is acute and may coexist with CKD.
- eGFR estimates filtration; urine albumin adds independent prognostic information.
- Hyperkalemia and fluid overload can be immediately dangerous even when the patient has few subjective symptoms.
