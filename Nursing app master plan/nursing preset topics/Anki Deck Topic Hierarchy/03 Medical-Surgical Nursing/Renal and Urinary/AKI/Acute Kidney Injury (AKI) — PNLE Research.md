# Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Definition and causes
AKI is an abrupt decline in kidney function reflected by rising creatinine and/or reduced urine output. Causes are categorized as prerenal (reduced perfusion), intrinsic renal (e.g., tubular injury, glomerular disease, nephrotoxins) and postrenal (obstruction). Use current KDIGO criteria and baseline creatinine when available.
## Assessment and diagnostics
Trend urine output, creatinine, BUN, potassium, bicarbonate, fluid balance, weight and BP. Assess dehydration, edema, lung sounds, medications, sepsis and obstruction. Urinalysis and renal/bladder imaging may help identify cause. A single creatinine value may lag behind injury.
## Nursing priorities
Treat cause, maintain perfusion without causing fluid overload, avoid nephrotoxins where possible, adjust medication doses for renal function and monitor electrolytes. Urgent escalation for refractory hyperkalemia, pulmonary edema, severe acidosis, uremic complications or rapidly worsening oliguria/anuria. Dialysis is based on complications and clinical context, not creatinine alone.
## Medication safety
Review NSAIDs, contrast exposure, aminoglycosides and other nephrotoxins; never independently stop essential drugs without a plan. Renally cleared medicines may accumulate. Monitor potassium with ACEi/ARB and potassium-sparing drugs.
## PNLE traps
AKI may be prerenal, intrinsic or postrenal; identify reversible causes. Fluid resuscitation is not automatic if overloaded. Hyperkalemia and pulmonary edema can be immediately life-threatening.
