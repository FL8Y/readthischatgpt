# Hemodialysis: Nursing Care and Complications

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Principles and assessment
Hemodialysis removes solutes and fluid across a semipermeable membrane. Before treatment, verify prescription/access, weight relative to target, BP, edema, lung sounds, symptoms, medications and labs. Some antihypertensives may be timed around dialysis only per prescriber; never hold all medicines automatically.
## During and after treatment
Monitor BP, HR, symptoms, access, circuit alarms and fluid removal. Complications include hypotension, cramps, nausea, dysrhythmia, bleeding, access infection and rarely dialysis disequilibrium (headache, confusion, seizure, especially in high-risk new dialysis). After dialysis, reassess weight, vitals, access hemostasis and symptoms.
## Medication/lab safety
Potassium, phosphate and fluid plans are individualized. Review timing/removal of medicines by dialysis and renal dose adjustment. Heparin may be used in circuit; assess bleeding risk. Avoid taking unreviewed OTC products or magnesium/phosphate-containing laxatives in advanced CKD.
## Emergency signs
Chest pain, severe hypotension, altered mental status, seizure, air embolism suspicion, major bleeding or access failure requires immediate response.
## PNLE traps
Rapid ultrafiltration can cause hypotension. Post-dialysis weight helps assess fluid removal; access must be protected. Do not assume every medicine is dialyzable or that all should be held.
