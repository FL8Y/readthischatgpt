# Prostatectomy: Perioperative Nursing Care

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Postoperative priorities
After prostate surgery, monitor airway, vitals, urine output, pain, bleeding, infection and fluid balance. A three-way catheter with continuous bladder irrigation (CBI) may be used after transurethral resection; follow surgeon-specific orders for irrigation rate and target urine appearance.
## CBI calculations and safety
Measure total drainage and subtract irrigant infused to calculate actual urine output. Maintain closed system and catheter patency; do not force irrigation against resistance. If outflow stops, assess for kinks/clots and follow protocol; notify the team promptly if unable to restore flow. Bladder spasms may occur; medications are prescribed after assessing obstruction and contraindications.
## Hemorrhage and complications
Bright-red output with large clots, increasing bleeding, hypotension, tachycardia, reduced drainage, severe suprapubic pain or bladder distention requires urgent assessment. Watch for infection, retention, electrolyte disturbance depending on procedure/irrigant, and thromboembolism.
## Teaching
Follow catheter care, hydration guidance, activity/lifting restrictions and follow-up. Report fever, worsening pain, inability to drain, heavy bleeding/clots or dizziness. Discuss sexual/urinary function concerns without stigma.
## PNLE traps
For CBI, actual urine output = total drainage minus irrigant infused. Do not remove or manipulate catheter without an order; sudden no drainage with pain suggests obstruction.
