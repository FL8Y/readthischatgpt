# Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Pathophysiology and manifestations
BPH is nonmalignant enlargement of prostate tissue that can obstruct urinary outflow. Symptoms include hesitancy, weak stream, intermittency, incomplete emptying, frequency, urgency and nocturia. Acute retention causes painful inability to void and requires urgent assessment.
## Assessment and diagnostics
Assess urinary symptoms, bladder distention, postvoid residual, urinalysis, renal function and complications. PSA may be discussed based on age/risk and shared decision-making; BPH itself and other conditions can elevate PSA, so it is not a cancer diagnosis. Hematuria, recurrent infection, stones or renal impairment require evaluation.
## Management
Options include watchful waiting/lifestyle changes, alpha-1 blockers (e.g., tamsulosin) for symptom relief, 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors (finasteride/dutasteride) for enlarged prostate and selected procedures. Alpha blockers can cause orthostatic hypotension; caution with other BP-lowering agents and PDE5 inhibitors. 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors can cause sexual adverse effects, lower PSA and are contraindicated in pregnancy exposure handling precautions due to fetal risk.
## Nursing teaching
Timed voiding, reducing evening fluids/caffeine/alcohol when relevant, medication adherence and fall precautions if dizzy. Review OTC decongestants/anticholinergics, which may worsen retention.
## PNLE traps
Acute retention is urgent. PSA can be affected by BPH and medicines; interpret trend and medication history. Alpha blockers improve symptoms but do not shrink prostate quickly.
