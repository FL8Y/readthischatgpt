# BPH Management and Medication Safety

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Treatment selection
Choice depends on symptom burden, prostate size, complications, patient preference and comorbidities. Alpha blockers (tamsulosin, alfuzosin, doxazosin, terazosin) relax smooth muscle and act relatively quickly; 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors reduce prostate volume over months and are most useful with enlargement. Combination therapy may be used for selected patients.
## Key adverse effects and contraindications
Alpha blockers: dizziness, orthostatic hypotension and syncope; monitor BP and falls, especially at initiation/dose changes. Tamsulosin can cause ejaculatory changes and intraoperative floppy iris syndrome—tell eye surgeons before cataract surgery. Finasteride/dutasteride: decreased libido/erectile or ejaculatory changes, breast symptoms, fetal risk with handling crushed tablets (finasteride) or leaking capsules (dutasteride); pregnant people should avoid contact. These drugs lower PSA, which must be considered in screening interpretation.
Antimuscarinics or beta-3 agonists may be used for storage symptoms in selected patients, but assess residual urine/retention risk and BP/other contraindications.
## Escalation
Acute inability to void, fever/flank pain, gross hematuria with clots, renal impairment or recurrent infection requires urgent evaluation.
## PNLE traps
Alpha blockers can cause first-dose/postural hypotension; use fall precautions. Do not interpret PSA without accounting for 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor use. Review OTC sympathomimetic decongestants.
