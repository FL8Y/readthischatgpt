# Peritoneal Dialysis

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Principle and technique
Peritoneal dialysis uses the peritoneal membrane for solute and fluid exchange through dialysate inflow, dwell and drainage. Maintain strict aseptic technique during connections and follow prescribed concentration, volume and cycle schedule.
## Nursing assessment
Record inflow/outflow, appearance, dwell times, weight, BP, edema, respiratory status and glucose as relevant. Effluent should generally be clear; cloudy drainage, abdominal pain, fever or tenderness raises concern for peritonitis and requires urgent reporting, specimen collection and prescribed treatment.
## Complications
Peritonitis, exit-site/tunnel infection, catheter obstruction, leaks/hernias, poor ultrafiltration and hyperglycemia from glucose-containing dialysate can occur. Ensure drainage bag is below abdomen and check for kinks/positioning problems when outflow is poor; do not force flush unless protocol/order directs.
## Teaching
Hand hygiene, mask/aseptic connection technique, exit-site care, supply storage and daily weight are important. Report cloudy effluent, fever, abdominal pain, reduced drainage or breathlessness. Diet/fluid guidance is individualized.
## PNLE traps
Cloudy effluent is peritonitis until evaluated. Aseptic technique is central. Do not delay notifying the dialysis team while waiting for severe symptoms.
