# Hemodialysis Access: AV Fistula and Graft Care

**Status:** Researched draft; source and clinical audit pending. **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.

## Access types and assessment
An arteriovenous fistula (AVF) connects an artery and vein; an AV graft uses a prosthetic conduit. A central venous catheter may be used when needed but carries greater infection/thrombosis risk. Assess thrill/bruit, skin integrity, pain, swelling, distal perfusion and signs of infection.
## Protect the access
Avoid BP cuffs, venipuncture, IVs, tight clothing and sustained pressure on the access arm. Check for a thrill/bruit per local protocol; absence of a previously present thrill/bruit requires urgent dialysis-team notification. Do not sleep on or compress the access.
## Complications
Thrombosis/stenosis, bleeding, infection, aneurysm, steal syndrome (cold/painful/numb hand) and high-output cardiac strain can occur. Active bleeding requires direct pressure and emergency escalation; do not use clamps or improvised devices.
## Nursing care
Before dialysis, assess weight, BP, volume status and access function. After dialysis, assess hemostasis, dizziness/hypotension and access. Teach access protection and prompt reporting of redness, fever, swelling, pain, hand ischemia or absent thrill.
## PNLE traps
No thrill/bruit may indicate access occlusion; protect access arm. Catheter infection can progress rapidly to sepsis.
