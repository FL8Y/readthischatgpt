# Hemodialysis Vascular Access: Nursing Care

## Access types
Hemodialysis relies on dependable vascular access. An arteriovenous (AV) fistula connects an artery and vein and is preferred when feasible because of durability and lower infection risk. An AV graft uses a synthetic conduit. A central venous dialysis catheter may be used when other access is unavailable or immature, but carries greater risk of bloodstream infection and thrombosis. Choice is individualized with the patient and renal team.

## Assessment and safety
Inspect access for redness, drainage, swelling, bleeding, aneurysmal change or skin breakdown. Palpate for a thrill and auscultate for a bruit according to training and policy. Sudden absence/change of thrill or bruit may suggest thrombosis or access failure and needs urgent notification. Cold/pale/painful hand, prolonged bleeding, fever or purulent drainage also requires prompt assessment. Catheter exit sites and hubs require strict aseptic technique by trained staff.

## Protect the AV access limb
Avoid routine BP cuffs, venipuncture, IV infusions and constrictive clothing/jewelry on the access arm, unless an emergency/specialist-directed exception applies. Teach the patient to check the thrill as instructed and report changes, swelling, pain, coolness, bleeding or infection signs. Do not cannulate an immature or compromised access except under dialysis-team protocol.

## Dialysis monitoring
Verify the dialysis prescription, access, anticoagulation plan and baseline assessment. Monitor for hypotension, cramps, nausea, headache/confusion, bleeding and access complications during/after dialysis. After needle removal, obtain hemostasis without excessive pressure that occludes flow; check access flow remains present. Follow local policy for catheter dressing/cap care and documentation.

## PNLE high yield
- The bruit/thrill supports the presence of flow but does not rule out every stenosis.
- Absent thrill/bruit may signal access thrombosis: escalate urgently.
- Protect the AV-access limb from routine BP and venipuncture.
- Fever in a catheter-dependent dialysis patient raises concern for bloodstream infection.
