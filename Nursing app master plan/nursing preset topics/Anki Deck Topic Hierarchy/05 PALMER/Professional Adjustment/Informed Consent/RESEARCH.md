# Informed Consent — PNLE Research

## Definition and essential elements
Informed consent is a voluntary, informed authorization by a person with decision-making capacity for a proposed intervention. Valid consent generally requires:
- adequate disclosure of the nature and purpose of the intervention;
- material benefits, risks, and reasonable alternatives, including no treatment where relevant;
- understanding, an opportunity to ask questions, and a voluntary choice free from coercion;
- decision-making capacity for the specific decision;
- documentation consistent with law and institutional policy.

A signature is evidence of a process, not a substitute for understanding. The clinician responsible for the intervention ordinarily explains its material details; the nurse supports understanding, witnesses/signs only within policy, checks for unresolved questions, and alerts the responsible clinician when consent appears invalid or incomplete.

## Nursing responsibilities
Confirm the correct patient and procedure; check that the consent process is completed before a non-emergency intervention; ensure the patient has an opportunity to ask questions and interpreter/accessibility support when needed; do not pressure the patient; clarify that the patient may withdraw consent; document according to policy. Do not perform the role of the procedural clinician by giving explanations outside competence.

## Capacity, surrogates, minors, and emergencies
Capacity is decision-specific and may fluctuate. Do not equate disability, age, language difference, or refusal with incapacity. Follow applicable Philippine law and institutional rules for minors, representatives, guardians, and patients unable to decide. Emergency exceptions are narrow and jurisdiction-dependent; do not assume that convenience or delay alone removes consent requirements. Seek the responsible clinician/supervisor when authority is uncertain.

## Refusal
A capable patient may refuse after receiving understandable information. Assess immediate risk, clarify the patient's understanding, inform the responsible clinician, offer alternatives without coercion, and document the discussion and notifications.

## PNLE safety points
- Consent must precede the intervention, not be obtained retrospectively.
- A sedated, coerced, or inadequately informed signature may not represent valid consent.
- Witnessing a signature does not automatically mean the nurse supplied the full procedural disclosure.
- For a change in procedure or material new risk, reassess whether additional consent is required.

## Philippine context
Apply RA 9173, applicable Philippine patient-rights and consent law, current DOH/PRC guidance, and facility policy. Research involving humans has additional ethics-review requirements and should follow the applicable research ethics committee and national ethical guidelines.
