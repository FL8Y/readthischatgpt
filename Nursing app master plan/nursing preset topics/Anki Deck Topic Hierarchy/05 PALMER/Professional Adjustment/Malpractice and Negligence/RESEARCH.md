# Malpractice and Negligence in Nursing — PNLE Research

## Concepts
**Negligence** is failure to exercise reasonable care in circumstances where a duty exists, with legally relevant causation and harm. **Professional malpractice** generally refers to professional negligence assessed against the applicable professional standard. The precise legal definition and elements depend on Philippine law and current jurisprudence.

Common risk patterns include medication errors, failure to monitor or report deterioration, wrong-patient/procedure events, falls where precautions were indicated, unsafe delegation, breaches in infection prevention, inadequate handoff, and inaccurate documentation. Not every complication or adverse outcome is malpractice; assess the standard of care, facts, causation, and harm.

## Prevention
- Use patient identifiers, allergy checks, medication rights, and independent checks when required.
- Follow infection-prevention and procedure protocols.
- Assess and reassess; recognize and escalate red flags promptly.
- Clarify questionable orders and tasks outside scope or competence.
- Delegate only when lawful and safe; supervise and follow up.
- Give structured handoffs and document objective, timely facts.
- Report incidents and near misses according to policy; support learning and patient safety.

## After an error or adverse event
First protect and assess the patient, initiate appropriate urgent care, notify the responsible clinician/supervisor, and follow disclosure and reporting policies. Document the clinical facts, assessment, actions, notifications, and response objectively according to policy. Do not alter the chart, conceal the event, assign blame in the medical record, or delay care while completing paperwork. Incident reports are handled according to institutional policy and are not a substitute for clinical documentation.

## Legal/ethical distinctions
- Negligence is not the same as an intentional tort such as battery or assault.
- Professional discipline, civil liability, and criminal liability are different processes with different standards.
- A poor outcome alone does not prove malpractice.
- A nurse remains responsible for personal conduct and must also comply with lawful professional standards and reporting duties.

## Philippine context
Use RA 9173, current PRC/BON professional standards, applicable Civil Code and jurisprudence, and institutional policy. Foreign legal textbooks can explain concepts but cannot establish the controlling Philippine rule.
