# Patient Autonomy — PNLE Research

## Meaning
Autonomy is respect for a person's ability and right to make informed, voluntary decisions about their own care. It includes receiving understandable information, asking questions, choosing among reasonable options, accepting or refusing treatment, and having privacy and dignity respected.

## Nursing application
- Provide information within role and ensure the responsible clinician answers clinical questions outside nursing scope.
- Assess whether the patient understands, has decision-making capacity for this choice, and is free from coercion.
- Support communication with interpreters and accessible formats; do not assume language difficulty means incapacity.
- Respect informed refusal, explore concerns without judgment, explain foreseeable consequences, notify the care team, and document.
- Protect confidentiality and involve family/support people according to the patient's wishes and applicable law.
- Advocate when the patient's preferences are ignored or substituted by others without proper authority.

## Limits and difficult cases
Autonomy does not mean every requested intervention must be provided; clinicians must consider professional standards, safety, resource allocation, and law. Capacity is decision-specific and may fluctuate. A surrogate's authority depends on applicable law and the circumstances; relatives do not automatically override a capable adult. Emergency and public-health exceptions are legally bounded and should not be invented by bedside staff.

## Ethical tensions
Beneficence may conflict with autonomy when a patient refuses a recommended intervention. The nurse should communicate risks, assess understanding and capacity, seek a responsible clinician/ethics consultation as needed, and avoid coercion. A patient's choice may differ from the team's recommendation without being irrational or invalid.

## PNLE prioritization
Immediate danger prompts rapid assessment and communication, but urgency alone does not automatically cancel a capable patient's informed choice. Distinguish incapacity from an unpopular decision; distinguish a patient's preference from a legally authorized surrogate's decision.

## Philippine context and references
Apply RA 9173, current PRC/BON Code of Ethics, relevant Philippine patient-rights law, and facility policy. The ICN Code provides an international ethical framework.
