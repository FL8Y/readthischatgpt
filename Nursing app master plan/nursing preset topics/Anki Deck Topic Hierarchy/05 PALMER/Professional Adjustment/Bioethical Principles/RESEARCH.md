# Bioethical Principles in Nursing — PNLE Research

## Core framework
Bioethics applies moral reasoning to decisions affecting patients, families, and communities. Four widely taught principles are:
- **Autonomy:** respect an informed person's right to make voluntary decisions.
- **Beneficence:** act to promote the patient's welfare.
- **Nonmaleficence:** avoid or minimize preventable harm; weigh foreseeable risks against benefits.
- **Justice:** distribute care, risks, benefits, and resources fairly without unjust discrimination.

Related duties include fidelity (keeping commitments), veracity (truthfulness), confidentiality, accountability, respect for dignity, and advocacy. Principles can conflict; use facts, capacity, patient preferences, proportionality, law, professional standards, consultation, and documentation rather than treating one principle as an automatic answer.

## Clinical application
1. Identify the decision, affected people, urgency, and clinical facts.
2. Assess decision-making capacity for this decision and time; do not equate disagreement with incapacity.
3. Explain material benefits, risks, alternatives, and consequences in understandable language.
4. Respect a capable patient's informed refusal while explaining risks and offering alternatives; follow emergency and legal procedures where capacity or immediate danger is in question.
5. Protect privacy, disclose only on a lawful/necessary basis, and document reasoning and consultation.
6. Escalate unresolved conflicts to the responsible clinician, ethics service, or appropriate authority.

## PNLE prioritization and pitfalls
- Immediate life threats require prompt safety action, but do not casually erase a capable patient's rights.
- Beneficence does not mean paternalism; “best interests” must not automatically replace a capable patient's choice.
- Justice means fair access and non-discrimination, not identical treatment when clinical needs differ.
- Confidentiality has lawful exceptions; avoid promising absolute secrecy.
- Apply Maslow/ABCs to clinical urgency, not as a shortcut to dismiss ethical rights.

## Philippine context
Use the Philippine Nursing Act (RA 9173), the current PRC Board of Nursing Code of Ethics and applicable laws/policies. Privacy duties also engage the Data Privacy Act (RA 10173). Distinguish professional ethics frameworks from specific legal requirements; consult current official texts and facility policy.
