# Doctrines of Negligence — PNLE Research

## Negligence and elements
Negligence is a failure to use the level of care a reasonably prudent person would use in comparable circumstances, causing legally recognized harm. In a clinical claim, commonly examined elements are **duty**, **breach**, **causation** (actual and legally recognized/proximate), and **damages**. Exact legal tests and burdens depend on the applicable jurisdiction and case.

## Related doctrines
- **Res ipsa loquitur** (“the thing speaks for itself”): in some circumstances, the nature of an event may support an inference of negligence when it ordinarily would not occur without negligence, the relevant instrumentality was under the defendant's control, and other causes are sufficiently excluded. It is not automatic proof in every adverse outcome.
- **Negligence per se:** in some legal systems, violation of a safety statute may help establish breach if statutory conditions are met; applicability is jurisdiction-specific.
- **Vicarious liability / respondeat superior:** an employer may be responsible for certain employee acts within the scope of employment, depending on law and facts; it does not erase individual professional responsibility.
- **Contributory/comparative negligence** and **assumption of risk** vary greatly by jurisdiction and should not be generalized to Philippine cases without legal authority.

## Nursing risk reduction
Follow current standards and lawful orders; verify patient identity, allergies, medications, calculations, and procedures; monitor and escalate deterioration; communicate handoffs clearly; document contemporaneously and objectively; preserve evidence and report incidents through policy. Never alter records, backdate entries, speculate, or conceal errors.

## PNLE distinctions
An undesirable outcome alone does not establish negligence. A breach must be assessed against the applicable standard and causal relationship to harm. Conversely, an omission can be negligent even if no injury is ultimately proven for a particular claim, depending on the legal issue. Do not confuse civil negligence with intentional torts or professional discipline.

## Philippine context and limits
Philippine civil liability and professional regulation require jurisdiction-specific legal analysis. Use RA 9173, applicable Civil Code provisions, current jurisprudence, and official professional standards; textbook doctrines from US law may not map directly onto Philippine doctrine. This educational summary is not legal advice.
