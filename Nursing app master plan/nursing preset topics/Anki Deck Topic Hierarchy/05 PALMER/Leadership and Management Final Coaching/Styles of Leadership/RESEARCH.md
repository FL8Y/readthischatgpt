# Leadership Styles in Nursing — PNLE Research

## Common styles
- **Autocratic/directive:** leader decides quickly and gives clear direction. Useful in emergencies or when rapid coordination is essential; overuse suppresses input.
- **Democratic/participative:** staff contribute to decisions. Supports engagement and problem solving but may take more time.
- **Laissez-faire/delegative:** high autonomy with limited direction. May work with expert, self-directed teams and clear accountability; unsafe when staff need guidance.
- **Transactional:** emphasizes defined expectations, performance, and consequences/rewards; useful for clarity and routine compliance but may not inspire broader change alone.
- **Transformational:** communicates vision, encourages development and improvement, and motivates beyond simple exchange.
- **Servant/supportive:** prioritizes staff development, listening, and removal of barriers while maintaining standards.
- **Situational/contingency:** adapts style to staff readiness, task risk, urgency, and context; no single style is best for every situation.

## Leadership vs management
Leadership influences direction, motivation, and change; management coordinates resources, processes, and performance. They overlap and both are needed for safe nursing services.

## Clinical application
In an emergency, clear directive leadership and closed-loop communication can reduce ambiguity. For quality improvement, participative or transformational approaches may improve ownership. A novice staff member may need structured coaching; an experienced team may function with greater autonomy. Regardless of style, the leader must uphold scope, fairness, accountability, and patient safety.

## PNLE high-yield
- Emergency does not mean abusive or disrespectful leadership.
- Democratic leadership is not always optimal when seconds matter.
- Laissez-faire is not the same as appropriate delegation; absence of oversight is unsafe.
- Effective leaders adapt while preserving ethical and legal standards.
