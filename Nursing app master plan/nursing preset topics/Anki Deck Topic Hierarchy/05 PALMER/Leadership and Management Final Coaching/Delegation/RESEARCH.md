# Delegation in Nursing — PNLE-Focused Research
Last reviewed: 2026-10-17.

## Definition
Delegation is the authorization of a specific activity to a competent person when law, role, policy, training, and circumstances permit it. It is clinical judgment, not merely workload distribution. The delegating nurse retains responsibility for the delegation decision, nursing judgment, and appropriate supervision of overall care. The delegatee is responsible for accepting only work they can safely perform, completing it correctly, reporting concerns, and documenting.

## Assignment, delegation, and handoff
- **Assignment:** allocating duties already within a person's role, scope, and competence.
- **Delegation:** authorizing a specific activity with clear directions and appropriate oversight.
- **Handoff:** transfer of information/continuity between clinicians, such as RN-to-RN report; NCSBN/ANA distinguishes this from delegation.
Delegation cannot expand legal scope or make an unqualified person qualified.

## Five rights
| Right | Check | Safe application |
|---|---|---|
| Task | Is it legally/institutionally delegable? | Do not delegate nursing judgment or assessment/evaluation requiring licensed judgment. |
| Circumstance | Is the patient stable and situation predictable? | Deterioration requires prompt licensed assessment and intervention. |
| Person | Is recipient authorized, trained, and competent now? | Verify competence; do not infer it from job title. |
| Directions/communication | Are task, limits, timing, reporting triggers, and documentation clear? | Give specific instructions and invite clarification. |
| Supervision/evaluation | Can suitable oversight and follow-up be provided? | Monitor according to risk, reassess patient, and check outcome. |

The five rights are a professional safety framework, not a substitute for Philippine law or facility policy.

## Responsibilities
**Delegating nurse:** assess patient stability, task risk, recipient competence, resources, and supervision; ensure delegation is permitted; provide clear directions; follow up and reassess; intervene and report competency concerns as needed.
**Delegatee:** accept only authorized work within competence; ask questions or decline/escalate if unsafe; perform correctly; promptly report abnormal findings, changes, errors, or inability to complete; document accurately. Under the NCSBN/ANA model, the delegatee does not re-delegate.
**Organization/nurse leader:** maintain lawful policies, clear roles, training and competency validation, adequate supervision/resources, and reporting systems.

## Tasks requiring nursing judgment and safety exceptions
Assessment and interpretation, clinical prioritization, care planning, evaluation, and decisions to change the plan generally require licensed nursing judgment. Do not delegate a task outside the recipient's authorized scope or demonstrated competence, or where risk exceeds the person's role or available supervision. Routine predictable activities for a stable patient may be considered only if local law/policy allows, competence is verified, directions are clear, and follow-up is feasible.

## Red flags
- Deterioration: notify licensed nurse promptly; reassess/escalate.
- Recipient lacks competence or refuses: do not coerce; arrange qualified help and inform supervisor.
- Scope or directions unclear: pause and clarify.
- Error/near miss: protect patient first, notify responsible clinician/supervisor, document objectively, and follow incident policy.
- No supervision/resources: do not delegate work that cannot be safely overseen.

## PNLE prioritization
1. ABCs and immediate threats take precedence over routine task distribution.
2. Maslow: threatened physiologic needs and immediate safety outrank routine comfort tasks.
3. Nursing process: assessment, interpretation, planning, and evaluation depend on nursing judgment; implementation tasks may be delegated only when lawful and safe.
4. Actual acute deterioration usually outranks a stable patient's routine activity.
5. Reassess: changing circumstances can invalidate the delegation decision.

## Philippine context
RA 9173 (Philippine Nursing Act of 2002) provides the legal framework for nursing practice. Consult current PRC/Board of Nursing standards, relevant DOH guidance, institutional policy, and the person's actual role/competence before treating a task as delegable. NCSBN/ANA guidance is a US professional framework, **not Philippine law**. Sources reviewed here do not establish a universal Philippine task-by-task delegation list.

## Common misconceptions
| Incorrect idea | Correction |
|---|---|
| Delegation transfers the whole patient. | It authorizes a specific activity; the nurse retains overall nursing accountability and oversight duties. |
| Delegatees have no responsibility. | They must be competent, perform correctly, communicate concerns, and document. |
| Stable patient means any task can be delegated. | Authorization, competence, communication, resources, and supervision still matter. |
| Five rights are Philippine law. | They are a framework; Philippine law and applicable policies govern. |
| No follow-up is needed. | Supervision, follow-up, evaluation, and reassessment remain essential. |

## Practical delegation decision pathway

Before delegating, ask in sequence:
1. **Is the activity permitted?** Check Philippine law, current PRC/BON guidance, facility policy, role description, and any required order or supervision.
2. **Does the activity require licensed nursing judgment?** Assessment/interpretation, prioritization, care planning, evaluation, and decisions about changes in condition generally require an appropriately licensed nurse.
3. **Is the patient/situation appropriate?** Consider stability, predictability, risk, urgency, complexity, and likelihood that the task may require nursing judgment during performance.
4. **Is the person authorized and competent now?** Confirm training, demonstrated skill, experience, workload, and willingness; job title alone is insufficient.
5. **Can directions and reporting triggers be made clear?** State task, patient-specific limits, timing, expected result, what must be reported immediately, and documentation requirements.
6. **Can the nurse supervise and evaluate?** Ensure resources and availability for questions, observation, reassessment, and intervention if the situation changes.
7. **Did the outcome meet expectations?** Reassess the patient, verify completion, address concerns, and review whether the delegation decision remains appropriate.

If any condition is not met, do not proceed as planned. Clarify, provide the required licensed care, arrange a qualified person, or escalate the staffing/resource issue. Do not use delegation to conceal inadequate staffing.

## Scenario-based application

- **Stable patient needing a routine permitted activity:** delegation may be considered only if the task is authorized, competence is verified, instructions are clear, and supervision is available.
- **New confusion, respiratory distress, unexpected bleeding, or rapid vital-sign change:** requires prompt licensed assessment and escalation; do not delegate the judgment to an unqualified person or leave the finding for routine reporting.
- **Delegatee reports uncertainty or refuses:** treat this as a safety signal, not insubordination; clarify and arrange appropriate care.
- **Workload prevents supervision:** delegation may be unsafe even if the task and person are otherwise suitable; escalate the resource problem.

## Philippine legal caution

The five-rights model is a decision aid, not a task-by-task legal authorization. Philippine scope and accountability must be determined from current RA 9173/IRR, applicable PRC/BON guidance, DOH rules where relevant, facility policy, the actual role of the delegatee, and the patient's circumstances. Do not import a U.S. task list as Philippine law. If local authority is unclear, obtain supervisor/regulatory guidance before delegating a nonurgent task; urgent patient-safety threats require immediate action through the proper clinical chain.
