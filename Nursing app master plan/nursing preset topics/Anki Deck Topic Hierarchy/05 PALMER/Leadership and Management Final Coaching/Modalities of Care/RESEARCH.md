# Modalities of Care — Nursing Management and PNLE Research

## Concept
Care modalities describe how nursing services are organized and how patient-care responsibility is allocated. Models differ by continuity, accountability, staffing needs, patient acuity, and coordination.

## Common models
- **Total patient care/case method:** one nurse provides most or all care for assigned patients during a shift. Can support continuity during that shift but requires adequate skill and staffing.
- **Functional nursing:** tasks are divided among staff. May be efficient but can fragment care and blur ownership unless communication is strong.
- **Team nursing:** a team led by an RN cares for a group of patients. Roles, coordination, and RN oversight must be clear.
- **Primary nursing:** a primary nurse coordinates an individualized plan across the stay, with associate nurses providing care when needed. Supports continuity but requires strong communication and competent staffing.
- **Case management:** coordinates services, transitions, resource use, and outcomes across settings; it does not mean the case manager performs every intervention.
- **Patient-/family-centered care:** emphasizes partnership, shared decisions, respect, and coordination around patient needs.

## Choosing a modality
Consider acuity, complexity, staff competence, workload, continuity, resources, and setting. No model compensates for unsafe staffing or poor handoff. Clearly define who assesses, plans, implements, evaluates, escalates, and communicates with the patient/family.

## PNLE safety points
Functional nursing can fragment care; maintain a clear coordinator and reliable handoffs. Team nursing never permits tasks outside scope or competence. Primary nursing requires associate staff to communicate changes. Patient deterioration overrides routine task allocation.
