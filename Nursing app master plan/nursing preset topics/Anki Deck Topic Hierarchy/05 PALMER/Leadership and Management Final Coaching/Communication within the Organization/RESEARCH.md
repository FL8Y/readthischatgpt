# Communication Within the Healthcare Organization — PNLE Research

## Definition and channels
Organizational communication is the exchange of information needed to coordinate care, decisions, and operations. It may be **downward** (leaders to staff), **upward** (staff to leaders), **horizontal** (peers/units), or **diagonal/cross-functional**. Channels include verbal, written, electronic, formal reports, huddles, handoffs, and incident escalation.

## Safety-critical communication
- Use structured handoff such as SBAR (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) where adopted.
- Identify the patient and communicate current status, relevant history, risks, pending tests/actions, and explicit next steps.
- Use closed-loop communication for critical instructions: receiver repeats/acknowledges; sender confirms.
- Escalate abnormal findings promptly using the chain of command; do not let hierarchy delay emergency care.
- Document clinical communication accurately, including who was notified, when, the information conveyed, orders received, and patient response according to policy.

## Barriers and remedies
Noise, interruptions, language differences, jargon, assumptions, fatigue, hierarchy, poor documentation, and ambiguous orders increase error risk. Use plain language, interpreters when needed, read-back for verbal orders when policy requires, standardized formats, and opportunities for questions. Electronic messages do not replace urgent direct escalation.

## Confidentiality
Share only with authorized persons for legitimate care or lawful purposes. Avoid public discussions and insecure messaging. Follow privacy/security policy and applicable Philippine law.

## PNLE priorities
An urgent change in condition requires direct timely escalation, not merely a routine note or email. For a handoff, prioritize current instability, allergies, critical medications, safety risks, and time-sensitive tasks. Communication is complete only when information is understood and necessary action is taken.
