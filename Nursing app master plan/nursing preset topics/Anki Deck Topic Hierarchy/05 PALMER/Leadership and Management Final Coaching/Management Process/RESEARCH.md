# Management Process in Nursing — PNLE Research

## Core functions
A common management framework is **planning, organizing, staffing, directing/leading, and controlling**. Terminology varies by textbook; some frameworks combine or rename functions. They form a continuous cycle rather than isolated steps.

- **Planning:** define goals, assess needs, forecast resources, choose actions, and set measures.
- **Organizing:** define roles, authority, workflows, and coordination.
- **Staffing:** recruit, orient, schedule, develop, and maintain a competent workforce.
- **Directing/leading:** communicate, motivate, supervise, coach, resolve conflict, and support decisions.
- **Controlling:** measure performance, compare it with standards, correct gaps, and evaluate results.

## Nursing unit application
For a rise in falls: assess data and contributing factors; set a measurable target; assign roles and resources; train and communicate with staff; implement prevention; audit outcomes and adjust. A manager should involve frontline staff and patients where appropriate and avoid choosing interventions before understanding the problem.

## Levels and skills
Top-level managers emphasize strategy and organizational direction; middle managers translate strategy into programs; first-line managers coordinate daily operations. Effective managers use technical, interpersonal, communication, and conceptual skills. Authority is the formal right to direct work; responsibility is the duty to perform; accountability is answerability for outcomes within one's role.

## Priorities and ethics
Patient safety, lawful staffing/scope, continuity, and essential resources take precedence over cosmetic targets. Delegation does not eliminate oversight. Management decisions should be fair, transparent, evidence-informed, and responsive to staff concerns.
