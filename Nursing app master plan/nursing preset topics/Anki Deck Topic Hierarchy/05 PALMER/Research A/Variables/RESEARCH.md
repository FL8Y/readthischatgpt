# Variables in Nursing Research

## Definition and types
A variable is a characteristic that can take different values. An operational definition states exactly how it is measured or categorized.

- **Independent/exposure variable:** predictor, exposure, or intervention of interest; in observational research, it is not necessarily manipulated.
- **Dependent/outcome variable:** result measured.
- **Confounder:** associated with exposure and outcome, and can distort their apparent relationship if not addressed.
- **Effect modifier:** a variable across whose levels the association/effect differs; this is not simply the same as confounding.
- **Control/covariate:** variable included in design or analysis to account for differences.
- **Extraneous variable:** other factor that may influence results; whether it biases an estimate depends on its relationship and handling.

## Measurement and operationalization
Specify construct, instrument, units, timing, scoring, categories, and cutoffs. A construct such as anxiety may be operationalized using a named validated scale, but scores are not identical to the full construct. Measurement must fit language, culture, age, and clinical setting.

## Causal caution
An independent variable in a regression model is not automatically a cause. Confounding, reverse causation, selection bias, and measurement error can produce misleading associations. Random assignment strengthens causal inference but does not remove every problem.

## PNLE example
When studying falls, patient acuity may influence both staffing allocation and fall risk; it can confound the staffing-outcome association. If staffing's effect differs by acuity level, acuity may also be an effect modifier, depending on the causal question and analysis.
