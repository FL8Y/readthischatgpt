# Reliability, Validity, and Trustworthiness — Nursing Research

## Quantitative measurement
- **Reliability:** consistency of a measurement under specified conditions. Forms include test-retest, inter-rater, internal consistency, and parallel forms.
- **Validity:** evidence that supports the intended interpretation and use of scores. Content validity examines coverage; construct validity examines theoretical relationships; criterion validity compares with a relevant criterion.
- A tool can be reliable but invalid: a scale may consistently measure the wrong construct. Reliability is necessary for many uses but not sufficient for validity.

## Qualitative trustworthiness
Common criteria include:
- **Credibility:** confidence that interpretations represent participants/data.
- **Dependability:** transparent, traceable process over time.
- **Confirmability:** findings grounded in data rather than unexamined researcher preference.
- **Transferability:** enough contextual detail for readers to judge applicability elsewhere.

Strategies include triangulation, reflexivity, audit trails, peer debriefing, thick description, and participant feedback when appropriate.

## Practical appraisal
Check the population and setting in which a tool was evaluated, language/cultural adaptation, scoring, missing-data handling, and whether reliability/validity evidence matches the intended use. A coefficient does not establish all aspects of validity. Translation alone does not validate an instrument.

## PNLE high-yield
- Reliability = consistency; validity = accuracy/fitness of interpretation; trustworthiness = rigor in qualitative inquiry.
- High internal consistency can reflect redundant items.
- Validity is about interpretation/use in context, not a permanent label attached to a tool.
