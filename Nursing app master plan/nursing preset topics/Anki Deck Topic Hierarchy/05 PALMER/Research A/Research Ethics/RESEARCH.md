# Research Ethics — Nursing Research

## Definition and purpose

Research ethics is the application of moral principles, professional obligations, and governing requirements to the design and conduct of research. Its purpose is to protect the dignity, rights, safety, privacy, and welfare of participants while supporting scientifically trustworthy knowledge. Ethical research is not achieved by obtaining a signature or an ethics approval alone; ethical duties continue throughout recruitment, data collection, analysis, publication, and follow-up.

A study can be ethically unacceptable even if its question is interesting: an invalid or poorly designed study may expose people to burden or risk without a reasonable prospect of producing useful knowledge.

## Core principles

| Principle | Meaning | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Respect for persons / autonomy | Recognize a person's capacity for self-determination and provide protection where autonomy is limited. | Meaningful informed consent, voluntary recruitment, respect for refusal, accessible information, and safeguards for decision-making limitations. |
| Beneficence | Promote participant welfare and seek a favorable balance of potential benefits and risks. | Design risk-reduction procedures, use competent staff, monitor safety, and make referral plans. |
| Nonmaleficence | Avoid or minimize preventable harm. | Do not expose participants to avoidable physical, psychological, social, legal, financial, or privacy harms. |
| Justice | Distribute burdens and benefits fairly. | Use defensible recruitment criteria; do not select groups merely because they are easy to pressure or exclude groups without scientific/ethical justification. |
| Scientific integrity | Use methods capable of answering the research question and report results honestly. | Prespecify methods, preserve records, report limitations and adverse findings, and avoid fabrication, falsification, and plagiarism. |
| Accountability and transparency | Make decisions reviewable and disclose relevant interests and procedures. | Maintain an audit trail, disclose conflicts, document protocol changes, and report required events to the REC and sponsor. |

These principles overlap and may conflict in a particular case. Researchers should explain and document the reasoning rather than invoke a principle as a slogan.

## Ethical review and the research ethics committee

Before starting human-participant research, determine whether the project requires REC review under the applicable PHREB guidance, institutional policy, and other regulatory requirements. Do not decide on one's own that a project is exempt merely because it is a student project, uses a survey, or seems low risk. Submit the protocol or seek a formal determination from the responsible office.

An REC's review commonly considers:
- scientific and social value and whether the design can answer the question;
- foreseeable risks, potential benefits, and risk-minimization measures;
- participant selection, recruitment language, incentives, and power imbalances;
- informed-consent documents, comprehension, voluntariness, and special safeguards;
- privacy, confidentiality, data access, storage, retention, sharing, and disposal;
- qualifications of the research team, safety monitoring, referral plans, and handling of research-related injury;
- conflicts of interest, vulnerable circumstances, and plans for reporting adverse events or protocol deviations.

Approval is not a guarantee that no harm will occur, and it does not make an unsafe procedure acceptable. Researchers must follow the approved protocol, seek approval before implementing changes when required, report required events, and remain accountable for participant protection.

## Consent and respect during participation

Consent should be an ongoing process, not a one-time administrative task. Participants need information in a language and format they can understand, an opportunity to ask questions, time to decide where feasible, and freedom from coercion or undue influence. Explain purpose, procedures, duration, foreseeable risks, potential benefits, alternatives when relevant, privacy protections and limits, compensation/injury arrangements where applicable, contacts, and withdrawal conditions.

The person obtaining consent should assess understanding and decision-making capacity for the specific decision, follow rules for minors or authorized representatives, and avoid implying that routine clinical care or benefits depend on enrollment. If new material information arises, the protocol and REC determine whether participants must be informed and re-consent obtained.

## Risk assessment and ongoing safety

Risk is not limited to physical injury. Depending on the study, harms may be psychological (distress), social (stigma or relationship damage), economic (job or financial consequences), legal, cultural, or informational (re-identification or disclosure). Assess both likelihood and seriousness, minimize unnecessary collection or exposure, and ensure the research team can recognize and respond to problems.

A defensible risk-benefit analysis asks whether risks have been minimized, whether the design is valid, whether the remaining risk is reasonable relative to the expected value and potential benefit, and whether participants receive appropriate information and safeguards. A favorable balance does not permit coercion or remove the duty to monitor harm.

## Research integrity and misconduct

- **Fabrication:** making up data or results that were never observed.
- **Falsification:** manipulating research materials, procedures, data, or results so the record is inaccurate.
- **Plagiarism:** presenting another person's words, ideas, or work as one's own without appropriate attribution.

Other serious practices include selective or misleading reporting, undisclosed conflicts of interest, improper authorship, duplicate publication, inappropriate data manipulation, and failure to preserve records. Correct errors transparently; do not conceal unfavorable results or alter data to fit a hypothesis. Distinguish an honest error from deliberate misconduct, but address both through the proper research-integrity process.

## Privacy, confidentiality, and data management

Collect only data needed for a stated legitimate purpose. Explain who will access the data, how identifiers will be handled, how records will be stored and secured, whether data may be shared or reused, how long records will be retained, and what happens at the end of the study. Coding is not the same as anonymization if a linkage key or indirect identifiers remain. Use institution-approved systems and report suspected breaches promptly through the required channel.

In the Philippines, RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012) and applicable rules are relevant to personal-data processing. Health information is sensitive personal information under the Act. Do not assume that the word “research” automatically removes privacy duties; check the applicable legal provisions, guidance, and safeguards for the actual project.

## Nursing responsibilities and PNLE application

- Protect participants before, during, and after data collection; REC approval does not replace day-to-day vigilance.
- Escalate suspected coercion, invalid consent, unexpected harm, protocol deviation, or privacy breach through the approved process.
- Separate research participation from access to clinical care, grades, employment, or other benefits where possible.
- Maintain accurate records and report results honestly, including null or unfavorable findings and limitations.
- In a priority scenario involving immediate participant deterioration, address urgent safety needs first and activate the appropriate clinical response; research procedures never take priority over emergency care.
- When asked to choose the most ethical action, prefer the option that protects participants' rights and safety, respects voluntary choice, follows approved procedures, and documents/escalates concerns rather than concealing or improvising around them.

## PNLE high-yield distinctions

- Ethics review is independent oversight; investigator responsibility continues after approval.
- Scientific validity is an ethical concern because invalid research can impose burden without adequate social value.
- Consent is voluntary and informed; a signature alone is insufficient.
- Confidentiality is a duty to protect information, not a promise that information can never be disclosed under any lawful circumstance.
- Vulnerability calls for situation-specific safeguards, not automatic exclusion.
- Fabrication = inventing data; falsification = manipulating the research record; plagiarism = misappropriating others' work.

## Scope note

This study summary does not replace the current PHREB guideline, an REC decision, institutional policy, or legal advice. Confirm the rules that apply to the specific research design and setting before implementation.
