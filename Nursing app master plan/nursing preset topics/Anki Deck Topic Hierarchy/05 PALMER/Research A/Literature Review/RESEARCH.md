# Literature Review — Nursing Research

## Purpose
A literature review critically identifies, evaluates, and synthesizes existing evidence relevant to a research question. It establishes what is known, where findings disagree, what gaps remain, and why the proposed study matters. It is not a list of article summaries.

## Process
1. Translate the question into searchable concepts and synonyms.
2. Select appropriate databases and sources (for example, PubMed/MEDLINE, CINAHL where available, Cochrane Library, Philippine government/academic repositories).
3. Set inclusion/exclusion criteria and document search dates, terms, and limits.
4. Screen titles/abstracts, then full texts; record reasons for exclusions where systematic methods are used.
5. Assess credibility, methods, risk of bias, relevance, and applicability.
6. Extract comparable details and synthesize patterns, disagreements, and limitations.
7. Cite sources accurately and keep an audit trail.

## Review types
A narrative review offers broad synthesis but may be less reproducible. A systematic review uses prespecified, transparent methods to answer a focused question. A scoping review maps the breadth and nature of evidence. A meta-analysis statistically combines comparable quantitative results and is not synonymous with every systematic review.

## Research integrity
Avoid plagiarism, selective citation, fabricated references, and overclaiming. Distinguish primary studies from reviews and guidelines. Check publication date, corrections/retractions, setting, sample, and conflicts of interest.

## Philippine relevance
Search local evidence and official DOH/PRC/Philippine research repositories when relevant; explain when evidence from other countries may not generalize to Philippine settings.
