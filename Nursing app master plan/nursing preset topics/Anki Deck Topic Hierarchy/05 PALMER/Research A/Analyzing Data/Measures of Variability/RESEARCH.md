# Measures of Variability — Nursing Research

## Purpose
Variability describes how spread out observations are. A central value without spread can be misleading because two samples may have the same mean but very different distributions.

| Measure | Meaning | Main caution |
|---|---|---|
| Range | Maximum minus minimum | Uses only extremes; highly sensitive to outliers |
| Interquartile range (IQR) | Q3 minus Q1; spread of middle 50% | Does not describe tail extremes |
| Variance | Average squared deviation from mean (sample variance uses n−1 denominator) | Squared units are less intuitive |
| Standard deviation (SD) | Square root of variance; spread in original units | Sensitive to outliers; interpretation depends on distribution |
| Coefficient of variation | SD divided by mean, often expressed as % | Meaningful mainly for ratio-scale data with a meaningful nonzero zero |

## Interpretation
For approximately normal data, about 68% of observations lie within 1 SD of the mean, about 95% within 2 SD, and about 99.7% within 3 SD (the empirical rule). Do not apply this rule automatically to skewed or multimodal data. Median with IQR is often preferable for skewed distributions.

## Nursing research application
When comparing blood pressure or length-of-stay data, report a center and spread. A smaller SD means less dispersion in that sample, not necessarily better care or more accurate measurement. Separate variability among people from uncertainty in an estimated mean (standard error/confidence interval).

## PNLE distinctions
- SD describes individual-data spread; standard error describes precision of an estimate.
- IQR is resistant to extreme values; range is not.
- Variance uses squared units; SD returns to original units.
