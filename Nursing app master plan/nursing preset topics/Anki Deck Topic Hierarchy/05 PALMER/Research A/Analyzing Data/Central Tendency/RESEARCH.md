# Measures of Central Tendency — Nursing Research / PNLE

## Purpose
Central tendency summarizes the center of a distribution. The main measures are mean, median, and mode. The appropriate measure depends on measurement level, distribution shape, outliers, and the research question.

| Measure | Meaning / calculation | Best use | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean | Sum of values divided by number of observations | Interval/ratio data; roughly symmetric distributions | Sensitive to outliers and skew |
| Median | Middle ordered value; average of two middle values when n is even | Ordinal data or skewed numeric data | Does not use the exact magnitude of every observation |
| Mode | Most frequent value/category | Nominal categories; identifying common category | May be multiple modes or no useful unique mode |

## Interpretation
For a symmetric distribution, mean and median are often close. In a right-skewed distribution, the mean is often greater than the median; in a left-skewed distribution, the mean is often lower. These are patterns, not guarantees. Report the unit, sample size, and spread alongside the center when useful.

## Nursing-research examples
For length of stay or cost, a few extreme cases can inflate the mean, so the median may better describe a typical patient. For blood group or preferred teaching language, mode is meaningful but mean is not. Do not treat ordinal categories as equal intervals without justification.

## PNLE high-yield
- Nominal variables: mode/counts/percentages.
- Ordinal variables: median and distribution are often appropriate.
- Interval/ratio variables: mean and standard deviation when assumptions fit; median and IQR for skewed data.
- A mean alone can conceal variability and outliers.
