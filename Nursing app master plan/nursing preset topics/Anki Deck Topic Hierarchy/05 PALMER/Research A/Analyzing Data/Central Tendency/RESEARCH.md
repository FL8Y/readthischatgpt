# Measures of Central Tendency — Nursing Research / PNLE

## Purpose

Central tendency summarizes the center or typical value of a distribution. The main measures are **mean, median, and mode**. The appropriate measure depends on measurement level, distribution shape, outliers, and the research question. A central value alone is incomplete without information about spread and sample size.

## Main measures

| Measure | Definition | Useful when | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mean** | Sum of observed values divided by the number of values included. | Numeric data with a meaningful arithmetic scale; often useful for approximately symmetric distributions. | Sensitive to extreme values and skew. |
| **Median** | Middle ordered value; with an even number of observations, commonly the average of the two middle values for numeric data. | Ordinal data or skewed numeric data; robust to extreme values. | Does not use the magnitude of every observation as directly as the mean. |
| **Mode** | Most frequent value or category. | Nominal/categorical data and identifying the most common response. | There may be multiple modes or no unique useful mode; it may poorly represent the distribution. |

## Choosing the measure

- For **nominal categories**, use counts/percentages and possibly the mode; a numerical mean is generally meaningless.
- For **ordinal ratings**, report category frequencies and consider median or mode where meaningful; do not assume equal spacing between categories without justification.
- For **approximately symmetric continuous data**, mean with standard deviation is often useful.
- For **skewed data or strong outliers**, median with interquartile range often describes the center and spread better.
- For **multimodal distributions**, a single mean or median may conceal distinct groups; inspect the distribution and consider a justified stratified description.

Do not select the measure simply because it produces a preferred result. Choose it based on the data and question, and report the choice transparently.

## Worked illustration

For values 2, 3, 3, 4, and 18:
- Mean = 30 / 5 = 6.
- Median = 3.
- Mode = 3.

The large value 18 pulls the mean upward, while the median better reflects the center of the four smaller observations. This does not mean the 18 should automatically be removed; first determine whether it is a valid observation, data error, or meaningful extreme.

## Nursing examples

- Average length of stay may be skewed by a small number of very long admissions; report median/IQR when appropriate.
- A patient satisfaction item with ordered categories may be better described using category percentages and ordinal summaries than an arithmetic mean alone.
- The most common reason for missed care is categorical; frequencies and percentages are more informative than a mean.

## Common errors

- Reporting mean without spread or sample size.
- Using a mean for nominal categories.
- Treating median and mean as interchangeable in skewed data.
- Removing outliers solely because they change the mean.
- Comparing means across groups without considering design, uncertainty, and confounding.
- Treating a sample mean as the exact population mean.

## PNLE high-yield review

- Mean = arithmetic average; sensitive to extremes.
- Median = middle ordered value; robust to extremes.
- Mode = most frequent value/category; can be used for nominal data.
- Match summary to data type and distribution; pair central tendency with a measure of spread.
- An unusual value should be investigated, not automatically deleted.

## Scope note

Measure selection depends on scale, distribution, study design, and analytic purpose. Use a statistician when assumptions or data structure are uncertain.
