# Error in Nursing Research — PNLE Research

## Major error types
- **Random error:** chance variation that reduces precision; often addressed by adequate sample size, reliable measurement, and statistical methods.
- **Systematic error/bias:** consistent distortion due to design, selection, measurement, confounding, or analysis; a larger sample does not automatically remove it.
- **Type I error (alpha):** rejecting a true null hypothesis (false positive).
- **Type II error (beta):** failing to reject a false null hypothesis (false negative).
- **Measurement error:** difference between observed and true value due to instrument, observer, participant, or context.
- **Sampling error:** difference between sample estimate and population parameter due to chance sampling.

## Statistical power
Power is the probability of detecting a specified effect when it truly exists under the assumed model; power = 1 − beta. It is affected by sample size, effect size, variability, alpha, and design. Raising alpha can increase power but also raises Type I error risk.

## Bias prevention
Use a well-defined population and sampling frame, validated tools, calibration, standardized procedures, blinding where feasible, prespecified analysis, transparent reporting, and appropriate handling of missing data. Randomization can balance confounders on average but does not guarantee perfect balance, especially in small samples.

## PNLE high-yield
- Type I = false positive; Type II = false negative.
- Random error affects precision; systematic bias threatens validity.
- More participants may improve precision but cannot repair a fundamentally biased sample or invalid instrument.
- A nonsignificant result does not prove no effect; consider power and confidence intervals.
