# Research Hypotheses — Nursing Research

## Definition and types
A hypothesis is a testable prediction about a relationship, difference, or effect. It should be clear, specific, and capable of being examined with defined variables and a suitable design.
- **Null hypothesis (H0):** commonly states no difference, association, or effect under the model.
- **Alternative hypothesis (H1/Ha):** states a difference, association, or effect.
- **Directional/one-tailed:** predicts direction.
- **Non-directional/two-tailed:** predicts a difference/association without specifying direction.
- **Research hypothesis:** substantive prediction in words; statistical hypotheses express parameters in formal terms.

## Quality criteria
Specify population, variables, expected relationship, and measurable outcomes. Align the hypothesis with the research question, design, sampling plan, and analysis. Qualitative exploratory studies often use research questions rather than formal statistical hypotheses.

## Testing and interpretation
A p-value is the probability, under the null model and assumptions, of data at least as incompatible with H0 as those observed; it is not the probability that H0 is true. If p is below the prespecified alpha, reject H0; otherwise, fail to reject H0 rather than “prove” it true. Interpret effect size and confidence intervals, not p-values alone.

## PNLE high-yield
- Type I error: reject a true H0.
- Type II error: fail to reject a false H0.
- A statistically significant finding may be clinically trivial.
- Do not change the hypothesis after seeing results without transparently labeling exploratory analyses.
