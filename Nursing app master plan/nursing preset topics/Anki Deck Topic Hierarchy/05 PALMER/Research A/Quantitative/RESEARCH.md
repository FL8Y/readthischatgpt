# Quantitative Research — Nursing Research / PNLE

## Purpose
Quantitative research measures variables numerically and uses statistical methods to describe patterns, estimate parameters, test hypotheses, or evaluate relationships/effects. Quality depends on design, sampling, measurement validity/reliability, control of bias/confounding, appropriate analysis, and transparent reporting.

## Common designs
- Descriptive surveys/observational studies summarize characteristics or frequency.
- Cross-sectional studies measure exposure/outcome at a point or period; temporal order may be unclear.
- Cohort studies follow exposed/unexposed groups to compare outcomes.
- Case-control studies select by outcome and look back for exposures; useful for rare outcomes but vulnerable to recall/selection bias.
- Randomized controlled trials allocate interventions randomly and can strengthen causal inference when well conducted.
- Quasi-experimental designs evaluate interventions without full randomization.

## Variables and measurement
Define independent/exposure, dependent/outcome, confounders, and operational definitions. Use tools with evidence of validity and reliability for the target population and language. Numeric coding of categories does not make a variable quantitative.

## Analysis
Use descriptive statistics to summarize data and inferential statistics to estimate uncertainty/test hypotheses. Check assumptions and study design. Report effect sizes and confidence intervals, not p-values alone. Statistical significance does not guarantee clinical importance or causality.

## Ethics
Obtain appropriate REC review, valid consent or approved waiver, privacy protections, and transparent data management. Randomization does not remove the obligation to minimize harm or monitor adverse events.

## Design choices and inference

Quantitative methods may be descriptive, correlational/analytical, quasi-experimental, or experimental. Cross-sectional studies assess variables at a defined period; cohort studies follow groups over time; case-control studies compare people with and without an outcome and look back at exposures; randomized trials assign interventions by chance. The design should fit the question and determine which claims are justified. Observational association alone does not establish causation.

## Measurement and data quality

Define each variable operationally, choose measures with appropriate validity and reliability evidence, specify timing and units, train data collectors, and standardize procedures. Check data entry, missingness, outliers, and protocol deviations without altering legitimate observations. A precise numerical result can still be wrong if the instrument measures the wrong construct or the sample is systematically biased.

## Statistical interpretation

Use descriptive statistics to summarize observed data and inferential methods to estimate population quantities or evaluate hypotheses under assumptions. Report effect estimates and uncertainty where appropriate, not only p-values. Statistical significance is not synonymous with clinical importance; a nonsignificant result is not proof of no effect. Consider confounding, selection bias, multiplicity, missing data, and whether the design supports causal language.

## Strengths and limitations

Strengths include transparent operational definitions, reproducible measurement, estimation of frequency/magnitude, and testing of prespecified hypotheses. Limitations may include reduced context, measurement error, response bias, residual confounding, model dependence, and failure to capture complex lived experience. Quantification does not eliminate researcher judgment; choices about constructs, outcomes, exclusions, and analysis must be justified.

## Ethics and Philippine context

Quantitative research involving people still requires appropriate ethics review and consent or a valid REC-approved exception where applicable. Protect personal data and explain data access, retention, and confidentiality limits. In the Philippines, consult current PHREB guidance and the responsible REC; RA 10173 is relevant to personal-data processing. Statistical analysis should never be used to disguise protocol deviations or selectively report favorable outcomes.
