# Types of Research According to Design — Nursing Research

## Descriptive
Describes characteristics, experiences, or frequencies. It can establish what is observed in a defined sample but does not by itself explain causation.

## Correlational/observational
Estimates relationships without researcher assignment of exposure. Cross-sectional, cohort, and case-control designs answer different questions and have distinct biases. Association does not automatically mean causation.

## Experimental
Researcher assigns an intervention. Randomized controlled trials use random allocation and, when well implemented, can reduce confounding. Blinding, allocation concealment, adherence, attrition, and outcome assessment still affect validity.

## Quasi-experimental
Evaluates an intervention without full random assignment, such as a non-equivalent comparison group or interrupted time series. Useful in real-world nursing settings but needs analysis of secular trends and alternative explanations.

## Qualitative and mixed methods
Qualitative designs explore meaning/process/context. Mixed methods intentionally integrate numeric and narrative evidence to answer complementary parts of a question.

## Selection rule
Match design to aim, ethics, feasibility, time, and risk of bias. Do not confuse random sampling with random assignment or survey method with design.
