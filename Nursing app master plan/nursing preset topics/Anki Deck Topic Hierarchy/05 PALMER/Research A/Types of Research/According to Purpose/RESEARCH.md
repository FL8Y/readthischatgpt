# Types of Research According to Purpose — Nursing Research

## Main categories

- **Basic/pure research:** expands knowledge or develops/tests theory without immediate practical implementation as the primary aim. It can later inform applied work.
- **Applied research:** addresses a practical problem and seeks usable knowledge, such as evaluating a nursing intervention or improving care delivery.
- **Descriptive research:** describes the distribution or characteristics of a phenomenon, population, service, or experience.
- **Exploratory research:** investigates a poorly understood issue, clarifies concepts, and generates questions or hypotheses for further study.
- **Explanatory/analytical research:** examines relationships and possible explanations. Whether it supports causal conclusions depends on design, assumptions, and evidence.
- **Evaluative research:** assesses the implementation, effectiveness, efficiency, acceptability, or impact of a program, service, policy, or intervention.
- **Action research / participatory inquiry:** involves practitioners or communities in cycles of inquiry, action, reflection, and refinement to address a local issue; methods vary by tradition.
- **Methodological research:** develops or evaluates instruments, measurement methods, procedures, or analytic approaches.

These categories overlap. A study can be applied and evaluative, descriptive and quantitative, or exploratory and qualitative.

## How purpose shapes the question

| Purpose | Example question | Common approaches |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | How does a theoretical construct relate to a nursing phenomenon? | Theory development, experimental or observational study, qualitative work as appropriate. |
| Applied | Does a defined teaching strategy improve medication-safety competence in a specified setting? | Experimental/quasi-experimental, mixed methods, or implementation study. |
| Descriptive | What proportion of nurses report a defined practice in a target setting? | Survey or observational design with explicit sampling/measurement. |
| Exploratory | How do newly diagnosed patients describe barriers to self-management? | Qualitative interviews, focus groups, exploratory mixed methods. |
| Explanatory | Is staffing associated with missed nursing care after accounting for specified factors? | Observational analytic design; causal claims require additional assumptions. |
| Evaluative | Was a community health program delivered as intended and did defined outcomes change? | Process/outcome evaluation, quasi-experiment, mixed methods. |
| Action/participatory | How can a clinical team improve a local handover process through iterative cycles? | Collaborative cycles of planning, action, observation, and reflection. |

Examples are illustrative and do not prescribe a single design.

## Purpose, design, and evidence are different

Purpose states **why** the inquiry is being done; design states **how** it is conducted; approach describes the nature of data and analysis. A descriptive purpose does not automatically mean qualitative, and an applied purpose does not automatically mean experimental. Choose the design based on the actual question, feasibility, ethical limits, and intended inference.

## Practical quality checks

- State the problem and purpose clearly.
- Align aims, objectives, outcomes, methods, and analysis.
- Identify who can use the findings and what decisions they may inform.
- Define limits: a local program evaluation may not generalize to every facility.
- Distinguish outcome evaluation from process evaluation; a program may fail because implementation differed from plan, not only because the underlying intervention is ineffective.
- Use appropriate ethics review when the project involves human participants and follow current institutional requirements.
- Report methods, limitations, and unfavorable results transparently.

## PNLE high-yield review

- Basic = knowledge/theory; applied = practical problem; descriptive = what is observed; exploratory = clarify/generate; explanatory = examine relationships; evaluative = assess a program/intervention.
- Purpose and design are separate dimensions.
- Research purpose does not automatically dictate quantitative or qualitative methods.
- Match claims and generalization to the actual design and setting.

## Scope note

Classification varies among disciplines and textbooks. Describe the actual purpose and procedures rather than relying on a label alone.
