# Ethical Principles in Nursing Research — PNLE Research

## Purpose

Research ethics protects the dignity, rights, welfare, and interests of human participants while supporting valid and trustworthy knowledge. The four principles commonly taught in nursing—respect for persons/autonomy, beneficence, nonmaleficence, and justice—must be applied to the entire study: design, recruitment, consent, data collection, analysis, reporting, and follow-up.

Ethics is not a substitute for sound methods. If a study cannot reasonably answer its research question, exposing participants to its burdens may lack sufficient scientific or social value.

## Four principles and their research applications

| Principle | Research meaning | Practical application |
|---|---|---|
| **Respect for persons / autonomy** | Recognize self-determination and protect people whose ability to make or communicate decisions is limited. | Meaningful informed consent, voluntary recruitment, understandable information, respect for refusal/withdrawal, and added safeguards when needed. |
| **Beneficence** | Seek a favorable balance of potential benefits and risks. | Use a scientifically sound design, qualified staff, risk-minimization procedures, monitoring, referral plans, and appropriate response to harm. |
| **Nonmaleficence** | Avoid or minimize preventable harm. | Consider physical, psychological, social, financial, legal, cultural, and informational risks; do not collect unnecessary data or expose participants to avoidable danger. |
| **Justice** | Distribute burdens and benefits fairly. | Use defensible selection criteria; avoid exploiting easy-to-recruit dependent groups; justify inclusion/exclusion and consider fair access to potential benefits. |

Other obligations include confidentiality, honesty, accountability, transparency, management of conflicts of interest, and responsible reporting. Principles may interact; document the rationale for decisions rather than treating one principle as an automatic answer.

## Scientific validity as an ethical obligation

A study should have a clear question, appropriate design, feasible methods, competent investigators, and an analysis plan capable of producing interpretable results. A weak design may waste participants' time and expose them to risk without meaningful contribution. Researchers must not manipulate methods or data to obtain a preferred answer.

Pre-specify methods and outcomes when appropriate, keep an audit trail, explain missing data and limitations, and report unfavorable or null results honestly. Fabrication means inventing data; falsification means manipulating the research record; plagiarism means presenting another person's work as one's own.

## Independent ethics review

For health research involving human participants in the Philippines, determine the review requirements under the current PHREB National Ethical Guidelines, institutional requirements, and other applicable rules. Do not self-declare an exemption merely because the study is small, uses a questionnaire, or is conducted by students. Submit the protocol or request a formal determination from the responsible REC.

An REC commonly examines:
- scientific/social value and design validity;
- foreseeable risks, potential benefits, and risk-minimization plans;
- recruitment, selection fairness, incentives, and power imbalances;
- consent content, comprehension, voluntariness, and representative/assent arrangements where relevant;
- privacy, confidentiality, data access, retention, sharing, and disposal;
- investigator qualifications, conflicts of interest, safety monitoring, and plans for reporting adverse events.

Approval is not a substitute for researcher accountability. Follow the approved protocol, seek required approval for amendments, report required deviations or safety events, and protect participants throughout the study.

## Consent and vulnerability

Explain purpose, procedures, duration, risks/discomforts, potential benefits without exaggeration, alternatives where relevant, confidentiality limits, contacts, and withdrawal conditions in a form the person can understand. Consent should be voluntary and ongoing. A signature does not prove understanding, and an investigator should not pressure someone to participate.

Vulnerability is situational. It may arise from limited decision-making capacity, dependence on a care provider or teacher, economic pressure, stigma, or restricted access to alternatives. Identify the specific risk and apply proportionate safeguards—such as neutral recruitment, private consent discussions, accessible information, independent support, and clear separation between study participation and routine care/grades/employment. Do not automatically exclude a group without scientific and ethical justification.

## Privacy and data protection

Collect only information needed for the stated purpose. Explain who will access it, how it will be secured, whether it will be shared or reused, retention duration, and confidentiality limits. Coded data are not necessarily anonymous if a linkage key or indirect identifiers remain. Restrict access and use approved storage/transfer systems.

In the Philippines, RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012) is relevant to personal-data processing; health information is sensitive personal information under the Act. Do not assume that a research label is a blanket exemption from privacy safeguards. Interpret applicable law with current National Privacy Commission guidance, institutional data-protection procedures, and REC requirements.

## Nursing responsibilities and PNLE priorities

- If a participant has an urgent clinical problem, prioritize appropriate assessment and care; research procedures must not delay emergency response.
- Stop and escalate if consent appears coerced or invalid, a protocol deviation creates risk, or an unexpected harm occurs.
- Report adverse events, privacy incidents, and deviations through the approved process.
- Maintain confidentiality, objective records, and honest reporting.
- For prioritization, weigh immediate safety, actual versus potential harm, participant autonomy, and the approved protocol; do not apply a principle mechanically to justify preventable harm.

## PNLE high-yield review

- Autonomy/respect = voluntary informed choice; beneficence = favorable benefit-risk balance; nonmaleficence = minimize harm; justice = fair selection and distribution.
- Scientific validity is an ethical concern, not just a technical detail.
- REC approval does not remove the investigator's ongoing responsibility.
- Vulnerability requires context-specific safeguards, not automatic exclusion.
- Consent waivers are exceptional and must be determined under applicable PHREB/REC requirements.
- Fabrication, falsification, and plagiarism are distinct forms of research misconduct.

## Scope note

This is a study aid, not a substitute for the current PHREB guideline, REC decision, law, or institutional policy. Verify requirements for the actual research design and setting.
