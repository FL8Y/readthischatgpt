# Heterogeneity in Research — Nursing Research

## Meaning
Heterogeneity means that participants, interventions, settings, outcomes, or study results differ. It can be clinical (differences in patients or care), methodological (differences in design or measurement), or statistical (observed effect estimates vary beyond what sampling error alone would explain).

## Why it matters
Different age groups, disease severity, settings, intervention doses, follow-up periods, and outcome definitions can make results difficult to compare or combine. Heterogeneity can affect generalizability and the appropriateness of meta-analysis.

## Assessing and managing
- Define the target population and intervention clearly.
- Compare study designs, inclusion criteria, outcome definitions, and follow-up.
- In systematic reviews, examine forest plots and measures such as I² alongside clinical/methodological judgment; I² is not a simple percentage of studies that disagree.
- Consider subgroup analysis or meta-regression only when justified and interpreted cautiously; post hoc subgroup findings can be spurious.
- Use random-effects models when their assumptions fit, but they do not make incomparable studies comparable.
- Report heterogeneity and explore plausible reasons transparently.

## PNLE high-yield
Heterogeneity is not automatically a flaw: it may reflect real-world diversity. The key question is whether differences undermine the specific inference. A pooled average can hide important differences in patient groups or effects.
