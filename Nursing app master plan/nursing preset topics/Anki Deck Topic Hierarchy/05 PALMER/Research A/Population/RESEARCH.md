# Population in Nursing Research — PNLE Research

## Definitions
A **target population** is the full group to which the researchers want to generalize. An **accessible/source population** is the group practically available for recruitment. A **sample** is the subset actually studied. A **sampling frame** is the operational list or mechanism from which eligible participants are selected. **Eligibility criteria** specify inclusion and exclusion.

## Sampling and generalizability
Define population by relevant characteristics, setting, and time. A mismatch between target population and sampling frame can cause coverage bias. Convenience recruitment may be feasible but limits representativeness. Probability sampling supports known selection probabilities, but nonresponse and measurement problems can still bias estimates.

## Sample size
Sample size depends on the primary outcome, expected effect or precision, variability, design, alpha, desired power, clustering, and anticipated attrition. Bigger is not automatically better if the sampling or measurement is biased. Avoid using an arbitrary rule without rationale.

## Nursing research example
A study of one tertiary hospital's ICU nurses does not automatically represent all nurses in the Philippines. Describe setting, recruitment, participant characteristics, response rate, and limitations so readers can judge transferability/generalizability.

## Ethics
Recruit fairly, avoid unnecessary exclusion, minimize burden, protect privacy, and ensure consent/ethics review as applicable. Vulnerable populations need appropriate safeguards rather than automatic exclusion.
