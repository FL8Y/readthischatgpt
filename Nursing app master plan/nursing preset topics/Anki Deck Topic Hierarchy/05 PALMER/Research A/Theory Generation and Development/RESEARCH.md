# Theory Generation and Development — Nursing Research

## Concepts
A theory is an organized set of concepts and propositions explaining or predicting phenomena. Concepts are abstract ideas; constructs are concepts developed for theoretical/scientific use; variables are measurable representations; propositions describe relationships among concepts.

## Inductive and deductive reasoning
- **Induction:** move from observations/patterns toward concepts or theory; common in grounded theory and exploratory work.
- **Deduction:** derive testable predictions from an existing theory and examine them with data.
- **Abduction:** develop the most plausible explanation for observations and revise it as evidence accumulates.

## Theory development process
Identify a phenomenon and existing explanations; define concepts; examine relationships; collect and analyze relevant evidence; refine propositions; test or compare explanations; state boundary conditions and limitations. Theory is revised when evidence contradicts it.

## Nursing application
Theories can guide assessment, intervention selection, research questions, and interpretation. A framework should fit the population and context rather than being forced onto data. Grounded theory aims to develop theory from data through iterative collection and analysis; it is not simply any study that mentions theory.

## Evaluation
Consider clarity, internal consistency, scope, parsimony, empirical support, usefulness, and explanatory/predictive power. A theory may be useful without explaining every situation; identify assumptions and context.
