# Sampling Definitions — Nursing Research

## Population and sample

- **Target population:** group to which the researcher intends the findings to apply.
- **Accessible/source population:** eligible group available to the study under practical and geographic constraints.
- **Sampling frame:** operational list or source from which units are selected. It may omit eligible people or include ineligible ones.
- **Sample:** units/participants actually included in data collection or analysis.
- **Sampling unit:** unit selected at a stage of sampling, such as a patient, household, clinic, or ward.
- **Element/unit of analysis:** the entity about which data are analyzed; it may differ from the sampling unit in cluster or multilevel designs.
- **Census:** attempt to collect data from every unit in the defined population rather than select a sample.
- **Eligibility criteria:** prespecified inclusion and exclusion rules that determine who can participate.

## Selection and recruitment

- **Probability sampling:** selection uses a random process with known, nonzero selection probabilities under the design.
- **Non-probability sampling:** selection probabilities are unknown; recruitment may be based on access, judgment, quotas, or referrals.
- **Simple random sampling:** selection by a random mechanism from the frame.
- **Systematic sampling:** select a random starting point and then every kth unit; inspect ordering for periodicity.
- **Stratified sampling:** divide the population into strata and sample within each stratum.
- **Cluster sampling:** randomly select groups/clusters and study all or some units within selected clusters.
- **Multistage sampling:** select units in successive stages, potentially using different methods at each stage.
- **Convenience sampling:** recruit accessible participants.
- **Purposive sampling:** select participants because they fit a reasoned, study-specific purpose, often to obtain information-rich cases.
- **Quota sampling:** recruit to prespecified subgroup counts without necessarily random selection.
- **Snowball/network sampling:** use participant or network referrals to recruit others.
- **Consecutive sampling:** approach every eligible person available in a defined setting/time period; it is systematic recruitment but is not automatically probability sampling.

## Sample adequacy and participation

- **Sample size:** number of units studied. Adequacy depends on the design, objective, variability, effect/precision/power needs, qualitative information needs, feasibility, and ethics.
- **Sampling error:** chance variation between a sample estimate and the population quantity.
- **Selection bias:** systematic distortion from who is selected, who is missed, or who remains in the study.
- **Nonresponse:** eligible selected individuals do not participate; bias depends partly on whether nonresponse relates to relevant characteristics/outcomes.
- **Attrition/loss to follow-up:** participants stop contributing data over time; differential attrition may distort results.
- **Overcoverage:** frame includes units outside the target population.
- **Undercoverage:** eligible units are missing from the frame.
- **Representativeness:** how well the sample reflects the characteristics relevant to the intended inference; it is not guaranteed by a large sample.
- **Generalizability:** applicability of findings to a defined target population or setting.
- **Transferability:** applicability of qualitative insights to another context, supported by rich description of context and participants.
- **Saturation / information adequacy:** qualitative concepts used to discuss whether data are sufficiently rich for the analytic purpose; definitions and assessment vary by methodology and should be explained.

## Common confusions

- A sample can be large but biased.
- A sampling frame is not the same as the target population.
- Consecutive recruitment is not automatically random.
- Stratification selects from every defined stratum; cluster sampling selects some clusters.
- Non-probability sampling may be appropriate for qualitative or exploratory questions, but claims must match the sampling design.
- “Representative” should not be asserted merely because the sample resembles the population on one demographic characteristic.

## PNLE high-yield review

- Target population ≠ accessible population ≠ sampling frame ≠ sample.
- Random selection supports probability inference only when the design is properly executed and nonresponse/frame problems are addressed.
- Sample size alone does not guarantee representativeness.
- Sampling unit may differ from unit of analysis.
- Qualitative sample adequacy should fit the purpose and methodology, not a universal numeric rule.

## Scope note

Define the population, frame, unit, and sample explicitly in each study; terminology may vary across disciplines and designs.
