# Probability Sampling — Nursing Research

## Definition
Probability sampling uses a random selection procedure so each eligible population unit has a known, nonzero probability of selection. This supports design-based estimates when the frame, selection, weighting, and nonresponse are handled appropriately.

## Methods
- **Simple random:** each unit has equal selection probability; requires a suitable frame.
- **Systematic:** select every k-th unit after a random start; periodic ordering in the frame can bias results.
- **Stratified random:** divide population into meaningful strata, then sample within each; improves subgroup representation and may improve precision.
- **Cluster:** randomly select naturally occurring groups (e.g., hospitals or barangays), then sample within them or include all members. Cluster similarity affects precision.
- **Multistage:** select at successive levels, such as regions, facilities, then people; analysis may need weights and design effects.

## Strengths and limitations
Probability sampling supports stronger population inference than convenience sampling but does not guarantee a representative achieved sample if nonresponse or coverage bias occurs. Cluster sampling may be cheaper but often increases variance. Stratification requires accurate information for the strata.

## PNLE distinctions
Random sampling = how participants are selected; random assignment = how participants are allocated to study groups. They are not interchangeable. A census attempts to include all population members but can still suffer nonresponse and measurement error.
