# Non-Probability Sampling — Nursing Research

## Main methods
- **Convenience:** recruit accessible participants; fast but vulnerable to selection bias.
- **Purposive/judgmental:** select information-rich participants who meet a study-specific rationale; common in qualitative work.
- **Quota:** recruit until target counts for categories are reached, without random selection within categories.
- **Snowball/network:** participants refer others; useful for hard-to-reach groups but can overrepresent connected networks.
- **Consecutive:** recruit every eligible participant encountered over a defined period until the target is reached; often more systematic than convenience sampling but still limited by setting/time.
- **Theoretical sampling:** in grounded theory, choose further participants/data to develop emerging categories.

## Strengths and limitations
These methods can be feasible for exploratory, qualitative, or hard-to-reach population research. Selection probabilities are unknown, so conventional population-level margins of sampling error generally cannot be justified as if the sample were random. Bias may remain even with a large sample.

## Reporting and ethics
Explain why the method fits the question, how participants were approached, who declined, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and the context. Protect confidentiality, avoid coercive recruitment, and describe limits of transferability/generalizability.

## PNLE high-yield
- Convenience = easiest to access.
- Purposive = selected for relevant characteristics/information.
- Quota = fill category counts without random selection.
- Snowball = participant referrals.
- Consecutive = every eligible case in sequence during a defined window.
