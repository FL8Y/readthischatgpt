# Types of Research Design — Nursing Research

## Design families
- **Descriptive:** characterizes a population or phenomenon without testing a causal intervention.
- **Correlational:** estimates association; does not alone establish causation.
- **Experimental:** researcher assigns an intervention; randomized controlled trials use random allocation.
- **Quasi-experimental:** evaluates an intervention without random assignment or with incomplete experimental control.
- **Observational analytic:** examines exposures and outcomes without assigning exposure, such as cohort and case-control studies.
- **Qualitative:** explores experience, meaning, context, or process.
- **Mixed methods:** intentionally integrates quantitative and qualitative approaches.

## Selection logic
Start with the research question, not the name of a preferred design. Ask whether the goal is prevalence, association, temporal sequence, causal effect, lived experience, or implementation. Consider feasibility, ethics, confounding, measurement, sampling, and required precision.

## Common misconceptions
A survey is a data-collection method, not always a complete design. Random sampling selects participants; random assignment allocates interventions. A large sample cannot repair major bias. “Experimental” does not automatically mean randomized, blinded, or high quality.
