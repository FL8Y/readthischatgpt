# Research Design — Nursing Research

## Meaning and role

Research design is the overall plan linking the research question, population/sample, measurement, data collection, timing, comparison, analysis, and intended inference. A sound design makes the study answerable and reduces avoidable bias. The design should be selected before data collection and documented in a protocol where appropriate.

## Core decisions

1. **Question and objective:** What is being described, compared, explained, experienced, or evaluated?
2. **Approach:** Quantitative, qualitative, or mixed methods; choose based on the nature of the question.
3. **Population and sampling:** Define target/accessibile population, sampling frame, eligibility, recruitment, and sample-size rationale.
4. **Variables/constructs:** Specify outcomes, exposures/interventions, confounders, constructs, and operational definitions.
5. **Timing:** Cross-sectional or longitudinal; prospective or retrospective; define follow-up and measurement time points.
6. **Intervention/comparison:** State whether the researcher assigns an intervention, how allocation occurs, and what comparison is used.
7. **Data collection:** Standardize procedures, instruments, training, calibration, and data quality checks.
8. **Analysis:** Prespecify primary outcomes and methods, missing-data handling, confounder control, and sensitivity analyses where relevant.
9. **Ethics and feasibility:** Evaluate risks, consent, privacy, resources, equipoise where relevant, and required REC/regulatory review.
10. **Reporting and interpretation:** Identify the guideline appropriate to the design and state limits to causal inference and generalization.

## Broad design families

- **Experimental:** intervention assignment; randomized designs are common where feasible and ethical.
- **Quasi-experimental:** intervention evaluated without full random assignment; may use comparison groups, interrupted time series, or other structured designs.
- **Observational:** researcher does not assign the exposure; includes cross-sectional, cohort, case-control, and other designs.
- **Qualitative:** explores meaning, experience, process, or context using methodology-specific data collection and analysis.
- **Mixed methods:** deliberately integrates quantitative and qualitative components.
- **Evidence synthesis:** systematic/scoping review and related methods synthesize prior research using explicit procedures.

Design labels are not interchangeable. State the actual allocation, sampling, timing, measurement, and analytic procedures.

## Validity threats to anticipate

- **Selection bias:** who enters or remains in the study differs systematically.
- **Measurement/information bias:** variables are measured or classified inaccurately.
- **Confounding:** another factor distorts the exposure-outcome relationship.
- **Temporal ambiguity:** exposure and outcome timing do not support the intended inference.
- **Attrition/missing data:** missingness may bias results or reduce precision.
- **Contamination/nonadherence:** participants receive different conditions from those planned.
- **History/secular trend:** outside events or time trends explain an observed change in a before/after study.
- **Regression to the mean:** extreme measurements tend to be less extreme on repeat, even without a true intervention effect.
- **Selective reporting/analytic flexibility:** outcomes or methods are chosen based on results.

No design eliminates every threat. Use design features and analysis appropriate to the question, then state residual limitations.

## Ethical design

A design should be scientifically capable of answering the question and should minimize unnecessary participant burden/risk. For human-participant health research in the Philippines, determine the applicable PHREB, REC, and regulatory requirements before recruitment. Ensure consent, privacy, fair selection, safety monitoring, and protocol adherence as applicable. A methodologically weak study may be ethically problematic if it exposes people to burden without meaningful knowledge value.

## PNLE high-yield review

- The research question drives the design; do not choose a design merely because it is familiar.
- Experimental designs assign interventions; observational designs study exposures as they occur.
- Quasi-experimental designs need careful attention to baseline differences and time trends.
- Validity depends on sampling, measurement, timing, confounding, missing data, and analysis—not just sample size.
- Ethical review, consent, and participant safety are part of design planning, not afterthoughts.

## Scope note

This is a design-planning overview. Specific designs require methods expertise and current ethical/regulatory review appropriate to the project.
