# Research Design — Nursing Research

## Meaning
Research design is the overall plan linking the question, sampling, measurement, data collection, analysis, and inference. A sound design reduces bias and makes the conclusions proportionate to the evidence.

## Broad categories
- **Quantitative:** descriptive, correlational/observational, experimental, quasi-experimental.
- **Qualitative:** phenomenology, grounded theory, ethnography, case study, narrative inquiry, and descriptive approaches.
- **Mixed methods:** integrates quantitative and qualitative data intentionally, with a stated rationale and integration plan.

## Key choices
Define the question and primary outcome; determine whether the aim is description, association, prediction, explanation, or intervention evaluation; establish time frame; identify comparison groups; plan sampling and sample size; choose valid tools; anticipate confounding, missingness, attrition, and ethical constraints.

## Causal inference
Random assignment can balance known and unknown confounders on average, but implementation, attrition, measurement, and analysis still matter. Observational association alone does not prove causality. Quasi-experimental studies may strengthen inference but need careful consideration of alternative explanations.

## PNLE high-yield
The design must fit the question. A cross-sectional survey is efficient for describing a snapshot but weak for establishing temporal order. A qualitative study is suitable for lived experience, not estimating a national prevalence. Mixed methods must integrate findings, not merely place two data types side by side.
