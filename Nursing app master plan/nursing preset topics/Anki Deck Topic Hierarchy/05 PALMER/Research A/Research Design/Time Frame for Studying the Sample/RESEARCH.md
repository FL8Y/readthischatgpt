# Research Design by Time Frame — Nursing Research

## Main distinctions
- **Cross-sectional:** exposure/characteristics and outcomes assessed at one point or defined period. Useful for prevalence and description; temporal order and causality are often difficult to establish.
- **Longitudinal:** observations collected repeatedly over time. Can assess change and temporal sequence but requires attention to attrition, time-varying confounding, and repeated-measure methods.
- **Prospective:** participants are followed forward from a defined starting point to observe outcomes.
- **Retrospective:** uses existing records or reconstructs past exposures/outcomes. Efficient but dependent on record quality and recall.
- **Ambidirectional/ambispective:** combines retrospective data with prospective follow-up.

“Prospective/retrospective” and “cross-sectional/longitudinal” describe related but not identical features; avoid treating them as perfect synonyms.

## Nursing examples
A one-time survey of nurses' burnout is cross-sectional. Following a cohort of nurses over two years is longitudinal. Reviewing prior charts for a risk factor and outcome is retrospective and may be a case-control or cohort analysis depending on how participants are selected.

## Bias and ethics
Plan follow-up, minimize loss to follow-up, define index dates and time windows, secure records, and address privacy/consent/REC requirements. Retrospective data use is not automatically exempt from ethics review.
