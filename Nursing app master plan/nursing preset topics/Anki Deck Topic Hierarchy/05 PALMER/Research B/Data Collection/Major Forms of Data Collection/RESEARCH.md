# Major Forms of Data Collection

## Common forms
- **Questionnaire/survey:** standardized responses from many participants; efficient but subject to nonresponse, recall, and social-desirability bias.
- **Interview:** structured, semi-structured, or open-ended; permits clarification but requires interviewer training and reflexivity.
- **Focus group:** group discussion reveals shared norms and differing views; confidentiality between participants cannot be fully guaranteed.
- **Observation:** records behavior or events directly; observer presence and interpretation can affect results.
- **Physiologic/clinical measurement:** vital signs, laboratory values, or device readings; requires validated/calibrated equipment and standard procedures.
- **Document/record review:** uses existing records; limited by missing, inconsistent, or historically collected data.
- **Digital/sensor data:** may provide repeated measurements but raises device validity, access, privacy, and data-security issues.

## Choosing a method
Use the question, variable, population, feasibility, accuracy, burden, and ethics to choose. Combining methods may triangulate perspectives but requires a plan for integrating findings.

## Quality safeguards
Pilot tools, train collectors, standardize definitions, document missing data, protect identifiers, and avoid leading prompts. Ensure consent covers recordings and secondary use where applicable.

## Comparing methods for a study question

| Method | Particularly useful for | Main risks and safeguards |
|---|---|---|
| Questionnaire/survey | Standardized information from many people; knowledge, attitudes, self-reported behavior. | Nonresponse, recall, literacy, social-desirability and item-order effects; pilot wording, use accessible formats, and report response/missingness. |
| Interview | Detailed accounts, meanings, decision processes, and clarification. | Interviewer influence, power relationships, recall and distress; train interviewers, use neutral prompts, protect privacy, and use a distress/escalation plan. |
| Focus group | Shared norms, group dynamics, differences in viewpoints. | Dominant voices, conformity, sensitive disclosures and confidentiality limits; set ground rules, facilitate fairly, and never promise that other participants will keep information private. |
| Observation | Actual behavior, workflow, interactions, or environmental conditions. | Observer effects, inconsistent coding, and privacy intrusion; define behaviors, train observers, and obtain required permission/consent. |
| Clinical/physiologic measurement | Objective physiologic or clinical variables. | Device calibration, operator technique, timing, units and clinical conditions; standardize protocols and document device/source. |
| Record/document review | Historical events, service patterns, outcomes already recorded. | Missing, inconsistent or changed definitions; define the time window and coding rules, validate abstraction, and account for data not collected for research. |
| Digital/sensor data | Repeated or continuous measures and behavior patterns. | Device validity, algorithm changes, digital exclusion, surveillance and data security; validate the source and limit data to the approved purpose. |

## Triangulation and mixed methods

Using more than one method may illuminate different dimensions of a question, but it does not automatically eliminate bias. Plan what each method contributes and how conflicting findings will be interpreted. In mixed-methods research, explain when data are collected, how samples relate, which strand has priority if any, and where integration occurs.

## Standardization and data integrity

Use a written protocol for definitions, instructions, timing, units, equipment, training, coding, and handling of deviations. Keep a traceable record of data corrections and missingness. Do not change a response to make it fit the expected result. For audio/video recording, obtain the required consent, explain access/retention and de-identification, and use approved secure storage.

## Nursing example

To study bedside handoff, direct observation may reveal workflow while interviews explain staff perceptions and a brief survey measures acceptability. The methods answer different questions; observer presence may change behavior, and staff may fear criticism. Use clear definitions, neutral observers where feasible, confidentiality protections, and transparent integration of findings.
