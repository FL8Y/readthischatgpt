# Criteria for an Effective Data-Collection Tool

## Core criteria
- **Validity:** evidence that the tool measures what it is intended to measure for the intended use/population.
- **Reliability:** consistency under specified conditions, including test-retest, inter-rater, and internal consistency where relevant.
- **Objectivity:** scoring and interpretation are as independent as possible from personal bias, supported by clear procedures.
- **Sensitivity/responsiveness:** ability to detect meaningful differences or change where required.
- **Feasibility/usability:** acceptable time, cost, burden, literacy demands, accessibility, and workflow fit.
- **Clarity and cultural/linguistic appropriateness:** questions are understandable and appropriate to the population.
- **Standardization:** instructions, administration, scoring, and handling of missing items are documented.

## Tool selection
Match the tool to the construct, age, language, setting, and intended interpretation. Review validation studies, permissions, scoring rules, floor/ceiling effects, and responsiveness. Translation/back-translation alone does not establish cultural validity. Pilot testing identifies practical problems but does not replace formal validation.

## PNLE high-yield
A reliable tool can consistently measure the wrong thing; reliability alone does not prove validity. A tool validated in another population may need further evaluation locally.

## How to evaluate a tool before use

1. **Define the construct and intended use.** State what the tool is supposed to measure, for whom, in which setting, and for what decision. A tool appropriate for screening may not be appropriate for diagnosis or outcome evaluation.
2. **Review content and response process.** Check whether items cover the construct, are understandable, avoid leading/double-barreled wording, and are interpreted as intended by the target population.
3. **Examine validity evidence.** Depending on the tool, this may include content, construct (including convergent/discriminant or structural evidence), criterion-related evidence, and evidence that scores support the intended interpretation. No single coefficient proves validity in every use.
4. **Examine reliability.** Test-retest reliability concerns stability when the construct is expected to remain stable; inter-rater reliability concerns agreement/consistency between observers; internal consistency concerns relationships among items intended to measure a construct. A high internal-consistency coefficient alone does not prove unidimensionality or validity.
5. **Assess responsiveness and interpretability.** For outcome measures, determine whether the tool can detect meaningful change and whether floor/ceiling effects limit detection. A statistically detectable change may not be clinically meaningful.
6. **Check feasibility and equity.** Consider time, cost, burden, language, literacy, disability access, cultural fit, mode of administration, and missing-item patterns.
7. **Confirm permissions and scoring.** Follow licensing, training, administration, scoring, cutoffs, and handling of incomplete forms. Do not alter validated wording or scoring without assessing the impact.
8. **Pilot and document.** A pilot can reveal workflow, comprehension, and data-entry problems; it does not by itself establish full psychometric validity.

## Quantitative and qualitative tools

For a quantitative scale, examine measurement properties relevant to its score and intended use. For a qualitative interview guide, “validity” may not be established by a single coefficient; review alignment with the question, clarity, piloting, reflexivity, interviewer training, and the quality of elicited data. The tool should fit the methodology rather than forcing every quality criterion onto every instrument.

## Example

A translated pain questionnaire may appear clear after literal translation, yet patients may interpret response categories differently or find examples culturally inappropriate. Use a documented adaptation process, target-population testing, and appropriate validity/reliability evidence. Back-translation alone is not proof that the measure is equivalent.
