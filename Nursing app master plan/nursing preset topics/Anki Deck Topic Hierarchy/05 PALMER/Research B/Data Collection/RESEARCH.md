# Data Collection in Nursing Research

## Purpose and planning
Data collection obtains information needed to answer the research question. Define each variable, source, timing, method, staff responsibility, quality checks, and privacy safeguards before collection. Procedures should be standardized enough to reduce measurement differences while fitting the study design.

## Main methods
Questionnaires/surveys, interviews, focus groups, direct observation, physiologic/clinical measurements, record/document review, and digital or device-based measures. Mixed methods may combine methods to capture different aspects of a question.

## Quality and bias
Train collectors, pilot procedures, calibrate equipment, use validated tools, standardize instructions, document deviations, and monitor missingness. Recall bias, social desirability, observer effects, selection bias, and inconsistent measurement can distort results. Interviewers should avoid leading questions.

## Ethics and safety
Obtain consent/REC approval where required; collect only necessary data; secure records; limit access; explain privacy limits; and respond appropriately to distress or adverse events. Do not collect extra identifiers “just in case.”

## Data-collection protocol checklist

Before collection, define each variable or qualitative domain, data source, timing, unit/coding, instrument version, responsible collector, and procedure for incomplete or conflicting records. Train collectors and document competency where required. Pilot the workflow to identify confusing items, timing problems, equipment issues, and participant burden.

During collection:
- verify eligibility and follow the approved recruitment/consent process;
- use the same definitions, instructions, timing, and equipment procedures where the design requires standardization;
- check completeness and logical consistency without pressuring participants or fabricating answers;
- record deviations and reasons for missing data;
- protect identifiers and keep access limited to authorized personnel;
- respond to distress, unexpected risk, or urgent clinical findings before research convenience;
- report protocol deviations and safety/privacy incidents through the required channel.

After collection, secure and back up data according to policy, preserve an audit trail, document cleaning/coding decisions, separate identifiers from research data where appropriate, and follow approved retention and destruction rules. Do not claim data are anonymous if a key or indirect identifiers can link them back to a person.

## Common data-quality threats

- **Recall bias:** memory of past events is incomplete or influenced by current status.
- **Social-desirability bias:** respondents give answers perceived as acceptable.
- **Observer/interviewer effects:** data collectors influence behavior or responses.
- **Instrument drift:** devices or measurement practices change over time.
- **Inter-rater inconsistency:** collectors apply definitions differently.
- **Data-entry/coding error:** values, units, categories or reverse-coded items are entered incorrectly.
- **Missingness:** nonresponse or incomplete data may be related to participant characteristics or outcomes.

Use suitable safeguards and report remaining limitations. Do not automatically delete outliers or impute missing values without a documented, justified method.

## Philippine ethics and privacy

For human-participant health research, follow the current PHREB National Ethical Guidelines, the responsible REC's determination, the approved protocol, and institutional requirements. The Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) is relevant to personal-data processing, especially health information. Collect only necessary information, explain confidentiality limits and data use, and do not reuse or share identifiable records outside the authorized purpose.
