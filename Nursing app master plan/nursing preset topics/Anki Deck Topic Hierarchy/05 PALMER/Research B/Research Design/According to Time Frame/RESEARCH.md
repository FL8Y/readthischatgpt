# Research Design According to Time Frame — Research B

## Main categories
- **Cross-sectional:** observes variables at a point or defined period; useful for prevalence/snapshot description but weak for temporal order.
- **Longitudinal:** repeated observations over time; can examine change but must address attrition and time-varying confounding.
- **Prospective:** follows participants forward from a defined starting point.
- **Retrospective:** analyzes past exposures/outcomes or existing records; quality depends on record completeness and recall.
- **Ambidirectional/ambispective:** combines retrospective information with prospective follow-up.

Prospective/retrospective and cross-sectional/longitudinal are related but not interchangeable labels. A retrospective cohort can still be longitudinal.

## Nursing examples
A one-time burnout survey is cross-sectional. Following a cohort of nurses over two years is longitudinal. Reviewing prior records to compare exposure and outcome may be a retrospective cohort or case-control study depending on how participants are selected.

## Bias and ethics
Define index date and time windows, handle loss to follow-up, protect records, and obtain appropriate REC determination. Retrospective data are not automatically exempt from ethics/privacy requirements.
