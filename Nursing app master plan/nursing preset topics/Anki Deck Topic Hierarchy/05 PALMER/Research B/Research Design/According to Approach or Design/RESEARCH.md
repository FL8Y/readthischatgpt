# Research Design by Approach — Research B

## Quantitative approach
Uses numerical variables and statistical methods to describe distributions, estimate associations, test hypotheses, or evaluate effects. Quality depends on design, measurement, sampling, confounding control, assumptions, and transparent reporting.

## Qualitative approach
Explores meanings, experiences, processes, and contexts using interviews, observation, documents, or other nonnumeric data. Sampling and analysis are often iterative and purposive; rigor is evaluated through methodological coherence and trustworthiness.

## Mixed methods
Collects and integrates quantitative and qualitative evidence with an explicit rationale. Integration may occur in design, sampling, analysis, or interpretation. Two separate methods without meaningful integration do not automatically constitute strong mixed-methods work.

## Choosing
Select the approach that best answers the question, considering ethics, feasibility, data needed, and inference intended. Do not choose an approach solely because it is familiar.
