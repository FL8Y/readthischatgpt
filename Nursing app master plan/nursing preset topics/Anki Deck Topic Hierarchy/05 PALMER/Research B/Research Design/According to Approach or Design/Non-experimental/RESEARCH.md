# Non-Experimental Research Design — Nursing Research

## Definition
In non-experimental designs, researchers observe variables without assigning the exposure/intervention under study. Designs include descriptive surveys, cross-sectional studies, cohort studies, case-control studies, and correlational analyses.

## Strengths and limits
These designs can study real-world exposures, rare outcomes (case-control), long-term outcomes (cohort), and questions where intervention assignment is unethical or impractical. Confounding, selection bias, information bias, and reverse causation can limit causal inference.

## Common designs
- **Cross-sectional:** exposure/characteristics and outcome measured at a point/period; temporal order often unclear.
- **Cohort:** groups defined by exposure and followed or reconstructed to compare outcomes.
- **Case-control:** select by outcome status and compare prior exposures.
- **Correlational:** estimate association without intervention assignment.

## Interpretation
Adjusting statistically for measured confounders cannot guarantee that all confounding is removed. State assumptions and limitations; distinguish association from causation.
