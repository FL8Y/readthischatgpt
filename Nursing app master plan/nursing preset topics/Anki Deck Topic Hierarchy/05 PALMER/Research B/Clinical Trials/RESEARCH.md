# Clinical Trials — Nursing Research

## Definition and phases
A clinical trial prospectively assigns participants to one or more health-related interventions to evaluate effects on health outcomes. Drug development often uses phase I (initial safety/dose), phase II (preliminary efficacy and dose), phase III (larger confirmatory comparison), and phase IV (post-authorization monitoring); exact purposes overlap and vary by product and regulator. Not every clinical trial is a drug trial or follows these phases.

## Design and safeguards
Protocols specify eligibility, intervention/comparator, randomization when used, blinding where feasible, outcomes, sample size, analysis, safety monitoring, stopping rules, and adverse-event reporting. Randomization reduces selection confounding on average; blinding can reduce performance/detection bias but is not always feasible.

## Nursing responsibilities
Support informed consent without coercion; verify eligibility and protocol requirements; administer/monitor interventions within training; assess and report adverse events promptly; maintain accurate source documentation; protect confidentiality; and avoid unauthorized protocol deviations. Participants' clinical needs take priority over data collection.

## Ethics and interpretation
REC approval, informed consent, risk-benefit review, and safety oversight are central. A trial's results depend on design, adherence, attrition, outcome selection, and applicability to the intended population. Registration and transparent reporting support accountability.

## Philippine context
Follow current Philippine FDA requirements, PHREB/REC guidance, applicable DOH rules, and sponsor/institution protocols. Verify the current regulatory pathway for the specific intervention.
