# Population and Sampling Definitions — Research B

- **Target population:** group the findings are intended to inform.
- **Accessible population:** eligible group practically available.
- **Sample:** participants/units observed.
- **Sampling frame:** list or mechanism used to select units.
- **Sampling unit:** unit selected at a stage (person, household, facility, etc.).
- **Inclusion criteria:** characteristics required for participation.
- **Exclusion criteria:** justified characteristics that preclude inclusion.
- **Sampling error:** chance difference between sample statistic and population parameter.
- **Selection bias:** systematic distortion due to who enters/remains in the sample.
- **Nonresponse bias:** responders differ meaningfully from nonresponders.
- **Representativeness:** similarity to target population on relevant features; not guaranteed by size alone.
- **Power:** probability of detecting a specified effect under assumptions; not the same as representativeness.
- **Attrition:** loss of participants during follow-up.
- **Sampling fraction:** proportion of population selected, where meaningful.

## Additional terms and practical distinctions

- **Respondent:** a selected/invited person who provides data; respondents may differ from all selected units.
- **Analytic sample:** units included in a particular analysis after applying prespecified eligibility/data-completeness rules; it may be smaller than the recruited sample.
- **Coverage error:** mismatch between the target population and the sampling frame because eligible units are missing or ineligible units are included.
- **Sampling weight:** a design-related weight often based on inverse selection probability, with possible nonresponse/calibration adjustments; it requires appropriate construction and analysis.
- **Random assignment:** allocation to study groups by chance after enrollment; it is not random sampling from a population.
- **Generalizability:** how far findings can reasonably apply to a defined population or setting, based on design and evidence.
- **Transferability:** qualitative readers' ability to judge whether contextual findings may be relevant elsewhere based on rich description.
- **Power:** probability of detecting a specified effect under stated assumptions; it does not measure how representative a sample is.

## Example of participant flow

A study's target population may be all nurses employed in public hospitals in a region. The accessible population may be those in participating hospitals; the sampling frame may be the current roster; selected nurses are invited; some decline or do not respond; some provide incomplete data; and the analytic sample is the subset eligible for the prespecified analysis. Report these stages and exclusions instead of using “sample” to refer to every group.

## Reporting and ethics

State the population definition, location/time period, eligibility, frame source, selection method, recruitment channel, response/attrition, and analysis exclusions. Criteria should be justified and applied consistently; avoid excluding groups solely for convenience. Recruitment must respect consent, privacy, and freedom from undue influence, especially when researchers have clinical, supervisory, or teaching authority over potential participants.
