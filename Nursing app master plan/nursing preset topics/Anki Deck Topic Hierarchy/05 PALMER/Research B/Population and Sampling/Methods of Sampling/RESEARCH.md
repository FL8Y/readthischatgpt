# Methods of Sampling — Nursing Research

## Probability methods
- **Simple random:** random selection from a complete frame.
- **Systematic:** random start, then every k-th unit; periodic frame patterns can bias selection.
- **Stratified random:** sample within meaningful strata to ensure subgroup representation and potentially improve precision.
- **Cluster:** select groups/facilities, then sample units within or include all; clustering affects standard errors.
- **Multistage:** sample at multiple levels (e.g., regions, facilities, individuals); may require weights and design-effect adjustment.

## Non-probability methods
- **Convenience:** readily accessible participants.
- **Purposive:** information-rich cases chosen for a stated rationale.
- **Quota:** fill subgroup counts without random selection within groups.
- **Snowball:** participant referrals, useful for some hard-to-reach populations.
- **Consecutive:** every eligible case in sequence during a defined window.
- **Theoretical:** select further data/participants to develop categories in grounded theory.

## Choosing and reporting
Choose based on the question, population access, feasibility, and intended inference. State the frame, eligibility, recruitment, refusals/nonresponse, sample-size rationale, and limitations. Random sampling is not random assignment. Non-probability samples generally cannot support conventional design-based margins of sampling error.
