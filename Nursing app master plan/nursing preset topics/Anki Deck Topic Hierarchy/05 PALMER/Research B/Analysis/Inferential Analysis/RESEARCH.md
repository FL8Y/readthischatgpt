# Inferential Analysis — Nursing Research

## Purpose
Inferential statistics use sample data and a model/design to estimate population parameters or test hypotheses. Validity depends on sampling, design, assumptions, measurement, and transparent reporting.

## Key concepts
- **Estimate:** point estimate plus uncertainty, often a confidence interval.
- **Hypothesis test:** compares observed data with a null model using a prespecified procedure.
- **p-value:** probability, assuming the null model and assumptions, of results at least as incompatible with the null as observed; not the probability that the null is true.
- **Type I error:** reject a true null; **Type II error:** fail to reject a false null.
- **Power:** probability of detecting a specified effect under assumed conditions.

## Good practice
Choose a method based on outcome type, design, independence/clustering, distribution, and question. Report effect sizes and confidence intervals; assess clinical significance and possible bias. Do not infer causation from observational association alone.

## PNLE high-yield
Failure to reject the null does not prove no effect. A statistically significant difference may be clinically trivial. Multiple unplanned tests increase false-positive risk.
