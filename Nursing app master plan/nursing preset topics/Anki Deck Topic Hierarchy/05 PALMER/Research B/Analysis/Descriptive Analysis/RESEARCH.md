# Descriptive Analysis — Nursing Research

## Purpose
Descriptive analysis summarizes the data actually observed without, by itself, testing causal claims or generalizing beyond the sample. It is usually the first analytic step.

## Methods
- **Categorical data:** counts, proportions, percentages, cross-tabulations.
- **Numeric data:** mean, median, mode, range, IQR, variance, SD.
- **Visual displays:** bar charts for categories, histograms for numeric distributions, box plots for spread/outliers, line graphs for time trends.
Choose summaries based on measurement level and distribution.

## Interpretation
Report denominator, units, missingness, and sample size. A percentage without its denominator can mislead. Mean/SD suit roughly symmetric numeric data; median/IQR often better describe skewed data. Graphs can reveal outliers and subgroup patterns but must not distort scales.

## PNLE distinctions
Descriptive statistics describe sample characteristics; inferential statistics address uncertainty about a broader population. A descriptive table does not prove a treatment worked.
