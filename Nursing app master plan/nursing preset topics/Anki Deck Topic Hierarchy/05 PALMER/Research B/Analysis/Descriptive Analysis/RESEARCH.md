# Descriptive Analysis — Nursing Research

## Purpose

Descriptive analysis summarizes the data actually observed. It describes distributions, sample characteristics, frequencies, and patterns. By itself, it does not establish causation or automatically justify generalization beyond the studied sample. It is usually an early analytic step and may be the primary analysis for a descriptive question.

## Choose summaries by data type and distribution

| Data type / distribution | Useful summaries | Interpretation cautions |
|---|---|---|
| Nominal categories | Count, proportion, percentage, mode | State the denominator and handle missing responses transparently. |
| Ordinal categories | Frequencies, percentages, cumulative percentages, median or mode where meaningful | Category order exists, but equal distances between categories should not be assumed. |
| Approximately symmetric continuous data | Mean, standard deviation (SD), range, confidence interval when estimating a population quantity | Mean/SD can be misleading for highly skewed data or strong outliers. |
| Skewed continuous or ordinal data | Median, interquartile range (IQR), range or percentiles | Median/IQR often better represent the typical value and spread in skewed data. |
| Counts and rates | Counts, rates with a clear denominator and time period | A raw count can be misleading if exposure time or population size differs. |
| Repeated or time-based data | Summaries by time point and appropriate plots | Repeated observations from one person/unit are not independent; trend descriptions do not by themselves prove causation. |

### Key measures

- **Mean:** sum of values divided by the number of observed values; sensitive to extreme observations.
- **Median:** middle value after ordering; less influenced by extremes.
- **Mode:** most frequent value/category; more than one mode may occur.
- **Range:** maximum minus minimum, or sometimes the minimum-to-maximum span; specify usage.
- **Variance:** average squared deviation using a defined denominator; sample variance commonly uses n−1.
- **Standard deviation:** square root of variance, expressed in the original measurement units; describes spread around the mean.
- **Interquartile range:** third quartile minus first quartile; covers the middle 50% of the distribution.
- **Proportion:** part divided by the total eligible/observed denominator, with denominator clearly stated.
- **Rate:** events relative to a defined population or person-time denominator; do not call every percentage a rate.

## Tables, graphs, and data quality

Use tables for exact values and graphs to reveal patterns. Common displays include bar charts for categories, histograms for numeric distributions, box plots for medians/spread/outliers, and line plots for time trends. Match the display to the data type and avoid misleading axes or scales.

Before reporting:
- check units, category labels, impossible values, duplicates, and data-entry issues;
- state the number of observations and how missing values are handled;
- preserve legitimate outliers and investigate them rather than deleting automatically;
- distinguish counts, proportions, percentages, and rates;
- use consistent denominators and time periods;
- label axes and tables clearly.

## What descriptive analysis cannot establish

- A pattern or correlation alone does not prove causation.
- A sample mean is not automatically the population mean.
- A large sample does not correct systematic selection or measurement bias.
- A statistically unusual observation is not automatically an error.
- A descriptive percentage without its denominator can be hard to interpret.
- A summary can hide important subgroup differences; describe stratification only when justified and avoid overinterpreting small subgroups.

## Nursing example

A unit reports 12 falls in one quarter. This count alone cannot show whether risk increased. Compare with a defined denominator such as patient-days, examine reporting practices, case mix, and timing, and avoid concluding that a new intervention caused a change without an appropriate design and analysis.

## PNLE high-yield review

- Mean is sensitive to extremes; median is more robust to skew/outliers.
- SD describes spread in original units; variance is in squared units.
- IQR covers the middle 50%.
- Always identify denominator, unit, time period, and missing-data handling.
- Descriptive statistics summarize observed data; they do not establish causality by themselves.

## Scope note

Use summaries appropriate to the variable, distribution, sample design, and question. Consult statistical support when the distribution or data structure makes interpretation uncertain.
