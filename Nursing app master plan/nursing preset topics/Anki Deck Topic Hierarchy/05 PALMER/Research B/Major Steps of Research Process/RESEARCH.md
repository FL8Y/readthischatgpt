# Major Steps of the Research Process

## Typical sequence
1. Identify and refine the research problem.
2. Review and synthesize literature.
3. Develop the question, objectives, and framework/hypotheses where appropriate.
4. Select a design and methods.
5. Define population, sampling, sample size, and recruitment.
6. Select/develop tools and operational definitions; pilot as appropriate.
7. Obtain REC/ethics approval and other permissions before activities requiring approval.
8. Collect data according to protocol.
9. Clean, analyze, and interpret data.
10. Report findings, limitations, and implications transparently.
11. Disseminate and consider implementation/monitoring where appropriate.

## Important cautions
The process is not always strictly linear; qualitative designs may iterate between sampling, collection, and analysis. Ethics review must occur before relevant recruitment/data collection. Protocol changes may require approval. Do not fabricate or selectively omit results.

## Alignment checks across the process

Each stage should connect to the next: the problem determines the question; the question determines the design and data needed; design determines sampling and measurement; those choices determine analysis; and the analysis limits the conclusions. A mismatch at an early stage cannot usually be repaired by a sophisticated statistical test later.

- **Problem/question:** define the knowledge gap and intended decision or contribution.
- **Literature and framework:** synthesize evidence, identify uncertainty, and define concepts.
- **Design/methods:** choose an approach that can answer the question, with explicit eligibility, setting, timing, outcomes, and procedures.
- **Sampling and tools:** justify who is included, how data are obtained, and why measures are suitable.
- **Ethics and permissions:** obtain the required REC/institutional approvals and consent before recruitment or procedures that require them.
- **Data management:** define access, coding, security, retention, and quality checks before collection.
- **Analysis:** follow the prespecified plan or document and justify changes; label post hoc analyses as exploratory.
- **Interpretation/reporting:** separate results from interpretation, describe uncertainty and limitations, and use the relevant reporting guideline.
- **Utilization:** assess applicability, patient preferences, resources, local policy, and unintended effects before changing practice.

## Common process failures

- Starting data collection before required approval or consent.
- Selecting a sample only because it is convenient, then claiming population representativeness.
- Choosing a tool without checking validity for the intended population/use.
- Changing primary outcomes or excluding participants after seeing results without disclosure.
- Treating statistical significance as proof of clinical importance or causality.
- Failing to protect identifiers, record deviations, or report adverse events.
- Publishing only favorable results or overstating implications beyond the design.

## Practical nursing example

For a study of a bedside handoff intervention, define whether the aim is feasibility, staff experience, adherence, or patient outcomes before choosing the design. Identify the unit/population, define the intervention and comparator, select measures, determine sample size/precision needs, secure ethics review as required, train collectors, collect data consistently, analyze according to plan, and report both benefits and unintended effects. A favorable staff-satisfaction score alone does not prove reduced adverse events.
