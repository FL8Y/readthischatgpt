# Definitions in the Research Process

- **Problem statement:** concise description of the issue/gap and why it matters.
- **Research question:** focused question guiding the inquiry.
- **Aim/objective:** intended overall result or specific measurable steps.
- **Literature review:** critical synthesis of existing evidence.
- **Conceptual/theoretical framework:** concepts and relationships guiding interpretation.
- **Hypothesis:** testable prediction, usually in quantitative hypothesis testing.
- **Research design:** overall plan linking question, sampling, collection, and analysis.
- **Protocol:** prespecified procedures and safeguards for conducting the study.
- **Operational definition:** explicit specification of how a concept/variable is measured or categorized.
- **Sampling frame:** list/mechanism from which units are selected.
- **Data collection:** systematic acquisition of observations/records.
- **Data analysis:** procedures used to summarize or infer from data.
- **Results:** findings as analyzed.
- **Discussion:** interpretation, comparison with evidence, implications, and limitations.
- **Dissemination:** sharing findings responsibly with relevant audiences.

## Additional terms and distinctions

- **Research objective:** specific intended accomplishment; should be aligned with the research question and methods.
- **Study protocol:** detailed prespecified plan for design, participants, procedures, outcomes, analysis, safety, privacy, and governance.
- **Eligibility criteria:** inclusion/exclusion rules applied to determine who can participate; criteria should be justified and set before recruitment when possible.
- **Primary outcome:** main outcome used to answer the central question; prespecify its definition and measurement time.
- **Comparator/control:** reference condition used to interpret an intervention or exposure; its choice must be scientifically and ethically justified.
- **Bias:** systematic error that distorts selection, measurement, analysis, or interpretation.
- **Confounder:** factor that can distort an exposure-outcome association when not appropriately addressed.
- **Missing data:** values not observed or recorded as expected; amount, pattern, and handling affect interpretation.
- **Protocol deviation:** departure from the approved/planned procedures; document it and follow required reporting/approval processes.
- **Research ethics committee (REC):** independent body that reviews ethical acceptability and participant protections under applicable requirements.
- **Informed consent:** voluntary decision process based on understandable information; a signature alone does not guarantee comprehension.
- **Reporting guideline:** checklist/framework for transparent reporting of a study type; it does not substitute for sound design or conduct.

## How the terms connect

The problem statement explains the gap; the research question focuses it; objectives specify what the study will do; the framework organizes concepts; the design determines how evidence will be obtained; the protocol documents procedures; the sampling plan defines who is invited; operational definitions and tools specify what is measured; analysis summarizes or estimates results; discussion interprets findings with uncertainty/limitations; dissemination communicates them responsibly. A mismatch between these stages weakens the inference.

## Common confusions

- **Aim vs objective:** overall purpose versus specific planned accomplishments.
- **Protocol vs design:** the design is the methodological plan; the protocol records the full operational and governance plan.
- **Results vs discussion:** results report findings; discussion interprets them in relation to evidence and limitations.
- **Data collection vs analysis:** obtaining/recording evidence versus processing and interpreting it.
- **Research approval vs consent:** REC approval does not replace the participant's valid consent where required.
- **Statistical significance vs clinical importance:** a threshold decision does not by itself establish a meaningful patient benefit.
