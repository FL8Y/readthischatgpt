# Phases of the Research Process

## Conceptual phase
Identify problem, review literature, refine question/objectives, define framework, and state hypotheses or qualitative questions. Ensure the question is important and answerable.

## Design/planning phase
Choose design, population and sampling, tools, procedures, analysis plan, resources, timeline, and risk safeguards. Submit the protocol for ethics/REC review and obtain required permissions before recruitment or data collection.

## Empirical phase
Recruit participants fairly, obtain consent as required, collect data consistently, monitor safety, secure records, and document deviations.

## Analytic phase
Clean and analyze data using the planned methods, assess assumptions and missingness, and investigate alternative explanations. Any post hoc analysis should be identified as exploratory.

## Dissemination/utilization phase
Report results, limitations, conflicts, and adverse/unfavorable findings accurately. Share appropriately, consider implementation in context, and monitor effects if findings are translated into practice.

## Important note
Textbooks divide the process into different numbers/names of phases; these are useful functional groupings, not a universal mandatory taxonomy.

## Phase-specific deliverables and decision gates

| Phase | Useful deliverables | Gate before proceeding |
|---|---|---|
| Conceptual | Problem statement, focused question, literature synthesis, objectives, framework, hypothesis or qualitative aim. | The question is important, feasible, sufficiently focused, and not already answered adequately for the intended purpose. |
| Design/planning | Protocol, design justification, sampling plan, operational definitions, instruments, analysis plan, timeline, budget, risk/consent/privacy plan. | Methods can answer the question; the plan is feasible; required REC/institutional/regulatory review and permissions are identified. |
| Empirical | Recruitment and consent records, completed measurements/interviews, source records, deviation and safety logs. | Eligibility and consent requirements are met; procedures follow the approved protocol; participant safety and confidentiality are maintained. |
| Analytic | Data-quality checks, codebook, analysis outputs, missing-data decisions, sensitivity checks, audit trail. | Methods match the question/design; exclusions and deviations are documented; conclusions do not exceed the data. |
| Dissemination/utilization | Report/manuscript, appropriate reporting checklist, limitations, data-sharing plan, implementation/monitoring plan where relevant. | Results, conflicts, methods, and unfavorable findings are reported honestly; privacy and sharing permissions are respected. |

## Iteration without losing accountability

The phases provide structure but are not always linear. Qualitative studies may revise interview prompts or sampling as analysis develops; pilot findings may reveal a measurement problem; interim safety information may require an amendment or pause. Iteration must be documented, and protocol changes that require approval must receive it before implementation, except for urgent actions needed to remove an immediate hazard as governed by the applicable rules.

## Researcher and nurse responsibilities

The principal investigator and research team remain responsible for protocol compliance, participant protection, data integrity, and required reporting. A nurse who notices distress, a safety event, invalid consent, privacy breach, or an unexpected risk should respond within role and urgency, notify the appropriate investigator/clinical lead, and follow REC/institutional procedures. Research activities must not delay urgent clinical care.

## PNLE high-yield distinctions

- Ethics review and permissions precede the research activities for which they are required; they are not an after-the-fact paperwork step.
- A protocol deviation should be documented and assessed; do not silently alter procedures or conceal inconvenient data.
- Analysis and dissemination include null/unfavorable findings and limitations.
- A study's label or phase count may differ by textbook; explain the function of each phase rather than memorizing one supposedly universal number.
