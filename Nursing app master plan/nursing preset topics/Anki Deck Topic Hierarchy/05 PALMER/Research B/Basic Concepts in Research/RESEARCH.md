# Basic Concepts in Research — Research B

## What research is

Research is a systematic, transparent inquiry designed to answer a focused question using appropriate methods and evidence. In nursing, it supports clinical decision-making, education, management, public health, policy, and patient-safety improvement. Research is not simply gathering information or collecting opinions: the question, design, sampling, measurement, analysis, and interpretation must fit together.

## The research cycle

1. **Identify a problem or knowledge gap.** Use clinical observation, patient outcomes, quality data, theory, policy needs, or prior studies.
2. **Review the literature.** Search systematically enough for the purpose, appraise quality and relevance, and identify what is known and uncertain.
3. **Formulate the question and objectives.** Make the question focused, feasible, ethically acceptable, and answerable by available methods.
4. **Choose a design.** Quantitative designs estimate frequencies, associations, differences, or effects; qualitative designs explore meaning, experience, process, and context; mixed methods integrate both when justified.
5. **Define the population and sample.** Specify who the study aims to inform, eligibility criteria, sampling frame, recruitment, sample size rationale, and likely limits to generalizability/transferability.
6. **Operationalize variables or concepts.** Define how abstract ideas will be observed, measured, coded, or interpreted.
7. **Protect participants and obtain required review.** Address consent, risk, privacy, fair selection, conflicts, and REC requirements before recruitment where applicable.
8. **Collect and manage data.** Use standardized procedures, quality checks, secure storage, and an audit trail.
9. **Analyze and interpret.** Match the analysis to the design and question; separate results from interpretation and consider bias, uncertainty, and alternative explanations.
10. **Report and apply findings.** Use an appropriate reporting guideline, describe limitations, and decide whether findings apply to a particular clinical setting.

## Key concepts

| Concept | Definition / role | Important distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Research problem | Gap, uncertainty, or practical issue that motivates inquiry. | A broad topic is not yet a focused research problem. |
| Research question | Focused question the study seeks to answer. | It should determine design and data needs, not be retrofitted after seeing results. |
| Objective / aim | Specific intended accomplishment. | Objectives should align with the question, methods, and outcomes. |
| Hypothesis | Testable prediction, often about a relationship, difference, or effect. | Not every qualitative or exploratory study requires a formal hypothesis. |
| Variable | Attribute that can vary and be observed or measured. | Operational definitions specify how it will be measured in the study. |
| Construct | Abstract concept such as anxiety, trust, or quality of life. | It may require multiple indicators or a validated instrument. |
| Population | Target group about which the study seeks knowledge. | Accessible population and sample may differ from the target population. |
| Sample | Units actually selected or studied. | Sample results may not represent the population if selection or nonresponse is biased. |
| Data | Observations, measurements, documents, narratives, or records used in analysis. | Data quality and provenance affect how confidently results can be interpreted. |
| Bias | Systematic error that distorts findings. | A large sample does not automatically remove bias. |
| Reliability | Consistency of a measure or process under specified conditions. | Reliability alone does not establish validity. |
| Validity / trustworthiness | Degree to which an inference or qualitative account is credible and appropriate for its purpose. | Meaning and assessment differ across quantitative and qualitative traditions. |
| Generalizability / transferability | Whether findings may apply beyond the studied participants/context. | Depends on design, sampling, context, and evidence—not sample size alone. |
| Research ethics | Principles and procedures protecting participants and integrity. | Ethics approval does not replace ongoing researcher responsibility. |

## Quantitative, qualitative, and mixed methods

- **Quantitative research** uses numerical measurement and statistical analysis to estimate distributions, compare groups, test hypotheses, or evaluate associations/effects. Design determines whether causal inference is warranted.
- **Qualitative research** studies meanings, experiences, processes, and context using approaches such as interviews, focus groups, observations, or documents. Sampling is often purposive, and rigor depends on a transparent, reflexive, coherent process.
- **Mixed-methods research** intentionally integrates quantitative and qualitative components. Simply collecting both kinds of data is not enough; the rationale, timing, priority, and integration should be explicit.

## Evidence quality and interpretation

No single design is best for every question. Randomized trials may be useful for intervention effects; observational designs may address prevalence, prognosis, exposure, or questions where randomization is infeasible or unethical; qualitative designs may explain experience and implementation. Appraise whether the design answers the actual question and whether bias, imprecision, confounding, missing data, or measurement problems could explain the result.

Distinguish:
- **Association** from **causation**.
- **Statistical significance** from **clinical importance**.
- **A result in a sample** from **certainty about the target population**.
- **Failure to find evidence** from **evidence that an effect does not exist**.
- **A published claim** from **a claim supported by the underlying methods and data**.

## Ethics and Philippine context

For research involving human participants in the Philippines, determine the applicable PHREB, institutional REC, and other regulatory requirements. Address valid consent when required, risk minimization, fair recruitment, privacy, secure data handling, conflicts of interest, and honest reporting. RA 10173 is relevant to personal-data processing, especially health information; consult current law, National Privacy Commission guidance, and institutional privacy procedures rather than assuming research is a blanket exemption.

## PNLE high-yield review

- Start with the research question; it drives design, sampling, measurement, and analysis.
- A broad topic is not a research problem until the gap or uncertainty is stated.
- Research ethics and scientific validity are connected: invalid methods can expose people to burden without adequate knowledge value.
- A large sample cannot repair systematic bias or an inappropriate design.
- Quantitative and qualitative approaches answer different kinds of questions; choose the approach for the purpose.
- Interpret findings with the design, limitations, uncertainty, and clinical context—not the p-value alone.

## Scope note

This is an introductory study aid. Methodological decisions require topic-specific literature review, statistical expertise where needed, and the current ethics requirements for the actual study.
