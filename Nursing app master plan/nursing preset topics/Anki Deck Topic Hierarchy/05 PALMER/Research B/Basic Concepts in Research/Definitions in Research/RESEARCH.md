# Definitions in Nursing Research — Research B

This glossary defines frequently used research terms. Meanings can vary slightly by discipline or design; use each term consistently in a protocol and define study-specific meanings operationally.

## Question and study planning

- **Research problem:** a gap in knowledge, uncertainty, or practical issue that motivates systematic investigation.
- **Research topic:** the broad subject area; it becomes researchable only when narrowed to a specific problem or question.
- **Research question:** the focused question the study is designed to answer.
- **Aim / purpose:** the overall intended accomplishment of the study.
- **Objective:** a specific, observable or assessable task or outcome aligned with the question.
- **Hypothesis:** a testable prediction about a relationship, difference, or effect. Exploratory and many qualitative studies may not require a formal hypothesis.
- **Rationale / significance:** why the problem matters and what value the study may contribute.
- **Literature review:** critical synthesis of relevant prior work, used to establish context, identify gaps, and inform methods.
- **Conceptual framework:** an organized explanation of concepts and expected relationships guiding the inquiry.
- **Theoretical framework:** a theory used to explain or organize the research problem and interpretation.
- **Protocol / research proposal:** the plan describing the question, design, participants, procedures, analysis, ethics, and management before implementation.

## Participants, sampling, and data

- **Target population:** the group to which the researcher wants the findings to be relevant.
- **Accessible population:** the portion of the target population realistically available for recruitment.
- **Sampling frame:** the list or operational source from which potential participants or units are selected.
- **Sample:** the people, records, events, or other units actually included in the study.
- **Sampling method:** the rule or process used to select the sample (e.g., probability or non-probability sampling).
- **Sample size:** the number of units studied; its adequacy depends on the design, objective, variability, precision/power needs, feasibility, and ethical considerations.
- **Participant / research subject:** a person who takes part in a study; “participant” is often preferred because it emphasizes agency.
- **Inclusion criteria:** conditions a person/unit must meet to be eligible.
- **Exclusion criteria:** conditions that make an otherwise eligible person/unit ineligible for a justified reason.
- **Variable:** a characteristic that can take different values or categories.
- **Independent variable / exposure:** the variable treated as a predictor or exposure in a particular analysis; the term does not automatically imply that it was manipulated.
- **Dependent variable / outcome:** the outcome measured or analyzed in relation to predictors, exposure, or intervention.
- **Confounder:** a factor related to both the exposure/predictor and outcome that can distort their apparent relationship if not addressed appropriately.
- **Operational definition:** the specific way a construct or variable is measured, classified, or observed in a study.
- **Data:** recorded observations, measurements, documents, narratives, or other material used in analysis.
- **Primary data:** data collected directly for the current study.
- **Secondary data:** existing data originally collected for another purpose or by another source.

## Measurement and quality

- **Construct:** an abstract concept (e.g., anxiety, trust, resilience) that may require one or more indicators to measure or interpret.
- **Indicator:** an observable measure or sign used to represent a construct.
- **Instrument / measure:** a tool or procedure used to collect observations (e.g., questionnaire, scale, monitor, structured checklist).
- **Reliability:** consistency of a measurement or process under specified conditions.
- **Validity:** evidence that a measure or inference is appropriate for its intended interpretation and use; it is not a single universal property proven by one statistic.
- **Trustworthiness:** qualitative research criteria and practices supporting credibility, dependability, confirmability, and transferability, used as appropriate to the methodology.
- **Bias:** systematic error that distorts the estimate, observation, selection, or interpretation.
- **Random error:** chance variation that contributes to imprecision.
- **Confounding:** distortion of an exposure-outcome relationship by other factors.
- **Missing data:** values not observed or recorded when expected; amount and mechanism can affect results.
- **Outlier:** an observation unusually distant from other values; it should be checked for data error and assessed substantively, not removed automatically.

## Designs and analysis

- **Quantitative research:** research using numerical data and quantitative analysis to describe, compare, estimate, or test relationships.
- **Qualitative research:** research that explores meaning, experience, process, or context using methods such as interviews, observations, or documents.
- **Mixed-methods research:** a planned approach integrating quantitative and qualitative components; integration is essential.
- **Descriptive statistics:** methods that summarize observed data, such as distributions, means/medians, proportions, and variability.
- **Inferential statistics:** methods that use sample data to estimate population quantities or evaluate hypotheses under stated assumptions.
- **Confidence interval:** a range generated by a method with a stated long-run coverage property under its assumptions; it expresses statistical uncertainty, not a probability that a fixed parameter lies in this one computed interval in the frequentist interpretation.
- **P-value:** under a specified statistical model and null hypothesis, the probability of data at least as incompatible with the null as those observed; it is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true.
- **Statistical significance:** a result meeting a prespecified statistical decision threshold; it does not by itself establish clinical importance, causality, or freedom from bias.
- **Effect size:** a quantitative expression of the magnitude of a difference or association.
- **Generalizability:** the extent to which findings can apply to a target population or other settings, depending on design and evidence.
- **Transferability:** the extent to which qualitative insights may be useful in another context, supported by sufficiently rich contextual description.

## Ethics and reporting

- **Informed consent:** a voluntary, informed decision process with documentation as required; a signature alone does not guarantee comprehension.
- **Research ethics committee (REC):** an independent committee that reviews research for ethical acceptability and participant protections under applicable requirements.
- **Confidentiality:** duties and safeguards limiting access and disclosure of identifiable information.
- **Anonymity:** a state in which identity is not known or cannot reasonably be linked to the data; coded data are not necessarily anonymous.
- **De-identification:** removing or transforming identifying information to reduce linkage risk.
- **Research misconduct:** serious practices such as fabrication, falsification, or plagiarism; definitions and procedures depend on applicable policies.
- **Protocol deviation:** departure from the approved or planned protocol; whether it requires reporting or approval depends on its nature and governing rules.
- **Reporting guideline:** a structured recommendation/checklist for transparent reporting of a particular study type; it does not replace appropriate design or conduct.

## Philippine research context

For human-participant health research, use the current PHREB National Ethical Guidelines, the responsible REC's requirements, and other applicable regulations. RA 10173 is relevant to personal-data processing and health information. Do not assume that definitions from a textbook automatically determine legal or regulatory outcomes; confirm the governing guidance for the specific study.

## PNLE high-yield distinctions

- Topic ≠ problem ≠ question ≠ objective ≠ hypothesis.
- Target population ≠ accessible population ≠ sample.
- Reliability is consistency; validity concerns the intended interpretation/use.
- Random error affects precision; bias is systematic distortion.
- A p-value is not the probability that H₀ is true; statistical significance is not clinical significance.
- Coded data are not automatically anonymous.
- A reporting checklist improves transparency but cannot rescue an inappropriate design.

## Scope note

This is a reference glossary, not an exhaustive methods textbook. Define terms as used in each study and consult a statistician or methods specialist when analytical decisions require it.
