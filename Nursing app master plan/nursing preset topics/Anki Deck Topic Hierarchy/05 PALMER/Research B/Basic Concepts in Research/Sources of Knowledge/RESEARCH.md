# Sources of Knowledge — Nursing Research

## Common sources
- **Empirical/scientific evidence:** systematic observation, measurement, and transparent analysis.
- **Clinical expertise:** experience and skill developed through practice; valuable but vulnerable to bias if not checked against evidence.
- **Patient preferences and lived experience:** essential for person-centered decisions.
- **Authority/textbooks/guidelines:** useful synthesis, but currency, methods, conflicts, and applicability must be assessed.
- **Tradition/common practice:** may provide starting points but is not proof of effectiveness.
- **Intuition:** can alert clinicians to concerns but should trigger assessment and verification rather than replace evidence.

## Evidence-based nursing
Integrate best available research, clinical expertise, and patient values within the care setting. Appraise source quality, date, population, study design, consistency, and conflicts of interest. Distinguish primary research from reviews and expert opinion.

## PNLE high-yield
A widely repeated practice can still be ineffective or harmful. A single study is not automatically superior to a well-conducted systematic review. Guidelines can be outdated or inapplicable to the patient or Philippine setting.
