# Sources of Research Problems — Nursing Research

## Common sources
- **Clinical practice:** recurring complications, care delays, patient-safety events, adherence barriers, or variation in outcomes.
- **Literature review:** gaps, contradictory findings, under-studied groups, limitations, and calls for replication.
- **Theory:** untested relationships or questions about how a model applies in a new context.
- **Policy and technology changes:** new guidance, service models, or digital tools requiring evaluation.
- **Community/patient priorities:** lived experience, unmet needs, access barriers, and health inequities.
- **Quality data:** trends in falls, infections, readmissions, missed care, or patient experience.
- **Professional observation:** questions arising from education, leadership, or clinical workflow.

## Screening a potential problem
Check whether the question is novel enough to matter, answerable, feasible, ethically acceptable, and relevant to Philippine practice. Review existing evidence before assuming that a local observation is a general problem.

## Turning a source into a defensible problem

A source is a starting point, not proof that a research study is needed. For each candidate issue, ask: What exactly was observed? Is the pattern supported by reliable data? Could it reflect reporting or measurement changes? What has already been published? Which population or context is underrepresented? What uncertainty remains after reviewing current evidence? What decision could the answer inform?

| Source | Example | Next step before defining the problem |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical practice | Repeated delays in recognizing deterioration. | Review documented timelines, case mix, escalation processes, and existing guidance. |
| Literature gap | Studies report inconsistent effects of a discharge intervention. | Compare design quality, population, intervention fidelity, outcomes, and certainty. |
| Theory | A model predicts that social support improves self-management. | Define the construct and examine whether the relationship is untested or context-dependent. |
| Policy/technology change | New documentation or monitoring system is introduced. | Identify the implementation question, potential benefits/harms, and appropriate evaluation design. |
| Patient/community priorities | Patients report barriers to follow-up. | Engage stakeholders respectfully and assess whether the issue is common, consequential, and under-studied. |
| Quality data | Falls rise after a workflow change. | Check denominator, acuity, reporting changes, concurrent interventions, and temporal pattern before inferring cause. |
| Professional observation | Staff report frequent interruptions during medication preparation. | Define the phenomenon, examine existing evidence, and consider observational, qualitative, or improvement methods. |

## Prioritizing among problems

Consider clinical importance, number/severity of people affected, equity, urgency, knowledge gap, feasibility, ethics, stakeholder priority, and likelihood that findings can inform action. Do not select a topic solely because it is fashionable, easy to measure, or likely to yield a significant p-value.
