## 1. Scope and purpose

Maternal and child health (MCH) is a continuum of preventive, promotive, screening, clinical, rehabilitative and supportive services for people before and during pregnancy, childbirth and the postnatal period, and for newborns, infants, children and families. Community MCH connects household and community services with skilled maternity care, hospitals, laboratories, immunization, nutrition, reproductive health, social protection and follow-up.

The continuum matters because risk and opportunity change across time. Preconception and antenatal care can identify health needs and prepare for birth; skilled intrapartum care and timely emergency obstetric/newborn care address acute complications; postnatal care detects maternal and newborn problems and supports recovery, feeding and adjustment; ongoing child-health services support growth, development, prevention and timely treatment.

The WHO 2022 postnatal guideline treats a positive postnatal experience as a meaningful goal and recommends consistent information, reassurance and support for women, newborns, partners and families, delivered by a resourced health system that respects context and individual needs. Essential newborn care includes immediate care at birth and continuing care in the newborn period, in facilities and at home. [1][2]

## 2. Philippine context and legal framework

### Kalusugan at Nutrisyon ng Mag-Nanay Act — RA 11148 (2018)
Republic Act No. 11148 scales up integrated maternal, neonatal, child-health and nutrition programs in the **first 1,000 days**, including pregnant and lactating women, adolescent girls and children from birth through 24 months, with priority for nutritionally at-risk and underserved groups. The law describes facility- and community-level services, coordination among government agencies/LGUs and other partners, and interventions across the prenatal, birth/postnatal and early-childhood periods. Exact service packages and operational responsibilities should be checked in the full law and current implementing guidance. [3]

### Other relevant laws and frameworks
- **RA 10354, Responsible Parenthood and Reproductive Health Act (2012):** relevant to reproductive-health information and services. Apply the full law and current guidance to consent, eligibility, service scope and individual circumstances.
- **RA 9288, Newborn Screening Act of 2004:** establishes the national newborn-screening framework; screening results require the defined confirmatory/follow-up pathway. A screen-positive result is not itself a definitive diagnosis. [4]
- **RA 10028, Expanded Breastfeeding Promotion Act (2009):** relevant to breastfeeding support and lactation facilities; verify current implementing rules and applicable workplace requirements.
- **RA 11223, Universal Health Care Act (2019):** broader system context for integrated services and primary care.

These laws do not replace current clinical guidelines, service-specific eligibility, consent requirements, professional scope or local referral procedures. Do not infer a clinical schedule, dose, screening threshold or reporting deadline from a law's title alone.

## 3. The MCH continuum

| Stage | Community/facility priorities | Examples of nursing responsibilities | Safety focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preconception and reproductive health | Health information, voluntary family-planning options, chronic-condition review, nutrition and risk reduction. | Provide unbiased counseling, assess needs, respect reproductive autonomy and refer for indicated services. | Do not coerce a method or assume pregnancy intentions. Verify contraindications and method-specific guidance. |
| Pregnancy/antenatal period | Early enrollment, regular quality antenatal contacts, risk assessment, nutrition, appropriate immunization/supplements, birth/emergency planning and referral for high-risk pregnancy. | Track follow-up, assess warning symptoms, teach when/where to seek care, document and coordinate referral. | Acute maternal warning signs require prompt assessment; routine education must not delay emergency care. |
| Labor and birth | Skilled attendance, respectful maternity care, safe monitoring, readiness for complications and timely emergency obstetric care. | Work within role, communicate changes promptly, prepare referral/transfer and support informed decision-making. | Hemorrhage, seizures, severe respiratory/circulatory compromise, obstructed/prolonged labor or fetal/newborn compromise are time-critical concerns. Use current protocols. |
| Immediate newborn period | Essential newborn care, warmth, breathing assessment, feeding support, infection prevention, screening and timely referral when indicated. | Observe, document, support feeding and skin-to-skin care when appropriate, identify danger signs and escalate. | Respiratory difficulty, poor feeding, marked lethargy, convulsions, abnormal temperature or other severe signs need urgent clinical assessment. |
| Postnatal period | Maternal recovery, newborn assessment, feeding support, mental-health needs, contraception counseling, immunization/screening follow-up and home/community support. | Ensure follow-up, assess maternal/newborn warning signs, support lactation and connect to services. | Do not assume discharge means risk has ended; serious bleeding, fever, severe headache/visual symptoms, chest pain/dyspnea, severe mood/psychotic symptoms or newborn danger signs require prompt escalation. |
| Infancy and childhood | Growth/development monitoring, immunization, nutrition, oral health, injury prevention, developmental surveillance and treatment/referral for illness. | Measure and interpret growth using the appropriate chart, counsel caregivers, track missed services and refer developmental/clinical concerns. | Use current age-specific protocols; deterioration, dehydration, breathing difficulty, altered consciousness or other severe signs require urgent care. |

The table is a planning framework, not a replacement for detailed clinical guidance. Exact contact schedules, test timing, thresholds and interventions vary by current Philippine protocol, risk status and clinical context.

## 4. Community assessment and case-finding

A community MCH assessment should identify who is served, who is missed, and why. Useful information includes:
- Population and pregnancy/birth cohort estimates with source, date and data limitations.
- Antenatal/postnatal service access, skilled birth attendance, referral pathways and emergency transport.
- Maternal/newborn/child morbidity and mortality indicators, with clearly defined numerators, denominators and reporting periods.
- Nutrition status, food security, breastfeeding support, immunization, water/sanitation and social-protection access.
- Barriers related to cost, distance, transport, service hours, disability, language, stigma, violence, family support and documentation.
- Availability of trained staff, medicines/supplies, laboratory/screening services, cold chain, transport and receiving facilities.
- Community strengths, women's/parent groups, barangay health workers, local organizations and culturally appropriate supports.

Use authorized records and current reporting definitions. Avoid double-counting people who use multiple facilities; distinguish residents from clients who travel from outside the catchment. Protect identifiable maternal, reproductive and child-health information.

## 5. Community-health nurse workflow

1. **Assess:** identify the person's stage, needs, risks, protective factors, preferences and access barriers.
2. **Screen/recognize:** use only appropriate, current tools and protocols; distinguish screening from diagnosis.
3. **Prioritize:** immediate danger and rapidly deteriorating status first, then urgent clinical needs, prevention gaps, continuity and population-level priorities.
4. **Plan with the person/family:** agree on feasible actions, informed choices, follow-up, warning signs and who will help; involve family/supporters only when appropriate and safe.
5. **Provide care within scope:** health education, authorized preventive services, monitoring, documentation and care coordination.
6. **Refer safely:** identify urgency and destination, communicate relevant information securely, arrange transfer when needed and document handover.
7. **Follow up:** check whether the person reached the next service and whether symptoms, feeding, recovery or child development need reassessment.
8. **Evaluate:** monitor reach, timeliness, quality, equity, referral completion and outcomes; adapt the plan based on evidence and feedback.

### Prioritization
ABCs, acuity and Maslow's hierarchy can support reasoning but are not rigid algorithms. A person with severe bleeding, seizures, respiratory distress, shock, altered consciousness or another time-critical problem needs immediate appropriate response; do not delay care to complete routine records, screening, counseling or a home-visit checklist.

## 6. Danger signs and referral principles

### Maternal warning concerns
Examples that warrant prompt clinical assessment under the applicable protocol include heavy bleeding; seizure; severe headache or visual disturbance; severe abdominal pain; fever with significant illness; breathing difficulty or chest pain; fainting/collapse; signs of shock; reduced fetal movement when relevant; and severe mental-health symptoms, including risk of harm to self or infant. This list is not exhaustive, and urgency depends on the presentation. Do not attempt to manage a serious complication solely through community education.

### Newborn/child warning concerns
Examples include difficulty breathing, cyanosis, inability or marked difficulty feeding, convulsions, severe lethargy or reduced responsiveness, abnormal temperature, severe dehydration, significant bleeding, rapidly worsening jaundice or other signs specified in current neonatal/child-health guidance. Newborns can deteriorate quickly; a caregiver's report of a major change deserves careful assessment.

### Safe referral
- Explain why assessment is needed and what will happen next.
- Use the current referral route and verify the receiving facility when possible.
- Communicate relevant history, findings, interventions and urgency securely.
- Arrange transport/escort appropriate to the clinical risk.
- Do not send an unstable person through an ordinary routine referral process.
- Document the reason, advice, contacts, handover and follow-up plan.
- If the service is unavailable, escalate to the next appropriate authority/pathway rather than assuming the referral is complete.

## 7. Newborn screening and follow-up

Newborn screening is designed to identify infants who may need further evaluation for selected conditions so that confirmatory testing and timely care can occur. It is not a diagnostic conclusion by itself. Community nurses should:
- Explain the purpose and process using current program materials.
- Follow the current collection, timing, specimen handling, consent and documentation requirements.
- Ensure the specimen is submitted through the approved pathway and results can be tracked.
- Treat an abnormal/positive screen as requiring prompt follow-up according to the program—not as proof that the infant definitely has the condition.
- Help locate families and coordinate repeat/confirmatory testing or specialist review when directed.
- Escalate symptoms or clinical deterioration independently of a pending screening result.

The exact conditions screened, timing, specimen method, repeat rules and responsible facilities depend on current DOH/Newborn Screening Reference Center guidance and must be verified in the dedicated Newborn Screening child topic. [4]

## 8. Respectful, family-centred and rights-based care

- Obtain informed consent where required and respect the person's right to ask questions and participate in decisions.
- Support voluntary, informed reproductive choices without pressure or discrimination.
- Use respectful, non-stigmatizing communication; protect privacy in home visits and community records.
- Consider adolescents' needs and the legal/ethical rules for consent, confidentiality and safeguarding in the specific setting.
- Assess intimate-partner violence, abuse, neglect or safeguarding concerns sensitively and follow current law/protocol; do not confront a suspected perpetrator in a way that increases danger.
- Support breastfeeding according to current guidance while avoiding blame or shaming; assess feeding problems and refer when needed.
- Include partners/family only when the client wants this and it is safe.
- Adapt information for language, disability, literacy and cultural context.
- Do not promise confidentiality beyond its lawful limits or share information broadly because a case is “community health.”

## 9. Program monitoring and evaluation

Select indicators that reflect quality and continuity rather than activity counts alone.

| Domain | Example measure | Key caution |
|---|---|---|
| Access/coverage | Proportion of eligible population receiving a defined service. | State the denominator, time period and eligibility rule; check for duplicate/missing records. |
| Antenatal/postnatal continuity | Timely follow-up and completed referrals. | A visit count alone does not show quality of care. |
| Emergency readiness | Functioning referral pathway, transport and receiving-facility communication. | A written protocol is not proof that transfer is feasible today. |
| Newborn screening | Specimens collected/submitted; abnormal results followed through the required pathway. | A specimen count does not prove results were received or acted on. |
| Nutrition/growth | Age-appropriate assessment and follow-up for identified risk. | Use correct measurement technique and growth reference; do not infer diagnosis from one measurement alone. |
| Immunization | Coverage and timely completion using current target definitions. | Target denominators and schedules are program-specific and may change. |
| Equity | Reach and outcomes for underserved groups where lawful and appropriate. | Protect privacy in small populations and avoid stigmatizing labels. |
| Experience/rights | Client feedback on respect, informed choice, privacy and accessibility. | Provide a safe way to report concerns. |

Use trends to investigate gaps and improve services. Changes can reflect altered definitions, reporting completeness, population movement or access—not only changes in health status.

## 11. PNLE high-yield distinctions

- MCH is a continuum from reproductive/preconception care through pregnancy, birth, postnatal care, infancy and childhood—not a single clinic visit.
- The first 1,000 days are central to RA 11148's integrated maternal/neonatal/child-health and nutrition strategy; check the law for exact scope and current implementing guidance.
- A screening result is not a definitive diagnosis; ensure confirmatory testing and follow-up.
- Postnatal care is important for both maternal recovery and newborn well-being; do not assume risk ends at discharge.
- Danger signs and unstable status take priority over routine teaching, paperwork or scheduled outreach.
- Family involvement is not automatic; respect the client's preferences, privacy and safety.
- Legal frameworks, WHO recommendations, local protocol and professional scope are distinct sources of authority.
- Use current Philippine DOH guidance for exact service schedules, vaccine schedules, screening timing, drug doses, eligibility and reporting deadlines.
- The parent note is an integration map; every child topic still requires topic-specific research.
