## 1. Purpose and guiding principles

Community maternal and child health (MCH) activities aim to promote health, prevent avoidable illness and death, detect complications early, support timely care and maintain continuity from reproductive/preconception health through pregnancy, childbirth, postnatal recovery, newborn care and childhood.

The Philippine **Kalusugan at Nutrisyon ng Mag-Nanay Act (RA 11148)** provides a framework for integrated maternal, neonatal, child-health and nutrition services during the first 1,000 days. Current service details, eligibility, reporting and implementation responsibilities must be checked in the full law, current implementing guidance and applicable DOH/LGU program instructions. [1]

Activities should be:
- based on assessed population needs and current evidence;
- accessible, equitable and culturally/linguistically responsive;
- respectful of informed choice, consent, privacy and dignity;
- coordinated across community and facility services;
- linked to a feasible referral and follow-up pathway;
- delivered within professional scope and authorized protocol; and
- evaluated with clear indicators, not attendance counts alone.

This note is a framework for researching MCH activities, not a claim that every listed activity is available or required in every locality. The exact package depends on current national policy, facility level, age/stage, clinical risk and local resources.

## 2. Activity map across the MCH continuum

| Stage/program area | Common community-health activities | Nursing role and safety considerations |
|---|---|---|
| **Preconception/reproductive health** | Health information, voluntary family-planning counseling/referral, assessment of health risks and access barriers. | Respect reproductive autonomy; method-specific eligibility and contraindications require current guidance. |
| **Antenatal care** | Pregnancy identification/enrollment, follow-up reminders, health education, risk assessment, birth preparedness, nutrition and referral coordination. | Use current ANC schedule and clinical protocol; identify maternal warning signs and arrange timely assessment. |
| **Birth preparedness and emergency readiness** | Discuss intended birth facility, transport, support person, emergency contacts and referral route. | Verify the facility's actual capability and transport plan; a written plan alone does not ensure access. |
| **Intrapartum/skilled birth care** | Link clients to skilled birth attendance and emergency obstetric care; communicate referrals. | Do not substitute community education for skilled clinical care; urgent complications require immediate escalation. |
| **Immediate newborn care/EINC** | Support thermal care, skin-to-skin when safe, appropriate cord management, early feeding support and newborn observation. | Resuscitation/urgent stabilization takes priority over routine sequence; follow current EINC protocol. |
| **Postnatal care** | Follow-up for maternal recovery, newborn condition/feeding, mental-health needs, family planning and danger signs. | Do not assume risk ends at discharge; use current postnatal schedule and referral rules. |
| **Newborn screening** | Explain screening, ensure specimen/result tracking, support confirmatory referral when needed. | A positive screen is not diagnosis; missing/rejected specimen is not a negative result. |
| **Immunization** | Review eligible cohorts, provide/coordinate vaccination, track due doses and missed opportunities. | Use current Philippine schedule, product-specific precautions, cold chain and AEFI procedures. |
| **Nutrition and breastfeeding** | Nutrition assessment, breastfeeding support, growth monitoring and referral for identified risk. | Use correct measurement/age-appropriate standards; avoid blaming caregivers for structural food/access barriers. |
| **Child growth/development** | Monitor growth and developmental concerns, provide caregiver education and refer when indicated. | Screening/monitoring does not replace diagnostic assessment; follow current age-specific guidance. |
| **Adolescent health** | Age-appropriate education, health promotion, mental-health/SRH information and safeguarding referral. | Follow current consent/confidentiality and child-protection rules; provide privacy and nonjudgmental communication. |
| **Community outreach/data** | Home visits, missed-service follow-up, service mapping, health teaching, data review and community engagement. | Protect confidentiality, document accurately, avoid duplicate counts and use data for improvement. |

These activities are common service areas, not a universal checklist. The current DOH/LGU service package and the specific course source determine which tasks are required and how they are delivered.

## 3. Plan an activity using a standard workflow

1. **Define the need:** use community assessment, routine service data, client feedback and current epidemiology.
2. **Identify the eligible group:** specify age/stage, risk group, geographic catchment and period.
3. **Set a measurable objective:** describe the intended change in service access, skill, follow-up or outcome.
4. **Check evidence and authority:** use current DOH/program guidance and confirm the activity is within scope.
5. **Plan resources:** staff, training, supplies, venue, transport, privacy, records and referral capacity.
6. **Co-design delivery:** involve the community and relevant partners; account for language, disability, cost and safety.
7. **Implement and document:** follow the authorized protocol and record actual services, refusals, referrals and barriers accurately.
8. **Follow up:** check whether referral, screening, next dose, postnatal visit or other next step occurred.
9. **Evaluate:** compare results with baseline and target; examine equity, quality and unintended consequences.
10. **Revise:** address system barriers and update the plan when guidance or local conditions change.

## 4. Prioritization and safety

**Immediate danger takes priority over routine activities.** Examples include severe maternal bleeding, seizures, shock, breathing difficulty, altered consciousness, severe abdominal pain with concerning findings, newborn respiratory distress, convulsions, inability to feed with deterioration or another time-critical emergency. Follow the relevant clinical protocol and arrange urgent referral.

Do not delay care to finish a home-visit form, health talk, immunization tally, screening collection, attendance sheet or routine data report. Stabilize and refer within competence; documentation should support care rather than obstruct it.

For routine planning, prioritize using:
- severity and urgency;
- population burden and preventability;
- inequity and underserved groups;
- feasibility and resource availability;
- community priorities and acceptability;
- referral capacity and sustainability; and
- expected benefit and possible harms.

ABCs and Maslow's hierarchy can aid clinical reasoning but should not be applied mechanically to a deteriorating patient.

## 5. Nursing responsibilities by activity type

### Health education
Assess the learner's needs and barriers, use current information, communicate in plain language, check understanding and provide a clear next step. Attendance does not prove learning.

### Screening and assessment
Use the approved tool and eligibility criteria; explain purpose and limits; document findings; distinguish a screen-positive result from diagnosis; arrange confirmatory assessment/referral.

### Immunization and preventive services
Verify identity, eligibility, history, product and current protocol; maintain cold chain and safe administration; document accurately; recognize/report AEFIs according to current rules.

### Home visits and follow-up
Plan safe contact, respect privacy, assess the household context without assumptions, identify danger signs/access barriers and coordinate services. Do not disclose sensitive information to neighbors or unsafe family members.

### Referral and coordination
Know the receiving service, urgency, transport, handover and follow-up process. A referral slip or message sent is not proof that the person reached care.

### Records and data
Use defined numerators/denominators and current reporting instructions. Separate observed facts from assumptions; do not fabricate missing values or change definitions to improve apparent coverage.

## 6. Community participation and intersectoral coordination

MCH outcomes depend on health services and social/environmental conditions. Depending on the activity, partners may include barangay health workers, local health offices, schools, nutrition programs, social welfare, water/sanitation services, transport providers and community organizations.

Effective coordination should clarify:
- who is responsible for each task;
- what information may be shared and through which authorized channel;
- the referral/contact route and response expectation;
- what resources are available;
- how community feedback will influence planning; and
- how results and unresolved barriers will be communicated back.

Community participation should be meaningful and voluntary. Do not substitute unpaid community labor for required clinical staff or blame families for inaccessible services.

## 7. Documentation and ethical safeguards

For each activity, record according to current policy:
- purpose and eligible group;
- date/location and responsible staff;
- assessment and relevant findings;
- intervention/service actually delivered;
- education and understanding checked;
- consent/refusal where required;
- referrals, handover and follow-up plan;
- barriers, missed services and corrective actions; and
- data source and any limitations.

Protect identifiable reproductive, pregnancy, newborn and child-health information. Explain confidentiality and its legal limits. Use only authorized systems and avoid sharing case details in public group chats or community meetings.

## 8. Monitoring and evaluation

Choose indicators that match the activity:
- **Reach:** eligible people reached compared with the defined target.
- **Timeliness:** service delivered within the relevant protocol's time window.
- **Quality/safety:** required steps completed, documentation accuracy, adverse events and corrective action.
- **Continuity:** referral, screening follow-up, next-dose or postnatal follow-up completion.
- **Outcome:** change in a relevant health indicator, interpreted with other determinants.
- **Equity:** whether underserved groups were reached and barriers were reduced.
- **Experience:** client feedback on respect, privacy, informed choice and accessibility.

For each measure, define numerator, denominator, period, geography, data source and responsible person. A higher activity count is not necessarily better quality. More identified cases may reflect improved detection; lower counts may reflect under-reporting or access barriers.

## 9. Philippine context and current-source requirements

RA 11148 provides the first-1,000-days policy context for integrated maternal/neonatal/child-health and nutrition services. RA 9288 provides the newborn-screening legal framework; RA 10152 concerns mandatory infants/children immunization; RA 10354 addresses reproductive health; RA 11223 provides broader universal-health-care context. Each law has its own scope and implementation rules; do not infer exact clinical schedules or local entitlements from the law title alone. [1][2][3][4]

Before implementing a specific activity, verify:
- the current DOH manual/administrative order and amendments;
- the correct target group and service schedule;
- clinical eligibility/contraindications and professional scope;
- reporting/recording requirements;
- local facility capacity and referral route; and
- the current version/date of the guidance.

## 10. PNLE high-yield distinctions

- MCH is a continuum from reproductive health/preconception through pregnancy, birth, postnatal care and childhood.
- Health education, screening, treatment, referral and follow-up are different activities with different objectives.
- Screening is not diagnosis; abnormal findings need the appropriate pathway.
- A referral is not complete until the next service is reached or the unresolved barrier is addressed.
- Use current DOH schedule and eligibility rules for immunization, screening, ANC/postnatal care and other services.
- Emergency assessment and referral take priority over routine program activities.
- Community participation and family involvement must respect autonomy, privacy and safety.
- Evaluate quality, continuity and equity—not just number of sessions or forms completed.
- The folder label “Activities” is broad; verify the course's intended activity list before treating this framework as the final assigned scope.
