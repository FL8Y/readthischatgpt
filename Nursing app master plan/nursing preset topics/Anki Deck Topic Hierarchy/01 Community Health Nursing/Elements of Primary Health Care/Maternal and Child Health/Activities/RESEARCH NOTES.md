# Maternal and Child Health Activities in Community Health Nursing

**Topic path:** `Elements of Primary Health Care/Maternal and Child Health/Activities`  
**Status:** Substantive research draft — current Philippine DOH schedules, clinical protocols and source links require final verification.  
**Last researched:** 2026-10-17  
**Mandatory standard:** Community Health Nursing `RESEARCH TOPIC GUIDE.md`.

## 1. Purpose and continuum of care

Maternal and child health (MCH) activities aim to protect health and reduce preventable illness, complications and deaths across preconception, pregnancy, childbirth, postpartum, newborn, infancy, childhood and adolescence. Services should be continuous, respectful, accessible and linked across home, community, primary-care and referral facilities. One visit or educational session is not a complete continuum of care.

Community-health nursing combines assessment, health promotion, prevention, early recognition, referral, follow-up, documentation and coordination with families and other sectors. The exact service package, schedules, eligibility and clinical actions must follow current Philippine DOH guidance and the person's needs.

## 2. Core activity areas

### A. Preconception and reproductive health
- Offer respectful, voluntary reproductive-health/family-planning information and referral.
- Assess relevant health conditions, medications, nutrition and risk factors within scope.
- Support informed choice and access to appropriate services.
- Avoid coercion, stigma or assumptions about desired family size.
- Verify method eligibility, contraindications and current guidance before method-specific counseling or administration.

### B. Antenatal care
- Encourage early and regular antenatal care according to current DOH guidance.
- Assess maternal history, current symptoms, risk factors and barriers to attendance.
- Support recommended screening, immunization, supplementation and preventive interventions only according to current protocol.
- Provide education on nutrition, rest, danger signs, birth preparedness, emergency transport and postpartum/newborn planning.
- Coordinate timely referral for high-risk findings or complications.
- Document visits, results, counseling, referrals and follow-up accurately.

**Danger signs require urgent clinical assessment**, including severe bleeding, severe headache/visual changes, seizures, severe abdominal pain, difficulty breathing, fever with serious illness, reduced fetal movement when relevant under current guidance, or other severe/worsening symptoms. Use current local triage/referral protocols rather than an incomplete memory list.

### C. Birth preparedness and skilled childbirth
- Help the pregnant person/family identify a skilled birth attendant and an appropriate facility.
- Plan transport, finances/support, contact numbers and backup arrangements.
- Explain why facility capability and referral availability matter.
- Encourage respectful maternity care and informed communication.
- Ensure rapid referral if labor complications or maternal/newborn danger signs arise.
- Do not assume a facility can perform comprehensive emergency obstetric/newborn functions without checking current capability.

### D. Intrapartum and emergency obstetric/newborn care
- Recognize and escalate emergencies such as severe bleeding, eclampsia/seizures, suspected sepsis, obstructed labor, maternal deterioration or a newborn who is not breathing adequately.
- Activate the appropriate emergency response and referral pathway.
- Provide immediate care only within training, scope, equipment and authorized protocol.
- Communicate the condition, timing, relevant history, observations and actions clearly to the receiving team.
- Do not delay lifesaving care for routine paperwork or a program target.

### E. Postpartum and postnatal care
- Support timely maternal and newborn postnatal checks under current DOH guidance.
- Assess maternal recovery, bleeding, infection signs, blood pressure concerns, pain, feeding, emotional well-being and access to support as appropriate.
- Assess newborn feeding, temperature, breathing, activity, jaundice/illness signs and other findings according to current protocol.
- Reinforce danger signs and how to access urgent care.
- Discuss voluntary postpartum family planning and follow-up where appropriate.
- Refer maternal or newborn deterioration promptly; do not treat a routine home visit as a substitute for emergency assessment.

### F. Newborn care and screening
- Support immediate essential newborn care and safe thermal/feeding practices under current guidance.
- Facilitate newborn screening within the official timing and referral process.
- Ensure caregivers understand the purpose of screening and the importance of follow-up for abnormal results.
- Support timely birth registration and connection to routine newborn/child services where this falls within local workflow.
- Never interpret a screening result as a definitive diagnosis without the required confirmation pathway.

### G. Child health and growth
- Support routine immunization according to the current Philippine schedule and product-specific rules.
- Monitor growth with the correct age-specific chart and measurement technique.
- Assess feeding, nutrition, development, illness history and environmental barriers.
- Provide age-appropriate health education and injury-prevention messages.
- Recognize severe illness/dehydration, breathing difficulty, altered consciousness, seizures, inability to feed or other danger signs and refer urgently.
- Follow current integrated child-illness and referral guidance; do not improvise drug doses or vaccine schedules.

### H. Nutrition, development and psychosocial support
- Assess food security, feeding practices, growth trajectory and barriers to nutritious food.
- Support breastfeeding and age-appropriate complementary feeding according to current guidance.
- Identify developmental concerns and refer for appropriate assessment/services.
- Consider caregiver well-being, mental health, violence, disability and social support when relevant and safe.
- Use non-stigmatizing communication and respect family/caregiver context.

### I. Immunization and communicable-disease prevention
- Review vaccination records and use the current schedule/catch-up guidance.
- Maintain cold chain and safe injection practices under the authorized protocol.
- Screen for contraindications/precautions using current product/program guidance.
- Explain expected reactions and when to seek care.
- Follow adverse-event reporting requirements.
- Coordinate disease prevention, surveillance and referral when indicated.

### J. Family education, follow-up and coordination
- Use plain language, teach-back and accessible materials.
- Explain who to contact, when to return and which danger signs require immediate care.
- Coordinate with midwives, nurses, physicians, barangay health workers, local health offices, social welfare, schools and other services as appropriate.
- Track missed appointments and referrals while avoiding blame; ask about transport, cost, safety, language and service availability.
- Protect confidentiality and share information only through authorized channels.

## 3. Community-health nursing workflow

1. **Assess:** identify the maternal/child life stage, health status, risk factors, protective factors, social context and access barriers.
2. **Prioritize:** urgent danger and rapid deterioration first, then time-sensitive screening/referral, then routine prevention and education.
3. **Plan:** choose evidence-based activities and define roles, timelines, supplies, referrals and follow-up.
4. **Implement safely:** follow current DOH/clinical guidance, consent/privacy rules, infection prevention and professional scope.
5. **Refer:** state urgency, communicate key information and use the current receiving-facility pathway.
6. **Document:** record observations, services, counseling, consent/refusal, referrals and follow-up accurately.
7. **Evaluate:** assess coverage, quality, referral completion, outcomes and equity.
8. **Adapt:** address gaps and community feedback; do not treat attendance counts as proof of improved health.

## 4. Safety, contraindications and scope

- Use current Philippine clinical/program guidance for vaccine schedules, supplements, screening, medications and pregnancy-specific decisions.
- Do not prescribe, stop or change medication outside professional scope, a valid order or authorized protocol.
- Check product-specific contraindications/precautions and relevant history; do not rely on a generic MCH note for exact eligibility.
- Protect consent and autonomy; family members should not override the competent adult's choices.
- Explain confidentiality and its lawful limits, including applicable child-protection/public-health reporting.
- Do not delay emergency referral for a survey, educational session or documentation task.
- Do not assume that every household has safe transport, clean water, food or family support; assess structural barriers and coordinate services.
- Use age-appropriate, nonjudgmental communication with children/adolescents and follow safeguarding procedures when safety concerns arise.

## 5. Evaluation indicators

| Area | Possible indicator | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Antenatal care | Timely initiation/visits according to current definition; high-risk referral completion. | Use the current schedule and denominator. |
| Skilled birth/emergency care | Birth preparedness, referral arrival, emergency response. | A plan or referral issued does not prove care received. |
| Postnatal care | Maternal/newborn follow-up within the defined period. | Define timing and eligible population. |
| Newborn screening | Eligible newborns screened and abnormal results followed up. | Screening volume is not diagnostic confirmation. |
| Immunization | Coverage among eligible children; cold-chain and safety checks. | Use current schedule, denominator and valid records. |
| Nutrition/growth | Correct measurements, follow-up for abnormal growth and referral completion. | Measurement quality and chart choice matter. |
| Equity/access | Reach by geography and relevant population groups. | Protect privacy and use valid denominators. |
| Quality/safety | Protocol adherence, safety incidents and corrective actions. | Increased reporting may indicate improved detection. |

Define numerator, denominator, period, source and responsible person. Interpret changes with context and avoid unsupported causal claims.

## 6. Philippine context

MCH service packages and workflows depend on current DOH maternal/newborn/child-health guidelines, immunization manuals, newborn-screening rules, facility licensing and local referral arrangements. RA 11223 (Universal Health Care Act) provides broader context for integrated services and navigation, but it does not specify every current schedule or clinical threshold. Consult the current primary source for the exact service.

WHO recommendations provide international evidence-based context; they do not replace Philippine law, DOH program rules or individual clinical assessment.

## 7. PNLE high-yield distinctions

- MCH is a continuum from preconception through adolescence, not a single prenatal visit.
- Birth preparedness includes skilled care, transport, emergency contacts and backup plans.
- Maternal/newborn danger signs require prompt escalation.
- BEmONC/CEmONC capability must be confirmed; referral should not be delayed.
- Newborn screening is not a diagnosis; abnormal results need the official follow-up pathway.
- Use current immunization schedules and product-specific contraindications.
- Growth monitoring requires correct measurement, chart and follow-up.
- Respectful, voluntary reproductive-health counseling is essential.
- Counts/coverage are not proof of quality or health impact.
- Immediate danger and deterioration override routine program targets.
