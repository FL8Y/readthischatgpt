# Essential Intrapartum and Newborn Care (EINC) — Community Health Nursing

**Topic path:** `Elements of Primary Health Care/Maternal and Child Health/EINC`  
**Status:** Substantive research draft — current Philippine protocol comparison and independent verification remain.  
**Last researched:** 2026-10-17  
**Mandatory standard:** Community Health Nursing `RESEARCH TOPIC GUIDE.md`.

## 1. Definition and purpose

**Essential Intrapartum and Newborn Care (EINC)** is a package of evidence-based practices intended to improve care during labor/birth and the immediate newborn period, protect maternal and newborn safety, support physiologic transition after birth and prevent avoidable complications. In Philippine nursing education, the package is commonly associated with the DOH's “Unang Yakap” approach. The exact sequence, documentation, exceptions and responsibilities must follow the current authorized Philippine protocol and facility policy.

WHO's essential newborn care framework emphasizes immediate and ongoing care, including thermal protection, early and exclusive breastfeeding support, infection prevention, recognition of danger signs and timely referral. WHO's postnatal-care guidance stresses that the mother and newborn both need assessment and support, and that respectful communication and family-centred care are part of a positive postnatal experience. [1][2]

EINC is not simply a checklist of tasks. The team must assess the mother and baby continuously, protect the mother–newborn dyad when clinically appropriate, recognize when emergency care takes priority, and avoid unnecessary separation or interventions that disrupt beneficial contact without a clinical reason.

## 2. Core immediate practices

The traditional Philippine EINC teaching package emphasizes a sequence of immediate care practices often remembered as:
1. **Immediate and thorough drying** of the newborn.
2. **Immediate skin-to-skin contact** with the mother when both are stable and no clinical reason prevents it.
3. **Properly timed cord clamping/cutting**, with delayed clamping consistent with current guidance and the clinical situation.
4. **Non-separation and support for early breastfeeding**, including helping the infant initiate breastfeeding when ready.

The exact protocol order and operational steps must be verified against the current Philippine DOH EINC issuance and facility procedure. Do not treat a mnemonic as permission to ignore maternal/newborn instability or a resuscitation need.

### Immediate drying and thermal protection
Newborns lose heat rapidly, particularly when wet, exposed or preterm/low birth weight. Dry promptly and thoroughly, remove wet material and maintain warmth according to protocol. Skin-to-skin contact and appropriate covering can support thermoregulation when safe. Avoid unnecessary bathing or exposure during the early transition period.

### Skin-to-skin contact
When mother and newborn are clinically stable, place the dried newborn skin-to-skin, maintain warmth and observe breathing, color, tone and responsiveness. Ensure the newborn's airway remains unobstructed and that the mother can safely hold the baby. Skin-to-skin is not passive: the team must continue observation and respond to deterioration.

### Cord clamping
WHO recommends that the umbilical cord not be clamped earlier than one minute after birth in routine circumstances, with clinical exceptions when immediate maternal or newborn care requires earlier clamping. Delayed clamping supports placental transfusion and neonatal outcomes. Coordinate with the resuscitation team and follow current guidance if the newborn needs urgent resuscitation or maternal condition is unstable. [3]

Do not automatically equate EINC/AMTSL with immediate cord clamping. Historical protocols may differ; current guidance and the clinical situation govern.

### Early breastfeeding and non-separation
Support initiation of breastfeeding as soon as the mother and newborn are ready, commonly within the first hour when clinically appropriate. Assist positioning and latch, observe feeding cues and swallowing, and provide practical support rather than pressure or blame. If direct breastfeeding is not possible, follow current lactation/newborn-care guidance for expressed milk and alternative feeding plans. Do not separate a stable dyad for routine procedures that can safely be performed with the baby with the mother or after initial contact.

## 3. When immediate newborn resuscitation takes priority

A newborn who is apneic/gasping, has ineffective breathing, poor tone or other signs requiring resuscitation needs immediate assessment and appropriate resuscitation by trained personnel under the current neonatal resuscitation protocol. Do not delay effective ventilation or emergency care to complete routine drying/skin-to-skin/feeding steps. The team should use the protocol's pathway for cord management and resuscitation; do not improvise beyond training.

If the mother is unstable, has major hemorrhage, requires urgent intervention or cannot safely hold the newborn, stabilize the mother and provide appropriate newborn care by trained staff. Preserve contact/reunification as soon as safe and feasible. Clinical urgency outranks a routine EINC sequence.

## 4. Continued essential newborn care

After immediate transition, care includes assessment, prevention, support and follow-up appropriate to the newborn's condition and current national guidance:

- **Breathing and transition:** observe breathing, color, tone, temperature and responsiveness; identify distress promptly.
- **Thermal care:** prevent hypothermia and overheating; use current protocols for preterm/low-birth-weight infants.
- **Feeding:** assess latch/transfer and feeding effectiveness; support exclusive breastfeeding when appropriate and feasible, and evaluate concerns such as poor feeding, dehydration or ineffective milk transfer.
- **Infection prevention:** hand hygiene, clean equipment, appropriate cord care and aseptic practice for procedures.
- **Identification and documentation:** follow facility procedures for safe newborn identification, records and handover.
- **Prophylaxis, screening and immunization:** follow current Philippine policies, eligibility, timing, consent and documentation rules. These are separate interventions with their own guidance; do not infer drug doses or schedules from the EINC overview.
- **Postnatal observation:** assess the newborn and mother at the recommended intervals and before discharge; provide a clear follow-up plan and danger-sign teaching.
- **Family education:** explain feeding, warmth, hygiene, safe sleep according to current guidance, follow-up and when to seek urgent care.
- **Referral:** arrange appropriate evaluation for abnormalities, prematurity, low birth weight, illness or other risks.

## 5. Maternal care and respectful intrapartum practice

EINC sits within the broader intrapartum and postpartum continuum. Safe care includes maternal assessment, monitoring, respectful communication, consent, privacy, appropriate pain/support measures, recognition of complications and timely escalation. The mother should not be reduced to a “birth environment” for the newborn; her health and preferences remain central.

Community-health nurses support continuity by:
- ensuring birth preparedness and a feasible referral plan before labor;
- helping families understand skilled birth care and emergency warning signs;
- coordinating transfer and handover when complications arise;
- checking maternal recovery and newborn feeding/condition during postnatal follow-up;
- linking families to newborn screening, immunization, nutrition and other services under current guidance; and
- identifying barriers to timely follow-up, including transport, cost, language and service availability.

## 6. Newborn danger signs and escalation

Urgent clinical assessment is required for concerning findings such as:
- apnea, gasping, severe breathing difficulty or central cyanosis;
- convulsions or markedly reduced responsiveness;
- inability to feed or marked deterioration in feeding;
- severe lethargy, limpness or rapidly worsening condition;
- abnormal temperature or suspected serious infection;
- significant bleeding, severe dehydration or other acute deterioration;
- rapidly worsening/severe jaundice or other danger signs defined by current neonatal guidance.

This list is not exhaustive and does not replace age-specific assessment or local protocols. Newborns may deteriorate quickly. Do not reassure solely because the baby initially cried or had a normal first assessment.

### Referral principles
1. Recognize the danger sign and activate the appropriate clinical/emergency pathway.
2. Maintain warmth and provide immediate supportive care within competence while preparing transfer.
3. Communicate the relevant birth history, gestational age when known, feeding, observations, interventions and time course.
4. Contact the receiving service and arrange safe transfer according to the protocol.
5. Document the reason, assessment, actions, response and handover.
6. Do not delay transfer for routine teaching, forms, screening or vaccination when urgent care is needed.

## 7. Nursing responsibilities and safety checks

### Before birth/at the bedside
- Confirm that trained personnel, equipment, warmth supplies and emergency/resuscitation resources are available.
- Know each team member's role and the escalation pathway.
- Review maternal/newborn risk and prepare for additional support if indicated.
- Protect privacy, explain actions and obtain consent as required.

### Immediately after birth
- Assess maternal and newborn condition; respond to emergency needs first.
- Dry and protect the newborn from heat loss.
- Support skin-to-skin when safe and monitor the newborn's breathing/condition.
- Follow current cord-clamping guidance and coordinate with the neonatal team.
- Support early feeding when mother and baby are ready.
- Avoid unnecessary separation and non-indicated routine interventions.
- Document timing, assessments, cord management, feeding and any exceptions/reasons.

### Before discharge and during community follow-up
- Assess maternal recovery, newborn condition and feeding effectiveness.
- Explain danger signs and a feasible route for urgent care.
- Confirm the newborn's follow-up, screening, immunization and other services under current guidance.
- Check caregiver understanding using teach-back when appropriate.
- Follow up on abnormal screening results and missed appointments through authorized channels.
- Respect consent, confidentiality and family circumstances; do not blame families for barriers beyond their control.

## 8. Infection prevention, medication safety and contraindications

EINC is a care package, not a single drug regimen. Medications, vaccines, screening procedures, eye care and other interventions have separate indications, eligibility rules, contraindications/precautions, doses, routes and timing. Verify each from the current Philippine DOH/program guidance and product information.

- Use hand hygiene and appropriate infection-prevention measures.
- Use sterile/clean equipment as required for procedures; never reuse single-use equipment contrary to policy.
- Follow current cord-care guidance; do not apply unapproved substances.
- Verify newborn identity and the correct order/protocol before administering any medication or vaccine.
- Do not administer a routine intervention if the infant's condition requires urgent stabilization or if eligibility/contraindications are unclear; obtain qualified review.
- Do not invent doses, vaccine schedules, screening timing or prophylaxis instructions.
- If the newborn requires resuscitation, emergency stabilization takes precedence over non-urgent routine care.

## 9. Evaluation and quality improvement

EINC quality is not demonstrated by a checked form alone. Review whether essential practices occurred when indicated, whether unnecessary separation/interventions were avoided, whether mother and newborn were assessed and monitored, and whether danger signs triggered timely action.

Possible indicators include:
- documentation of immediate drying/thermal care;
- appropriate skin-to-skin contact when eligible;
- cord-clamping timing and reason for exceptions;
- early breastfeeding support and assessment;
- newborn/maternal observation and recognition of danger signs;
- timely referral/transfer and handover quality;
- completeness of follow-up, screening and immunization pathways;
- family understanding and respectful-care feedback; and
- adverse events or avoidable delays reviewed through a quality-improvement process.

Define numerator, denominator, eligible population, period and source for each indicator. Stratify by relevant groups when lawful and appropriate to detect inequity. Do not penalize staff for documenting legitimate clinical exceptions; honest records are necessary for learning.

## 10. Philippine context

The Philippine DOH EINC/“Unang Yakap” approach is a national implementation framework taught in maternal/newborn care. The parent MCH topic links EINC to RA 11148, the Kalusugan at Nutrisyon ng Mag-Nanay Act, which supports integrated maternal, neonatal, child-health and nutrition interventions in the first 1,000 days. Current clinical steps, documentation, resuscitation procedures, referral requirements, screening, prophylaxis and immunization must be verified in the applicable DOH issuance and current facility protocol. [4]

WHO guidance provides international recommendations, but it does not automatically replace a current Philippine national policy or authorize a health worker to act outside their professional scope. If a local protocol differs from an older textbook mnemonic, check the date and governing national issuance rather than assuming the mnemonic is current.

## 11. PNLE high-yield distinctions

- EINC is a package of evidence-based intrapartum/immediate newborn practices, not a single procedure.
- The traditional “Unang Yakap” sequence emphasizes immediate drying, skin-to-skin, properly timed cord clamping and early breastfeeding/non-separation.
- Assess maternal/newborn stability continuously; routine sequence must not delay resuscitation or emergency care.
- WHO recommends delayed cord clamping not earlier than one minute in routine circumstances, with clinical exceptions.
- Skin-to-skin requires safe positioning, airway observation and ongoing assessment.
- Early breastfeeding support should be timely and non-coercive; assess feeding effectiveness and barriers.
- Newborn danger signs require prompt assessment/referral even if initial transition appeared normal.
- Screening/prophylaxis/immunization have separate schedules and safety rules; do not infer them from EINC alone.
- Respectful care includes maternal autonomy, privacy, informed choice and appropriate support.
- Documentation must record what was actually done and why a clinically necessary exception occurred.
