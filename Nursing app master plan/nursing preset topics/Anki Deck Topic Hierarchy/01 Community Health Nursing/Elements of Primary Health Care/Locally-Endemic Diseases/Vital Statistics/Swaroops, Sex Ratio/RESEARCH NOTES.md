## 1. Sex ratio

A **sex ratio** compares the number of males and females in a defined population. The formula depends on the convention used by the data source. Philippine census reporting commonly expresses sex ratio as the number of males per 100 females:

**Sex ratio = male population ÷ female population × 100.**

Always check the source's definition before calculating or comparing. Some countries/textbooks express the reverse convention (females per 1,000 males), so a result is meaningless without its formula and multiplier.

### Example
If a locality has 5,100 males and 5,000 females, the Philippine-style male-to-female ratio is 5,100 ÷ 5,000 × 100 = **102 males per 100 females**. This is a demographic description, not a health diagnosis.

### Uses
- Describe population composition by geography and age group.
- Support planning for maternal/child, reproductive, occupational and older-adult services.
- Identify unusual patterns that may warrant review of migration, age structure or data quality.
- Compare groups only when definitions, dates and denominators match.

### Interpretation limits
Sex ratio may vary with births, age-specific mortality, migration, conflict/disaster and data collection. A local imbalance may reflect movement of workers/students or institutional populations rather than a health event. Do not infer discrimination, disease or social causes from the ratio alone.

## 2. Swaroop's index (Swaroop–Uemura index)

**Swaroop's index**, often called the **Swaroop–Uemura index**, is a traditional mortality indicator: the percentage of all registered deaths that occur at age 50 years or older.

**Formula = deaths among people aged 50 years and older ÷ total deaths × 100.**

It is a crude summary of the age distribution of deaths. In traditional public-health teaching, a higher index is generally interpreted as a more favorable mortality pattern because a larger share of deaths occurs at older ages. However, it is not a direct measure of life expectancy and is strongly affected by population age structure, registration completeness, cause-of-death patterns and the number of deaths in the dataset.

### Example
If a locality records 400 deaths in a year and 280 occurred among people aged 50 years or older, Swaroop's index = 280 ÷ 400 × 100 = **70%**. The result means 70% of recorded deaths were in the 50+ group. It does not, by itself, establish why the pattern occurred or that the health system is performing well.

### Traditional interpretation bands: use caution
Some older public-health/nursing textbooks use bands such as >75%, 50–75%, 25–50% and <25% to describe increasingly favorable/unfavorable mortality patterns. These are historical teaching conventions, not universal current standards. Do not use them as official Philippine performance thresholds unless the assigned authoritative source explicitly specifies them.

## 3. Sex ratio vs Swaroop's index

| Feature | Sex ratio | Swaroop's index |
|---|---|---|
| What it measures | Relative numbers of males and females in a population. | Share of registered deaths occurring at age 50+. |
| Numerator | Male or female population, depending on convention. | Deaths among people aged 50 years or older. |
| Denominator | Opposite-sex population, with stated multiplier/convention. | Total deaths in the defined population/period. |
| Main use | Describe demographic composition and support planning. | Traditional summary of age distribution of mortality. |
| Common trap | Failing to state whether ratio is males per 100 females or females per 1,000 males. | Treating a higher index as direct proof of longer life expectancy or better care. |

## 4. Data-quality requirements

For sex ratio:
- Confirm population reference date, geographic area and age group.
- Check whether the data are census counts, estimates or projections.
- Use the same sex categories and formula for both numerator and denominator.
- Review age/sex misclassification, undercount and migration where relevant.

For Swaroop's index:
- Use deaths from the same defined place and period.
- Confirm the age boundary (50 years and older) and the official death-count definition.
- Check whether deaths are classified by residence or occurrence.
- Assess completeness and accuracy of age-at-death data.
- Do not compare registered deaths with a denominator from a different period/geography.
- State the number of deaths; percentages from small totals can be unstable.

## 5. Community-health nursing application

These measures can help describe population composition and mortality patterns, but neither replaces more detailed epidemiology:
- Use sex/age-specific population data when planning services.
- Pair mortality measures with cause-specific mortality, age-specific rates, life expectancy, morbidity, service coverage and social determinants.
- Investigate unexpected changes using current official data and appropriate public-health channels.
- Avoid drawing causal conclusions from a single ratio/index.
- Communicate the measure, formula, period, source and limitations clearly.

If a small-area sex ratio or death pattern is unusual, check data quality, migration, institutional populations and age structure before assuming a health crisis.

## 6. PNLE high-yield points

- Philippine-style sex ratio is commonly males per 100 females; verify the specific source convention.
- Swaroop's index = deaths aged 50+ ÷ total deaths × 100.
- A higher Swaroop's index is traditionally viewed as favorable because more deaths occur at older ages, but it is not a direct life-expectancy measure.
- Historical interpretation bands are not universal current thresholds.
- Counts, ratios, proportions and rates are not interchangeable.
- Always state numerator, denominator, multiplier, period, geography and source.
- Check registration completeness, age accuracy and migration before interpretation.

## 7. Philippine context and sources

The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) is the primary source for census age/sex tables and official demographic publications. Use the exact census/table/reference date and its stated sex-ratio convention. Civil-registration/vital-statistics publications should be used for death data, with attention to completeness and residence/occurrence rules. [1][2]

1. **Philippine Statistics Authority.** Official population, census and housing statistics portal; select the exact census/table and sex-ratio definition. https://psa.gov.ph/statistics/population-and-housing
2. **Philippine Statistics Authority.** Civil Registration and Vital Statistics resources/publications; verify current death tables and definitions. https://psa.gov.ph/
3. **World Health Organization.** *Civil registration and vital statistics* (CRVS topic page; background on quality and completeness of vital-event data). https://www.who.int/data/data-collection-tools/civil-registration-and-vital-statistics-(crvs)
