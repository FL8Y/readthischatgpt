# Field Health Services Information System (FHSIS)

**Topic path:** `Elements of Primary Health Care/Locally-Endemic Diseases/FHSIS`  
**Status:** Substantive research draft — current manual/forms and source links require final verification.  
**Last researched:** 2026-10-17  
**Mandatory standard:** Community Health Nursing `RESEARCH TOPIC GUIDE.md`.

## 1. What FHSIS is for

The **Field Health Services Information System (FHSIS)** is the Philippine routine health-information system used to record, summarize and report defined health-service and public-health information from local health service delivery. Its value is not just completing forms: properly defined, complete and timely data help local health teams monitor service coverage, identify unmet needs, plan resources, follow program performance and inform public-health action. DOH regional offices publish FHSIS reports, including quarterly reports, demonstrating its continuing use in routine health reporting. [1][2]

The exact forms, indicators, reporting schedules, responsible staff and denominator rules depend on the current FHSIS Manual of Operations and current DOH issuances. Do not treat an old form, textbook table or a regional reporting page as proof that the same indicator definition applies everywhere today.

## 2. What FHSIS data may support

Depending on the current manual and program, routine health information may be used to monitor:
- maternal, newborn and child health services;
- immunization and other preventive services;
- communicable-disease control and selected surveillance-related activity;
- family planning/reproductive-health services;
- nutrition and other local health programs;
- health-service utilization, referrals and follow-up;
- targets, coverage, gaps and resource needs.

This list is an overview, not a complete current indicator catalogue. For each indicator, use the current official definition, source form, reporting period, eligible population and denominator.

## 3. Essential data concepts

| Concept | Meaning | Common error to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Count** | Number of events/people/services recorded. | Treating a count as a rate or coverage percentage. |
| **Numerator** | Events/people meeting the indicator definition. | Counting all clients when only a defined eligible group belongs in the numerator. |
| **Denominator** | Population at risk/eligible population to which the numerator relates. | Using an outdated or incompatible population estimate. |
| **Rate** | Frequency of an event in a population over a defined period, often with a multiplier. | Omitting the time period, population or multiplier. |
| **Ratio** | Comparison of two quantities where numerator and denominator are not necessarily subsets of one another. | Calling every ratio a percentage. |
| **Proportion/coverage** | Numerator is a part of the denominator; often expressed as a percentage. | Reporting a percentage without a clear eligible population. |
| **Incidence** | New cases/events in a defined population over a specified period. | Mixing new and existing cases or omitting the population/time. |
| **Prevalence** | All existing cases at a point or over a period in a defined population. | Treating prevalence as new cases only. |
| **Completeness** | Required records/reports received relative to expected records/reports. | Assuming a submitted report is complete. |
| **Timeliness** | Report received by the required deadline. | Confusing late data with no cases or no activity. |

**Illustrative calculation:** if 72 of 90 eligible children received a specified service during the reporting period, coverage is 72 ÷ 90 × 100 = 80%. This calculation is valid only if 72 and 90 use the same eligibility criteria, geography and period, and if the denominator is the correct current official denominator. It is not a substitute for an FHSIS indicator's specific definition.

## 4. Data-quality checks before interpreting results

1. **Completeness:** are all expected reports/records present?
2. **Timeliness:** were they submitted within the applicable period?
3. **Accuracy:** do totals reconcile with source records and plausible ranges?
4. **Consistency:** are definitions, units, periods and categories used consistently across facilities/time?
5. **Validity:** does the indicator measure what the program intends?
6. **Uniqueness/duplication:** are repeated entries or transfers handled according to the current rules?
7. **Denominator quality:** is the target population current, geographically aligned and appropriate to the indicator?
8. **Disaggregation:** where lawful and useful, can data show who is missed by age, location or other relevant group?
9. **Documentation:** are corrections, missing data and calculation methods traceable?
10. **Confidentiality:** are identifiable records protected and only shared through authorized channels?

Do not fill gaps by inventing numbers, silently change a denominator, remove an outlier without explanation or mark a missing report as zero cases. A zero may mean no event occurred, but it can also reflect under-detection, incomplete reporting or a service disruption.

## 5. FHSIS in epidemiology and disease control

FHSIS routine data can inform public-health planning, but it is not interchangeable with every disease-surveillance system or laboratory-confirmed case dataset. A routine service count, a suspected-case report, a confirmed case and a population estimate may come from different systems and use different definitions.

For an apparent rise in disease or a cluster:
- check the current disease case definition and relevant surveillance protocol;
- verify the reporting period, geographic catchment and data completeness;
- compare with an appropriate historical baseline/seasonal pattern where available;
- assess whether testing, reporting, access or population size changed;
- notify the appropriate surveillance unit under the current rules;
- prioritize clinical care for urgent cases;
- avoid announcing an outbreak solely from an unverified count or claiming causation from a simple trend.

RA 11332 provides a Philippine legal framework for mandatory reporting of notifiable diseases and public-health events, but the exact notifiable conditions, timelines and procedures must be checked in current DOH guidance and implementing rules. [3]

## 6. Community-health nursing responsibilities

- Record data at the source accurately and promptly.
- Use current definitions, forms and approved reporting procedures.
- Verify identity and avoid duplicate/missing entries according to system rules.
- Calculate indicators using the correct numerator, denominator, period and multiplier.
- Reconcile totals with source registers and investigate implausible changes.
- Escalate data-quality issues and suspected outbreaks rather than altering figures to meet targets.
- Use reports to identify missed groups, service gaps, stock/staffing needs and referral failures.
- Share findings with the team/community in a clear, non-stigmatizing way while protecting individual privacy.
- Maintain secure records and follow authorized retention, access and data-sharing rules.
- Document corrections and limitations transparently.

## 7. Privacy and ethical use

Health information may be sensitive and identifiable. Apply the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173), applicable public-health reporting laws and authorized facility/DOH procedures. Use only the information needed for the legitimate purpose; restrict access; use approved secure systems; and avoid posting identifiable patient lists in public or informal group chats. Legal public-health reporting may be required, but it should follow the lawful channel and minimum necessary information principle.

For teaching, audit or research use, follow the applicable institutional authorization, ethics review and de-identification rules. A report being “for school” does not automatically authorize disclosure of identifiable patient data.

## 8. Interpretation: what the data can and cannot tell you

- **More reported cases** may mean more disease, better detection, changed case definitions, improved reporting or a combination.
- **Lower service coverage** may reflect true unmet need, denominator error, incomplete reporting, service interruption or access barriers.
- **High coverage** may reflect good reach, but it does not by itself prove service quality or health impact.
- **A missing report** is not the same as zero cases or zero activity.
- **A single percentage** should not be compared with another area/period unless the definitions and denominators are compatible.
- **Routine FHSIS data** may not capture every private-sector encounter, informal service or undiagnosed case; check the current system's scope.
- **Association is not causation:** do not claim a program caused a trend without an appropriate evaluation.

Use data as one input alongside clinical judgment, surveillance alerts, community feedback and contextual information.

## 9. Philippine context and sources of current guidance

- **DOH FHSIS Manual of Operations:** the governing manual/version should be checked for indicator definitions, forms, reporting schedule and responsible roles. The PSA technical-notes page references the FHSIS Manual of Operations Version 2018 for selected water-supply definitions, but this does not establish that every part of that manual remains current. [2]
- **DOH regional FHSIS reports:** regional offices publish periodic summaries; the specific region/report date and definitions must be stated when using data. [1]
- **RA 11332:** Mandatory Reporting of Notifiable Diseases and Health Events of Public Health Concern Act; follow current implementing rules and disease-specific guidance. [3]
- **RA 10173:** Data Privacy Act of 2012; apply relevant privacy/security obligations to identifiable health data.
- **PSA population statistics:** use the appropriate official population source/estimate required by the indicator and reporting year. Do not substitute an unrelated census denominator without checking the manual.

## 10. PNLE high-yield distinctions

- FHSIS is a routine health-information/reporting system; it is not a clinical diagnosis and not identical to every disease-surveillance platform.
- Use the current manual/form and official indicator definition.
- Always specify numerator, denominator, period, population and multiplier.
- Counts, rates, ratios, proportions, incidence and prevalence are not interchangeable.
- Check completeness, timeliness, consistency and denominator quality before interpreting a trend.
- Missing report is not zero; a high count is not necessarily a high rate.
- RA 11332 relates to mandatory reporting of notifiable diseases and public-health events.
- RA 10173 is relevant to personal-data protection.
- Do not fabricate or silently revise data to satisfy targets.
- Urgent clinical care and legally required escalation take priority over routine report completion.
