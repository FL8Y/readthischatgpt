# Target Setting for the Philippine National Immunization Program (NIP)

**Topic path:** `Elements of Primary Health Care/Expanded Program on Immunization/Target Setting`  
**Status:** Substantive research draft — source/version review and independent verification remain.  
**Last researched:** 2026-10-17  
**Mandatory standard:** Community Health Nursing `RESEARCH TOPIC GUIDE.md`.

## 1. Meaning and purpose

**Target setting** is the process of estimating the number of people in a defined catchment area who are eligible for a specified immunization service during a stated period. It translates population needs into service plans, vaccine and supply requirements, staffing, outreach, monitoring and coverage calculations.

In the Philippine NIP, target setting should aim to identify and reach every eligible person, including underserved and hard-to-reach groups. The DOH NIP monitoring manual describes calculating an annual target population, using the most accurate and updated local population information available, and dividing an annual infant target into monthly targets for monitoring. WHO's Reaching Every District (RED) microplanning guide likewise links local target estimates to operational maps, session plans, outreach for unreached groups, defaulter tracking, supply management and routine data review. [1][2]

**Target setting is not the same as a coverage target.**
- **Target population:** how many eligible people should receive the service.
- **Coverage target/goal:** the intended proportion of that population to reach.
- **Doses delivered:** the number of recorded doses administered during a period.
- **Coverage estimate:** doses/people reached relative to a defined eligible target population, interpreted according to the indicator's definition.

If the denominator is wrong, coverage can look artificially high or low even when service delivery has not changed.

## 2. Define the target correctly

Before calculating, document:

1. **Intervention/antigen:** which vaccine dose or immunization service is being planned.
2. **Eligible group:** the age cohort, pregnancy group, school grade, older-adult group or other population defined by the current NIP directive.
3. **Geographic catchment:** the actual population for which the facility/team is responsible, including remote sitios, informal settlements, mobile families and underserved communities.
4. **Time period:** annual, quarterly, monthly, campaign period or session-specific.
5. **Population source and date:** local census/line list, barangay records, PSA/local planning estimates or the current authorized DOH denominator.
6. **Eligibility rules:** the current Philippine schedule, dose history, age limits and any program-specific inclusion/exclusion rules.
7. **Data-quality limitations:** births/deaths, migration, cross-boundary service use, duplicate records, missing birth dates and outdated registers.

A single population percentage should not automatically be applied to every vaccine, age group or special campaign. Some services target different age cohorts or groups. Use the current program's specified target definition and denominator.

## 3. Estimate the annual target population

### Preferred approach: use the most accurate current local data

The DOH NIP monitoring material instructs implementers to use the most accurate and updated available population figures for infants under one year, where possible. Local city/municipal/barangay census data may provide a more useful estimate than a broad percentage, provided that the source, date and method are reliable and accepted for program reporting. Reconcile local estimates with the current authorized DOH/LGU denominator when one has been issued. [1]

### If actual target-population data are unavailable

The DOH NIP Manual of Operations, Booklet 7 (Monitoring, Supportive Supervision, and Evaluation), posted by DOH in 2023, describes a historical fallback estimate for infants under one year: **total population × 2.7%**. It also explicitly says the factor should be reviewed against recent Philippine Statistics Authority census data and that NIP should provide guidance when a new factor is officially used. Therefore:

- Treat 2.7% as a factor documented in that manual version—not a timeless biological constant or a guarantee that it remains the current authorized factor for every locality/program.
- Confirm the latest DOH/NIP instruction and the denominator being used for the reporting period.
- Prefer validated local cohort data when authorized and available.
- Record the method and source so another person can reproduce the estimate. [1]

**Illustrative calculation only:** if a local population is 10,000 and the applicable authorized planning instruction permits the 2.7% fallback, estimated infants under one year = 10,000 × 0.027 = **270**. This is an estimate, not a count of actual infants and not a substitute for current local guidance.

Do not use this example to estimate all vaccine target groups. The target definition may differ for pregnant clients, school-based vaccines, older adults, catch-up services or supplementary campaigns.

## 4. Convert annual targets to monthly or session targets

Where the annual target is 270 infants, a simple average is:

`Monthly average = annual target ÷ 12 = 270 ÷ 12 = 22.5`

Because people are whole persons, the operational monthly plan should distribute the total across months (for example, a mix of 22 and 23) so the planned monthly values sum to 270. Do **not** round every month up to 23 and accidentally plan for 276 unless the program intentionally sets a larger operational target and records why.

Monthly plans may need adjustment for local birth patterns, outreach dates, seasonal access barriers, campaign periods, service disruptions and the actual number of eligible clients. Any adjustment should follow current program instructions and preserve a transparent denominator.

A monthly target is a planning/monitoring aid; it is not permission to deny vaccination to an eligible person who presents later than the target month. Apply current catch-up guidance and the person's actual documented dose history.

## 5. Map and validate the target population

A usable microplan connects target numbers to actual places and service delivery:

- Map every barangay/sitio, settlement, island, upland area, school or other relevant service location in the catchment.
- Identify fixed, outreach, mobile or school-based service points and their planned dates.
- Compare target estimates with previous birth cohorts, facility registers, home-based records, local lists and routine reports.
- Identify missed communities, zero-dose children, under-immunized children, defaulters and groups facing access barriers using authorized definitions and data.
- Reconcile duplicate or conflicting records; document uncertainty rather than silently deleting records.
- Coordinate with barangay health workers, local leaders, schools and other partners while protecting personal information.
- Review whether transport, staff, cold-chain capacity, safe injection supplies, vaccine stock, waste disposal and referral arrangements match the plan.

WHO's RED microplanning guide recommends a bottom-up approach that begins with facility/service-delivery microplans and informs district plans. It emphasizes local immunization data, operational maps, special activities for hard-to-reach/problem areas, session plans, workplans, monitoring charts, community engagement, defaulter tracking and supplies. [2]

## 6. Link target setting to vaccine and logistics planning

A population target is only one input into vaccine requirements. The number of doses/vials and other supplies depends on the antigen and dose schedule, eligible group, expected service uptake, vial presentation, wastage assumptions, stock on hand, usable expiry dates, safety stock/lead time and the current DOH supply-planning method.

**Do not calculate or order vaccine quantities from a generic multiplier alone.** Use the current NIP manual/authorized logistics tool for each vaccine and supply, confirm wastage and buffer assumptions, check cold-chain capacity and storage/transport requirements, and reconcile the forecast with stock records and physical inventory. The DOH NIP annexes include antigen-specific planning formulas in the manual version available online; these are version-dependent and must not be assumed current without checking the applicable issuance. [3]

Nursing and program-management checks include:
- Review stock cards and physical counts; investigate discrepancies.
- Check expiry dates and vaccine vial monitor (VVM) status where applicable.
- Ensure adequate functioning cold-chain equipment and temperature monitoring under current protocol.
- Plan safe injection equipment, sharps containers, forms, registers and waste handling.
- Escalate stock-outs, temperature excursions or unsafe storage promptly under current procedures.
- Never use a target shortfall as justification to compromise eligibility checks, vaccine safety or documentation.

## 7. Monitor coverage and dropout carefully

### Basic coverage calculation

For a defined antigen/dose, age group, geography and period:

`Coverage (%) = (number of eligible target-group people who received the specified dose during the defined period ÷ target population for that indicator) × 100`

Use the exact numerator/denominator definition in the current DOH reporting guidance. Some indicators count doses administered during a period; others may be cohort-based or use a different reporting rule. Do not mix them.

**Illustrative example:** if 216 of an authorized target of 270 eligible infants are recorded as receiving the specified dose, the crude ratio is 216 ÷ 270 × 100 = **80%**. This result is only as reliable as the numerator, denominator, period and dose definition.

Coverage above 100% can occur when people from outside the catchment are vaccinated, the target denominator is underestimated, or data are duplicated/mismatched. It is a prompt to investigate—not proof of perfect performance.

### Dropout monitoring

Dropout indicators compare people who started a multi-dose series with those who reached a later required dose, using the precise pair of doses and calculation prescribed by the current NIP indicator definition. For example, a program may compare first and third doses of a vaccine series; the exact antigen/dose pair must follow current Philippine reporting guidance.

Generic calculation structure:

`Dropout rate (%) = (number receiving the first specified dose − number receiving the later specified dose) ÷ number receiving the first specified dose × 100`

Use only when the current indicator defines that numerator and denominator. A high dropout rate may indicate missed appointments, access barriers, stock-outs, poor caregiver communication, service quality problems, incomplete recording or other factors. It requires investigation rather than blaming caregivers.

### Interpret the data before acting

Review:
- Coverage and dropout trends by month and area.
- Missed/zero-dose and under-immunized groups.
- Timeliness, not just whether a dose was eventually recorded.
- Missed opportunities during health contacts.
- Data completeness, duplicate records and denominator changes.
- Stock-outs, cancelled sessions, staffing and cold-chain disruptions.
- Equity: which communities are not being reached and why.

The DOH NIP monitoring manual describes charts comparing doses given with eligible infants and using first-dose versus completed-dose counts to monitor dropout. It also emphasizes targeting all infants, especially those who are hard to reach. [1]

## 8. Nursing responsibilities and clinical reasoning

### Before planning
- Verify the current NIP schedule and target-group definition from an official source.
- Check local population data and the approved denominator; document date and method.
- Review prior coverage, dropout, missed-dose lists and data quality.
- Map underserved areas and identify practical access barriers.
- Consult supervisors/LGU partners where denominator or policy conflicts exist.

### During implementation
- Confirm identity, age/eligibility and documented vaccination history according to protocol.
- Screen for contraindications/precautions relevant to the specific vaccine and current guidance.
- Maintain cold chain, safe injection, infection prevention and accurate records.
- Explain the service and expected next visit in understandable language.
- Record doses in the required records/registers and follow current reporting rules.
- Provide current post-vaccination advice and instructions for concerning symptoms/AEFI reporting.
- Protect confidential information in lists, line-lists, maps and follow-up contacts.

### After each session/reporting period
- Reconcile doses administered with registers, tally sheets, home-based records and reports according to local procedure.
- Compare achievement with target and investigate discrepancies.
- Identify clients due/overdue for subsequent doses under current catch-up guidance.
- Reassess session plans, staffing, transport, stock and community engagement.
- Escalate serious safety concerns, cold-chain excursions, data falsification or persistent access gaps.

### Prioritization
A child or other client with acute danger signs needs appropriate immediate clinical assessment and referral; target-setting, routine tallying and vaccination-session throughput do not override emergency care. Conversely, a low coverage estimate is a signal to find access gaps and improve safe service delivery—not to lower safety standards or falsify numbers.

## 9. Philippine context and source currency

The National Immunization Program is the current DOH program name; some historical materials and educational resources use “Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI).” DOH regional NIP pages list implementation materials, including Booklet 7 on monitoring/supportive supervision/evaluation and Booklet 9 annexes. The exact schedule, target definition, vaccine requirements, reporting forms and denominator factors can change. Use the current national directive applicable to the reporting period and confirm any regional/LGU adaptation through authorized channels. [1][4]

Republic Act No. 10152 (Mandatory Infants and Children Health Immunization Act of 2011) is part of the Philippine legal context for routine immunization; read the current law and applicable implementing guidance for legal obligations. A general target-setting calculation does not by itself establish a legal target, vaccine eligibility or a specific reporting requirement. [5]

## 10. Common errors and PNLE high-yield distinctions

- **Target population ≠ doses needed ≠ doses delivered ≠ coverage percentage.**
- Define the antigen/dose, age/eligibility group, geography and period before calculating.
- Prefer current validated local population data; if a fallback factor is used, verify it is still authorized.
- The 2.7% under-one estimate appears in the DOH NIP manual version posted in 2023, which itself says the factor should be reviewed against updated census data. Do not memorize it as a universally current factor without a version caveat.
- Monthly targets must reconcile to the annual total; careless rounding can inflate the annual plan.
- Coverage above 100% often signals denominator or catchment problems and should be investigated.
- A dose count alone does not show whether the right people were reached on time or completed the series.
- A positive discrepancy in the numbers is not proof of success; reconcile registers, reports and denominators.
- High dropout may reflect system/access barriers, not simply caregiver noncompliance.
- Target setting informs outreach and logistics but never replaces individual eligibility, dose-history, vaccine-safety and cold-chain checks.
- Do not use an outdated schedule or formula to make a current clinical or procurement decision.
