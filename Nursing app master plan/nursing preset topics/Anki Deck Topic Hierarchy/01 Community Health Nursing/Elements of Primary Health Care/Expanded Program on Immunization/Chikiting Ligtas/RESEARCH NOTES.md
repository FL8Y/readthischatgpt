# Chikiting Ligtas — Supplemental Immunization Campaign in the Philippines

**Topic path:** `Elements of Primary Health Care/Expanded Program on Immunization/Chikiting Ligtas`  
**Status:** Substantive historical/program draft — current campaign status and campaign-specific operational guidance require verification.  
**Last researched:** 2026-10-17  
**Mandatory standard:** Community Health Nursing `RESEARCH TOPIC GUIDE.md`.

## 1. What Chikiting Ligtas refers to

**Chikiting Ligtas** is the campaign name used by the Philippine Department of Health (DOH) for supplemental immunization activities (SIAs) intended to protect children against vaccine-preventable diseases. DOH regional communications document a nationwide 2023 campaign described as a measles-rubella and bivalent oral polio vaccine SIA (MR-bOPV SIA). The campaign mobilized local government units (LGUs), health facilities, schools/community partners and health workers to reach eligible children, including those who may have missed routine vaccination. [1][2]

**Date/version warning:** the sources inspected in this cycle document the 2023 campaign and mention 2024 activity. They do not establish that a campaign with the same name is currently active in October 2026, nor do they establish current target ages, dates or vaccine products. Campaign eligibility, target population, vaccine combination, interval, geography, start/end dates and reporting rules are campaign-specific and must be confirmed from the latest national DOH issuance and the local health office. Do not reuse 2023 targets as a current schedule.

## 2. Why supplemental immunization activities are used

Routine immunization is the continuing foundation of protection. SIAs are additional, time-limited public-health actions used when national authorities determine that supplementary doses are needed to close immunity gaps, respond to outbreak risk or improve population protection. They do not automatically replace the routine schedule or a child's need for routine doses.

The 2023 Chikiting Ligtas communications described the campaign in the context of concern about measles, rubella and polio outbreaks and disruption of routine services. The campaign used whole-of-government/whole-of-society mobilization and regional microplanning to reach children. These are historical descriptions of that campaign, not evidence that the same epidemiologic conditions or campaign rules apply today. [1][2][3]

### Routine immunization vs SIA

| Feature | Routine immunization | Supplemental immunization activity |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Provide scheduled vaccines across the life course. | Supplement routine services for a defined public-health objective. |
| Timing | Ongoing according to the current national schedule. | Time-limited; dates are set by current campaign guidance. |
| Eligibility | Defined by current routine schedule and product guidance. | Defined by the specific SIA circular; may differ from routine eligibility. |
| Dose interpretation | Counts toward the routine schedule when valid under rules. | Campaign doses may be additional; how they count toward routine requirements depends on current guidance. |
| Planning | Facility/community service plan and routine target population. | Microplan, campaign target, outreach and specific monitoring/reporting arrangements. |
| Key safety rule | Follow current NIP manual and product instructions. | Follow both relevant safety rules and campaign-specific instructions; never assume the campaign supersedes all routine rules. |

## 3. Campaign planning and microplanning

A campaign must be planned against current official instructions. Community-health nursing and local implementation teams may contribute to:
- Identify the exact target population, geographic area and campaign period from the current DOH issuance.
- Estimate the target population and validate lists/denominators using approved methods.
- Map barangays, hard-to-reach households, schools, health facilities, outreach points and transport constraints.
- Plan staffing, training, vaccine supply, cold chain, injection/oral-administration supplies, waste management and emergency readiness.
- Coordinate with LGUs, education/social-service partners, community leaders and local health workers.
- Prepare culturally and linguistically appropriate communication, including campaign purpose, eligibility, safety, dates and where services are offered.
- Define recording, reporting, supervision, adverse-event response and escalation responsibilities.
- Identify missed children and design safe mop-up/follow-up activity if authorized.
- Evaluate coverage and data quality after the campaign.

A target percentage is a planning goal, not proof of coverage. Do not copy the 2023 target or target age range into a new campaign without confirming the current circular.

## 4. Nursing responsibilities during campaign delivery

### Before the session
1. Read the current national SIA circular and local implementation plan; verify campaign dates, geography, eligible ages, vaccine products and minimum intervals.
2. Confirm staff competency, authorized roles and emergency procedures.
3. Check vaccine supply, lot/batch, expiry, product integrity, storage history and cold-chain equipment.
4. Prepare the approved record forms/electronic system, consent/communication process and safe sharps/waste arrangements.
5. Confirm outreach sites are accessible and can maintain safe storage/transport conditions.

### At the point of service
- Confirm the child's identity/age and campaign eligibility under the current rules.
- Review available immunization history and check required intervals/contraindications/precautions using current guidance.
- Explain the campaign dose, expected reactions, warning signs and follow-up in understandable language.
- Obtain consent according to applicable policy and law.
- Administer only the specified vaccine/product using the authorized route, technique and product instructions.
- Document dose, date, product, lot and required campaign fields accurately.
- Provide a clear record/reminder for routine follow-up when applicable.
- Observe/respond according to the current protocol and ensure emergency readiness.

### After the session
- Reconcile doses used, remaining stock, wastage and records.
- Report required data and safety events through authorized channels.
- Identify missed eligible children and follow the approved mop-up plan.
- Investigate discrepancies between dose counts and unique recipients.
- Preserve privacy; do not post identifiable child data in public campaign reports.

## 5. Vaccine safety and cold-chain controls

The campaign name does not alter vaccine-specific contraindications, precautions, storage requirements or administration rules. Use the current NIP manual, SIA circular, product label and approved local protocol.

- Verify the correct vaccine, product, dose, route and age/eligibility.
- Distinguish contraindications from precautions; do not exclude children based on unverified assumptions.
- Maintain the required cold chain and monitor temperature.
- If an excursion or product-quality concern occurs, segregate/hold the affected stock and obtain authorized assessment before use or disposal.
- Do not use expired, damaged, improperly stored or uncertain product.
- Follow infection prevention, safe equipment use and waste disposal.
- Prepare and use reconstituted vaccines only within product-specific permitted time and conditions.
- Keep emergency response available and follow current AEFI procedures.
- Report an event according to the current protocol; an event after vaccination does not automatically prove causation.

## 6. AEFI and risk communication

An adverse event following immunization (AEFI) is an untoward medical occurrence after vaccination that does not necessarily have a causal relationship with the vaccine. Staff should assess and manage the patient first, especially in emergencies, then document and report according to current policy. WHO explains that vaccine safety is continuously monitored and that suspected events are assessed to determine whether they are vaccine-related, coincidental or associated with an error. [4]

Communication should:
- Explain expected reactions and red flags using current campaign materials.
- Avoid promises of zero risk or unsupported claims.
- Encourage prompt assessment for severe or concerning symptoms.
- Listen respectfully to caregivers' concerns and provide accurate, non-coercive information.
- Avoid stigmatizing parents who have questions or children who missed prior doses.
- Correct misinformation with evidence and explain what is known/unknown.
- Keep individual medical information confidential.

## 7. Recording, coverage and the Synchronized Electronic Immunization Repository (SEIR)

DOH regional reports from 2023 described the Synchronized Electronic Immunization Repository (SEIR) as a centralized record system used to manage campaign vaccination data. Its use, configuration and current status must be confirmed from the applicable current DOH instructions; a historical report does not establish current national platform use or access procedures. [3]

For campaign evaluation, distinguish:
- **Doses administered:** number of doses given.
- **Unique children vaccinated:** deduplicated recipients who meet the campaign definition.
- **Coverage:** valid vaccinated target-population count divided by the defined target population, using the campaign's official numerator/denominator rules.
- **Reach/equity:** whether underserved, remote or otherwise missed groups were reached.
- **Safety/process quality:** cold-chain compliance, correct administration, record completeness, AEFI response and waste management.

Coverage may be inaccurate when targets are outdated, children cross local boundaries, records are duplicated, doses are counted incorrectly or reports are late. A reported rate above 100% should prompt denominator/numerator review rather than automatic celebration. Keep identifiable child data secure and use only authorized systems.

## 8. Community engagement and barriers

Potential barriers include transport/distance, clinic hours, caregiver work, lack of campaign information, language, disability, fear of adverse effects, missed routine care, stock availability, migration and difficulty finding records. Assess barriers before labeling caregivers as unwilling.

Nursing/community actions:
- Share the verified campaign schedule through appropriate local channels.
- Use clear local-language information and answer questions.
- Coordinate safe outreach to underserved communities.
- Provide accurate information about eligibility and what the dose does/does not replace.
- Ensure service points have the supplies and trained staff needed to deliver safe vaccination.
- Use reminders and authorized mop-up activities.
- Evaluate which groups remain unreached and adapt the plan.

Campaign success is not just high throughput. It requires safe, valid doses, equitable reach, accurate records and continuation of routine immunization.

## 9. Philippine context and current-source rule

The DOH Center for Health Development–Cordillera and Eastern Visayas published campaign descriptions in 2023. These sources are useful for the historical campaign design, target-setting, stakeholder mobilization and evaluation context. They are **not current operational instructions for 2026**. The current campaign status, target ages, product, intervals, geographic scope, dates, consent requirements, data system and reporting deadlines must be verified from the latest DOH national issuance and local health office. [1][2][3]

No exact current age range, campaign date, vaccine dose, interval or target percentage is provided here because the available campaign evidence is historical and these details are version-sensitive.

## 10. PNLE high-yield distinctions

- Chikiting Ligtas is a campaign label used for supplemental immunization activity; the cited 2023 campaign involved MR-bOPV SIA.
- A supplemental campaign is not identical to routine immunization and does not automatically replace the routine schedule.
- Campaign eligibility and timing must come from the current campaign circular; do not reuse historical 2023 ages/dates as current.
- Microplanning includes target-population estimates, logistics, cold chain, staffing, communication, recording and monitoring.
- A dose count is not the same as unique children reached or valid coverage.
- AEFI is a temporal event after vaccination, not automatic proof of causation.
- Correct product, eligibility, interval, administration, cold chain and documentation remain essential during a mass campaign.
- Stabilize/manage an emergency before completing campaign paperwork.
- Protect child records and follow current DOH reporting/data-system rules.
- Continue routine immunization and follow-up as directed by current national guidance.
