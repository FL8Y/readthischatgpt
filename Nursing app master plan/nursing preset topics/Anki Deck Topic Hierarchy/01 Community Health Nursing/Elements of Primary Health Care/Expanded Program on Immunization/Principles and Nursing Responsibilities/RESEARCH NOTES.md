# Principles of Immunization and Nursing Responsibilities

**Topic path:** `Elements of Primary Health Care/Expanded Program on Immunization/Principles and Nursing Responsibilities`  
**Status:** Substantive nursing-practice draft — vaccine-specific protocols and independent review remain.  
**Last researched:** 2026-10-17  
**Mandatory standard:** Community Health Nursing `RESEARCH TOPIC GUIDE.md`.

## 1. Core principles

Immunization practice aims to protect individuals and communities against vaccine-preventable diseases through safe, effective, timely and equitable vaccination. The essential principles are:
- **Evidence-based eligibility and timing:** follow current Philippine DOH National Immunization Program (NIP) guidance and product-specific instructions.
- **Vaccine quality:** preserve potency through approved storage, transport, preparation and handling.
- **Safe administration:** verify the recipient, product, indication, dose, route, time, site and technique as required by current protocol.
- **Screening and informed communication:** assess vaccine-specific contraindications/precautions and explain benefits, expected reactions, warning signs and follow-up.
- **Accurate documentation:** record the dose and required product details in the authorized record/system.
- **Safety surveillance:** recognize, manage, document and report adverse events following immunization (AEFI) and errors according to current procedures.
- **Continuity:** arrange remaining doses and catch-up using the current schedule; do not invent a restart or interval.
- **Equity and community engagement:** identify access barriers, avoid stigma and support underserved populations.
- **Accountability:** never falsify records, conceal errors or use questionable stock without authorized disposition.

## 2. Before vaccination: nurse's checklist

1. **Confirm the person:** use approved identifiers and verify age/date of birth.
2. **Review history:** examine the available immunization record, prior doses, serious reactions and the reason for today's vaccine.
3. **Verify current eligibility:** use the current DOH NIP schedule/circular and product instructions for age, dose, interval, catch-up and co-administration.
4. **Screen appropriately:** ask about prior severe allergic reactions, relevant allergies, current illness, immune status, pregnancy when relevant, medicines and other vaccine-specific factors.
5. **Distinguish contraindication from precaution:** do not deny a vaccine automatically based on a vague history or a minor illness; use current vaccine-specific guidance.
6. **Check the product:** correct vaccine/formulation, lot/batch, expiry, vial integrity, required VVM, storage history and approved diluent/preparation.
7. **Check cold chain:** confirm no unresolved excursion or quality concern; if uncertain, hold stock and seek authorized assessment.
8. **Explain and obtain consent:** discuss purpose, expected effects, serious warning signs, follow-up and questions in accessible language; follow applicable consent rules.
9. **Prepare safely:** hand hygiene, standard precautions, correct equipment and emergency readiness.
10. **Clarify uncertainty:** pause and contact the authorized clinician/supervisor if the product, order, record, eligibility or protocol is unclear.

## 3. During administration

- Use the correct product, dose, route, anatomical site, technique and interval as defined in the current manual/product instructions.
- Use the specified diluent and aseptic preparation method where applicable.
- Do not mix vaccines in the same syringe unless the exact product and authorized guidance explicitly permit it.
- Use sterile single-use equipment as required and never reuse needles/syringes.
- Maintain infection prevention, safe sharps handling and waste disposal.
- Observe the recipient according to current protocol and maintain readiness to respond to an acute reaction.
- Do not leave prepared/reconstituted product outside its permitted conditions/time.
- Protect privacy and dignity throughout the encounter.

This note intentionally does not supply a universal dose/route/site table. These are vaccine- and age-specific and must be checked against the current DOH manual and product label.

## 4. After vaccination

- Document the vaccine/product, dose, date, lot/batch, route/site and required provider/record fields accurately.
- Record the next due date or follow-up instructions according to current policy.
- Explain expected reactions and specific warning signs; give clear instructions on where and when to seek help.
- Maintain the authorized observation process and emergency readiness.
- Report an AEFI, medication/immunization error or product-quality concern according to the current system.
- Update the official record/register and ensure the recipient's card/record is consistent with the authorized system.
- Reconcile vaccine use, remaining stock and wastage under the current program rules.
- Arrange referral or follow-up for missed doses, abnormal findings or other care needs.
- Never count an invalid dose as valid coverage; follow the official correction/catch-up algorithm.

## 5. Contraindications and precautions

A **contraindication** is a condition in which a particular vaccine should not be administered because risk is unacceptable under the applicable guidance. A **precaution** is a condition requiring a benefit-risk assessment, possible deferral or other specific action. These terms are vaccine-specific and are not interchangeable.

Examples of issues requiring protocol-based assessment include:
- Severe allergic reaction to a previous dose or a vaccine component.
- Certain live vaccines in pregnancy or severe immunocompromise.
- A moderate/severe acute illness when current guidance recommends deferral.
- A history of a specific serious adverse event after a prior dose.
- Product-specific age, interval or medical restrictions.

Do not generalize these examples into a single rule for every vaccine. Minor illness is often not a contraindication, but staff must apply the current guidance for the specific vaccine and recipient. A vague family history, a rumor or a prior expected mild reaction should not automatically become a contraindication. WHO stresses the importance of avoiding false contraindications and following vaccine-specific guidance. [2]

## 6. AEFI recognition and response

An **AEFI** is an untoward medical occurrence after immunization that does not necessarily have a causal relationship with the vaccine. AEFI can include a symptom, abnormal finding, coincidental illness, product reaction, immunization error or another event. Timing alone does not prove causation. [1]

### Priority response
1. Assess and manage the person first.
2. If anaphylaxis, respiratory compromise, collapse, altered consciousness or another emergency is suspected, activate emergency response and follow the current authorized protocol immediately.
3. Document the clinical event, timing, vaccine/product, lot, dose, route, findings, treatment and outcome.
4. Notify the appropriate clinician/supervisor and report through the applicable current channel.
5. Preserve relevant product/cold-chain information if required for investigation.
6. Explain the next steps to the person/caregiver and arrange follow-up.
7. Participate in investigation and corrective action when directed.

Do not delay lifesaving care to complete a report. Do not withhold a report that meets current criteria while waiting to prove causality. Do not tell a family that an event was definitely caused—or definitely not caused—by the vaccine before appropriate assessment.

## 7. Cold chain and product quality responsibilities

The nurse must verify that the vaccine was stored and transported under the required conditions. Many routine vaccines use +2°C to +8°C storage, but the exact range and permitted handling depend on the product and authorized instructions. Controlled-temperature-chain use is allowed only for specifically authorized/labeled vaccines. [3][4]

If a temperature excursion or product-quality concern is suspected:
- Hold/segregate the affected stock according to procedure.
- Label it “DO NOT USE—PENDING ASSESSMENT” as required.
- Record product/lot, temperature data, duration and incident.
- Notify the designated cold-chain supervisor.
- Obtain authorized disposition before use, return or disposal.
- Correct the underlying cause and document actions.

Do not use or discard stock based solely on appearance, expiry date or personal judgment.

## 8. Communication and caregiver education

Use plain language and invite questions. Explain:
- Which vaccine is being offered and why.
- Whether it is routine or campaign-related and what it does/does not replace.
- Expected reactions and serious warning signs based on current guidance.
- The next dose/follow-up and how to keep records.
- Where to seek urgent care and how to report concerns.
- That an AEFI occurring after vaccination does not by itself prove causation, while every concerning event should be taken seriously.

Avoid coercion, shaming, dismissing concerns or promising zero risk. Tailor communication to language, literacy, disability and cultural context. Keep individual immunization status confidential.

## 9. Documentation, reporting and data quality

Records should be complete, accurate, timely and traceable. Use authorized systems; correct errors according to policy without hiding or falsifying the original information. Protect identifiable data and share it only through lawful, authorized channels.

For coverage reporting:
- Use the current defined numerator, denominator, geography and period.
- Distinguish doses administered from unique recipients and valid doses.
- Check for duplicate entries, late reporting, migration and invalid dose timing.
- Investigate coverage above 100% or unexplained changes.
- Report stock-outs, adverse events and missed groups honestly.

A signed card or a dose count is not proof that the entire schedule is complete or that the dose was valid.

## 10. Ethical, legal and scope considerations in the Philippines

RA 10152, the Mandatory Infants and Children Health Immunization Act of 2011, provides a statutory framework for basic immunization services for infants and children; RA 11223 provides broader UHC context. Applicable consent, privacy, professional scope and operational rules must be checked against current law, DOH issuances and facility policy. The statute itself is not the current schedule. [5][6]

Nurses must act within professional scope, competency, orders and authorized protocols. Do not independently prescribe, alter vaccine schedules, determine stock disposition, declare an AEFI causal or make legal decisions beyond authority. When uncertain, pause and escalate through the designated chain.

## 11. PNLE high-yield distinctions

- Screening, administration, documentation, follow-up, cold chain and AEFI response are all part of safe immunization practice.
- Verify current DOH guidance; old EPI tables may be outdated.
- Contraindication ≠ precaution; a minor illness is not automatically a contraindication.
- An AEFI is not automatically caused by the vaccine.
- Treat an emergency before completing reporting paperwork.
- Temperature excursion: hold/segregate, document, notify and obtain authorized disposition.
- Reconstituted-vaccine and open-vial limits are product-specific.
- Coverage requires valid doses and a valid denominator.
- Never guess a dose, route, interval, catch-up rule, contraindication or reporting deadline.
- Protect privacy and never falsify immunization records.
