# Other Vaccines in Community Health Nursing — Program and Safety Overview

**Topic path:** `Elements of Primary Health Care/Expanded Program on Immunization/Principles and Nursing Responsibilities/Other Vaccines`  
**Status:** Substantive overview draft — each vaccine's current Philippine schedule/product instructions and independent review remain.  
**Last researched:** 2026-10-17  
**Mandatory standard:** Community Health Nursing `RESEARCH TOPIC GUIDE.md`.

## 1. Scope and use of this folder

This folder provides a framework for vaccine-specific study beyond the dedicated BCG and pentavalent vaccine notes. It summarizes common vaccine types and safety distinctions but **does not replace individual vaccine research** or the current Philippine National Immunization Program (NIP) schedule.

WHO's routine-immunization summary tables are updated from its position papers and help national decision-makers review schedules. WHO explicitly says the tables are not intended for direct use by vaccinators: country-specific schedules must reflect national policy, epidemiology, program capacity and resources. Therefore, use the latest DOH NIP Manual of Operations/Procedures, current DOH circulars and exact product label for every operational decision. [1][2]

## 2. Vaccine types and the principles they protect

| Vaccine/type | Main purpose | Safety/implementation points to verify |
|---|---|---|
| **Hepatitis B (HepB)** | Prevents hepatitis B infection, including chronic infection and long-term liver disease. | Verify whether the product is a monovalent birth-dose vaccine or part of a combination; do not assume a combination vaccine replaces the birth dose. Confirm timing, product, dose and risk-specific rules from current DOH guidance. |
| **Oral polio vaccine (OPV; product may be bivalent or another formulation)** | Supports protection against specified poliovirus types and may contribute to interruption of transmission, depending on formulation/program strategy. | Confirm exact product and type, eligible age, campaign/routine indication, dose, oral route, handling and contraindications. OPV and inactivated polio vaccine (IPV) are not interchangeable in every program decision. |
| **Inactivated polio vaccine (IPV)** | Induces systemic protection against poliovirus without a live replicating vaccine virus. | Confirm schedule, product, route and dose from current DOH guidance; do not substitute IPV for OPV or vice versa without authorization. |
| **Pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (PCV)** | Prevents disease caused by pneumococcal serotypes included in the product, including serious invasive disease. | Product valency and schedule may vary. Verify age/interval, product, co-administration and high-risk guidance; do not assume protection against every pneumococcal serotype. |
| **Rotavirus vaccine** | Prevents severe rotavirus gastroenteritis in infants/young children. | Product-specific maximum age and dose schedule matter. Verify the exact product and current age limits; do not improvise catch-up outside the authorized rules. |
| **Measles-containing vaccine (MCV), MR or MMR** | Prevents measles; MR also targets rubella; MMR additionally targets mumps. | Distinguish products and campaign vs routine use. Live-vaccine restrictions, age, interval, co-administration and pregnancy/immunocompromise rules are product-specific. |
| **Japanese encephalitis (JE) vaccine** | Prevents Japanese encephalitis in populations/areas where vaccination is recommended. | Verify local epidemiology, program eligibility, product type, age, dose/interval and contraindications. Do not assume it is recommended in every locality. |
| **Human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccine** | Prevents infection from targeted HPV types and reduces risk of HPV-related cancers/disease. | Verify current target group, product, dose schedule, campaign policy and special-population guidance. It is preventive, not treatment for existing HPV infection or cervical lesions. |
| **Influenza vaccine** | Reduces risk of influenza and complications; formulation can change by season. | Verify current product, formulation/season, age-specific dose, contraindications/precautions and target group. Prior season's product or schedule may not be current. |
| **Pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccine (PPV/PPSV)** | Provides protection against included pneumococcal serotypes in specified populations. | Not identical to PCV; indications, age, sequence and revaccination rules depend on current guidance and risk status. |
| **Tetanus/diphtheria-containing vaccines (Td/TT-containing products)** | Prevent tetanus/diphtheria in age- and context-appropriate schedules, including certain maternal/adolescent/adult programs. | Verify product, prior history, intervals and current program recommendations; do not treat every tetanus-containing formulation as interchangeable. |

This table is an orientation aid. The vaccine formulation and national policy are decisive; names alone do not establish the schedule.

## 3. Universal nursing workflow for vaccine-specific decisions

### Before vaccination
1. Confirm identity, age, immunization history and indication.
2. Read the current DOH schedule/circular and exact product information.
3. Check product, formulation, dose, route, interval, expiry, lot/batch, storage history and preparation requirements.
4. Assess vaccine-specific contraindications/precautions, allergies, pregnancy when relevant, immune status, current illness and prior serious reactions.
5. Explain purpose, expected reactions, serious warning signs and follow-up in accessible language.
6. Obtain consent under applicable policy/law.
7. Pause and seek authorized clinical clarification when a contraindication, product, record or interval is unclear.

### During administration
- Use the correct product, recipient, dose, route, site, time and technique according to current guidance.
- Maintain hand hygiene, aseptic technique, standard precautions and sharps safety.
- Do not mix products in a syringe unless explicitly authorized for that exact product.
- Preserve cold chain and follow product-specific preparation/use limits.
- Maintain readiness for emergency response.

### After vaccination
- Document product, dose, date, lot/batch, route/site and required record fields.
- Provide clear aftercare, warning signs and next-dose/follow-up information.
- Recognize, manage, document and report adverse events following immunization (AEFI) under current policy.
- Reconcile vaccine stock and wastage.
- Protect personal data and use authorized records/reporting channels.

## 4. Contraindications and precautions: avoid blanket rules

Contraindications vary by vaccine and product. A severe allergic reaction to a previous dose or a vaccine component can be a contraindication; live vaccines such as BCG and measles-containing vaccines have additional restrictions in some immunocompromised or pregnancy-related circumstances. A precaution may require risk-benefit assessment or deferral rather than permanent exclusion. Use the exact current product label and DOH guidance.

Do not assume:
- All vaccines are live or all vaccines are inactivated.
- Every vaccine has the same pregnancy restrictions.
- Any fever or minor illness is an automatic contraindication.
- A family history of an adverse event automatically excludes a person.
- A prior expected mild reaction means the next dose is contraindicated.
- A vaccine protects against every strain/serotype of a pathogen.
- A campaign dose automatically replaces routine doses.
- A missing scar or absent local reaction proves a vaccine failed.

When a serious prior event or immune-status concern is reported, obtain authorized assessment and document the actual history; do not diagnose or make a product substitution outside scope.

## 5. Live vs non-live vaccines

A live attenuated vaccine uses a weakened organism that can replicate to a limited extent. Examples among common program vaccines include BCG and some measles-containing vaccines; some oral polio formulations are live. Inactivated, toxoid, subunit and conjugate vaccines use different mechanisms and have different safety considerations.

**Practical rule:** classify the exact vaccine/product before applying guidance. Live-vaccine restrictions cannot be applied indiscriminately to all vaccines, and the absence of live organisms does not mean a vaccine has no contraindications or adverse effects. Verify pregnancy, immunocompromise, allergy and co-administration rules for the specific product.

## 6. AEFI: recognition, escalation and reporting

An **AEFI** is an untoward medical occurrence after immunization that does not necessarily have a causal relationship with the vaccine. Timing alone does not prove causation. WHO describes ongoing vaccine-safety monitoring and investigation of suspected events. [3]

- Manage the patient first. For anaphylaxis, respiratory compromise, collapse, altered consciousness or another emergency, activate emergency response and follow the current authorized protocol.
- Document the event, timing, product/lot, dose, route, findings, treatment and outcome.
- Notify the appropriate clinician/supervisor and report through the current DOH/NIP system when criteria are met.
- Preserve product/cold-chain information when required.
- Do not dismiss the event or claim causality before assessment.
- Do not delay emergency treatment to complete reporting paperwork.

## 7. Cold chain and product quality

Many vaccines use +2°C to +8°C storage, but requirements are product-specific. Verify the exact label, NIP manual, diluent, reconstitution and open-vial rules. A controlled temperature chain is permitted only for products specifically authorized/labeled for it. [4]

If a temperature excursion or product-quality concern is suspected, hold/segregate the stock, document the product/lot and temperature evidence, notify the designated supervisor, and obtain authorized disposition. Do not administer or discard stock based on appearance or personal judgment alone.

## 8. Philippine program and legal context

The DOH NIP covers routine immunization and may include catch-up or supplemental activities; target populations and vaccine offerings can change. RA 10152 provides a statutory framework for mandatory basic immunization services for infants and children; it does not itself function as the current operational schedule. Check the current DOH NIP manual, circulars and applicable local instructions. [5][6]

The DOH-hosted 2023 NIP session booklet includes a historical routine schedule table, but exact ages, doses, routes, intervals and target groups must be checked against the current manual/version and product labeling before use. A WHO schedule summary is international policy guidance, not a direct substitute for Philippine instructions.

## 9. PNLE high-yield distinctions

- The exact product matters: similar vaccine names can represent different antigen compositions or formulations.
- HepB birth-dose requirements are not automatically satisfied by a later combination vaccine; verify current national guidance.
- OPV and IPV have different characteristics and cannot be substituted without authorized guidance.
- PCV and PPSV/PPV are not identical.
- MR and MMR differ by antigens; do not treat them as interchangeable.
- HPV vaccine prevents targeted HPV-related disease; it does not treat existing infection or lesions.
- Campaign eligibility and doses may differ from routine immunization.
- Contraindications and precautions are vaccine-specific.
- AEFI is not automatic proof of vaccine causation.
- Emergency response precedes reporting paperwork.
- Do not guess dose, route, interval, catch-up rule, contraindication or cold-chain disposition.
