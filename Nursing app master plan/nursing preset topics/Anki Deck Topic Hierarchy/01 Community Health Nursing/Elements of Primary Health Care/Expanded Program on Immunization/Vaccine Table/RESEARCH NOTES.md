## 1. Mandatory version warning

Vaccine schedules, products, target groups, doses, routes, sites, intervals, catch-up rules and campaigns can change. The table below summarizes entries visible in the **DOH-hosted 2023 “Conducting Immunization Sessions” booklet**. It is included as a dated comparison aid, not as proof of the current Philippine National Immunization Program (NIP) schedule in October 2026. The current complete NIP Manual of Operations/Procedures, latest DOH circulars and exact product label must be checked before administration, counseling or exam content is finalized. [1]

The WHO routine-immunization tables were updated in December 2025, but WHO explicitly states that they are intended to support national decision-makers and are **not intended for direct use by vaccinators**. Do not substitute the WHO table for the Philippine DOH schedule. [2][3]

## 3. How to build/maintain the current master vaccine table

For every vaccine, maintain these fields in the research system:
- Current DOH schedule version and circular/date.
- Vaccine generic/product name and antigen composition.
- Disease/target group.
- Minimum age, routine age and any maximum age.
- Dose number, dose volume and minimum interval.
- Route and anatomical site.
- Diluent/reconstitution and maximum use period, where applicable.
- Storage range, freeze/light sensitivity and VVM instructions.
- Contraindications and precautions, including special populations.
- Co-administration and spacing rules.
- Catch-up/interrupted-series rules.
- Expected reactions and serious AEFI warning signs.
- Documentation, reporting and next-dose requirements.
- Source link and date checked.
- Verification status and reviewer.

Do not merge information from different products or years into one row without explicit version labels. If guidance differs, show the source/date and explain which current Philippine instruction governs.

## 4. Vaccine table safety checks

Before using a table for clinical care or teaching:
1. Confirm it matches the current Philippine DOH NIP manual and relevant circulars.
2. Verify the exact product and formulation; similar names can represent different products.
3. Confirm dose, route, site, age, interval and catch-up rules from current product/DOH guidance.
4. Check contraindications and precautions for the exact vaccine and recipient.
5. Confirm the source's date and whether it is national policy, a regional report, a global recommendation or an older educational table.
6. Check cold-chain, reconstitution, open-vial and expiry instructions.
7. Reopen the linked source and verify that it supports the exact row.
8. Mark unresolved items “Needs Verification” rather than guessing.

## 5. Nursing responsibilities

- Do not administer based only on a printed or copied schedule if its version is uncertain.
- Check the recipient's record and current eligibility.
- Ask about prior severe reactions and relevant conditions.
- Verify product, lot, expiry, storage history and correct preparation.
- Follow the authorized administration route/site and infection-prevention procedures.
- Explain expected reactions, serious warning signs and follow-up.
- Document accurately and use current reporting systems.
- For a suspected AEFI, manage the patient first, then document/report according to current policy.
- If a temperature excursion or product problem is suspected, hold/segregate stock and obtain authorized disposition.
- Never invent a dose, interval, contraindication or current campaign eligibility.

## 6. Philippine context and PNLE distinctions

- The DOH NIP manual and current DOH circulars govern Philippine program implementation; WHO tables support national policy decisions but are not direct vaccinator schedules.
- A dated table can be useful for historical study only if its date is explicit.
- MR and MMR differ by antigen composition.
- OPV and IPV differ in formulation and program role.
- PCV and PPV/PPSV are different vaccine types.
- Monovalent hepatitis B birth-dose requirements must not be assumed to be replaced by a later combination dose.
- A vaccine schedule is not complete without eligibility, contraindications/precautions, product, route, cold chain, documentation and follow-up.
- An AEFI occurring after vaccination does not automatically prove vaccine causation.
- Never count an invalid dose as valid coverage or restart a series without the current catch-up rule.
