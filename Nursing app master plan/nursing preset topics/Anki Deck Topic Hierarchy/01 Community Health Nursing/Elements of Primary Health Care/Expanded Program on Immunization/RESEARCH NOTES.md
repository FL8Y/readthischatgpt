## 1. Program overview and terminology

The Philippine government launched the **Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI)** on 12 July 1976 with support from the World Health Organization (WHO) and UNICEF. The Department of Health (DOH) now commonly uses **National Immunization Program (NIP)** in current program materials. The program aims to protect eligible populations from vaccine-preventable diseases through safe, effective vaccination services and a strong delivery, monitoring and response system. DOH regional program pages describe services for newborns, infants, older children, pregnant people and older adults, with eligibility and vaccine offerings determined by current national guidance. [1][2]

The folder name “Expanded Program on Immunization” is retained to preserve the repository hierarchy and common nursing-course terminology. In current work, distinguish the historical EPI name from the current NIP label. Do not assume that an older EPI table remains the current national schedule.

Immunization is one classic essential element of primary health care. It requires more than giving an injection or oral dose: correct eligibility, vaccine/product handling, informed communication, safe administration, documentation, adverse-event response, follow-up and equitable reach are all essential.

## 2. Objectives and program components

An effective immunization program seeks to:
- Prevent illness, complications, disability and death from vaccine-preventable diseases.
- Ensure eligible people receive recommended vaccines at the appropriate time.
- Reduce missed opportunities and inequities in access.
- Maintain vaccine potency through correct storage, transport and handling.
- Ensure safe administration and respond appropriately to adverse events following immunization (AEFI).
- Maintain accurate records and reliable coverage data.
- Detect immunity gaps and outbreaks and conduct authorized catch-up or supplemental activities.
- Communicate transparently with communities and build confidence through respectful, evidence-informed counseling.

Typical system components include policy and planning; service delivery; vaccine forecasting/procurement; cold chain and logistics; workforce training/supervision; communication and community engagement; recording/reporting; safety surveillance; and monitoring/evaluation. The precise operational requirements are defined by current DOH NIP guidance and local protocols.

## 3. Schedule and eligibility: the mandatory rule

**Use the current DOH National Immunization Program Manual of Operations/Manual of Procedures and the latest applicable DOH circulars for every schedule, dose, route, catch-up rule, target group and campaign.** Do not copy exact ages or doses from older textbooks or from a regional page without confirming the current national version.

For any vaccine-specific decision, verify:
- Target population and minimum/maximum age, if applicable.
- Number of doses and interval.
- Dose, route, site, product/formulation and any special preparation.
- Contraindications, precautions and deferral criteria.
- Catch-up rules for delayed or unknown records.
- Co-administration and spacing rules.
- Pregnancy, immunocompromise and other special-population guidance.
- Documentation, reporting and follow-up requirements.
- Current local campaign or outbreak-specific guidance.

A missed dose does not automatically mean a series must be restarted. Use the current national catch-up guidance and vaccine-specific rules. Do not invent an interval or advise an unauthorized restart.

## 4. Screening before vaccination

Before vaccination, follow the approved checklist and current vaccine-specific guidance:
1. Confirm identity and review the immunization record/history.
2. Confirm the intended vaccine, indication, eligibility, dose and timing under the current schedule.
3. Ask about prior doses and serious reactions, relevant allergies, current illness, pregnancy when relevant, immune status, medicines and other conditions required by the protocol.
4. Distinguish a true contraindication from a precaution or a minor illness; do not deny vaccination automatically based on a vague or irrelevant history.
5. Explain expected reactions, warning signs, the next dose/follow-up and how to seek help.
6. Obtain consent according to applicable policy and law; protect privacy.
7. Verify vaccine/product, expiry, lot/batch, vial monitor where applicable, appearance, storage history and preparation instructions.
8. If the record, product, eligibility or order is unclear, pause and consult the authorized supervisor/clinician rather than guessing.

WHO emphasizes that a contraindication is a condition in which vaccination poses an unacceptable risk, while a precaution requires a benefit-risk assessment. False contraindications can create missed opportunities. The exact rule varies by vaccine and product; a blanket statement should never replace current product/program guidance. [3][4]

## 5. Cold chain and vaccine logistics

The **cold chain** is the system of people, equipment and procedures that maintains vaccines within the storage/transport conditions required to preserve quality from manufacturer to administration. WHO notes that most vaccines used in immunization programs traditionally require storage in the +2°C to +8°C range, but product-specific labels and authorized controlled-temperature-chain provisions can differ. Never assume that all vaccines tolerate freezing, all vaccines tolerate the same temperature, or all products share the same excursion rules. [5][6]

### Nursing/facility responsibilities
- Use the current NIP manual and product-specific instructions for storage, temperature monitoring, transport, diluent and reconstitution.
- Monitor and document temperatures using approved equipment and frequency.
- Protect freeze-sensitive products from freezing; do not place products against frozen packs unless the approved transport method permits it.
- Maintain stock cards/records, batch/lot and expiry data, inventory control and FEFO where applicable.
- Check vaccine vial monitors and product integrity where applicable.
- Prepare and use reconstituted vaccines only within the product-specific permitted time and conditions.
- Separate and label any stock affected by a suspected temperature excursion; **do not use or discard it until an authorized assessment determines disposition**.
- Document the excursion, time/temperature evidence, affected lots and corrective action, and report through the approved chain.
- Maintain backup plans for power failure, equipment breakdown, transport delays and outreach sessions.

**Critical safety rule:** never decide independently that an exposed vaccine is safe because it “still feels cold,” looks normal or has not expired. Potency loss may not be visible. Conversely, do not discard stock without the required assessment; follow the official excursion procedure.

## 6. Safe administration and infection prevention

For every dose:
- Perform hand hygiene and follow standard precautions.
- Prepare the correct vaccine and diluent with aseptic technique using the authorized product instructions.
- Use the correct route, site, equipment and technique for that vaccine/age group according to the current manual.
- Do not combine vaccines in the same syringe unless the specific product and official guidance explicitly authorize it.
- Use a new sterile needle and syringe as required; never reuse single-use equipment.
- Dispose of sharps immediately in approved puncture-resistant containers and follow healthcare-waste policy.
- Do not leave prepared/reconstituted vaccine outside its permitted conditions or time.
- Observe the recipient and maintain readiness to respond to an acute reaction under local protocol.
- Record vaccine name, dose, date, lot/batch and other required information accurately.
- Explain expected reactions, red flags and the next appointment.

This overview intentionally does not provide a universal dose/route/site table. Those details are product- and age-specific and must be checked in the current DOH NIP manual and product information before administration.

## 7. Adverse events following immunization (AEFI)

An **AEFI** is an untoward medical occurrence after immunization that does not necessarily have a causal relationship with the vaccine. It may be a symptom, abnormal finding, coincidental illness, product-related reaction, immunization error or another event. Timing after vaccination alone does not prove causation. WHO describes ongoing vaccine-safety monitoring and investigation of potential events. [3]

### Nursing response
1. **Assess and manage the patient first.** For anaphylaxis, respiratory compromise, collapse, altered consciousness or another emergency, activate emergency response and follow the current treatment protocol immediately.
2. Document the event, timing, vaccine/product, lot, dose, route, clinical findings, treatment and outcome according to policy.
3. Notify the appropriate clinician/supervisor and public-health reporting channel within the applicable current rules.
4. Preserve relevant product/lot and cold-chain information for investigation when directed.
5. Communicate calmly and accurately; do not dismiss the concern or assert causality before investigation.
6. Support follow-up and provide clear return precautions.
7. Participate in investigation/corrective action when required.

Do not wait for proof of causation before reporting an event that meets the current reporting criteria. Do not improvise a reporting deadline or case definition; use the current national/manual instructions.

## 8. Contraindications, precautions and common traps

- A history of severe allergic reaction to a prior dose or a component can be a contraindication for that vaccine; verify the exact product-specific rule.
- Some live vaccines have restrictions in pregnancy or severe immunocompromise; verify the vaccine and current guideline rather than applying a generic rule to all vaccines.
- Moderate/severe acute illness may warrant deferral in some circumstances, while minor illness is often not a contraindication; follow current guidance.
- A precaution is not the same as an absolute contraindication.
- A previous mild expected reaction is not automatically a contraindication to future doses.
- Do not assume an antibiotic course, mild respiratory symptoms or a nonspecific family history automatically excludes vaccination.
- Do not delay all vaccines because one product is temporarily contraindicated; assess each vaccine/indication separately under the schedule.
- An uncertain immunization history requires the current catch-up algorithm, not a guessed dose count.
- Do not treat a temperature excursion as a routine stock issue; isolate the affected stock and obtain authorized disposition.
- AEFI does not mean “caused by vaccine”; report and investigate according to policy.

## 9. Coverage, target setting and data quality

Immunization coverage estimates the proportion of a defined eligible population that received a specified dose or completed a defined schedule in a specified period. It is not merely the number of doses administered.

A meaningful indicator defines:
- Numerator: people in the target population who received the specified valid dose/schedule.
- Denominator: the defined eligible target population for the same geography/period.
- Time period and geography.
- Data source and rules for deduplication/valid doses.
- Whether the measure is administrative coverage or survey-based coverage.

Check for inaccurate target estimates, duplicate records, migration, missed outreach areas, invalid doses and late reporting. Coverage above 100% may indicate denominator or numerator problems, catchment movement or data-quality issues; it should be investigated, not celebrated automatically. Disaggregate by relevant groups where lawful and useful to identify inequity. Keep identifiable health records secure.

## 10. Community engagement and missed opportunities

Community-health nurses can:
- Map eligible populations and identify missed/under-immunized groups using approved data.
- Coordinate with local government, schools, community leaders and outreach teams.
- Explain the benefits and known risks accurately without coercion or ridicule.
- Address practical barriers such as clinic hours, transport, documentation, language, disability, stock availability and fear of adverse events.
- Use reminder/recall and catch-up activities only according to current policy.
- Maintain privacy and avoid public disclosure of a person's immunization status.
- Check that referrals and next-dose appointments are feasible.
- Evaluate whether outreach improves valid coverage and equity rather than only counting contacts.

## 11. Philippine program context

The DOH NIP includes routine immunization and may include supplemental immunization activities or targeted campaigns. Program offerings and eligible groups can change as disease epidemiology, vaccine availability and national guidance evolve. The DOH regional program page confirms the historic 1976 EPI launch and current NIP framing, and lists the NIP Manual of Operations as implementation support. The official DOH manual and current circulars—not a static summary in this note—must govern the actual schedule and field procedure. [1][2]

This note does not provide exact routine schedules, campaign dates, doses, routes, target-setting values or reporting deadlines because those are version-sensitive. ## 12. PNLE high-yield distinctions

- EPI is the historic program name; current DOH materials commonly use National Immunization Program (NIP).
- Immunization is primary prevention for the target vaccine-preventable disease.
- Correct vaccine selection, eligibility, dose/route, cold chain, administration, documentation and follow-up are all parts of safe vaccination.
- A contraindication differs from a precaution; minor illness is not automatically a contraindication.
- AEFI is a temporal association, not automatic proof that the vaccine caused the event.
- Stabilize/manage the patient before completing reporting paperwork.
- A temperature excursion requires isolation/hold and authorized assessment; appearance and expiry date do not prove potency.
- Reconstituted vaccines have product-specific time/temperature limits.
- Coverage requires a valid numerator and denominator; dose counts alone are not coverage.
- Use current DOH schedule/catch-up guidance; old tables and regional summaries may be outdated.
- Never invent dose, route, interval, contraindication, cold-chain disposition or AEFI deadline.
