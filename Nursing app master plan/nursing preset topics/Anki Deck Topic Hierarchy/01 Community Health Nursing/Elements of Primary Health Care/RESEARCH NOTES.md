## 1. Purpose of this parent folder

This folder organizes classic service elements associated with the 1978 Declaration of Alma-Ata into individual child topics. This note provides the shared framework and mapping; **every child folder remains a separate assignment and must receive its own substantive research**. This parent note does not complete any child.

The eight classic elements in paragraph VII.3 of Alma-Ata are: (1) health education about prevailing problems and methods of prevention/control; (2) food supply and proper nutrition; (3) safe water and basic sanitation; (4) maternal and child health, including family planning; (5) immunization against major infectious diseases; (6) prevention/control of locally endemic diseases; (7) appropriate treatment of common diseases and injuries; and (8) essential drugs. The declaration also emphasizes equity, community participation, intersectoral collaboration, appropriate technology, trained health workers and integrated referral systems. PHC cannot be reduced to an eight-item checklist. [1][2]

## 3. Distinguish the classic elements from PHC principles and modern frameworks

### Classic service elements
The eight elements describe core service areas and are useful for traditional nursing exam recall.

### Broader PHC principles
Equity, community participation, intersectoral action, appropriate technology, trained teams and integrated referral systems describe how PHC should be organized and connected. They support all eight elements but are not interchangeable with the service-element list. [1]

### Modern WHO–UNICEF framework
Contemporary PHC is organized around three connected components:
1. Integrated health services, with primary care and essential public-health functions central.
2. Multisectoral policy and action on determinants of health.
3. Empowered individuals, families and communities.

The 2020 WHO–UNICEF Operational Framework identifies 14 interdependent strategic/operational levers to help implement these components. This is not simply another eight-item list. [3][4]

### PHC vs primary care
Primary care is a key service-delivery component within the broader PHC approach. PHC also includes public-health functions, multisectoral action and community empowerment. Complex illness and emergencies may require higher-level services; a functioning referral system is essential.

## 4. Integrated nursing workflow

1. **Assess:** define population, health needs, strengths, exposures, barriers and data limitations; consult residents.
2. **Prioritize:** immediate threats to life and rapid deterioration first, then burden, inequity, preventability, community priorities and feasibility.
3. **Connect services:** determine which element(s) apply and whether coordinated action is needed.
4. **Co-plan:** set objectives, activities, roles, timeline, supplies, referral routes, safety controls and evaluation.
5. **Coordinate sectors:** involve non-health partners when food security, water, sanitation, education or transport are determinants.
6. **Implement within scope:** use current DOH/program guidance, consent/privacy requirements, infection prevention and safe documentation/medication practices.
7. **Refer and follow up:** establish urgency, secure handover, explain return precautions and check referral completion where feasible.
8. **Evaluate and adapt:** measure reach, quality, outcomes and equity; revise using evidence and community feedback.

## 5. Cross-element safety principles

- Routine surveys, education, immunization targets and audits must not delay emergency response or urgent referral.
- Never guess medication doses or alter a regimen outside professional scope, a valid order or authorized protocol.
- Immunization schedules and contraindications are product/program-specific and must be checked against current Philippine guidance.
- Maternal/child danger signs require current escalation protocols.
- Suspected outbreaks require current surveillance/reporting pathways; do not invent deadlines or case definitions.
- Do not declare water safe based on appearance; use authorized testing/advisories.
- Protect privacy, informed choice and confidentiality within lawful limits.
- Do not fabricate data or treat service counts as proof of health impact.

## 6. Evaluation across elements

- **Education:** demonstrated understanding/skill, not attendance alone.
- **Nutrition:** valid age-appropriate assessment and follow-up.
- **Water/sanitation:** access to services meeting applicable standards and corrective-action completion.
- **Maternal/child health:** timely coverage, referral and follow-up using defined denominators.
- **Immunization:** valid eligible-population coverage, dose accuracy, cold-chain/safety checks and follow-up.
- **Endemic disease control:** disease-specific surveillance and intervention coverage interpreted in context.
- **Common illness/injury care:** timely assessment, appropriate treatment/referral and follow-up.
- **Essential drugs:** availability/stock-outs, expiry/storage, correct use and adverse-event monitoring.

Every indicator needs a defined population, numerator, denominator, period, source and responsible person. Protect identifiable data and document uncertainty.

## 7. Philippine context

Alma-Ata is a global framework; current Philippine service packages, immunization schedules, medicine rules, disease reporting, licensing and scope of practice depend on current law and applicable DOH/PhilHealth/LGU issuances. RA 11223 is the Universal Health Care Act and provides broader system context, but a general PHC concept does not establish a specific local entitlement or clinical requirement. Verify current legal text and implementing rules before making specific claims. [5]

Use the current official Philippine source for each child topic: DOH program manuals, immunization guidance, disease-specific clinical guidelines, medicine references and reporting rules. Do not infer current vaccine schedules, drug regimens, thresholds or deadlines from the historical eight-element list.

## 8. PNLE recall anchors

- The classic eight elements are listed in Alma-Ata (1978), paragraph VII.3.
- The hierarchy maps the elements to child folders but is not evidence of research completion.
- Eight elements differ from PHC principles/pillars and from the modern three-component/14-lever framework.
- PHC includes promotion, prevention, appropriate treatment and rehabilitation and relies on referral continuity.
- Education alone cannot fix structural barriers such as unsafe water or absent supplies.
- Screening is not diagnosis; positive results need a safe follow-up pathway.
- Immediate danger and deterioration take priority over routine program activities.
- Exact clinical schedules, doses, contraindications and reporting rules must be verified from current topic-specific sources.
