## 1. First distinguish the waste setting

**Do not use one color chart for every kind of waste.** Community/municipal solid waste and healthcare waste have different categories, risks and handling/disposal routes. Exact color codes can depend on the applicable DOH/healthcare-waste standard, facility policy, waste category, and local solid-waste rules. The container must be correctly labelled and the contents segregated at the point of generation; color alone does not prove that waste is correctly classified. Check the current DOH Health Care Waste Management Manual and the facility's approved procedure before applying a color to a clinical waste stream. [1][2][3]

### General/municipal solid waste
Republic Act No. 9003 (Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000) requires separate containers/identification for waste types such as compostable, non-recyclable, recyclable and special waste. It does not mean every local government uses one universally identical color chart for all contexts. Local rules and the applicable implementing regulations matter. [4]

### Healthcare waste
Healthcare waste includes waste from diagnosis, treatment, management, immunization, related research and other health-care activities. It can include general non-hazardous waste and hazardous waste such as infectious, pathological, sharps, pharmaceutical/cytotoxic, chemical and radioactive waste. Hazardous categories require appropriate segregation, packaging, labelling, storage, transport, treatment and disposal. [2][3][5]

## 2. General community solid-waste categories

| Category | Meaning/examples | Handling principle |
|---|---|---|
| **Compostable/biodegradable** | Food scraps, fruit/vegetable peelings, yard waste where accepted by the local program. | Separate at source; keep suitable for composting/biological processing according to local rules. |
| **Recyclable** | Clean paper/cardboard, selected plastics, metals, glass or other materials accepted by the local recycling system. | Keep clean/dry where required; follow local accepted-material list. |
| **Non-recyclable/residual** | Materials not accepted for composting or recycling under the local program. | Store securely and dispose through the authorized collection route. |
| **Special waste** | Certain household hazardous/special items (e.g., batteries, electronics, lamps or other materials defined by local rules). | Do not mix with ordinary waste; follow the appropriate authorized collection/disposal process. |
| **Healthcare hazardous waste** | Infectious materials, sharps, pharmaceutical/chemical waste and other healthcare-specific categories. | Do not treat as ordinary household/municipal waste; follow the healthcare-waste rules. |

The examples are illustrative. The exact category for a material depends on its contamination, composition, local acceptance rules and the applicable regulatory definition.

## 3. Healthcare waste categories and color-coding principles

Healthcare waste must be segregated at the point of generation into the correct category and container. Philippine DOH materials reference color-coded containers/liners, including schemes in which yellow is used for infectious waste, red for sharps, black for non-hazardous general waste, white/translucent for pharmaceutical waste and blue for chemical waste. However, the complete applicable color scheme must be confirmed against the **current official DOH Healthcare Waste Management Manual (4th edition where applicable), DOH AO 2013-003 and other current rules**, plus the facility's approved procedure. Do not treat an incomplete list from a procurement document or secondary page as a universal table. [1][2][3]

| Waste type | Examples | Safe handling principle | Color-code caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **General non-hazardous waste** | Uncontaminated packaging, paper and other ordinary waste not otherwise classified as hazardous. | Segregate from hazardous waste; recycle where appropriate and accepted. | Black is used for general waste in some Philippine healthcare-waste schemes; verify the current manual/facility policy. |
| **Infectious waste** | Materials contaminated with blood/body fluids or infectious agents where classified as infectious under the protocol. | Use the designated leak-resistant, labelled container/bag; avoid manual sorting and exposure. | Yellow is used for infectious waste in cited Philippine healthcare settings; verify the applicable official scheme. |
| **Sharps** | Needles, blades, lancets and other items capable of puncturing/cutting. | Immediately place in an approved puncture-resistant sharps container at point of use; never recap, bend or hand-pass used sharps. Seal at the specified fill line and route as regulated waste. | Red is used for sharps in cited Philippine healthcare guidance; follow current official/facility protocol. |
| **Pathological/anatomical waste** | Human tissues or other materials defined as pathological under the applicable protocol. | Segregate, label and route through authorized handling/treatment/disposal. | May share or differ from infectious-waste color depending on the governing scheme; check the exact current standard. |
| **Pharmaceutical waste** | Expired, unused or contaminated medicines and related pharmaceutical materials. | Do not pour into drains or place in ordinary waste unless an authorized rule specifically permits it; segregate and route to the approved handler. | White/translucent is cited for pharmaceutical waste in some Philippine facility guidance; verify current category-specific rule. |
| **Chemical waste** | Laboratory reagents, solvents, disinfectants, heavy-metal-containing materials and other chemicals. | Identify the chemical, follow the safety data sheet and authorized hazardous-waste procedure; never mix incompatible chemicals. | Blue is cited for chemical waste in some Philippine facility guidance; verify current category-specific rule. |
| **Cytotoxic/genotoxic waste** | Medicines/materials with cytotoxic or genotoxic hazards. | Use the specific high-hazard protocol, PPE and authorized treatment/disposal route. | May have a distinct color/label under the current manual; do not assume it matches general pharmaceutical waste. |
| **Radioactive waste** | Materials contaminated with radionuclides. | Follow radiation-safety and regulator-specific rules; do not place in ordinary healthcare-waste streams. | Follow the specific radiation-safety classification and labelling. |

**Rule:** if the waste type or correct container is uncertain, stop and ask the designated infection-prevention/environmental-health or waste-management officer. Do not guess from color alone.

## 4. Point-of-generation segregation and labelling

- Segregate immediately where the waste is generated; do not sort mixed waste by hand later.
- Place waste in a container suitable for the hazard (e.g., puncture-resistant sharps container for sharps).
- Use the designated color-coded container/bag **and** the required label/symbol/category.
- Do not overfill bags or sharps containers; close/seal at the specified limit.
- Keep containers covered where required, secure and accessible to the intended users.
- Separate incompatible chemical wastes; do not mix unknown chemicals.
- Keep hazardous waste secure from children, visitors, scavengers and unauthorized handlers.
- Follow the facility's collection schedule, storage-area controls, documentation and authorized transporter/treatment/disposal route.
- Never reopen sealed waste bags to correct segregation errors; follow the approved incident/corrective-action procedure.

## 5. Sharps and exposure safety

Used needles and other sharps can cause puncture injuries and transmit bloodborne pathogens. The nurse should:
- Dispose of the sharp immediately after use into the approved sharps container at point of use.
- Never recap, bend, break or manually remove a used needle unless a specifically authorized safety procedure requires it.
- Avoid overfilling, compressing or reaching into containers.
- Keep containers stable and within safe reach without creating a hazard to clients/children.
- If a needlestick or other exposure occurs, wash/flush the site as directed by the current protocol and seek urgent occupational-health assessment and post-exposure management. Do not wait for symptoms.
- Report and document the exposure through the facility's approved channel.

## 6. Chemical, pharmaceutical and other hazardous waste

- Identify the material and consult the applicable safety data sheet/current procedure.
- Do not pour pharmaceuticals, solvents, disinfectants or unknown chemicals into drains or ordinary waste without an authorized basis.
- Do not mix incompatible substances or improvise neutralization.
- Use required PPE, ventilation and spill response; for unknown or large spills, isolate the area and call trained responders.
- Keep hazardous waste labelled, closed and stored in the designated compatible container.
- Use authorized handlers and documented transfer/manifest processes when required.
- Radioactive waste requires the applicable radiation-safety/regulatory route; it is not ordinary medical waste.
- Do not assume expired medicines, cytotoxic drugs and ordinary packaging share the same disposal route.

## 7. Philippine legal and policy framework

- **Republic Act No. 9003 (2000), Ecological Solid Waste Management Act:** establishes the general ecological solid-waste framework and source segregation/identification requirements for compostable, recyclable, non-recyclable and special waste. [4]
- **Presidential Decree No. 856, Code on Sanitation:** includes refuse disposal and sanitation requirements; consult the relevant chapter and implementing rules. [6]
- **Joint DENR–DOH Administrative Order No. 02, s. 2005:** policies and guidelines for handling, collection, transport, treatment, storage and disposal of healthcare wastes. [2]
- **DOH Administrative Order No. 2013-003:** cited in recent DOH facility materials for healthcare-waste segregation/color coding; verify the full order and current manual before using the exact table. [1]
- **DOH Healthcare Waste Management Manual, 4th edition:** DOH has referenced an updated manual through its Health Facility Development Bureau. Use the current official copy and the facility's approved procedure for exact categories, colors, labels, fill limits and disposal pathways. [3]
- Other rules may apply to hazardous/toxic waste, air emissions, water pollution, medicines and radioactive materials. Exact duties and penalties require the current legal text and applicable regulations.

## 8. Community-health nursing responsibilities

- Teach community members the local municipal segregation rules and explain why healthcare waste must not be mixed with household waste.
- In clinical settings, follow the current facility waste-management manual and verify the correct container before disposing.
- Ensure containers, labels, PPE and collection routes are available; escalate shortages rather than silently using an unsafe substitute.
- Monitor segregation at the source and give corrective feedback without blaming staff who report system gaps.
- Prevent public access to hazardous waste and report illegal dumping or unsafe handling through the authorized channel.
- Document incidents, exposures, waste transfer and corrective actions according to policy.
- Protect waste handlers and cleaners with training, vaccination/occupational-health support as applicable, PPE and safe work systems.
- Do not claim a color code is legally universal without verifying the current standard and context.

## 9. Evaluation indicators

- Proportion of waste streams correctly segregated at source using the current checklist.
- Number of sharps injuries or exposure events and timeliness of follow-up.
- Availability of the correct containers/liners and labels.
- Waste bags/containers closed at the prescribed limit and labelled correctly.
- Completion of collection, storage, transport and disposal documentation.
- Incidents of mixing hazardous and general waste.
- Staff training/competency and corrective-action completion.
- Safe access control for temporary waste-storage areas.

Define the denominator, period, data source and responsible person. A lower incident count may reflect improved safety or underreporting; interpret with audit findings and reporting culture.

## 10. PNLE high-yield distinctions

- **General solid waste categories** and **healthcare waste categories** are not interchangeable.
- Segregation occurs at the point of generation.
- Correct classification and labelling matter; color alone is insufficient.
- Sharps go directly into a puncture-resistant sharps container; never recap used needles.
- Infectious, pharmaceutical, chemical, cytotoxic and radioactive waste require category-specific handling and disposal.
- RA 9003 is the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act; Joint DENR–DOH AO 02 s. 2005 covers healthcare-waste management.
- Color coding can vary by context and governing manual; verify current DOH guidance and facility policy instead of memorizing an unsourced chart.
- Never sort mixed waste by hand or improvise chemical disposal.
