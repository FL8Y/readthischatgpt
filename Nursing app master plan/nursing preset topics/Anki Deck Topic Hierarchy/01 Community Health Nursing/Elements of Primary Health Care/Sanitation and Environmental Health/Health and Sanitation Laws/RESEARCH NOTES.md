## 1. Scope and safe use

Environmental health is governed by several overlapping laws and technical standards. Drinking-water quality, sewage, municipal solid waste, air pollution, hazardous chemicals, food safety and healthcare waste are not governed by one interchangeable law. This note is a study overview, not a legal opinion. For compliance decisions, inspect the full current law, amendments, implementing rules and competent agency's latest issuance. Do not invent penalties, deadlines, permit requirements or legal duties.

## 2. Major Philippine legal frameworks

| Law/issuance | Common title / focus | Broad relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **PD 856 (1975)** | Code on Sanitation of the Philippines | Broad sanitation framework; relevant chapters address water supply, food establishments, sewage/refuse and other public-health sanitation matters. |
| **RA 9003 (2000)** | Ecological Solid Waste Management Act | Source segregation, waste minimization, recycling/resource recovery, collection and appropriate disposal; LGUs have important responsibilities. |
| **RA 9275 (2004)** | Philippine Clean Water Act | Water-quality management and pollution control for water bodies and pollution sources. |
| **RA 8749 (1999)** | Philippine Clean Air Act | Air-pollution control and protection of air quality. |
| **RA 6969 (1990)** | Toxic Substances and Hazardous and Nuclear Wastes Control Act | Regulation of toxic chemicals and hazardous/nuclear wastes within the Act's scope. |
| **RA 10611 (2013)** | Food Safety Act of 2013 | Strengthens food-safety regulation across the food chain. |
| **Joint DENR–DOH AO 02, s. 2005** | Healthcare-waste management | Handling, collection, transport, treatment, storage and disposal of healthcare waste. |
| **DOH AO 2017-0010** | Philippine National Standards for Drinking Water of 2017 | Drinking-water quality standards, sampling/examination and surveillance framework. Verify later amendments. |
| **DOH AO 2014-0027** | Water Safety Plan policy | Risk management from source to consumer for drinking-water providers. |
| **DOH AO 2019-0054** | Philippine Approach to Sustainable Sanitation (PhATSS) | Philippine sanitation program/policy and indicators. |

## 3. Distinctions that matter

- **PD 856 vs RA 9003:** the Code on Sanitation is broader; RA 9003 focuses on ecological solid-waste management. Do not use the general solid-waste framework as a substitute for sewage, drinking-water or healthcare-waste rules.
- **RA 9275 vs PNSDW:** RA 9275 addresses water-quality management/pollution control; PNSDW specifies drinking-water quality requirements. A distribution-level label does not prove potability.
- **RA 6969 vs ordinary solid waste:** toxic chemicals and regulated hazardous wastes require specific classification and authorized handling; physical form alone does not make an item ordinary waste.
- **RA 10611 vs PD 856:** the Food Safety Act strengthens the food-safety system across the food chain; sanitation rules may also apply to establishments. Verify the current rules and responsible regulator.
- **Healthcare vs municipal waste:** infectious, sharps, pharmaceutical, chemical, cytotoxic and radioactive waste require category-specific handling and disposal; do not assume municipal color codes/routes apply.
- **Old issuance vs current rule:** a law may remain foundational while technical standards, agency procedures and amendments change. Check current versions.

## 4. Community-health nursing workflow

1. **Identify the hazard:** unsafe water, sewage overflow, solid/hazardous waste, air exposure, foodborne cluster or other environmental risk.
2. **Assess urgency:** clinical emergency or immediate exposure requires prompt protection/referral, not routine paperwork.
3. **Protect within scope:** use only current authorized risk-reduction advice and avoid direct contact with unknown hazards.
4. **Identify the responsible authority:** local health officer/sanitary inspector, DOH, DENR, LGU waste service, water provider, food-safety regulator or trained hazardous-waste team, as applicable.
5. **Select the relevant legal framework:** determine whether the issue concerns sanitation, water quality, pollution, solid waste, hazardous chemicals, food safety or healthcare waste.
6. **Document evidence and uncertainty:** separate direct observations from reports or assumptions; record time, location, affected group and actions taken.
7. **Notify/escalate through current procedures:** do not invent reporting deadlines or share confidential patient information in public channels.
8. **Follow up:** confirm that the referral was received and corrective action occurred where feasible.
9. **Evaluate equity:** identify groups lacking safe services and whether infrastructure/resource barriers require action beyond education.

Nurses do not replace environmental engineers, accredited laboratories, sanitary inspectors or authorized regulators. They identify concerns, protect clients, document and coordinate within their role.

## 5. Safety and ethical points

- Do not state a fine, permit requirement, sampling frequency or threshold without checking the complete applicable legal text and amendments.
- Do not tell the public water is safe based on appearance, taste or service level.
- Do not handle unknown chemicals, sewage or hazardous waste without training, PPE and authorization.
- Do not dispose of healthcare sharps, pharmaceuticals, chemicals or radioactive materials in ordinary trash/drains unless a current authorized rule permits it.
- Do not disclose identifiable health information in public reports or meetings.
- Do not delay emergency care while collecting evidence for an environmental report.
- Do not blame households for unsafe sanitation when infrastructure, affordability or access is the main barrier.
- Follow current emergency/reporting pathways for suspected outbreaks, poisoning, water contamination or serious hazards.
