## 1. Definition and scope

Malnutrition refers to deficiencies, excesses or imbalances in energy and/or nutrient intake or utilization. It includes undernutrition, micronutrient-related malnutrition, overweight/obesity and diet-related noncommunicable disease (NCD) risk. Different forms can coexist: a person can have micronutrient deficiency without being underweight, and excess weight does not guarantee adequate nutrient quality. [1]

Community-health nursing addresses malnutrition through prevention, assessment, growth monitoring, early recognition, referral, evidence-based nutrition education, food-security support and coordination with health, water/sanitation, education and social-service partners. Malnutrition is not solely an individual food-choice issue; poverty, food availability, maternal health, infection, caregiving conditions and access to services affect nutritional status.

## 2. Main forms and distinctions

| Form | Common indicator | Meaning and interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Wasting** | Low weight-for-height/length in children under 5; WHO standards commonly use below −2 SD for wasting and below −3 SD for severe wasting. | Often reflects recent/acute weight loss or failure to gain, inadequate intake and/or illness; moderate/severe wasting increases mortality risk. |
| **Stunting** | Low height-for-age/length-for-age; commonly below −2 SD. | Chronic/recurrent undernutrition and adverse conditions affecting growth over time. |
| **Underweight** | Low weight-for-age; commonly below −2 SD. | May reflect wasting, stunting or both; it is a composite indicator. |
| **Micronutrient deficiency** | Inadequate status of vitamins/minerals such as iron, iodine or vitamin A, depending on population. | Can affect growth, cognition, immunity and pregnancy; clinical criteria vary by nutrient and context. |
| **Overweight/obesity** | Excess weight relative to height/age standard; BMI is commonly used in adults. | May increase NCD risk and can coexist with micronutrient inadequacy. |
| **Low birth weight/poor growth** | Birth weight or growth trajectory below applicable standard. | May reflect prematurity, maternal/placental factors, illness or other causes; assess clinically. |

WHO Child Growth Standards are age- and sex-specific. Apply them with accurate measurements and the correct chart. Exact severe categories and referral thresholds must be checked against current national program guidance; do not estimate cutoffs from memory. [1][2]

## 3. Causes and risk factors

- **Inadequate intake/poor diet quality:** insufficient energy/protein, limited dietary diversity, feeding difficulty or micronutrient-poor foods.
- **Disease/impaired utilization:** recurrent infection, diarrhea, parasitic disease, malabsorption, chronic illness, increased needs or medication effects.
- **Maternal/early-life factors:** poor maternal nutrition, prematurity, low birth weight, inadequate feeding support or early illness.
- **Household/structural factors:** poverty, food insecurity, high food prices, displacement, unsafe housing/water/sanitation, limited healthcare access or inadequate social support.
- **Care/environment:** caregiver illness, disability-related barriers, oral/dental problems, unsafe food preparation or lack of nutrition services.
- **Excess intake/low-quality diet and low activity:** can contribute to overweight/obesity and diet-related NCD risk while nutrient quality remains poor.

Assess multiple causes rather than assuming one. Recurrent diarrhea may worsen nutritional status; malnutrition can increase vulnerability to infection and delay recovery.

## 4. Assessment

### History
Ask respectfully about usual intake, appetite, dietary diversity, recent weight/growth change, food availability/affordability, breastfeeding/complementary feeding, recent illness, diarrhea/vomiting, pregnancy/lactation, chronic disease, medications, swallowing/oral problems, disability, cultural food practices, family support and access to nutrition services.

### Anthropometry and examination
- Confirm identity, age/date of birth, measurement units and the applicable standard.
- Use appropriate equipment and correct technique for weight, length/height and other indicated measures.
- Plot child measures on the correct age-/sex-specific growth chart and assess trajectory, not only a single value.
- Check for edema, visible severe wasting, dehydration, altered alertness, feeding ability and signs of illness.
- Review laboratory/clinical findings only when indicated and interpreted by an appropriate clinician.
- Document measurements, chart/standard used, interpretation, limitations and follow-up plan.

A wrong age, inaccurate weight/height, unsuitable chart or incorrect unit can misclassify nutritional status. Repeat a measurement when technique or recording is doubtful. For population surveillance, use official indicator definitions and valid denominators.

## 5. Red flags and urgency

**Urgent assessment/referral is required** for severe illness or deterioration. In a child, bilateral pitting edema can indicate nutritional edema/severe acute malnutrition and must not be dismissed as ordinary swelling. Other danger signs may include lethargy/altered consciousness, inability to drink or breastfeed, persistent vomiting, severe dehydration, respiratory distress, convulsions, hypothermia/fever with poor condition or rapid deterioration. Use current national protocols for exact criteria and referral routes.

Do not delay referral to complete routine weighing, nutrition education, registration or paperwork. Emergency response and stabilization within scope/protocol take precedence.

## 6. Management principles and nursing responsibilities

Treatment depends on age, severity, edema, appetite, infection/medical complications, setting and current guidance. This note does not provide therapeutic-feed volumes, supplement doses or treatment algorithms.

Nursing responsibilities:
1. Identify and document risk using the appropriate tools and standards.
2. Recognize danger signs and refer promptly through the authorized pathway.
3. Coordinate assessment with trained clinical/nutrition personnel.
4. Support evidence-based nutrition counseling appropriate to age and condition.
5. Reinforce follow-up and the authorized plan without blaming the patient/family.
6. Assess barriers such as food access, transport, cost, disability, caregiver capacity and safe water.
7. Link families to nutrition, maternal/child health, social-welfare and community programs.
8. Monitor growth/status and response using the applicable interval/protocol.
9. Document assessment, education, referral, handover, follow-up and unresolved barriers.
10. Protect privacy and use non-stigmatizing language.

Severe acute malnutrition may require urgent therapeutic management. Clinical management differs by stability, edema, appetite and setting. Follow current WHO/DOH guidance and trained-provider protocols; do not improvise treatment or assume all patients can be managed as outpatients.

## 7. Prevention across the life course

- **Pregnancy/lactation:** support antenatal/postpartum care, nutrition assessment, food security and supplementation when recommended by current Philippine guidance.
- **Infants/young children:** support breastfeeding and age-appropriate complementary feeding under current guidance; promote safe food/water, growth monitoring and timely care for illness.
- **School-age children/adolescents:** promote nutritious foods and healthy food environments; avoid stigma and harmful body-image messaging.
- **Adults/older adults:** assess unintentional weight loss, appetite, swallowing/oral problems, chronic disease, medications, frailty and food access.
- **Community:** coordinate nutrition education with access to nutritious food, safe water/sanitation, infection prevention, maternal/child services, social support and food-security initiatives.

Fortification and supplementation can complement food-based approaches but do not replace dietary diversity, food security or clinical treatment of established deficiency.

## 8. Philippine context

DOH regional nutrition-program materials describe community-based nutrition interventions, education and monitoring, with attention to children, pregnant people, lactating mothers and nutritionally at-risk families. Use current national and local DOH/National Nutrition Council/DOST-FNRI guidance for program eligibility, indicator definitions and referral pathways. [3]

**Pinggang Pinoy**, developed by DOST-FNRI, is a visual guide for meal-level food-group proportions. It is a general education tool, not a therapeutic diet for severe malnutrition or complex disease. [4]

Food fortification is related but distinct. RA 8976 provides the Philippine food-fortification framework; specific standards and current implementation must be checked in the dedicated Food Fortification Program topic and current official rules.

## 9. Medication, supplement and therapeutic-food safety

- Verify the current program/clinical protocol, product, age/eligibility, indication, dose, route, schedule, duration, contraindications/precautions and monitoring.
- Avoid duplicate supplements or high-dose micronutrients without a supported indication; excess intake can be harmful.
- Consider pregnancy/lactation, childhood age, renal/hepatic impairment, medications, allergies and relevant laboratory findings.
- Use the current authorized therapeutic-feeding protocol for severe malnutrition and setting.
- Explain administration, follow-up, expected response and warning signs from the approved plan.
- Report suspected adverse effects/product problems through the applicable channel.
- Never guess a dose or assume different products/formulations are interchangeable.

## 10. Monitoring and evaluation

| Indicator | Meaning | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Growth-monitoring coverage | Eligible children assessed. | Does not prove accurate measurement or referral completion. |
| Wasting/stunting/underweight prevalence | Population nutritional status using defined standards. | Requires valid age/sex data and comparable methods. |
| Referral completion | Whether identified risk reached clinical assessment. | Referral issued is not referral completed. |
| Program coverage | Reach of a defined service/intervention. | Reach does not prove quality or benefit. |
| Growth trajectory | Change over time. | Consider measurement error, illness and other determinants. |
| Food security/diet diversity | Selected dimensions of household access/diet. | Define the tool and recall period. |
| Equity of reach/outcomes | Who is missed or benefits unequally. | Protect privacy and define denominators. |

Specify population, numerator, denominator, period, source and responsible person. More reports may reflect improved screening/reporting rather than worsening malnutrition alone.

## 11. PNLE high-yield distinctions

- Wasting = low weight-for-height/length; stunting = low height-for-age; underweight = low weight-for-age.
- Underweight may reflect wasting, stunting or both.
- Malnutrition includes deficiency and excess; overweight does not rule out micronutrient deficiency.
- Bilateral pitting edema in a child is a serious red flag for nutritional edema/severe acute malnutrition.
- Use correct age-/sex-specific charts; adult BMI cutoffs do not apply to young children.
- Severe malnutrition needs protocol-based clinical assessment; do not improvise therapeutic feeding or supplements.
- Consider food insecurity, infection, sanitation and social determinants alongside dietary intake.
- Education alone cannot fix lack of access to nutritious food or clinical care.
- Immediate danger and deterioration take priority over routine measurement/program targets.
