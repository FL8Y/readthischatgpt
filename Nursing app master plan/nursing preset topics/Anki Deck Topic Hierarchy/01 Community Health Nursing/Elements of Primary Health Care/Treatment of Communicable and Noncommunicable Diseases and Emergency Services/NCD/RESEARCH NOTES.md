# Noncommunicable Diseases (NCDs) in Community Health Nursing

**Topic path:** `Elements of Primary Health Care/Treatment of Communicable and Noncommunicable Diseases and Emergency Services/NCD`  
**Status:** Substantive research draft — current Philippine screening/program protocols and independent verification remain.  
**Last researched:** 2026-10-17  
**Mandatory standard:** Community Health Nursing `RESEARCH TOPIC GUIDE.md`.

## 1. Definition and public-health importance

**Noncommunicable diseases (NCDs)** are generally long-duration conditions arising from a combination of genetic, physiological, environmental and behavioral factors. WHO identifies four major NCD groups responsible for a large share of global NCD burden: cardiovascular diseases, cancers, chronic respiratory diseases and diabetes. Other important noncommunicable conditions include mental, neurologic, kidney, musculoskeletal and other chronic disorders, depending on the program and classification used. [1]

NCDs can cause premature death, disability, reduced quality of life and long-term financial strain. Many risk factors are modifiable, but NCD prevention cannot rely on individual behavior alone. Policies and environments affect access to healthy food, physical activity, tobacco/alcohol exposure, air quality, preventive services, medicines and continuity of care.

Community-health nursing addresses NCDs through health promotion, risk assessment, evidence-based screening, referral, chronic-disease support, medication-safety education, rehabilitation, family/community partnership and equitable access to primary care.

## 2. Major risk factors

### Modifiable risk factors
- Tobacco use and exposure to second-hand smoke.
- Harmful alcohol use.
- Unhealthy dietary patterns, including excessive sodium and low intake of fruit/vegetables where relevant.
- Physical inactivity and prolonged sedentary behavior.
- Air pollution and other environmental exposures.
- High blood pressure, abnormal blood glucose, abnormal blood lipids and excess adiposity as intermediate/metabolic risks.

### Non-modifiable or less-modifiable factors
- Age and family/genetic history.
- Prior disease, some congenital/biological factors and past exposures.
- Social and structural determinants that may not be under an individual's immediate control.

Risk is cumulative and interacts across the life course. Do not blame people for disease or assume a healthy behavior is equally accessible to every household. Prevention programs should address both individual support and structural barriers.

## 3. Primary, secondary and tertiary prevention for NCDs

| Level | Main aim | Examples | Safety/interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary prevention** | Prevent disease onset or reduce risk. | Tobacco-control support, healthy food environments, physical activity, reduction of harmful alcohol use, pollution control, risk-factor education. | Education alone cannot fix unsafe environments or lack of resources. |
| **Secondary prevention** | Detect disease/risk early and connect to appropriate evaluation. | Evidence-based blood-pressure assessment or screening for selected conditions in eligible populations. | Screening is not diagnosis; use current eligibility/interval guidance and ensure confirmatory follow-up. |
| **Tertiary prevention** | Reduce complications/disability in established disease. | Ongoing chronic-disease management, rehabilitation, adherence support, foot/eye/kidney complication prevention where indicated. | Tailor care to the diagnosis, current guideline and person's needs; new red flags require reassessment. |

The same person may need all three levels for different targets—for example, risk reduction to prevent another condition, screening for a separate disease and complication prevention for an established chronic illness.

## 4. Community assessment and risk stratification

A community NCD assessment should consider:
- population age structure and disease burden;
- prevalence and trends from reliable data sources;
- risk-factor patterns and social/environmental determinants;
- access to primary care, screening, laboratory services, medicines and follow-up;
- missed appointments, stock-outs, affordability, transport and other barriers;
- availability of trained staff, referral services and rehabilitation;
- community priorities, disability access and culturally appropriate resources; and
- data completeness, definitions and denominator quality.

For an individual, assess history, symptoms, risk factors, relevant family history, medicines, prior diagnoses, functional status and the person's goals. Use validated tools and current protocol. Do not use a risk score outside its intended population or substitute it for clinical judgment.

## 5. Screening vs diagnosis

Screening seeks to identify people who may need further assessment. It is not proof of disease and should be used when the test is appropriate for the population and the health system can provide follow-up.

For example:
- an elevated blood-pressure reading needs confirmation according to the current protocol; one reading does not automatically establish a chronic diagnosis;
- an abnormal blood-glucose screening result requires the appropriate confirmatory evaluation;
- cancer screening is condition-specific and should follow current eligibility, interval and follow-up guidance; and
- a person with symptoms or danger signs needs clinical assessment even if a previous screening result was normal.

False-positive results can lead to anxiety and unnecessary testing; false-negative results can create false reassurance; overdiagnosis can expose people to harms. Screening should not be expanded simply because a test is available. Verify the current Philippine DOH recommendation for the specific condition and target group.

## 6. Primary-care approach and continuity

WHO's Package of Essential Noncommunicable Disease Interventions (PEN) is intended to support evidence-based NCD management in primary-care settings, including risk assessment, protocols and access to essential interventions in resource-limited environments. Use the current version and local adaptation; WHO guidance does not automatically authorize a particular worker to diagnose/prescribe outside professional scope. [2]

A practical community-care pathway includes:
1. Identify the person/population and relevant risk or symptom.
2. Assess urgency, risk factors, prior diagnosis, medicines, barriers and preferences.
3. Use current approved screening/assessment and documentation procedures.
4. Explain results and limits in plain language.
5. Confirm abnormal findings or arrange referral according to protocol.
6. Link the person to appropriate care, prescribed treatment, counseling and follow-up.
7. Support self-management and adherence without blame.
8. Monitor response, adverse effects and complications within scope.
9. Reassess when symptoms change or targets are not met.
10. Evaluate access, continuity, outcomes and equity.

## 7. Nursing responsibilities

- Provide evidence-based, non-stigmatizing health education and shared decision support.
- Assess modifiable risks and social/access barriers.
- Use the current authorized screening and referral protocols.
- Recognize urgent symptoms and arrange emergency assessment when indicated.
- Explain that screening results are not necessarily a diagnosis.
- Support follow-up, medication adherence and safe use of prescribed treatment within role.
- Check whether the person can access medicines, testing, appointments and lifestyle resources.
- Coordinate care with primary-care teams, specialists, rehabilitation and community partners.
- Document findings accurately and protect confidential records.
- Avoid changing prescribed medicines, making diagnoses or recommending unverified doses outside scope.
- Advocate for population-level policies and accessible environments that support healthy behavior.

## 8. Emergency red flags in people with chronic disease

A known NCD does not make every new symptom routine. Seek urgent/emergency assessment according to local protocol for findings such as:
- chest pain/pressure, severe breathlessness, collapse or signs of shock;
- sudden facial droop, arm weakness, speech difficulty or other acute neurologic deficit;
- new confusion, seizure or loss of consciousness;
- severe symptomatic hyperglycemia/hypoglycemia or altered mental status in a person with diabetes;
- severe asthma/COPD exacerbation or cyanosis;
- severe sudden headache with neurologic symptoms; or
- other rapid deterioration.

This list is not exhaustive and does not replace emergency protocols. Do not delay urgent care to finish a screening form, calculate a risk score or provide routine lifestyle education.

## 9. Family/community support and equity

NCD management often requires sustained support rather than a single consultation. Community programs can support:
- accessible walking/activity opportunities;
- healthy-food availability and nutrition education;
- smoke-free environments and cessation support;
- medication access and follow-up systems;
- chronic-disease peer/community support;
- rehabilitation and disability inclusion;
- transport/referral assistance; and
- health literacy and self-management education.

Family involvement should respect the person's consent and privacy. Avoid moralizing language about weight, diet, smoking or medication use. Ask what barriers make the plan difficult and collaborate on realistic options.

## 10. Program monitoring and evaluation

Possible indicators include:
- proportion of eligible population reached by an evidence-based screening service;
- proportion of abnormal results receiving confirmatory assessment;
- time from abnormal finding to follow-up;
- continuity of care and missed-appointment follow-up;
- availability/stock-outs of essential medicines;
- control of selected risk factors using current defined measures;
- complication, hospitalization or mortality trends where reliable data exist;
- patient-reported access, understanding and quality of life; and
- equity of reach/outcomes across relevant groups.

Define numerator, denominator, period, eligibility, data source and responsible person. A higher screening count does not prove better outcomes if positive results are not followed up. Changes in diagnosis rates may reflect improved detection or reporting rather than a true change in disease occurrence.

## 11. Philippine context

RA 11223 (Universal Health Care Act) provides a broader legal framework for integrated health systems and primary care. Current Philippine NCD services, screening criteria, referral rules, clinical management, medication formularies and reporting requirements must be checked in the applicable DOH/PhilHealth/LGU issuance and disease-specific guideline. [3]

For local implementation:
- verify current DOH NCD/PhilPEN/HEARTS or condition-specific program materials where applicable;
- distinguish national recommendations from local service capacity;
- use current laboratory and screening criteria;
- confirm the referral pathway and availability of essential medicines;
- follow professional scope and facility policy; and
- do not use an old textbook threshold or drug regimen as current guidance without verification.

## 12. PNLE high-yield distinctions

- Major WHO NCD groups: cardiovascular diseases, cancers, chronic respiratory diseases and diabetes.
- Risk factors interact; many are modifiable, but structural determinants matter.
- Primary prevention reduces risk/onset; secondary prevention detects early and links to care; tertiary prevention reduces complications/disability.
- Screening is not diagnosis; abnormal findings need confirmation and follow-up.
- One elevated measurement may not establish a chronic diagnosis; use the condition-specific protocol.
- NCD care requires continuity, adherence support, monitoring and complication prevention—not education alone.
- New chest pain, stroke-like signs, severe dyspnea, collapse or altered consciousness require urgent assessment.
- Use current Philippine guidance for screening eligibility, intervals, diagnostic thresholds, medication and reporting.
- Do not blame patients for access barriers or assume a person can afford/obtain the recommended resources.
