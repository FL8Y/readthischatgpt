# Treatment of Communicable and Noncommunicable Diseases and Emergency Services — Parent Overview

**Topic path:** `Elements of Primary Health Care/Treatment of Communicable and Noncommunicable Diseases and Emergency Services`  
**Status:** Substantive parent-topic draft — disease-specific protocols and current Philippine guidance require verification.  
**Last researched:** 2026-10-17  
**Mandatory standard:** Community Health Nursing `RESEARCH TOPIC GUIDE.md`.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This parent topic covers how community-health systems recognize, prevent, manage and refer common communicable diseases, noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) and urgent emergencies. The three areas are connected but require different clinical pathways. Communicable-disease control may involve surveillance, infection prevention, contact management and public-health notification; NCD care often requires long-term risk reduction, screening, treatment continuity and complication prevention; emergencies require rapid assessment, stabilization within competence and timely referral.

**Do not use this parent overview as a treatment protocol.** Specific diagnoses, medications, doses, contraindications, screening thresholds, isolation periods and reporting deadlines must be researched in the relevant current Philippine DOH/program guideline and product information.

## 2. Core community-health approach

- **Prevention and health promotion:** reduce risk factors, improve health literacy and support safe environments.
- **Early recognition:** identify symptoms, red flags, exposure history and high-risk groups.
- **Evidence-based assessment:** use current case definitions, clinical guidance and authorized screening/diagnostic pathways.
- **Treatment within scope:** provide only care authorized by professional scope, valid orders and current protocols.
- **Referral and continuity:** communicate urgency, use the correct receiving service and follow up where feasible.
- **Surveillance and reporting:** use current disease-specific reporting channels and legal requirements.
- **Equity:** identify barriers such as cost, transport, disability, language, stigma and service availability.
- **Evaluation:** monitor reach, quality, safety, outcomes and gaps rather than counting activities alone.

## 3. Communicable diseases: principles

Communicable diseases are caused by infectious agents and may spread through different routes (airborne, droplet, contact, food/water, blood/body fluids, vectors or animals). Prevention and treatment depend on the specific agent, disease, transmission route, local epidemiology and current guidance.

Community-health nursing may include:
- early recognition and appropriate referral;
- standard precautions and disease-specific infection prevention;
- vaccination and other authorized preventive services;
- accurate surveillance/reporting using the current case definition;
- health education tailored to the transmission route;
- contact/follow-up measures where indicated by official guidance;
- safe food, water, sanitation and vector-control coordination;
- treatment adherence support and referral follow-up;
- protection against stigma and unnecessary disclosure.

**Important:** do not assume all infectious diseases require the same isolation, testing, treatment or reporting. A suspected case may require urgent care and public-health notification even before final confirmation, depending on the current protocol.

## 4. Noncommunicable diseases (NCDs): principles

Major NCD groups include cardiovascular diseases, cancers, chronic respiratory diseases and diabetes. Other chronic conditions and risk factors may also be addressed by current national programs. Risk is influenced by tobacco use, unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, harmful alcohol use, environmental exposures, genetics, age and social determinants.

Community-health nursing can support:
- evidence-based risk assessment and health promotion;
- screening according to current eligibility and protocol;
- referral for confirmatory diagnosis after abnormal screening;
- continuity and self-management support for diagnosed conditions;
- medication adherence support without independently changing prescriptions;
- complication prevention and monitoring;
- rehabilitation and psychosocial support;
- reduction of access barriers and coordination with primary-care teams.

Screening is not diagnosis. An abnormal result needs appropriate confirmation, communication and follow-up. Do not start or change medicines based only on a screening result or this overview.

## 5. Emergency services: universal safety rule

Emergency services are needed when a person's condition threatens life, function or rapid deterioration. Examples include severe breathing difficulty, shock, altered consciousness, uncontrolled severe bleeding, suspected stroke/acute coronary event, severe dehydration, seizures, anaphylaxis, major trauma, severe pregnancy/newborn danger signs or suspected poisoning.

**Prioritize immediate danger over routine program tasks.**
1. Recognize red flags and rapidly assess within training.
2. Activate the appropriate emergency response/referral pathway.
3. Provide immediate care within competence and current protocol.
4. Communicate key information and urgency to the receiving team.
5. Arrange safe transport/monitoring according to local procedure.
6. Document and follow up when safe; do not delay lifesaving care for routine paperwork.

The specific assessment sequence and interventions depend on training, facility capability and current emergency protocols. Do not use a mnemonic as a substitute for clinical assessment.

## 6. Medication and treatment safety

For communicable diseases, NCDs and emergencies:
- verify the current disease-specific guideline, authorized order and product information;
- confirm patient identity, indication, allergy history, age/pregnancy/organ-function considerations as relevant, contraindications/precautions, correct product/dose/route/time and monitoring;
- avoid unnecessary antibiotics and follow antimicrobial-stewardship principles;
- do not stop or alter long-term medication without an authorized plan;
- use current vaccine/medication contraindication guidance rather than memorized generic lists;
- check interactions and follow-up requirements using an authoritative current source;
- document adverse reactions and report through the authorized pathway;
- do not administer an expired, improperly stored or unidentified product.

This overview intentionally includes no doses or regimens. These must be researched in the relevant assigned disease/NCD/emergency topic using current authoritative sources.

## 7. Community-health nursing workflow

1. **Assess:** symptoms/exposure/risk, relevant history, red flags, access barriers and data limitations.
2. **Prioritize:** immediate danger and rapid deterioration first; then time-sensitive infectious/NCD concerns and high-risk follow-up; then routine prevention.
3. **Plan:** define intervention, objective, responsible person, supplies, referral route and follow-up.
4. **Implement:** follow current guidance, consent/privacy, infection prevention and scope.
5. **Coordinate:** involve laboratories, surveillance, primary-care team, hospitals, environmental services, social welfare or other sectors as relevant.
6. **Document:** separate observed facts, reported information and interpretation; use authorized channels.
7. **Evaluate:** track clinical follow-up, preventive coverage, outcomes, safety and equity.
8. **Revise:** update plan when new information or risk emerges.

## 8. Philippine legal and program context

- **RA 11332 (2019), Mandatory Reporting of Notifiable Diseases and Health Events of Public Health Concern Act:** provides the legal framework for reporting notifiable diseases and public-health events; exact conditions, timelines and procedures must be checked in current DOH guidance and implementing rules. [1]
- **RA 11223 (2019), Universal Health Care Act:** provides broader system context for integrated services and primary-care navigation; exact benefits and workflows depend on current implementing rules. [2]
- **DOH disease-specific manuals and NCD program guidance:** use current official manuals for case definitions, screening eligibility, referral, treatment, reporting and follow-up.
- **LGU/health-facility emergency pathways:** confirm local emergency numbers, receiving facilities, ambulance/transport arrangements and referral protocols.
- **Privacy:** protect identifiable data under RA 10173 and applicable facility rules while following lawful public-health reporting obligations.

## 9. Monitoring and evaluation

Possible indicators include timely referral, follow-up completion, prevention/screening coverage among eligible populations, treatment continuity, infection-prevention adherence, notification timeliness, medication/supply availability, adverse events and equity of access. Use the current program definition and denominator.

A rising case count can reflect increased disease, improved detection or reporting changes. A low NCD screening count may reflect access barriers rather than low need. A referral issued is not proof of care received. Document uncertainty and do not fabricate missing values.

## 11. PNLE high-yield distinctions

- Communicable-disease management requires disease-specific transmission prevention, treatment and surveillance.
- NCD care often requires long-term risk reduction, screening confirmation, continuity and complication prevention.
- Screening is not diagnosis; positive findings need a safe follow-up pathway.
- Emergency danger and deterioration override routine community-program tasks.
- Use current protocols for medicines, contraindications, thresholds and reporting.
- RA 11332 concerns mandatory reporting of notifiable diseases/public-health events; RA 11223 is the UHC Act.
- Do not infer cause from a trend without checking case definitions, denominators and reporting changes.
- Referral requires communication and follow-up, not merely a form.
