## 1. Why community health depends on a team

Community health nursing (CHN) works through a network of people and organizations, not the nurse alone. PHC requires integrated services, multisectoral action and empowered individuals/communities. Different actors contribute clinical care, prevention, surveillance, health education, environmental services, social support, financing, regulation and local accountability. [1]

Roles differ by law, professional scope, training, employment, local organization and current program guidance. A community-health worker, barangay health worker (BHW), midwife, public-health nurse, physician, sanitary inspector, social worker, local official and community leader are not interchangeable. Coordination should clarify who is responsible, who is authorized to perform each task, how referral works and how information is shared safely.

## 2. Key people and organizations

| Actor | Typical contribution | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Community/public-health nurse** | Population assessment, planning, health promotion, prevention, nursing care within scope, surveillance/reporting, referral, supervision/coordination and evaluation. | Must follow the Philippine Nursing Act, current PRC/BON rules, facility policy and authorized protocols. |
| **Midwife** | Maternal/newborn/reproductive-health services within licensure and authorized setting, health teaching, assessment and referral. | Scope and facility privileges must be verified; emergencies beyond capability require escalation/referral. |
| **Physician/other authorized clinician** | Diagnosis, clinical management and procedures within professional scope; consult/referral support. | Role depends on license, specialty, setting and applicable rules. |
| **Barangay Health Worker (BHW)** | Community outreach, basic health education, linking households to services, follow-up and defined program support after appropriate training/authorization. | A BHW is not automatically a licensed nurse or prescriber; must not independently diagnose, prescribe or perform restricted procedures outside authorization. |
| **Sanitary inspector/environmental-health officer** | Inspection and assessment of sanitation/environmental hazards under applicable authority; coordination on water, food, waste and environmental risks. | Technical certification/enforcement duties depend on appointment, training, law and local rules. |
| **Local health officer/RHU/health center team** | Local service delivery, program coordination, referral, surveillance and community-health operations. | Exact staffing, responsibilities and service package vary by LGU and current rules. |
| **Local Government Unit (LGU)/barangay** | Local planning, funding/resource coordination, community facilities, sanitation and other local public services within legal responsibilities. | Specific duties depend on law, devolution arrangements, local ordinances and current program agreements. |
| **Department of Health (DOH)** | National policy, technical guidance, public-health programs, regulation/standards and coordination within its mandate. | Use current official issuances; a national policy does not guarantee identical local implementation. |
| **PhilHealth** | National health insurance and purchasing/benefit arrangements under current rules. | Benefit eligibility, provider requirements and claims rules must be checked in current official guidance. |
| **Social workers/DSWD and local social welfare offices** | Social assessment, protection services, assistance/referral and support for vulnerable households within mandate. | Confidentiality, consent and legal safeguarding/reporting rules apply. |
| **Schools/teachers** | School health promotion, child/adolescent support, education and referral coordination. | Teachers do not replace clinicians; student privacy and safeguarding rules apply. |
| **Community leaders, people's organizations and civil society** | Identify local priorities/assets, facilitate engagement, support collective action and provide feedback. | Leaders do not automatically represent every resident or override individual consent/confidentiality. |
| **Families/caregivers and individuals** | Shared decisions, self-care where appropriate, prevention, caregiving and feedback. | Participation should be voluntary and respectful; do not blame people for structural access barriers. |
| **Laboratories, hospitals and referral facilities** | Diagnostic confirmation, emergency/specialist treatment, inpatient care and feedback to referring teams. | Referral pathways, acceptance criteria and communication procedures must be confirmed locally. |

This table gives common functions, not a complete legal job description. A person's exact duties must be verified from current law, professional rules, employer policy and program authorization.

## 3. Barangay Health Workers (BHWs)

BHWs help connect households and local health services. Their contribution can include outreach, basic health information, follow-up reminders, identifying barriers, supporting approved campaigns and directing people to the health center. Their local training and authorization determine the tasks they may perform.

**RA 7883, Barangay Health Workers' Benefits and Incentives Act of 1995**, recognizes and provides a framework for accredited BHWs and their benefits/incentives. It should not be read as granting every BHW the professional scope of a registered nurse, midwife or physician. Current accreditation, training, benefits and local implementation details must be checked in the statute and applicable rules. [2]

### Nurse–BHW collaboration
- Define the task and limits before assigning work.
- Provide training, supervision, job aids and a clear escalation route.
- Avoid assigning clinical tasks beyond training or authorization.
- Protect household information and clarify how records are collected/stored.
- Encourage BHWs to report concerns rather than guess a diagnosis.
- Provide feedback and recognize workload, safety and resource constraints.
- Confirm referral completion where feasible without violating privacy.

## 4. Roles across the community-health cycle

### Assessment
- Nurses and health teams review data and assess community needs/assets.
- BHWs may support household outreach and identify access barriers within their role.
- Residents/community organizations explain local priorities, practices and constraints.
- Environmental-health personnel assess hazards requiring technical expertise.
- Data and reports must be checked for completeness, validity and confidentiality.

### Planning
- Set measurable objectives with community input.
- Assign clear accountable owners and support roles.
- Check staffing, supplies, transport, funding, referral capacity and safety controls.
- Coordinate across health, education, sanitation, social welfare and other sectors where determinants overlap.
- Explain what decisions are open to community influence and what is constrained by law/safety.

### Implementation
- Licensed professionals perform clinical duties within scope.
- Trained community workers support authorized outreach and linkage.
- Facilities provide the services they are licensed/equipped to deliver.
- Local authorities and service providers address infrastructure/environmental responsibilities.
- Community members participate without coercion or exposure to unsafe work.

### Referral and continuity
- Identify urgency and receiving-service capability.
- Communicate necessary information securely.
- Explain transport, follow-up and return precautions.
- Confirm handover/referral completion where feasible.
- Escalate failed referrals or service gaps rather than blaming the patient.

### Evaluation
- Compare process, output, outcome and equity indicators with baseline/targets.
- Gather community feedback and identify groups not reached.
- Document data limitations and unintended effects.
- Adjust plans and resources based on evidence.
- Do not use attendance counts alone as proof of improved health.

## 5. Team communication and accountability

Effective teamwork requires:
- a shared objective and defined population;
- clear roles, authority and escalation thresholds;
- timely, accurate and respectful communication;
- secure documentation and handover;
- current protocols and access to supervision;
- reliable supplies, staffing and referral arrangements;
- mechanisms for feedback, incident reporting and improvement;
- transparent accountability for decisions and follow-up.

When responsibilities overlap, clarify the lead person/agency and the referral point. Avoid informal assumptions such as “the barangay will handle it” without confirming a responsible service and action.

## 6. Ethics, privacy and safety

- Share only information needed for the legitimate care/public-health purpose through authorized channels.
- Respect consent, autonomy, confidentiality and lawful reporting requirements.
- Do not share identifiable patient details in public community meetings or informal group chats.
- Do not ask community volunteers to handle sewage, sharps, unknown chemicals or hazardous waste without proper training, PPE and authorization.
- Do not assign diagnosis, prescribing or restricted procedures to a worker who lacks authority.
- Do not let a leader override an individual's right to refuse or control personal health information.
- Protect staff and volunteers from unsafe visits or confrontation; use the authorized response when violence/threats are possible.
- Escalate emergencies and urgent danger signs promptly; routine reporting must not delay care.

## 7. Philippine policy context

- **RA 7883:** Barangay Health Workers' Benefits and Incentives Act of 1995; relevant to accredited BHWs and the statutory framework for their benefits/incentives. [2]
- **RA 7160, Local Government Code of 1991:** establishes local-government structure and devolved/local responsibilities; exact health and sanitation responsibilities must be checked in the relevant provisions and later rules. [3]
- **RA 11223, Universal Health Care Act:** provides broader system context for integrated services, primary-care navigation and UHC implementation; specific provider duties/benefits require current implementing rules. [4]
- **Professional regulation:** nurses, midwives, physicians and other licensed professions must follow their applicable professional statutes, PRC/Board rules, facility policies and scope.
- **Local ordinances/program rules:** service workflows, reporting channels and environmental-health responsibilities can vary by LGU; verify current local guidance.

## 8. PNLE high-yield distinctions

- CHN is team-based and population-oriented; no single worker performs every role.
- BHWs support outreach/linkage but are not automatically licensed nurses or prescribers.
- Nurses remain accountable for work delegated within the applicable professional rules.
- LGU, DOH, PhilHealth, clinical providers and community organizations have different mandates.
- Community participation means meaningful influence, not merely attendance or unpaid labor.
- Referral requires a receiving service, clear communication and follow-up—not only issuing a form.
- Exact roles vary by law, credential, training, facility and current program guidance.
- Confidentiality, consent, safe scope and emergency escalation apply across the team.
