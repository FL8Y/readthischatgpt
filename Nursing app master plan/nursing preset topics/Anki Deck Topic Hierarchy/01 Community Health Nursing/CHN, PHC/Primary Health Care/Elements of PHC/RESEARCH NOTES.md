## 1. What “elements of PHC” means

In classic community-health nursing teaching, the **eight essential elements of primary health care** come from paragraph VII.3 of the 1978 Declaration of Alma-Ata. They describe important service areas that PHC should include, while the wider declaration also emphasizes equity, community participation, multisectoral action, appropriate technology, referral systems and trained health workers. The eight elements are:

1. Education concerning prevailing health problems and methods of preventing and controlling them.
2. Promotion of food supply and proper nutrition.
3. Adequate supply of safe water and basic sanitation.
4. Maternal and child health care, including family planning.
5. Immunization against major infectious diseases.
6. Prevention and control of locally endemic diseases.
7. Appropriate treatment of common diseases and injuries.
8. Provision of essential drugs. [1][2]

**Important distinction:** the classic eight-item list is not identical to the modern WHO–UNICEF PHC framework. Modern PHC is described through three connected components—integrated health services with primary care and essential public-health functions, multisectoral policy/action, and empowered people/communities—and 14 system levers to help implement them. The eight elements describe classic service content; the modern components and levers explain the broader approach and the system conditions needed to sustain it. [3][4]

## 2. The eight elements in practice

### Element 1 — Health education about prevailing health problems

**Purpose:** Help people understand priority health risks, prevention, early recognition, available services and when to seek help. Education should respond to locally identified needs and be understandable, culturally appropriate and actionable.

**Examples:** hand hygiene and safe food handling; maternal danger signs; immunization information; chronic-disease risk reduction; tobacco cessation; prevention of locally prevalent infections; appropriate use of health services.

**Community-health nursing responsibilities**
- Assess what people already know, their language/literacy needs, beliefs, misinformation and practical barriers.
- Set a specific learning objective rather than simply delivering a lecture.
- Use clear language, demonstration, teach-back and accessible formats as appropriate.
- Invite questions and respect informed choices; do not shame or coerce.
- Evaluate comprehension and feasible behavior/service outcomes, not just attendance.

**Safety and limits:** Health education cannot replace clinical assessment or structural action. If unsafe water is the cause of repeated illness, education about hygiene alone is inadequate. For red-flag symptoms, provide clear urgent-care instructions and activate referral/emergency pathways rather than continuing a teaching session.

### Element 2 — Food supply and proper nutrition

**Purpose:** Support food security, adequate nutrition and prevention or management of nutrition-related problems across the life course.

**Relevant concerns:** undernutrition, micronutrient deficiencies, food insecurity, growth faltering, unhealthy dietary patterns and diet-related chronic disease. The appropriate response depends on age, pregnancy/lactation, illness, cultural context, household resources and locally available foods.

**Community-health nursing responsibilities**
- Assess growth and nutrition using the applicable age- and population-specific tools; interpret measurements against the correct standard rather than guessing.
- Ask about food availability, feeding practices, illness, swallowing difficulties, medications and social barriers where relevant.
- Provide practical, culturally acceptable advice grounded in current national guidance.
- Promote appropriate maternal, infant and young-child nutrition services and referral for growth faltering, severe malnutrition or other red flags according to current protocols.
- Coordinate with nutrition services, social welfare, agriculture, schools and local food-security programs where appropriate.
- Monitor follow-up and avoid stigmatizing families experiencing poverty or food insecurity.

**Safety:** Do not recommend restrictive diets, supplements or therapeutic feeding regimens without considering clinical status and authoritative guidance. Severe acute malnutrition, dehydration, feeding intolerance or other danger signs require timely assessment and protocol-based management. Nutritional advice must be adapted to relevant medical conditions and life stages.

### Element 3 — Safe water and basic sanitation

**Purpose:** Reduce exposure to waterborne and sanitation-related hazards and support a healthy living environment.

**Examples:** safe drinking-water access, sanitation facilities, hygienic disposal of human waste, handwashing facilities, food hygiene and locally appropriate environmental-health measures.

**Community-health nursing responsibilities**
- Assess household/community water sources, storage and handling, sanitation access, waste disposal and hygiene barriers.
- Recognize clusters of gastrointestinal illness and follow the current surveillance/escalation pathway if an outbreak is suspected.
- Teach locally approved safe-water handling, hand hygiene and food safety; make recommendations realistic for available resources.
- Coordinate with local government and the responsible water, sanitation and environmental-health authorities when infrastructure or water-quality issues exceed nursing control.
- Protect privacy and communicate risks without stigmatizing affected households.
- Monitor illness trends and whether environmental actions actually occurred.

**Safety:** A health worker should not declare water safe solely because it looks clear or tastes normal. Use the responsible authority's testing and advisories. Severe dehydration, blood in stool, altered consciousness or other danger signs need prompt clinical assessment. Education alone cannot substitute for a safe water supply or functioning sanitation infrastructure.

### Element 4 — Maternal and child health care, including family planning

**Purpose:** Improve health and continuity of care for pregnant and postpartum people, newborns, infants, children and families, including access to informed reproductive choices.

**Potential service areas:** preconception and antenatal care; recognition of pregnancy danger signs; skilled childbirth and emergency referral; postpartum and newborn care; growth/development monitoring; breastfeeding and infant-feeding support; childhood illness prevention; reproductive-health counseling and voluntary family planning.

**Community-health nursing responsibilities**
- Identify people who need timely antenatal, postpartum, newborn, pediatric or reproductive-health services and facilitate access.
- Recognize urgent danger signs and arrange prompt referral under current maternal/newborn protocols.
- Provide accurate, non-coercive counseling that respects informed choice, privacy, cultural context and applicable law.
- Explain the importance of follow-up, immunization and age-appropriate preventive care according to current national guidance.
- Identify access barriers such as distance, cost, transport, disability, safety and lack of support.
- Coordinate with skilled maternity, newborn, pediatric and reproductive-health services; do not assume a community-level service can safely manage every complication.

**Safety:** Suspected ectopic pregnancy, heavy bleeding, seizures, severe headache/visual changes in pregnancy or postpartum, severe breathing difficulty, a newborn with poor feeding or lethargy, and other locally defined maternal/newborn danger signs require urgent evaluation. Use current protocols rather than a generic list to make treatment decisions.

**Family planning distinction:** counseling should support informed, voluntary choice—not pressure a person to accept or reject a method. Method selection depends on medical eligibility, preferences, potential interactions and current guidance.

### Element 5 — Immunization against major infectious diseases

**Purpose:** Prevent vaccine-preventable disease and reduce complications, disability and death through safe, effective and equitable vaccination services.

**Community-health nursing responsibilities**
- Use the current Philippine DOH immunization schedule and applicable program guidance; verify age, dose, interval, catch-up rules and product-specific requirements before administration.
- Assess eligibility and relevant contraindications/precautions using the current guidance and vaccine product information.
- Maintain the required cold chain, stock control, preparation, administration, documentation and safe waste disposal.
- Explain expected reactions, important warning signs and how to seek help; observe and respond according to the applicable protocol.
- Record doses accurately and use a valid eligible-population denominator when calculating coverage.
- Identify missed groups and barriers, and arrange follow-up without coercion or public disclosure of private health information.
- Follow official procedures for adverse events following immunization (AEFI) and suspected vaccine-preventable disease.

**Safety:** Do not rely on memory for current schedules or assume all vaccines share the same contraindications. A contraindication, precaution and expected mild reaction are not interchangeable. For a suspected severe allergic reaction or another emergency after vaccination, activate emergency response immediately and follow the current protocol. This topic intentionally does not reproduce a potentially outdated schedule.

### Element 6 — Prevention and control of locally endemic diseases

**Purpose:** Reduce diseases that are persistently present or predictably occur in a particular locality. The specific conditions and control measures vary with geography, season, ecology, population movement and changing surveillance data.

**Examples:** locally relevant vector-borne, zoonotic, waterborne, soil-transmitted or other communicable diseases, as identified by current official surveillance and local public-health priorities. Do not assume every example is endemic in every Philippine locality.

**Community-health nursing responsibilities**
- Review current local surveillance and identify unusual clusters, risk groups, seasonal patterns and environmental exposures.
- Use disease-specific current DOH guidance for prevention, screening, case definitions, notification, specimen collection, treatment referral and follow-up.
- Promote relevant measures such as vector-source reduction, environmental management, protective behaviors and timely care-seeking when supported by current guidance.
- Coordinate with surveillance officers, laboratories, environmental-health staff, local government and community partners.
- Use appropriate infection prevention and protect confidentiality.
- Monitor whether interventions reach the groups at greatest risk and whether incidence/coverage indicators are defined consistently.

**Safety:** Suspected outbreaks or legally reportable diseases require timely action through official channels. Do not independently invent a case definition, reporting deadline, isolation rule or drug regimen. Severe illness and rapid deterioration require urgent assessment; public-health investigation must not delay lifesaving care.

### Element 7 — Appropriate treatment of common diseases and injuries

**Purpose:** Make safe, timely assessment and management of common health problems available close to where people live, while ensuring that serious or complex conditions are referred appropriately.

**Community-health nursing responsibilities**
- Perform a focused history and assessment within scope, identify red flags and document findings.
- Distinguish health education, screening, nursing care, protocol-authorized treatment and medical diagnosis; do not imply that a screening result is definitive.
- Provide nursing interventions and treatment only within legal scope, valid orders or authorized protocols.
- Use standard precautions, patient identification, safe equipment handling and appropriate documentation.
- Explain self-care and return precautions using current guidance; arrange follow-up and check referral completion where feasible.
- Escalate when symptoms are severe, atypical, persistent, worsening or beyond local capability.

**Priority rule:** airway compromise, severe respiratory distress, signs of shock, altered consciousness, uncontrolled severe bleeding, suspected stroke or other time-critical emergencies take precedence over routine program work. Use ABCs and clinical acuity as reasoning aids, not as a substitute for assessment.

**Safety:** Do not delay referral to complete paperwork or meet a coverage target. Do not prescribe or alter medication outside scope, order or authorized protocol. A common illness can still be dangerous in a high-risk patient or when red flags appear.

### Element 8 — Essential drugs

**Purpose:** Ensure that appropriate, quality-assured, safe and effective medicines are available and used rationally to meet priority health needs. “Essential” does not mean that every medicine must be stocked at every facility, nor does availability alone ensure safe use.

**Community-health nursing responsibilities**
- Follow the applicable essential-medicines list, formulary, procurement rules and current program protocols.
- Check stock, expiry, storage conditions, product integrity, security and supply continuity.
- Follow medication-administration rights and local verification procedures; check patient identity, indication, allergies, dose, route, timing and documentation as applicable.
- Assess relevant contraindications, precautions, interactions and special-population risks using a reliable current reference and authorized order/protocol.
- Teach the purpose, administration, expected effects, important adverse effects, missed-dose advice and when to seek help using drug-specific guidance.
- Monitor therapeutic response, adverse reactions, adherence barriers and potential medication errors; report/escalate according to policy.
- Promote rational medicine use, antimicrobial stewardship and avoidance of unnecessary or inappropriate treatment.

**Contraindications and high-risk situations:** do not administer a medication when a known absolute contraindication applies or when required safety checks reveal a serious unresolved concern; pause and clarify an unclear order or suspected error according to local policy. Distinguish contraindications from precautions and monitoring needs. Pregnancy, lactation, pediatric/older-adult status, allergy, renal/hepatic impairment and drug interactions may materially change the risk-benefit assessment.

**Safety:** Never guess doses, dilute/infuse instructions, pediatric calculations, storage requirements or antidotes. Verify them against current authoritative drug information and the actual order/protocol. This broad PHC element is not a substitute for topic-specific medication references.

## 3. Cross-cutting features across all eight elements

The elements work as a coordinated approach, not eight isolated services.

- **Equity:** assess who is missed and why; identical outreach may not produce equal access.
- **Community participation:** involve people in defining needs, planning interventions and evaluating acceptability.
- **Intersectoral action:** work with sectors beyond health when determinants such as water, food, housing or transport are involved.
- **Appropriate technology:** use methods that are safe, evidence-informed, acceptable, affordable and maintainable in context.
- **Referral and continuity:** ensure care continues when needs exceed the local service's capacity.
- **Trained health teams:** staff need appropriate competencies, supervision, supplies and support.
- **Monitoring and accountability:** measure reach, quality, outcomes and equity; activity counts alone do not prove impact. [1][2][3]

## 4. Evaluation: how to measure whether an element is working

| Element | Example indicator | Interpretation caution |
|---|---|---|
| Health education | Proportion of target participants who demonstrate a specified skill or correctly explain a key action. | Attendance is not proof of understanding or behavior change. |
| Nutrition | Appropriate age-specific nutrition/growth indicators; referral completion for identified risk. | Use validated tools and standards; account for measurement quality. |
| Water/sanitation | Households with access to services meeting the applicable safety standard; completion of corrective actions. | Visual appearance is not proof of water safety. |
| Maternal/child health | Timely service coverage and follow-up, using defined eligible populations. | A visit count is not equivalent to quality or continuity. |
| Immunization | Valid coverage by eligible population, plus follow-up and data-quality checks. | Dose counts without a reliable denominator can mislead. |
| Endemic-disease control | Disease-specific surveillance trends and intervention coverage. | Trends may be affected by testing, reporting, season and population changes. |
| Common illness/injury care | Timely assessment, appropriate referral completion, follow-up and safety indicators. | More encounters do not automatically mean better health. |
| Essential drugs | Availability of required products, stock-outs, expiry/wastage, and safe-use indicators. | Stock availability alone does not prove correct prescribing or use. |

Define the numerator, denominator, period, data source and responsible person. Stratify by relevant groups where lawful and appropriate, protect personal information, and document missing or uncertain data instead of inventing values.

## 5. Philippine context and implementation

The classic eight elements are a global PHC framework; actual Philippine programs, service packages, professional responsibilities, medicine lists, immunization schedules and reporting requirements are governed by current national and local guidance. Republic Act No. 11223 (Universal Health Care Act) is important to the wider system context, but it does not make every local service or benefit identical in practice. Consult the current statute and implementing rules for legal claims and current DOH/PhilHealth/LGU issuances for operational requirements. [5]

For specific clinical decisions, use the relevant current Philippine disease/program guideline, immunization manual, formulary or drug reference. This research note does not supply exact vaccine schedules, doses, laboratory thresholds, disease-specific regimens or reporting deadlines because those must be verified for the specific intervention and date.

## 6. PNLE high-yield distinctions and traps

- The classic PHC **eight elements** are service areas named in the 1978 Alma-Ata Declaration, paragraph VII.3.
- The wider declaration also describes principles and system requirements; do not reduce all of PHC to the eight-item list.
- Modern WHO–UNICEF PHC framing has **three core components and 14 levers**, not a replacement list of eight.
- Health education should be tailored and evaluated; education alone cannot fix missing infrastructure or urgent clinical problems.
- Nutrition care must use age-/condition-appropriate standards and referral pathways.
- Safe water cannot be established by appearance alone.
- Maternal and child health includes prevention and continuity, but danger signs need urgent referral.
- Vaccine eligibility, schedules, contraindications and cold-chain requirements are product- and program-specific; verify current official guidance.
- Endemic disease control depends on local epidemiology; never assume one disease is endemic everywhere.
- Appropriate treatment includes recognition of limits and referral; screening is not diagnosis.
- Essential medicines require quality, availability, rational use and monitoring—not merely a stocked shelf.
- Immediate threats and deterioration override routine program targets.
- Do not invent doses, contraindications, laws, reporting deadlines or local service entitlements.
