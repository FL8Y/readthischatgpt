# Functions of the Philippine Department of Health (DOH)

**Topic path:** `CHN, PHC/DOH/Functions of DOH`  
**Status:** Substantive research draft — current DOH organizational issuances and implementation details require final verification.  
**Last researched:** 2026-10-17  
**Mandatory standard:** Community Health Nursing `RESEARCH TOPIC GUIDE.md`.

## 1. DOH's national role

The Department of Health (DOH) is the national government agency responsible for health-sector stewardship within its legal mandate: national health policy and planning, standards/guidance, technical support, public-health programs, health information/surveillance, and regulation of designated health facilities/products/services. Exact powers depend on the applicable statute, executive issuance, organizational order and service area.

The DOH's role must be read in the context of **devolution** under RA 7160 (Local Government Code of 1991) and later health-system reforms. Many frontline health services were devolved to local government units (LGUs), while national policy, standards, technical assistance, regulatory and national program functions remained important DOH responsibilities. RA 11223 (Universal Health Care Act) later provided a broader framework for integrated health systems and province-wide/city-wide health systems. These are related but not identical arrangements. [1][2][3]

## 2. Major functions

### A. National policy and stewardship
- Formulate national health policies, strategies, plans and priorities.
- Coordinate national programs and health-sector partners.
- Promote equitable access, quality, safety and system responsiveness.
- Monitor health-system performance and identify gaps.

**Community nursing use:** align local plans with current national priorities while adapting implementation to assessed community needs.

### B. Standards, guidance and technical assistance
- Issue applicable standards, technical guidance and program protocols within DOH authority.
- Provide training, supervision, capacity-building and technical assistance to LGUs/facilities.
- Support quality improvement and implementation of national programs.

Use the current applicable issuance, not an old textbook summary, when determining clinical/program procedure. Technical assistance does not mean the national DOH directly delivers every local service.

### C. Regulation and quality/safety oversight
- Regulate covered health facilities and health-related products/services under its legal mandate, through designated offices/agencies.
- Establish or implement licensing, safety and quality requirements.
- Monitor compliance and respond through authorized regulatory processes.

**Do not confuse regulatory roles:** nurse registration/licensure is primarily under the PRC and Board of Nursing under RA 9173, not a generic DOH function. DOH facility licensing, FDA product regulation and PhilHealth accreditation are distinct processes. [4]

### D. Public-health programs and disease prevention/control
- Lead or coordinate national programs such as relevant immunization, maternal/child health, nutrition, communicable-disease control, noncommunicable-disease prevention and health promotion.
- Support disease surveillance, outbreak response and risk communication.
- Coordinate with LGUs and other agencies during public-health threats.

Specific eligibility, schedules, clinical protocols and reporting deadlines must come from the current manual/issuance for the particular program.

### E. Health information, epidemiology and surveillance
- Support health information, monitoring, epidemiology and surveillance functions.
- Use data to monitor trends, detect threats, plan services and evaluate programs.
- Coordinate reporting and response to notifiable diseases/public-health events under applicable law.

Nurses must use current case definitions and reporting pathways, document accurately and protect confidentiality.

### F. Health-system integration and financing coordination
- Support implementation of UHC and integration across primary care, public-health functions and referral levels.
- Coordinate with LGUs, PhilHealth, other agencies and health providers within their respective legal mandates.

PhilHealth has its own statutory health-insurance/purchasing/accreditation role; it is not simply a DOH division.

### G. Direct services in designated settings
The DOH and its designated/attached facilities may directly provide services in particular hospitals, specialty centers, laboratories or national programs. This does not mean every barangay health station or rural health unit is directly operated by the national DOH. Governance, ownership, staffing and operational responsibility vary by facility and local system.

## 3. DOH vs LGU vs PhilHealth vs PRC/BON

| Institution | Broad role | Common confusion |
|---|---|---|
| **National DOH** | National stewardship, standards, technical support, public-health programs, surveillance and regulation within its mandate. | Assuming it directly operates every local health center. |
| **LGUs/local health offices** | Local governance and delivery of devolved/local health services, implementation of national programs and local planning under applicable law/UHC arrangements. | Assuming local implementation can ignore national laws and standards. |
| **PhilHealth** | Health insurance, purchasing and provider accreditation under its own law/rules. | Treating accreditation as identical to DOH licensing or program policy. |
| **PRC/Board of Nursing** | Professional nursing registration, licensure and regulation under RA 9173. | Assuming DOH is the sole authority for nursing licensure/scope. |
| **FDA and other designated regulators** | Regulation of health products and other matters assigned by law. | Treating every medicine/device issue as a generic local-health-office function. |
| **PSA and statistical agencies** | Census, civil registration and official population statistics within their mandate. | Treating all vital statistics as DOH-owned or mixing census and service-report denominators. |

Exact responsibilities may change with legislation and current issuances. For a legal/administrative question, identify the service, facility, period and responsible authority before answering.

## 4. Devolution and UHC: historical distinction

RA 7160 devolved many frontline services to LGUs. Executive Order No. 102 (1999) reorganized the DOH after devolution and emphasized national leadership, policy, standards, regulation and technical assistance. RA 11223 (2019) established a broader framework for integrated health systems, including province-wide/city-wide health systems and stronger coordination across levels. Do not treat UHC as a simple reversal of devolution or use a pre-UHC organization chart as current without checking current rules. [1][2][3]

For PNLE questions, determine whether the item asks about national policy/stewardship, local service delivery, facility regulation, professional licensure or health insurance/purchasing.

## 5. Community-health nurse interaction with DOH systems

A community nurse may:
- implement current national program guidance within scope and local policy;
- submit authorized health data and surveillance reports;
- participate in immunization, MCH, nutrition, disease-control and health-promotion programs;
- refer clients to the appropriate level of care;
- report public-health events and safety concerns through authorized channels;
- use approved education materials and clinical protocols;
- participate in training, supervision and quality improvement; and
- communicate local access/resource barriers to local and national planners.

Confirm whether an instruction comes from a national DOH issuance, regional DOH office, LGU/local health office, facility policy or another authorized agency. Verify version/date and applicability.

## 6. Emergency and public-health response

During outbreaks, disasters or public-health emergencies, national and local roles must coordinate. Depending on the event and applicable law, DOH may issue technical guidance, coordinate national response, support surveillance/laboratories and work with LGUs/other agencies. Local teams may provide immediate care, reporting, risk communication and operational response.

Nursing priorities:
1. Protect immediate patient/community safety.
2. Follow the current event-specific protocol.
3. Notify the authorized public-health contact when required.
4. Document accurate information and distinguish verified facts from hypotheses.
5. Protect identifiable data and avoid public disclosure of case details.
6. Coordinate referral and follow-up.
7. Report resource gaps through the correct channel.

Do not invent a notification deadline or assume all conditions use the same pathway.

## 7. Scope, authority and safety

- A DOH administrative issuance should be read alongside the governing statute and amendments.
- A national manual does not authorize a nurse to act outside professional scope, training or an authorized protocol.
- Facility licensing, product regulation, professional licensure, insurance accreditation and disease reporting are different functions.
- Local adaptation must remain consistent with applicable law and national standards.
- When sources conflict, compare authority, publication date, scope and whether one supersedes another; seek official clarification when necessary.

## 8. PNLE high-yield distinctions

- DOH: national health policy/stewardship, standards, public-health programs, technical support, surveillance and regulation within its mandate.
- LGUs have important local/devolved service responsibilities; DOH does not directly manage every local facility.
- RA 7160 relates to devolution; EO 102 (1999) reorganized DOH after devolution; RA 11223 is the UHC framework.
- DOH facility licensing differs from PRC/BON nursing licensure and PhilHealth accreditation.
- PhilHealth has its own insurance/purchasing role.
- Use the current DOH issuance for disease/program schedules, clinical protocols and reporting rules.
- Nurses implement policy within scope, report through authorized pathways and protect confidentiality.
