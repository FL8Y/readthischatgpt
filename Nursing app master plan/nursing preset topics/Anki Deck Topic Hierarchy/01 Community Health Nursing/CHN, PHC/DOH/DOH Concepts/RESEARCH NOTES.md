## 1. What “DOH concepts” means in this branch

This topic covers the key terms needed to understand the Philippine Department of Health (DOH) and its place in the health system: national stewardship, devolution, regulation, technical assistance, public-health programs, surveillance, health-system integration and relationships with LGUs/other agencies.

The DOH is the national government agency responsible for health-sector stewardship within its legal mandate. Its exact functions and organization are determined by law and current executive/administrative issuances. Do not rely on an old organizational chart, remembered mission statement or generic lecture slide as proof of the current structure.

## 2. Core concepts and distinctions

### Stewardship
Leadership and oversight of the health system: setting direction, policies and standards, coordinating partners, monitoring performance and promoting equity/quality. Stewardship does not mean the national agency directly delivers every service.

### Devolution
Transfer of specified functions/responsibilities from national government to local governments under law. RA 7160 (Local Government Code of 1991) is central to the Philippine health-sector devolution context. Local implementation remains subject to applicable national law and standards. [1]

### Technical assistance
Support provided to LGUs/facilities through training, guidance, supervision, capacity-building, tools and problem-solving. It is distinct from direct operation of a local facility.

### Regulation
Establishing/enforcing requirements for matters within the agency's legal mandate, such as covered facilities or products. Facility licensing, product regulation, professional licensure and insurance accreditation are different processes with different authorities.

### Public-health program
An organized set of interventions addressing a defined population/health issue, with target groups, eligibility, service package, responsibilities, resources, monitoring and evaluation. Program details change; use the current DOH manual/issuance.

### Surveillance and health information
Ongoing collection, analysis, interpretation and use of data to detect patterns/threats and guide action. Surveillance definitions, case reporting and vital statistics are related but not interchangeable; use the correct legal reporting route and denominator.

### Health-system integration
Coordination across primary care, public-health functions, referral levels, local health systems and other providers. RA 11223 (Universal Health Care Act, 2019) provides a broader framework for integrated systems and province-wide/city-wide health-system arrangements. It should not be described as a simple reversal of devolution. [2]

### National vs local implementation
National DOH may set policy/standards and coordinate programs; LGUs/local health offices and facilities carry out many local services. The exact division depends on the service, current rules and local system.

## 3. DOH and the broader Philippine health-system map

| Actor | Core concept | Do not confuse with |
|---|---|---|
| **National DOH** | National health stewardship, standards, public-health programs, technical assistance and regulation within mandate. | Local government ownership/operation of every facility. |
| **DOH regional offices/Centers for Health Development** | Regional implementation/support/coordination under current DOH organization. | A separate local government authority; exact names/functions require current organization documents. |
| **LGUs/local health offices** | Local governance and delivery of devolved/local health services and national-program implementation. | Freedom to disregard national law or applicable standards. |
| **PhilHealth** | National health insurance, purchasing and provider accreditation under its own legal framework. | DOH facility licensing or public-health policy. |
| **PRC/Board of Nursing** | Nursing professional registration/licensure/regulation under RA 9173. | DOH facility licensing. |
| **FDA and designated regulators** | Regulation of health products and matters assigned by law. | General community-health program implementation. |
| **PSA/statistical agencies** | Census, civil registration and official statistics within their mandate. | All health-service reporting or disease surveillance. |

The exact current structure, attached agencies and office names must be verified from the current DOH organizational directory and legal issuances before being memorized as an exam fact.

## 4. Devolution, EO 102 and UHC

- **RA 7160 (1991):** provides the Local Government Code framework for devolution and local government responsibilities.
- **EO 102 (1999):** reorganized DOH after devolution and redirected functions/operations, emphasizing national leadership, policy, standards, regulation and technical support.
- **RA 11223 (2019):** provides the Universal Health Care framework, including integrated health systems and stronger coordination across levels.

These are different legal/administrative stages. A historical textbook may describe the post-devolution structure, while current UHC implementation adds integration mechanisms. Check the date and scope of any organizational claim. [1][2][3]

## 5. How nurses use DOH concepts in practice

- Identify the current policy/protocol that governs a clinical or public-health activity.
- Check which agency/office issued it and the population/facility/service it covers.
- Use national guidance with local assessment, resource availability and referral arrangements.
- Report notifiable diseases/events through the authorized current route.
- Submit required health information accurately and protect privacy.
- Seek technical assistance or escalate resource gaps through the correct local/regional/national channel.
- Distinguish a recommendation, a legal requirement, a facility policy and an informal teaching mnemonic.
- Avoid acting outside nursing scope because a program document describes an intervention.
- Check current facility capacity before making a referral or promising a service.

## 6. Common terminology traps

- **DOH vs LGU:** national stewardship is not the same as local service delivery.
- **Policy vs program protocol:** a broad policy may not contain all clinical steps; consult the current operational manual.
- **Licensing vs accreditation:** DOH facility licensing and PhilHealth provider accreditation are distinct.
- **Professional vs facility regulation:** PRC/BON regulates nursing licensure; DOH regulates designated facilities/services within its mandate.
- **Primary care vs PHC:** primary care is a central service-delivery component; PHC is broader and includes public-health action, multisectoral work and community empowerment.
- **Surveillance vs vital statistics:** disease/event surveillance and civil-registration statistics have different definitions and systems.
- **Historical vs current organization:** a pre-UHC chart or old mission/vision statement may be outdated.
- **National vs regional/local instructions:** check authority, scope, date and amendments.

## 7. PNLE high-yield points

- DOH is the national health agency; LGUs have important devolved/local responsibilities.
- RA 7160 is central to devolution; EO 102 (1999) restructured DOH after devolution; RA 11223 provides the UHC framework.
- DOH stewardship includes policy/standards, national programs, technical support, surveillance and regulation within mandate.
- PhilHealth accreditation, DOH facility licensing and PRC/BON nursing licensure are not interchangeable.
- Current program schedules, eligibility, contraindications and reporting rules come from current topic-specific guidance.
- Nurses use DOH guidance within professional scope and document/report through authorized channels.
- Organizational charts, attached-agency lists and mission/vision wording require current-source verification.
