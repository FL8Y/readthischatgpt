# Evaluation in the Family Nursing Process

**Topic path:** `CHN, PHC/Family ADPIE/Evaluation`  
**Status:** Substantive research draft — course-specific terminology and local indicator protocols require final verification.  
**Last researched:** 2026-10-17  
**Mandatory standard:** Community Health Nursing `RESEARCH TOPIC GUIDE.md`.

## 1. Purpose

Evaluation determines whether planned family-nursing actions were implemented safely and whether the intended goals/outcomes were achieved. It compares evidence with the baseline, objectives and indicators agreed during planning. Evaluation is not simply checking whether a visit or teaching session occurred; it asks whether the intervention was appropriate, useful, equitable and safe.

The nursing process is iterative. Evaluation may confirm progress, reveal new needs, show that an intervention was ineffective, or require reassessment and a revised plan. PHC measurement also emphasizes monitoring, quality improvement, community feedback and equity. [1][2]

## 2. What should be evaluated?

- **Structure/resources:** were staff, supplies, time, equipment and referral services available?
- **Process:** were planned activities delivered as intended and within scope?
- **Outputs:** what services/actions were completed?
- **Outcomes:** did knowledge, behavior, access, health status or risk change?
- **Impact:** did longer-term population/family health change?
- **Equity:** who benefited, who was missed and whether barriers differ across groups?
- **Safety/unintended effects:** did the intervention create harm, burden, stigma, privacy risk or another adverse effect?
- **Experience/acceptability:** did the family find the plan respectful, feasible and aligned with priorities?

These levels are related but not interchangeable. A completed teaching session is an output, not proof of improved health. A change in health status may have several causes and cannot automatically be attributed to one nursing intervention.

## 3. Set measurable objectives and indicators

A useful objective identifies the expected change, target population, measurement and time frame. Indicators should be relevant, clearly defined, feasible to measure and aligned with the goal.

For every indicator, specify:
- **Numerator:** what is counted.
- **Denominator:** the eligible population or total to which it relates.
- **Time period:** when the measure applies.
- **Data source:** observation, record, report, measurement or family feedback.
- **Collection method/frequency:** how and when it is measured.
- **Responsible person:** who collects/reviews it.
- **Target/baseline:** what constitutes progress and what the starting point was.
- **Limitations:** missing data, measurement error or factors outside the intervention's control.

Do not invent a target after seeing results or change the denominator silently to make a program look successful.

## 4. Evaluation levels with examples

| Level | Question | Example in family nursing | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inputs/structure** | Were necessary resources available? | Nurse, BP cuff, referral contact and educational material available. | Resource availability does not prove quality. |
| **Process** | Was the action delivered as planned? | BP measured correctly; family teaching completed; referral discussed. | Count quality and fidelity, not attendance alone. |
| **Output** | What immediate service/action occurred? | Number of follow-up visits or referrals completed. | Outputs do not necessarily mean health improved. |
| **Outcome** | Did the intended change occur? | Improved understanding, follow-up attendance or better BP control. | Use a valid measure and appropriate time frame. |
| **Impact** | Did longer-term health change? | Reduced complications or improved population-level control over time. | Multiple factors contribute; causal attribution requires stronger evaluation. |
| **Equity** | Who benefited or was missed? | Compare follow-up completion by location/access barrier where lawful and appropriate. | Protect privacy and use valid denominators. |
| **Safety** | Were harms/risks identified and managed? | No unsafe medication change; referral for red flags completed. | A low incident count can reflect underreporting. |

## 5. Family-centred evaluation

Evaluation should include the family's perspective, not only the nurse's judgment:
- Did the family understand the agreed plan?
- Was the action acceptable, safe and feasible with available resources?
- What barriers prevented completion?
- Were goals still meaningful to the family?
- Did the intervention create burden, conflict, stigma or privacy risk?
- What strengths or supports helped?
- Does the family want to continue, modify or stop a nonessential intervention?

Respect autonomy and informed refusal, subject to applicable legal/safeguarding/public-health duties. A plan should not be judged a failure simply because a family cannot access an unavailable service or afford transport.

## 6. Evaluation methods

Select methods appropriate to the goal:
- **Repeat measurement:** e.g., a correctly performed clinical measurement using the same suitable method.
- **Record review:** service/referral/immunization or follow-up documentation under authorized access.
- **Observation/return demonstration:** assess a skill rather than assume it from verbal agreement.
- **Teach-back:** ask the person to explain the plan in their own words.
- **Interview/feedback:** explore acceptability, understanding and barriers.
- **Indicator comparison:** compare baseline with follow-up using the same definition/denominator where appropriate.
- **Audit/quality review:** examine adherence, data quality, safety and corrective action.
- **Referral tracking:** confirm whether the receiving service was accessed and what follow-up is needed.

Do not use a method that exposes the family to unnecessary risk or collects more sensitive information than needed.

## 7. Interpreting results fairly

- **No change:** reassess whether the intervention was appropriate, enough time elapsed, measurement was valid and barriers remain.
- **Partial progress:** identify what helped and what needs modification; do not discard all progress.
- **Unexpected worsening:** assess urgent safety, adverse effects, disease progression, new exposures and need for referral.
- **High activity but poor outcome:** review intervention quality, access to follow-up, supplies, assumptions and whether the chosen indicator reflects the goal.
- **Apparent improvement:** check whether other factors, regression to the mean, seasonal changes or measurement differences may explain it.
- **Missing data:** report the gap; do not treat missing follow-up as success or zero events.
- **Unequal outcomes:** examine whether certain groups face greater barriers or harms.

Correlation does not establish causation. Family-level evaluation is often limited by small numbers and multiple changing factors; state these limitations honestly.

## 8. Reassessment and plan revision

Use findings to decide whether to:
1. continue the intervention as planned;
2. modify the method, timing or support;
3. add a referral or intersectoral partner;
4. change the objective/indicator when it was poorly specified, documenting why;
5. reassess the nursing diagnosis/problem;
6. stop or replace an ineffective or harmful intervention;
7. escalate urgent deterioration or safety concerns.

Reassessment should be timely and proportional. Emergency danger signs require immediate action rather than waiting for the planned evaluation date.

## 9. Documentation and confidentiality

Document:
- goal/objective and indicator;
- baseline and follow-up findings;
- method, date, source and limitations;
- family feedback and identified barriers;
- progress/not met and the evidence supporting that judgment;
- safety concerns, referrals and follow-up;
- agreed changes, responsible person and next review date.

Use objective language; distinguish observed data, reported information and interpretation. Protect identifiable records under applicable privacy law and authorized facility procedures. Do not share individual family results in public community reports unless legally authorized and necessary.

## 10. Philippine community-health application

Use current DOH/LGU program definitions and approved reporting systems when evaluating public-health services. FHSIS and disease-surveillance indicators may have different definitions and scope; do not combine them without stating the source and limitations. RA 10173 is relevant to identifiable data. RA 11223 provides broader UHC context, but it does not define every family-level evaluation measure. [3][4]

Community feedback and equity assessment are essential. If a family cannot act because a service, transport or safe water is unavailable, the evaluation should record the structural barrier and identify a responsible system-level action—not merely mark the family as noncompliant.

## 11. PNLE high-yield distinctions

- Evaluation compares evidence with goals/indicators and informs plan revision.
- Structure/input, process, output, outcome, impact, equity and safety are different levels.
- Activity completed does not equal outcome achieved.
- Indicators require a clear numerator, denominator, period, source and target/baseline.
- Missing data is not success or zero.
- Include family feedback and barriers, not just the nurse's view.
- Reassess when results are poor or circumstances change.
- Emergency danger overrides a routine evaluation schedule.
- Do not infer causation from a simple before/after comparison.
- Protect confidentiality and document objectively.
