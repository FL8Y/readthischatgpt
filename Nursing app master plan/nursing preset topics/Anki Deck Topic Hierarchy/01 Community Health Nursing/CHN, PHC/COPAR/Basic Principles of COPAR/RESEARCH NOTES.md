## Scope and core concept
Community Organizing Participatory Action Research (COPAR) is a community-development approach that builds collective capacity to identify and address community problems. Describe community entry, integration, social investigation, organization-building, mobilization, and phase-out/evaluation as a commonly taught sequence; actual labels and sequencing vary by framework. Emphasize participation, empowerment, critical consciousness, local leadership, and community ownership—not doing activities for the community.

## High-yield learning points
- Use community participation and shared decision-making; avoid treating residents as passive recipients.
- Obtain informed consent, protect confidentiality, and return findings in a usable form.
- Evaluate both process (participation/leadership) and outcomes (health or social indicators).

## Nursing application and clinical reasoning
1. **Assess:** establish the population/client, baseline status, relevant risks, protective factors, and data quality.
2. **Prioritize:** address immediate threats to life and safety first; then consider population impact, preventability, inequity, feasibility, and community priorities. Maslow’s hierarchy may help organize needs but must not override an urgent clinical threat.
3. **Plan and act:** use evidence-based interventions, shared decision-making, culturally appropriate communication, clear roles, and referral pathways.
4. **Evaluate:** compare outcomes with baseline and measurable targets; document gaps, adverse events, and needed revisions.
