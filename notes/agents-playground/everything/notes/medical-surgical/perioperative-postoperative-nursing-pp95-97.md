# Perioperative and Immediate Postoperative Nursing (printed pp. 95–97)

**Source:** Power Development Center for Training • Study & Review, Inc., *Study Guide*, printed pages 95–97. Upload sequence matches page order: image 1 = p. 95, image 2 = p. 96, image 3 = p. 97.

> **Use note:** These are educational study notes, not patient-specific instructions. “Textbook answer” records the photographed quick-review answer. Some answers depend on procedure, patient status, current clinical standards, orders, and local policy.

## 1. Core idea

Immediate postoperative nursing prioritizes airway patency, adequate ventilation and oxygenation, circulation/perfusion, level of consciousness, temperature and pain, wound/drain status, elimination, and early detection of complications. OR counting and team communication aim to prevent retained surgical items and resolve discrepancies before the patient leaves the operating room.

## 2. Detailed source notes

**Count-timing item:** The source says the first sponge/instrument count is reported after abdominal surgery **before the peritoneum is closed**. This single quick-review line is not a complete contemporary count protocol. AORN describes standardized counts, communication and discrepancy resolution; count timing is governed by current policy and the operation. The team should resolve and document discrepancies according to institutional procedure before the patient leaves the OR.

### B. Abdominal closure — p. 95

- **Fascia:** The source associates fascia with sutures requiring long tensile strength, giving cotton, nylon, or silk as examples. Material selection is procedure- and surgeon-specific; retain this as the quiz's association, not universal suture advice.
- **Layer order as printed:** Peritoneum, followed by **muscle, fascia, subcutaneous tissue, skin**.
- **Atraumatic needle:** The source associates this with a patient prone to keloid formation and a low pain threshold. Clarification: an atraumatic needle reduces tissue trauma; it does not itself prevent keloids, which depend on individual and wound-healing factors.
- **Staple:** Another alternative to a suture for skin closure.

### C. Immediate postoperative monitoring and airway — p. 95

- In the PACU, the source says to monitor vital signs **every 15 minutes**.
- The follow-up postoperative assessment schedule printed in the source is: **every 15 minutes for the first half hour, every 30 minutes for 2 hours, every hour for 4 hours, then every 4 hours as needed.** This is a source-specific study schedule, not a universal protocol; follow current standards, patient acuity and orders.
- On arrival from post-anesthesia care/transfer to the surgical unit, the first stated action is **assess the patency of the airway**.
- When positioning a client for surgery, the source lists the priority as **access to the airway**.
- For an unconscious client newly admitted to PACU, with no contraindication to movement, the source gives: **extend the client's chin while on the side and place a pillow at the back.** This is an airway-opening position; protect the cervical spine when trauma is possible and use trained airway maneuvers and monitoring.

### D. Airway, anesthesia recovery and oxygenation — p. 96

- **After endoscopy using xylocaine (lidocaine) spray:** keep the patient **NPO until the gag reflex returns**. Topical pharyngeal anesthesia can impair protective swallowing and raise aspiration risk. Resume oral intake only after appropriate recovery assessment/instructions.
- **Possible sign an airway adjunct may be removed after general anesthesia:** **gagging** (source answer). In practice, airway-device removal depends on airway reflexes, ventilation, oxygenation, consciousness and device type; gagging alone is not a universal removal criterion.
- **After pneumonectomy:** deep tracheal suction should be performed with extreme caution because **the bronchial suture line may be traumatized** (source answer). Invasive suction should be performed only when indicated and by qualified staff using the appropriate technique and thoracic-surgery guidance.
- **Severe postoperative shivering after inhalation anesthesia:** source says **provide oxygen as prescribed**. Assess oxygenation, temperature and overall status; shivering management and warming follow orders/protocol because shivering raises metabolic oxygen demand.
- **Unconscious on PACU admission:** source answer is **lateral position**, when not contraindicated, to support secretion drainage and reduce aspiration risk. Individual airway and cervical-spine considerations take priority.
- **After splenectomy:** the source's priority assessment is **the quality of the client's respiration**.
- **Assessment preventing transfer to ward:** source identifies a **pulse oximeter reading of 80% (abnormal)**. This is critically low and calls for immediate assessment/escalation, not waiting.
- **Pulse oximeter reads 85%:** source says **awaken the patient and have him cough and deep-breathe**. Added safety context: urgently assess airway, respiratory rate/effort, consciousness, circulation and sensor signal; provide respiratory support/oxygen and summon anesthesia or clinical help per protocol. Coaching alone is not a sufficient standalone response to severe desaturation.

### E. Breathing and incentive spirometry — pp. 96–97

| Source prompt | Textbook answer | How to understand it |
|---|---|---|
| Best positioning for incentive spirometer | Head of bed elevated **45–90 degrees**. | Upright positioning supports lung expansion when tolerated. |
| Incentive spirometry has been effective if the patient has… | **Clear breath sounds.** | Breath sounds are one finding; they do not by themselves prove effectiveness. Also consider work of breathing, oxygenation, secretion clearance and trends. |
| Breathing technique | **Inhale through the mouth and hold for 5 seconds, then exhale through the mouth.** | This is the source's instruction. MedlinePlus describes slow inhalation and a 3–5 second hold; follow device/provider instructions. |
| Accurate incentive spirometer technique | **Slow, deep breaths to elevate the spirometer ball.** | Inhale steadily rather than sucking rapidly; follow the device's feedback instructions. |
| Reduce pain during deep breathing/coughing | **Splint the patient's chest with both hands during the exercises.** | Support the incision with hands or a pillow to reduce discomfort and enable an effective cough. |
| Monitor/promote postoperative respiratory status | **Instruct the client and monitor use of the incentive spirometer.** | Teach technique when prescribed; combine with cough, adequate analgesia and early mobilization as appropriate. |

**Source complication chain:** Retained pulmonary secretions after surgery may lead to **pneumonia**. The example of smoking three packs of cigarettes daily for ten years is associated with increased risk of **postoperative respiratory complications**. **Increasing restlessness** is identified as a concerning sign of an evolving complication. Restlessness is nonspecific but may be an early clue to hypoxemia, pain, delirium or deterioration; reassess rather than dismiss it.

### F. Circulation and limb safety — p. 96

- After open reduction and internal fixation (**ORIF**), call the doctor for **a left foot that is cold to touch and has an absent pedal pulse** (source answer). This can indicate impaired perfusion and requires urgent neurovascular reassessment/escalation. Assess color, capillary refill, sensation, movement, swelling and pain compared with baseline/other limb without delaying escalation.
- The source says avoid dangling the foot primarily to prevent **nerve and muscle damage**. This item lacks context in the image; dangling is not universally prohibited after ORIF. Follow the procedure-specific mobility/positioning orders and assess circulation, sensation and pain.
- To prevent accidents when a postoperative client returns to her room, the source says **make sure the side rails are up**. Use appropriate individualized fall precautions and facility policy; also consider bed height, call bell, assistance with mobilization and sedation.

### G. Drains — p. 97

- Action to avoid: **curl the drain tightly and tape it firmly to the body** (source answer). Secure tubing without kinks, tension, compression or obstruction; allow flow and safe movement.
- Expected surgical-site finding listed: **serous drainage**. Serous fluid is typically thin, clear to pale yellow. Consider amount, trend, odor, wound appearance and patient status. Increasing, purulent, foul-smelling or unexpectedly bloody drainage warrants assessment/escalation per protocol.
- To facilitate drainage of operative-site secretions, the source says **turn the client to the operative side every 2–3 hours**. This is procedure-dependent, not universal. Verify operative instructions because positioning after thoracic procedures varies with the specific resection and patient's physiology.

### H. Nutrition — p. 97

- Following tonsillectomy and adenoidectomy, the source says give a **soft diet when fully awake**. Follow surgeon-specific dietary guidance, confirm safe swallowing, and watch for bleeding or repeated swallowing.
- Immediate postoperative purpose of a nasogastric tube (**NGT**): **gastric decompression**, to remove air/contents and reduce distension or vomiting when indicated. NGT placement/use is not routine after every operation; follow orders and the clinical plan.

### I. Elimination — p. 97

- If the client has not voided since before surgery, eight hours earlier, first **assess the client for bladder fullness** (source answer). Also review intake/output, symptoms, medications, fluid balance and follow the postoperative retention protocol; use bladder scanning when indicated/available.
- Parameter to monitor closely: **urinary output of 20 mL/hour** (source answer). This is low for many adults and needs contextual assessment of trend, body size, perfusion, fluid balance, renal function, catheter patency and orders. Do not use one cutoff as a universal rule for every adult.

### J. Positioning — p. 97

- Unconscious on PACU admission: **lateral position** (source answer), if compatible with the operation and other precautions.
- Following tonsillectomy/adenoidectomy: **prone with the head on a pillow and turned to the side** (source answer), to allow secretion drainage; actual positioning should reflect airway, bleeding risk, consciousness and current facility guidance.
- Following pneumonectomy: source says **on the side of surgery**. Verify surgeon's order and exact procedure; do not generalize this positioning to every patient or surgical situation.

### K. Complications and management — p. 97

- Splint the patient's chest with both hands during deep breathing and coughing to reduce pain (source answer).
- Heavy smoking (three packs/day for ten years in the question) increases risk of postoperative respiratory complications.
- Correct spirometer technique: slow, deep inspiration to elevate the indicator/ball.
- **Increasing restlessness** is the source's concerning sign of an evolving complication.
- To monitor/promote postoperative respiratory status, instruct the client and monitor incentive-spirometer use (source answer), with other prescribed pulmonary hygiene and mobility measures as appropriate.
- Retained pulmonary secretions may lead to **pneumonia**.

## 3. Added explanations and external clinical cross-check

The following points are **not claims that the textbook printed them**; they are current/contextual interpretation.

1. **Surgical counts:** AORN emphasizes standardized counts, communication, documentation and resolution of discrepancies before the patient leaves the OR. Required count timing and discrepancy steps follow local policy and the operation. Sources: AORN (2024), “Prevention of Retained Surgical Items: AORN's Surgical Count Guidelines,” https://www.aorn.org/about-aorn/aorn-newsroom/periop-life/article/understanding-retained-surgical-items-%28rsi%29--importance--prevention--and-aorn-guidelines; AORN (2025), Sara Angelilli, “Using Adjunct Technology with the Surgical Count,” https://www.aorn.org/article/using-adjunct-technology-with-the-surgical-count.
2. **PACU recovery:** ASA's *Practice Guidelines for Postanesthetic Care* discuss assessment and management during emergence/recovery and recommend supplemental oxygen during transfer/recovery for patients at risk of hypoxemia. Persistent SpO₂ at 80–85% is a serious abnormality requiring prompt clinical action. Source: https://www.asahq.org/~/media/sites/asahq/files/public/resources/standards-guidelines/practice-guidelines-for-post-anesthetic-care.pdf.
3. **Incentive spirometry:** MedlinePlus describes sitting upright, slow inhalation, a 3–5 second breath hold and incision splinting when helpful: https://www.medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000451.htm. AARC guidance says incentive spirometry alone is not recommended as routine prophylaxis for every postoperative patient; where appropriate it is combined with deep breathing, directed cough, early mobilization and adequate analgesia. Sources: Restrepo et al., *Respiratory Care* (2011), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22008401/; Strickland et al., *Respiratory Care* (2013), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24222709/.
4. **Drains/wounds:** MedlinePlus advises monitoring drain-site redness/swelling, cloudy or foul drainage, sudden cessation of drainage and other changes. Manipulation such as stripping depends on drain type and instructions; do not apply one device's instructions to all drains. Source: https://www.medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000039.htm.
5. **Surgical-site care:** NICE recommends aseptic non-touch technique for dressing changes/removal and structured postoperative wound care, including patient advice about infection signs and whom to contact. Source: NICE NG125, https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng125/chapter/recommendations.

## 5. Priority cues / common exam traps

- For a newly arrived unconscious postoperative patient, prioritize **airway** while assessing breathing/oxygenation and circulation.
- SpO₂ **80% or 85% is not a minor finding**. Rapidly verify signal while immediately assessing and treating the patient and escalating under protocol.
- New restlessness can precede overt deterioration; reassess.
- Cold foot + absent pedal pulse after ORIF is an urgent neurovascular warning.
- “Serous” drainage cannot be judged by color alone; check amount, trend, character, odor, wound and patient status.
- Surgical counts are team-based. Discrepancies require immediate communication and the institutional resolution process.
- Keep textbook quiz answers separate from universal bedside instructions: assessment schedules, thoracic positioning, drain handling and incentive-spirometer indications depend on patient and protocol.

## 6. Quick recap

**OR safety:** scrub + circulating nurse coordinate counts; immediately communicate and resolve discrepancies under policy. **Closure order:** peritoneum → muscle → fascia → subcutaneous tissue → skin. **PACU:** assess airway, breathing, circulation, consciousness, vital-sign trends, pain and temperature; textbook vital-sign interval is every 15 minutes. **Respiratory care:** upright posture when tolerated, slow deep inspiration, brief breath-hold, effective cough with incision splinting, prescribed oxygen and mobility as appropriate. **Circulation:** cold foot + absent pedal pulse after ORIF is urgent. **Drains:** avoid kinks/tension and monitor drainage trend. **Elimination:** assess bladder fullness with delayed voiding and investigate low output. **Safety:** apply individualized fall precautions and follow procedure-specific positioning orders.
