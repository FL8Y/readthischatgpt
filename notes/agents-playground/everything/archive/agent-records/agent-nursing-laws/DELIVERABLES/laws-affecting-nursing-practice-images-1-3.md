## 3. Source notes: continuation of Republic Acts (Image 3)

- **RA 9262** — Anti-Violence Against Women and Their Children Act of 2004.
- **RA 9439** — Prohibiting detention of patients in hospitals/medical clinics on grounds of non-payment of hospital or medical expenses.
- **RA 5901** — An Act prescribing 40 hours/week for government and private hospital or clinic personnel (as printed; check current applicability and legal context).

- **RA 10931** — Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Education Act. The page explains that it allows students to pursue quality college education without tuition and other school fees, provides free access to technical-vocational education and training in public institutions, and offers financial aid and a loan program for students needing additional finances.
- **RA 11223** — Universal Health Care Act. The page states:
  - Filipino citizens are automatically enrolled in the National Health Insurance Program and PhilHealth coverage is expanded to include free medical consultations and laboratory tests.
  - “8 salient points” are introduced, including all Filipinos covered; direct contributors (e.g., employee-employer relationship) and indirect contributors (e.g., dependents).
  - The guide clarifies that care is not completely free: basic service accommodation may mean a regular meal, bed in a shared room with fan ventilation, and shared toilet/bath; essential benefits include primary care, medicines, diagnostics, and laboratory tests; private accommodation, air conditioning, television, and meal choices may cost extra.
  - PhilHealth is described as becoming the “national purchaser” of health goods and services.
  - Health systems are described as city-wide and province-wide, requiring DOH work with DILG.
  - Public-sector return service: graduates of health and health-related courses who received government-funded scholarships are described as required to serve in the public health sector for at least three full years to address workforce needs.
  - A “Health Technology Assessment Council” (HTAC) is described as assessing the safety and effectiveness of health technology, devices, machines, vaccines, health procedures, and related advances.
  - Health information is described as being collected to help address unequal access due to lack of funds in barangays/municipalities.
- **RA 11230** — Tulong-Trabaho Act. The page describes a Philippine labor-force competencies competitiveness program and free access to technical-vocational education and training (TVET), with funds for implementation.

## 4. Added explanation: how to study this page

These pages are primarily a **law-and-title recall list**, not a complete legal explanation. For each item, study it in three parts:

1. **Identifier** — RA/PD/EO/LOI/Board Resolution number and year, where given.
2. **Official title or subject** — verify the official title rather than memorizing a possibly shortened or misprinted description.
3. **Nursing relevance** — professional regulation and ethics; public health and prevention; maternal/child health; hospital/emergency obligations; employment and labor; vulnerable-population protections; health financing and system organization.

Do not assume every item remains current in the form summarized by this older review guide. Amendments, repeals, implementing regulations, court rulings, and later laws may change how an item applies.

## 5. External cross-checks and cautions

These are external legal references, not text extracted from the photographs:

- **RA 9173 is the Philippine Nursing Act of 2002**, approved October 21, 2002. The Senate Legislative Reference Bureau and Supreme Court E-Library provide the statute text: https://issuances-library.senate.gov.ph/legislative%2Bissuances/Republic%20Act%20No.%209173 and https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph//thebookshelf//showdocs/2/1459
- **RA 11223 is the Universal Health Care Act**, approved February 20, 2019. Its text says every Filipino citizen is automatically included in the National Health Insurance Program and provides immediate eligibility/access to defined health services, subject to the law's coverage and implementation framework. This should not be simplified to “all health services are free”: https://issuances-library.senate.gov.ph/legislative%2Bissuances/Republic%20Act%20No.%2011223
- **RA 10354 is the Responsible Parenthood and Reproductive Health Act of 2012**, approved December 21, 2012: https://issuances-library.senate.gov.ph/legislative%2Bissuances/Republic%20Act%20No.%2010354
- Several entries need **official-title/number verification before memorization**, including the RA 679 line in the PD section; the duplicate/ambiguous RA 1082 lines; “EO 2009 — Family Code” alongside EO 209; RA 7885; the RA 8042 amendment reference; and the RA 5901 work-hour description. The photo itself is not enough to resolve these safely.
- The CPD requirements printed in the guide are historical claims. Verify the current Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) and Board of Nursing requirements before treating the listed units as current.
- The statement about retaining health records for 10–25 years is attributed by the guide to a DOH regulation but does not identify the regulation. Do not treat this range as a universal current retention rule without checking the applicable regulation and record category.
