## Research findings / editorial choices
- NCSBN's current delegation page emphasizes jurisdictional differences; notes retain the textbook's five-rights framework and do not imply delegation rules are universal.
- The textbook calls the cycle PDCA (Plan–Do–Check–Act). AHRQ resources also use PDSA (Plan–Do–Study–Act); the notes preserve the textbook label and explain the related variant.
- Pareto's 80/20 rule is a heuristic; actual prioritization depends on data and the selected measure.
- QA/QI distinction is framed as emphasis, not an absolute separation. CMS describes QA and PI as mutually reinforcing.
- No external guidance was used to overwrite the page's claims. External context is labeled separately.
- Visible textbook citations include Zerwekh and Garneau (2018), Berman, Snyder, and Frandsen (2016), and Hospital Nursing Service Administration Manual (2008); full book details are not visible and were not guessed.
