## Research evidence and why it matters
1. **WHO, Surgical Safety Checklist** — https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/patient-safety/9789241598590-eng-checklist.pdf  
   Supports identity/site/procedure/consent verification before induction, known-allergy and airway/aspiration checks, pause before incision and sign-out checks.  
   Related implementation/resources page: https://www.who.int/teams/integrated-health-services/quality-of-care-and-patient-safety/patient-safety-guidance-and-tools/safe-surgery/tool-and-resources
2. **CDC, Clinical Safety: Hand Hygiene for Healthcare Workers** (page dated 2024-02-27) — https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/hcp/clinical-safety/index.html  
   Cross-check for hand hygiene, surgical hand antisepsis, and scrub duration; antimicrobial soap scrub is usually 2–6 minutes and product directions govern, so the textbook's 10–15-minute answer is flagged as outdated/generalized.
3. **CDC, Fundamental Elements Needed to Prevent Transmission** — https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/isolation-precautions/prevention.html  
   Supports hand hygiene as a core infection-prevention practice.
4. **MedlinePlus, Latex allergies — for hospital patients** — https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000499.htm  
   Supports risk factors, relevant medical supplies, and commonly associated foods: bananas, avocado, chestnuts; apples have a weaker association.
5. **MedlinePlus, Managing latex allergies at home** — https://www.medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000500.htm  
   Supports latex-containing common products including balloons and condoms and latex-fruit association.
6. **American College of Surgeons, Medication and Surgery** — https://www.facs.org/for-patients/preparing-for-surgery/medications/  
   Supports individualized planning for aspirin/antiplatelet medication before surgery; the review-book prompt must not be turned into a universal patient instruction.
7. **MHAUS, Management of MH-susceptible patients FAQs** — https://www.mhaus.org/faqs/category/frequently-asked-questions-about/management-of-mh-susceptible-patients-faqs/  
   Supports treating a patient with family MH history as potentially susceptible until evaluated and planning a non-triggering anesthetic approach as appropriate.
8. **MHAUS FAQs** — https://www.mhaus.org/faqs/  
   Supports the need for MH preparedness and emergency supplies/protocol.
9. **DailyMed, Vecuronium bromide injection** — https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=aa35d139-25c8-4802-b165-0db7d1c08913  
   Current label (search result reports update 2025-11-24) warns that it must be administered by trained professionals aware of its hazards; used to contextualize respiratory monitoring and paralysis.
10. **ASA, Practice Guidelines for Preoperative Fasting** — https://www.asahq.org/~/media/sites/asahq/files/public/resources/standards-guidelines/practice-guidelines-for-preoperative-fasting.pdf  
    Used only to emphasize that fasting is protocol- and patient-specific; selected healthy elective patients may have a 2-hour clear-liquid minimum, not a universal recommendation.
11. **CDC, Surgical Site Infection Basics** — https://www.cdc.gov/surgical-site-infections/index.html  
    Supports avoiding routine operative-site shaving. The current patient guidance says not to shave near the surgical site; the 2022 prevention update says hair should not be removed unless it interferes with surgery and, if needed, clippers or an appropriate depilatory should be used under protocol. This qualifies the textbook's brief “clipped” answer.
12. **Joint Commission, Universal Protocol site-marking FAQ (2026)** — https://www.jointcommission.org/en/knowledge-library/support-center/standards-interpretation/standards-faqs/000001461  
    Checked to contextualize the book's Universal Protocol quiz item; current emphasis is preprocedure verification, site marking when indicated, and a team time-out immediately before incision—not recording a video of the whole operation.
