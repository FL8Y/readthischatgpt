## 1. Adult cardiac arrest algorithm — p. 62

### Initial step and rhythm split
Step 1: Start CPR, give oxygen, and attach a monitor/defibrillator. Assess whether the rhythm is shockable.
- **Shockable:** ventricular fibrillation (VF) or pulseless ventricular tachycardia (pVT).
- **Non-shockable:** asystole or pulseless electrical activity (PEA).
- Reassess rhythm during the repeated 2-minute CPR cycles. If ROSC occurs, transition to post-cardiac-arrest care.

### Flowchart sequence as visible in the image
| Step | Pathway / decision | Actions printed in the chart |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Start CPR | Give oxygen; attach monitor/defibrillator. |
| 2 | Shockable rhythm: VF/pVT | Proceed to shock. |
| 3 | Shock | Defibrillate. |
| 4 | First post-shock cycle | CPR 2 minutes; IV/IO access; reassess rhythm. |
| 5 | Rhythm remains shockable | Shock; CPR 2 minutes; epinephrine every 3–5 minutes; consider advanced airway and capnography. |
| 6 | Rhythm reassessment | If shockable, proceed to shock. |
| 7 | Further shockable cycle | Shock; CPR 2 minutes; amiodarone; treat reversible causes. |
| 8 | Continuing shockable-cycle loop | Lower-left number/arrow placement is crowded; the chart shows continuation to rhythm reassessment/ongoing resuscitation, but no distinct independent action can be read at the numeral itself. |
| 9 | Non-shockable rhythm: asystole/PEA | Enter non-shockable pathway; do not defibrillate asystole/PEA. |
| 10 | Non-shockable CPR cycle | CPR 2 minutes; IV/IO access; epinephrine every 3–5 minutes; consider advanced airway and capnography; reassess rhythm. |
| 11 | Rhythm remains non-shockable | CPR 2 minutes; treat reversible causes; reassess rhythm. If it becomes shockable, chart says “Go to 5 or 7.” |
| 12 | ROSC decision | If no signs of ROSC, return to step 10 or 11 as appropriate; if ROSC, go to post-cardiac-arrest care. |

**Diagram logic:** Start CPR → rhythm check. “Yes” leads to VF/pVT and repeated shock/CPR/rhythm-check cycles. “No” leads to asystole/PEA and CPR/epinephrine/reversible-cause management. Both pathways loop back to rhythm assessment. ROSC redirects to post-cardiac-arrest care.

### Terms
- **CPR:** cardiopulmonary resuscitation; the chart uses 2-minute cycles before rhythm reassessment.
- **VF:** ventricular fibrillation.
- **pVT:** pulseless ventricular tachycardia.
- **Asystole:** no discernible ventricular electrical activity; non-shockable.
- **PEA:** electrical activity without a palpable pulse; non-shockable.
- **IV/IO:** intravenous/intraosseous access.
- **Epinephrine:** the chart places it every 3–5 minutes during CPR.
- **Advanced airway/capnography:** considerations listed during CPR.
- **Amiodarone:** listed later in the shockable pathway.
- **Reversible causes:** the chart says to treat them but does not enumerate them.

### External clinical cross-check (not text from the photographed page)
The AHA 2025 adult cardiac arrest algorithm retains the shockable VF/pVT versus non-shockable asystole/PEA distinction and includes CPR cycles, epinephrine, antiarrhythmic therapy for refractory shockable rhythms, airway/capnography considerations, and treatment of reversible causes. Use current local protocols and trained clinical supervision; this study note is not a medication or defibrillation order. Sources: [AHA 2025 algorithms](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/algorithms/) and [2025 Adult Advanced Life Support guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001376?download=true).

**Safety distinction:** Do not shock asystole or PEA. The photographed page does not show energy settings, drug doses, or the complete reversible-cause list, so none are attributed to the textbook.

## 2. WHO Surgical Safety Checklist — pp. 63–64

The table has three phases: **Sign In** before induction of anaesthesia; **Time Out** before skin incision; **Sign Out** before the patient leaves the operating room.

### A. Sign In — before induction of anaesthesia
- Patient confirms identity (name, DOB), site, procedure, and consent.
- Site marked / not applicable.
- Anaesthesia safety check completed.
- Pulse oximeter on patient and functioning.
- Known allergy? No / Yes.
- Difficult airway/aspiration risk? No / Yes, and equipment/assistance available.
- Risk of >500 mL blood loss (7 mL/kg in children)? No / Yes, and adequate IV access and fluids planned.

### B. Time Out — before skin incision
- Confirm all team members introduced themselves by name and role.
- Surgeon, anaesthesia professional, and nurse verbally confirm patient, site, and procedure.
- **Anticipated critical events:**
  - **Surgeon reviews:** critical or unexpected steps, operative duration, anticipated blood loss.
  - **Anaesthesia team reviews:** patient-specific concerns.
  - **Nursing team reviews:** sterility (including indicator results) confirmed; equipment issues/concerns.
- Antibiotic prophylaxis given within the last 60 minutes? Yes / Not applicable.
- Is essential imaging displayed? Yes / Not applicable (visible on p. 64 continuation).

### C. Sign Out — before patient leaves the operating room
Nurse verbally confirms with the team:
- Name of the procedure recorded.
- Instrument, sponge, and needle counts are correct (or not applicable).
- How the specimen is labelled, including patient name.
- Whether any equipment problems need to be addressed.
- Surgeon, anaesthesia professional, and nurse review key concerns for recovery and management.

### Meaning and memory cue
WHO describes the checklist as a tool to reduce errors/adverse events and improve teamwork/communication. The three checkpoints occur at different stages; they are not interchangeable. **Sign In:** patient identity, site/procedure/consent, anaesthesia and patient risks. **Time Out:** team confirmation and anticipated problems before incision. **Sign Out:** procedure record, counts/specimen/equipment, and recovery concerns. Sources: [WHO checklist resources](https://www.who.int/teams/integrated-health-services/quality-of-care-and-patient-safety/patient-safety-guidance-and-tools/safe-surgery/tool-and-resources) and [WHO implementation manual](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241598590).

## 3. Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) — p. 64

### Source table transcribed
| Component | Response as printed in the study guide | Score |
|---|---|---:|
| Eye opening | Spontaneous | 4 |
| Eye opening | To verbal command | 3 |
| Eye opening | To pain | 2 |
| Eye opening | No response | 1 |
| Motor response | To verbal command | 6 |
| Motor response | To localized pain | 5 |
| Motor response | Flexes and withdraws | 4 |
| Motor response | Flexes abnormally | 3 |
| Motor response | Extends abnormally | 2 |
| Motor response | No response | 1 |
| Verbal response | Oriented, converses | 5 |
| Verbal response | Disoriented, converses | 4 |
| Verbal response | Uses inappropriate words | 3 |
| Verbal response | Makes incomprehensible sounds | 2 |
| Verbal response | No response | 1 |

### Scoring and interpretation
- Eye (E): 1–4; verbal (V): 1–5; motor (M): 1–6.
- Total GCS = E + V + M. Minimum 3; maximum 15.
- Record components separately as well as the total when possible; different component patterns can produce the same total.
- The source paragraph states that 3 is the lowest/least responsive score and 15 the highest/most responsive score.
- The source says GCS 3–8 is generally accepted as indicating severe head injury.
- The source calls GCS a sensitive indicator of a lapse in neurologic functioning in TBI and says it is often an early sign of acute change in intracranial pressure (ICP).

The above severity/ICP statements are preserved as claims from the study guide; they are not stand-alone diagnoses.

### External cross-check: modern structured terminology
The official Glasgow Coma Scale site describes eye, verbal, and motor components and recommends a structured approach: check for factors interfering with assessment, observe spontaneous behavior, stimulate when needed, then rate. Modern terminology uses “to sound” and “to pressure” instead of the older “to verbal command” and “to pain” for eye opening; some verbal labels are also simplified. This is an external terminology update, not a silent rewrite of the photographed table. Sources: [official GCS site](https://www.glasgowcomascale.org/) and [GCS Aid](https://www.glasgowcomascale.org/gcs-aid/).

### Interpretation cautions
- Consider confounders such as sedation, intubation, language/hearing barriers, eye/facial injury, or paralysis under local policy.
- Track changes over time and interpret the score with the wider neurological assessment and clinical context.
- Use current structured assessment technique and local protocol; do not use a single score alone to determine treatment or prognosis.
