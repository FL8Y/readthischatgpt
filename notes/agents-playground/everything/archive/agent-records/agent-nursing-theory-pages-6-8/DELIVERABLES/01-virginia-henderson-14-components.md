# Virginia Henderson's Need Theory: 14 Components

**Source:** upload 1, printed study-guide page 6; adjacent context on page 7. Educational notes, not patient-specific advice.

## Source notes

The page is titled “14 Components of Virginia Henderson's Nursing Need Theory.” All fourteen items, in their original order:

1. Breathe normally.
2. Eat and drink adequately.
3. Eliminate body wastes.
4. Move and maintain desirable postures.
5. Sleep and rest.
6. Select suitable clothes; dress and undress.
7. Maintain body temperature within a normal range by adjusting clothing and modifying the environment.
8. Keep the body clean and well groomed, and protect the integument.
9. Avoid dangers in the environment and avoid injuring others.
10. Communicate with others in expressing emotions, needs, fears, or opinions.
11. Worship according to one's faith.
12. Work in such a way that there is a sense of accomplishment.
13. Play or participate in various forms of recreation.
14. Learn, discover, or satisfy curiosity that leads to normal development and health, and use available health facilities.

The photograph lays out items 1–7 in one group and 8–14 in another. The adjacent printed page 7 describes Henderson's theory as “14 Fundamental/Basic Needs” and says it concerns healthy and ill individuals gaining independence.

## Added explanation (not wording from the source)

Henderson's need-oriented framework helps nurses assess what a person can do independently and where assistance is needed. The overall direction is to support attainable independence, while respecting the person's condition, preferences, dignity, and goals.

**Memory grouping (study aid only):**
- Physical maintenance (1–8): breathing; food/fluid; elimination; movement/posture; sleep/rest; clothing; temperature; hygiene/skin.
- Safety and communication (9–10).
- Meaning, purpose, and growth (11–14): faith; accomplishment; recreation; learning and use of health services.

These are connected needs, not isolated boxes. A single concern such as pain can affect movement, sleep, communication, and learning. Assess each need individually rather than assuming dependence in one area means dependence in all areas.

**Example:** A postoperative patient might need help with mobility, dressing, hygiene, sleep, and discharge learning but still be able to eat independently. Document the different level of independence for each need.

## High-value distinctions

- These are fundamental human needs, not medical diagnoses.
- Assistance now does not mean permanent dependence.
- Spiritual care means respecting the patient's own faith or choice, not imposing the nurse's beliefs.
- Learning includes understanding and use of health resources, not merely handing out instructions.

## Retrieval check

1. Which component addresses preventing harm to the patient and others? **Answer:** No. 9.
2. Which item concerns a sense of accomplishment? **Answer:** No. 12.
3. What is the framework's overall direction? **Answer:** Support independence in meeting basic needs as far as possible.
4. Why are items 10 and 11 different? **Answer:** No. 10 is communication of emotions/needs/fears/opinions; no. 11 is worship according to one's faith.
5. A patient can eat independently but needs bathing assistance. How should this be recorded? **Answer:** Assess each need separately; do not label the person globally dependent.
