# Clinical Cross-Checks — Printed Pages 71–73

These sources are additions to the textbook-derived notes, not evidence that the same wording appeared in the photographed book. Access checked 18 October 2026.

## Leptospirosis

1. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). **Clinical Overview of Leptospirosis.** Updated 2026. https://www.cdc.gov/leptospirosis/hcp/clinical-overview/index.html  
   Supports: zoonotic exposure and current treatment principle that suspected cases should be treated promptly; mild and severe disease regimens differ. The book’s IP and older test/management list remain in the source-notes section.

2. CDC. **About Leptospirosis.** https://www.cdc.gov/leptospirosis/index.html  
   Supports: infected animal urine can contaminate water/soil; doxycycline or penicillin are among treatment examples and IV antibiotics may be needed for severe cases.

3. World Health Organization (WHO). **Leptospirosis Outbreak Toolbox.** Updated October 2024. https://www.who.int/emergencies/outbreak-toolkit/disease-outbreak-toolkits/leptospirosis-outbreak-toolbox  
   Supports: relevant manifestations include abrupt fever/chills, myalgia, jaundice, renal/cardiac failure and pulmonary hemorrhage.

## Leprosy / Hansen disease

4. WHO. **Leprosy — Fact Sheet.** https://www.who.int/en/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/leprosy  
   Supports: current WHO MDT consists of rifampicin, dapsone and clofazimine, with 6-month PB and 12-month MB durations; definitions/classification emphasize lesion count, nerve involvement and smear evidence. This is the main cross-check for the photographed PB table that omits clofazimine.

5. WHO. **WHO recommended multidrug therapy (MDT) regimens for leprosy.** https://www.who.int/teams/control-of-neglected-tropical-diseases/leprosy/  
   Supports: combination therapy, monthly rifampicin, daily clofazimine context, and medication counseling considerations.

## Rabies

6. WHO. **Rabies vaccines and immunization / post-exposure prophylaxis.** https://www.who.int/teams/control-of-neglected-tropical-diseases/rabies/vaccinations-and-immunization  
   Supports: all potentially significant exposures require urgent assessment; wound wash/flush for a minimum of 15 minutes; PEP includes wound treatment, vaccine and rabies immunoglobulin when indicated.

7. WHO. **Rabies — Fact Sheet.** https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies  
   Supports: PEP exposure categories, wound washing, vaccination and immunoglobulin/monoclonal antibody indications.

8. CDC. **Rabies Post-exposure Prophylaxis Guidance.** https://www.cdc.gov/rabies/hcp/clinical-care/post-exposure-prophylaxis.html  
   Supports: wound cleansing is immediate; vaccine/HRIG regimen must follow an applicable national recommendation. CDC schedule is jurisdiction-specific and should not be pasted over Philippine protocols.

## Influenza

9. CDC. **Treatment of Flu.** Updated 2 September 2025. https://www.cdc.gov/flu/treatment/  
   Supports: antivirals work best when started early, ideally within 48 hours; priority groups include severe/high-risk illness; antibiotics do not treat influenza viruses.

10. CDC. **Signs and Symptoms of Flu.** https://www.cdc.gov/flu/signs-symptoms/  
    Supports: sudden fever/chills, respiratory and systemic manifestations, complications including pneumonia, myocarditis, encephalitis, and Reye syndrome context.

11. CDC. **Preventing Seasonal Flu.** https://www.cdc.gov/flu/prevention/  
    Supports: vaccination as a key prevention strategy and pediatric aspirin caution due to Reye syndrome concern.

## Pneumonia

12. CDC. **About Pneumonia.** Updated 25 February 2026. https://www.cdc.gov/pneumonia/about/  
    Supports: pneumonia has multiple possible causes, and community and healthcare settings expose patients to different organisms; the agent is not always identified.

13. WHO. **Pneumonia.** https://www.who.int/health-topics/pneumonia/  
    Supports: alveolar fluid/exudate can limit oxygen intake; causes can be bacterial or viral; vaccination and environmental health contribute to prevention.

14. WHO. **Pneumonia in children.** 11 November 2022. https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/pneumonia  
    Supports: pneumonia may be caused by viruses, bacteria or fungi and prevention includes immunization, nutrition and addressing environmental factors. Pediatric-specific content is used only for general background, not adult treatment dosing.

## Malaria

15. WHO. **WHO guidelines for malaria.** Current guideline page, updated 10 September 2026 at time of access. https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/guidelines-for-malaria/  
    Supports: malaria recommendations are revised periodically; check current recommendations rather than relying on the simplified source-era scheme.

16. WHO. **Malaria — Fact Sheet.** Updated 4 December 2025. https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/malaria  
    Supports: *Plasmodium* species, female *Anopheles* transmission, clinical spectrum and urgent risk from severe falciparum malaria.

## Dengue

17. WHO. **Dengue and severe dengue — Fact Sheet.** 21 August 2025. https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/dengue-and-severe-dengue  
    Supports: DENV mosquito transmission, four serotypes, warning signs, risk of severe disease, and prevention by mosquito control.

18. CDC. **Manage Dengue.** https://www.cdc.gov/dengue/treatment/index.html  
    Supports: no specific dengue medicine; hydration, paracetamol/acetaminophen, avoid aspirin/ibuprofen, and urgent care for warning signs.

19. CDC. **Clinical Care of Dengue.** https://www.cdc.gov/dengue/hcp/clinical-care/index.html  
    Supports: monitoring, oral fluids for appropriate non-severe cases, follow-up, and avoidance of aspirin/NSAIDs.

## Use and limitations

- Guidance is jurisdiction-specific. For clinical practice in the Philippines, confirm current DOH / local infectious-disease protocols and clinician orders.
- Web page dates and titles are listed as available on access day; source links may be updated in the future.
- These are targeted cross-checks, not a systematic review and not a qualified nursing-review sign-off.
