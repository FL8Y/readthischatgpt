## External research and what it supports
1. DailyMed, buspirone label (accessed current entry updated June 2026): buspirone is not chemically/pharmacologically related to benzodiazepines/barbiturates. Used to qualify the table's “nonbenzodiazepine” label and avoid implying rapid sedative equivalence. https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=2c516eec-65d9-4481-8823-ae6b6da84062
2. DailyMed, meprobamate label (updated July 8, 2026 in accessed entry): dependence/abuse, withdrawal after abrupt discontinuation following prolonged excessive use, impaired driving, additive depressant effects. Supports a current safety caveat for a source-era medication row. https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=17d814e2-b277-4dce-8615-e7fd7cab773f
3. National Institute of Mental Health, “Schizophrenia”: contemporary psychotic, negative, and cognitive symptom groups; describes hallucinations, delusions, thought disorder, avolition, reduced pleasure, social withdrawal, and diminished emotional expression. Used to clarify (not silently replace) the source's “positive/hard” versus “negative/soft” columns. https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/schizophrenia
4. NICE CG178, “Psychosis and schizophrenia in adults: prevention and management”: monitoring for extrapyramidal side effects, tardive dyskinesia/dystonia/akathisia, weight, blood pressure, glucose/HbA1c, lipids, prolactin, and ECG when indicated. Supports additional context around antipsychotic safety. https://www.nice.org.uk/Guidance/CG178/chapter/recommendations
5. StatPearls, “Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome,” NCBI Bookshelf, last updated April 24, 2023: NMS can be life-threatening; typical features include mental-status change, fever, rigidity, autonomic instability; stop causative drug and provide urgent supportive care. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482282/
6. MedlinePlus, “Tardive dyskinesia”: involuntary movements associated with neuroleptic/antipsychotic medications, often after months or years but possibly earlier. https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000685.htm
7. DailyMed, lithium carbonate prescribing information (accessed current entry updated November 2025): toxic concentrations ≥1.5 mEq/L are close to therapeutic range; early GI and neurologic symptoms, possible delay while lithium distributes into brain tissue, kidney clearance, and interaction factors. Supports caution that symptoms and serum level must be assessed together. https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b839ff4b-f62d-41ab-a823-550a756d58ec
8. EXTRIP Workgroup systematic review/recommendations for lithium poisoning (2015): hemodialysis for selected severe poisonings; decisions consider kidney function, neurologic/cardiac manifestations, levels, clearance, and clinical status. This helps identify older source recommendations (gastric lavage/aminophylline/mannitol/urea) as not suitable for routine independent care. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4422246/
9. Merck Manual Professional Edition, “Overview of Personality Disorders”: current DSM-5-TR cluster membership and core trait distinctions; emphasizes assessment of pervasive patterns and functional impairment. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/psychiatric-disorders/personality-disorders/overview-of-personality-disorders
10. Open RN, “Personality Disorders — Nursing: Mental Health and Community Concepts,” NCBI Bookshelf: nursing education descriptions for personality-disorder categories and symptoms. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK617009/
