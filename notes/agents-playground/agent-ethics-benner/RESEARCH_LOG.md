## Research conclusions and caution
- The image contains 11 named values/concepts on p. 11 and 3 on p. 12, for 14 total. The six widely taught principles are autonomy, nonmaleficence, beneficence, justice, fidelity, and veracity; the image also includes accountability, responsibility, advocacy, confidentiality, epikia, paternalism, altruism, and utilitarianism.
- Related terms overlap but are not interchangeable: accountability is answerability for actions; responsibility is the duty/role obligation to perform tasks and may entail accountability.
- The image’s epikia example mentions restraint in an emergency. That is preserved as source content, not presented as universal clinical authorization. In actual care, restraint is a high-risk, least-restrictive intervention governed by law, policy, patient rights, and required assessment/monitoring; follow local protocol and authorized clinical direction.
- Benner’s five-stage model is a developmental framework, not a guarantee that years of service automatically produce expertise. Clinical experience, reflection, context, education, and feedback matter.
- Benner’s expert intuition is not supernatural or a substitute for analysis: expert pattern recognition uses accumulated clinical experience, and nurses should validate impressions against assessment findings, patient history, current evidence, and safety standards.
- “Epikia” is a less commonly discussed term than the six core principles; the exact meaning is presented according to the supplied study guide, with the ethical/legal caveat clearly separated.
